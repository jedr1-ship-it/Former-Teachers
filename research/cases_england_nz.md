# Case research: England (Return to Teaching pilot; RTTA service) and New Zealand (Returning to Teaching pathway)

Prepared for "Former Teachers: Barriers, Incentives and Policy Lessons for Re-entry". Research date: 4 August 2026.
All facts below were verified against the sources cited. Items that could not be verified are marked [UNVERIFIED].

## 1. Return to Teaching pilot programme (England, 2015–16)

### 1.1 Identification

- **Official name:** Return to Teaching pilot programme.
- **Responsible body:** National College for Teaching and Leadership (NCTL), then an executive agency of the Department for Education (DfE). The NCTL "established the Return to Teaching pilot programme in 2015" (Buchanan et al., 2018, p. 9). No dedicated legal instrument; delivered through grant funding to schools.
- **Timeline:** Schools and returners recruited autumn 2015 (grant bidding round); 60 school partnerships offered support from January 2016; support ran January–October 2016 (in March 2016 the pilot was extended from end-March to mid-July, with continued support allowed into the autumn term) (Buchanan et al., 2018, pp. 16–18).
- **Focus:** returning qualified teachers to **EBacc subjects** (English, maths, history/geography, the sciences, computer science, modern foreign languages) in state-funded **secondary** schools.

### 1.2 Eligibility (verified, Appendix A of the evaluation)

Participants had to: hold QTS (or QTLS plus Education and Training Foundation membership); not currently hold a permanent or fixed-term teaching post in a state-maintained or independent school/college in England; not be barred from teaching. The definition of "returner" was broad — it included teachers who qualified but **never taught**, primary-trained teachers, teachers from the independent or FE/HE sectors, supply teachers without a contract, teachers who taught overseas, and overseas-trained teachers holding QTS (Buchanan et al., 2018, p. 95). Teachers on maternity/paternity leave were excluded (still under contract).

### 1.3 Incentives offered

Non-financial only, delivered by grant-funded partnerships of School Direct schools that had under-recruited in EBacc subjects: subject-knowledge training modelled on Teacher Subject Specialism Training (TSST), refresher training (classroom practice, behaviour management, assessment), classroom experience and mentoring. Free to participants; **no payments or bursaries to returners**. Promoted through a national marketing campaign and the Return to Teaching registration website (Buchanan et al., 2018, pp. 16–17). Grant payments to schools totalled **£586,900** (average £1,425 per participant who started training) (ibid., p. 56).

### 1.4 Scale: targets vs actuals

| Indicator | Target | Actual |
|---|---|---|
| Registrations of interest | ~3,000 | **5,729** (by summer 2016) |
| Returners supported | ~650 supported and returning | **354** supported Jan–Sep 2016 (426 recruited; 412 started training) |
| In permanent EBacc post in a state-funded secondary school by Nov 2016 (2016 School Workforce Census) | ~650 | **27** (8% of supported) |
| Any state-funded teaching post by Nov 2016 | — | **62** total (18%), i.e. 27 + 35 in non-permanent/non-EBacc/non-secondary posts |

Around 4% of *non-supported* registrants returned over the same period, but the evaluators warn this comparison overstates programme impact because supported returners were more certain of returning to begin with (Buchanan et al., 2018, pp. 11–12).

### 1.5 Results and value for money (NFER 2018 — the actual findings)

- **Cost-effectiveness:** average cost per supported returner employed = **£17,800**; assuming half would have returned anyway (deadweight), cost per *additional* returner in a permanent EBacc secondary post = **£37,400** — "similar to or higher than the average cost of training a new teacher" (£33,300–£46,900 per trainee employed after one year, per Allen et al., 2016, as cited in the evaluation) (Buchanan et al., 2018, pp. 56–58).
- **Retention penalty:** returners retain worse than new teachers — of the 2011 returner cohort, 32% were out of service four years later (plus 2% retired) vs 29% of the 2011 NQT cohort; DfE analysis found the odds of returners leaving after their first year back are 60% higher than average (ibid., p. 57).
- **Headline conclusion:** "we find that the pilot was **unlikely to represent good value for money**", driven by timing (support delivered after the hiring cycle), recruitment challenges, suitability of some participants, and lack of practical teaching opportunities in some schools (ibid., p. 91).
- **Positive lessons:** demand vastly exceeded expectations (registrations nearly double target); five returner types identified (idealists 35%, pragmatists 24%, opportunists 16%, movers 13%, career breakers 11%); **career breakers returned at double the rate** of other groups and are best served by an offer of part-time work; a lack of flexible/part-time posts was a key barrier; national advertising played a small role relative to online search (Buchanan et al., 2018, pp. 10–12, 89–92; Worth and Sharp, 2018).
- Context data used by NFER: ~243,900 qualified teachers under 60 out of service in 2016; annual returners to the state-funded sector rose from 16,010 (2011) to 17,230 (2016) (Buchanan et al., 2018, p. 16).

### 1.6 Status and succession

Ended 2016. In November 2016 the NCTL announced the successor **Returners Engagement Programme (REP) pilot** — school-led, employment-focused support restricted to **maths, physics and MFL**, run in the North-West and South-East, with cohort 2 applications in January–February 2017; schools had to tie funded places to actual vacancies, directly addressing the 2015–16 pilot's timing/jobs mismatch (NCTL, 2017; Buchanan et al., 2018, p. 18).

### 1.7 Corrections/additions to the Excel

- The Excel's description is accurate but incomplete: it omits the delivery body (NCTL, not DfE directly), all quantitative results and the cost figure. Not truncated.
- Vague sources resolved: "2018 NFER Evaluation Report" = Buchanan et al. (2018); "2018 NFER Blog" = Worth and Sharp (2018), 11 July 2018 (full citations in bibliography).

### 1.8 FILTER VERDICT: **KEEP**

One of very few re-entry programmes anywhere with a full independent evaluation including a cost-per-additional-returner estimate; the negative value-for-money finding is itself a core policy lesson for the paper.

## 2. Return to Teaching Adviser / Return to Teaching Advisory (RTTA) service (England, c. 2019–present)

### 2.1 Identification

- **Official name:** DfE uses both "Return to Teaching Advisory (RTTA) service" (UK Parliament, 2022) and "return to teaching adviser" service (DfE, 2025a). Run by the **Department for Education** as part of the Get Into Teaching recruitment infrastructure. No legal instrument; an administrative service.
- **Lineage (verified):** Return to Teaching pilot (2015–16) → Returners Engagement Programme pilot (announced Nov 2016, cohorts from 2017) → adviser-based "Return to Teaching" programme restricted to **maths, physics and MFL** (DfE reports costs from 2019/20) → expanded to **all secondary subjects** in autumn 2022 ("The Department has recently expanded the remit of the RTTA service and is now offering support to former teachers considering returning to teach any secondary subject", UK Parliament, 2022, answered 11 October 2022) → by August 2023 covering **primary and secondary**, including UK-trained teachers overseas and overseas-trained teachers (DfE, 2023). A precise launch date for the adviser service itself is not published; the earliest costed years in the parliamentary record are 2019/20 [the 2018 start sometimes claimed elsewhere is UNVERIFIED].

### 2.2 Eligibility and offer

Free, one-to-one support from a named adviser (phone, text or email) for qualified teachers considering a return to a state-funded school in England: application and interview preparation, CV/personal statements, signposting to subject-knowledge and refresher courses, NPQs, vacancies (via Teaching Vacancies) and themed webinars (DfE, 2023; DfE, 2025a). Eligibility: previous teaching or teacher training in the UK; overseas-qualified applicants need QTS, at least one term of paid UK teaching experience and a Teacher Reference Number (DfE, 2025a). **Non-financial only** — England has no return-specific bursary; DfE bursaries (up to £27,000–£29,000 in 2023) and targeted retention incentives (up to £6,000/yr) are for trainees and early-career teachers respectively, not returners as such (DfE, 2023; GOV.UK targeted retention incentive guidance).

### 2.3 Scale, cost and results

The best public data is a parliamentary answer (UK Parliament, 2022):

- **Cost:** £361,344.69 in 2019/20; £325,562.73 in 2020/21.
- **Recorded returns while subject-restricted:** 2019/20 — maths 25, physics 8, MFL 29; 2020/21 — maths 24, physics 15, MFL 27 (≈128 recorded returns over two years; DfE notes returns in other subjects and unreported returns are not captured).
- Schools Week's analysis: **only 23 physics returners in two years**, from over 111 physics leads given an adviser; more than 600 former teachers across the three subjects received an adviser 2019–21 (Walker, 2022). Implied cost per recorded returner ≈ £5,400 [own calculation from the two figures above — far cheaper per head than the 2015–16 pilot, but additionality is unknown].
- Context: c. **17,000 teachers return to state schools each year, about one-third of all new entrants** (DfE, 2023).
- **No independent evaluation of the RTTA service has been published** (nothing found on GOV.UK, NFER or in the parliamentary record).

### 2.4 Status as of 2026

Active: the Teaching Vacancies jobseeker guide (updated 28 August 2025) and the Get Into Teaching return-to-teaching pages still offer adviser sign-up (DfE, 2025a). In a written statement of 1 July 2026 the Education Secretary reported that "More teachers are returning to state schools than at any point in the last ten years" (UK Parliament, 2026) — no returner-specific successor scheme or return bursary was announced; the post-2024 government's workforce package (pay awards, Teacher Training Entitlement, maternity-pay doubling, headteacher retention incentive pilot) is general, not returner-specific.

### 2.5 Corrections/additions to the Excel

- Excel entry is correct but very thin; "Education Hub" resolved to DfE (2023), The Education Hub blog, 9 August 2023. Add the cost/uptake data from UK Parliament (2022) and the lineage from the two pilots — the Excel treats the pilot and RTTA as unrelated entries, but RTTA is the direct descendant of the pilot the paper already covers.

### 2.6 FILTER VERDICT: **KEEP**

The standing national re-entry support service of a major system, with verifiable cost and (partial) outcome data showing cheap-but-low-conversion advisory support — the natural policy contrast to the expensive 2015–16 pilot; merge the two England entries into one narrative arc.

## 3. Returning to Teaching pathway (New Zealand), incl. TER waiver and Teacher Bonding Scheme

### 3.1 Identification — correction first

**"Returning to Teaching pathway" is not an official programme name.** It is the team's label for the Ministry of Education's "Returning to teaching" guidance on its Education Workforce site plus a bundle of distinct instruments: (a) a statutory relief-teaching allowance, (b) a Teacher Education Refresh (TER) waiver for returning relief teachers, (c) government payment of certification fees, (d) the Teacher Bonding Scheme, and (e) overseas-relocation incentives. Responsible bodies: **Ministry of Education (Te Tāhuhu o te Mātauranga)** for funding/incentives; **Teaching Council of Aotearoa New Zealand** for registration and certification rules.

### 3.2 The instruments (verified)

**(a) 20-half-days relief teaching allowance (statutory).** A registered teacher whose practising certificate has expired may relief teach up to **20 half-days per calendar year** without a practising certificate; the Teaching Council can grant an "Extension to Teach" beyond that where there is an employment offer. Statutory basis: Education and Training Act 2020, Schedule 3 (the Council's registration policy cites Schedule 3, clause 10, subclause 9) (Teaching Council, 2026; Ministry of Education, 2026a). This is a standing rule, not a shortage-response measure.

**(b) TER waiver for returning relief teachers.** Normally, teachers with no teaching in the previous five or more years must complete a Teacher Education Refresh programme to re-certificate. Under the waiver, a returner **with an offer of relief-teaching employment does not need TER even after 5+ years out**; instead they may be asked to complete a light-touch "Return to Practice Plan", now renamed **"Kia Maia | Future-Ready Teaching Plan"**, agreed with their principal (Ministry of Education, 2025a; Teaching Council, 2025). The offer was time-limited and repeatedly extended: indexed versions of the Ministry's pages state it applied to certificates expired 12+ months before 6 September 2024 and was "further extended" to expire **31 December 2025**; the pages as updated 18 June 2026 present the exemption with **no expiry date**, and the Teaching Council's registration policy (2026 TER update) now makes TER contingent mainly on **not having a job offer** — i.e., the waiver has effectively been mainstreamed (Ministry of Education, 2026a; Teaching Council, 2026). The Open Polytechnic TER programme is "closed to new learners" with teach-out deadlines in 2026 (Open Polytechnic, n.d.). The exact date the waiver was first introduced was not verified [UNVERIFIED].

**(c) Fees paid by government.** The government pays practising-certificate fees and levies for issue/renewal until **30 June 2028** (Ministry of Education, 2026a).

**(d) Teacher Bonding Scheme (TBS) — Excel needs correcting here.** Announced **19 September 2025** by Education Minister Erica Stanford; in force from **5 October 2025**. It **replaces** the Voluntary Bonding Scheme (VBS) and the BeTTER Jobs Programme. Any teacher — **beginning, experienced or overseas-trained** — appointed to a designated vacancy at a **Priority Staffing School** receives rising annual after-year payments of $5,000 / $6,500 / $8,000 / $9,500 / $11,000, up to **NZD 40,000 (before tax) over five years**; **185 places per year**, first-come-first-served, one place per school per year; applications for the 2025/26 cohort closed 26 November 2025 (New Zealand Government, 2025; Ministry of Education, 2025b; RNZ, 2025; NZ Herald, 2025). The predecessor VBS was open to **new teachers only** and capped at NZD 17,500 over five years (RNZ, 2025). So: (i) before October 2025 the correct name was "Voluntary Bonding Scheme", and it did **not** cover experienced teachers; (ii) the Excel's claim that the bonding scheme includes experienced teachers is correct **only for the new TBS from October 2025**.

**(e) Overseas relocation incentives (relevant to re-entry).** New Zealand-trained teachers returning from overseas (and overseas-trained teachers) can claim an **Overseas Relocation Grant of up to NZD 10,000** toward airfares, temporary accommodation, Teaching Council registration and family relocation costs. Conditions: registered and certificated; employment agreement of 12+ months; apply within 90 days of starting; no NZ teaching role in the previous 12 months; annual funding capped, first-come-first-served (Ministry of Education, 2025c). Recruitment agencies report **1,360 grants per year** [figure from ANZUK Education, a recruiter — not found on the official page: treat as indicative].

### 3.3 Scale, results, evaluations

**No published uptake figures or evaluation** were found for the TER waiver/relief-teaching return route (the Ministry's pages carry no participant counts, and no evaluation is listed). TBS is too new to have results (first cohort placed from late 2025). This is the key evidence gap: the NZ package is well documented as *policy design* (removing a re-certification barrier + targeted money) but undocumented as *outcomes*.

### 3.4 Corrections to the Excel

- "Returning to Teaching pathway" is a team label, not an official name (see 3.1).
- The Excel merges two distinct instruments into one sentence: the **20-half-day allowance** (standing statutory rule for teaching *without* a certificate) and the **TER waiver** (applies when *renewing* a certificate with a relief job offer). Both are real but separate.
- "Teacher Bonding Scheme" naming/eligibility corrected as in 3.2(d) — the "including to experienced teachers" clause only holds from October 2025.
- Source "Education Workforce (Ministry of Education)" resolved to the specific pages cited below.

### 3.5 FILTER VERDICT: **KEEP** (reframed)

Keep as the clearest OECD example of *regulatory* barrier removal for re-entry (re-certification waiver + free certification + a low-commitment relief-teaching on-ramp) now paired with sizeable place-based money open to returners; flag explicitly that no uptake or evaluation data exist — if the paper's bar is evaluated programmes only, downgrade to keep-as-brief-mention.
