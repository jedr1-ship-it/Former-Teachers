## 1. ITEM C4 — BLOCKING: Netherlands activation instruments (new 4.1 case)

### 1.1 What was found — Instrument 1: Regeling tegemoetkoming herintreders primair onderwijs (returner subsidy, primary education)

**Legal basis (primary source, verified in full):** Regeling van de Staatssecretaris van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap van 24 oktober 2017, nr. PO/1237137, houdende regels voor de subsidieverstrekking voor tegemoetkoming in de kosten voor de ondersteuning en begeleiding van herintreders in het primair onderwijs, *Staatscourant* 2017, No. 62002 (published 1 November 2017; signed by State Secretary S. Dekker), https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stcrt-2017-62002.html. Consolidated version with amendment and repeal history: https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0040130 (identifier BWBR0040130; status: "ingetrokken" — no longer in force; expiry 1 January 2022).

**Scheme design (article pinpoints):**
- **Art. 1 (definition):** a *herintreder* is a "leraar die zijn loopbaan in het primair onderwijs heeft onderbroken of niet heeft aangevangen" — a qualified teacher who interrupted, or never started, a career in primary education. Note the definition therefore covers both classic returners and never-served qualified teachers.
- **Art. 3 (applicant):** the school board (*bevoegd gezag*), not the individual teacher, applies. The subsidy is a contribution to the board's costs of **induction, guidance and support** of the returner — not a signing bonus to the teacher.
- **Art. 4 (amount):** **EUR 2,500, one-off, per herintreder.**
- **Art. 9 (eligibility):** appointment of **at least 6 months** (temporary or permanent); the person must **not have worked as a teacher for at least 12 consecutive months** before the appointment; appointment dated between 1 August 2017 and (after amendment) no later than 1 November 2020.
- **Art. 6 (allocation):** first come, first served.
- **Administration:** DUS-I (Dienst Uitvoering Subsidies aan Instellingen), per the Staatscourant text.
- **Budget:** original ceiling **EUR 1.25 million**, explicitly "room for 500 herintreders" (toelichting). Announced in the Kamerbrief on the primary-education teacher shortage of **26 June 2017** as a measure to "activate the silent reserve" — the toelichting cites a silent reserve of **at least 31,000** qualified persons in primary education and invokes the earlier **"Terug voor de klas" project (1998–2002)** as evidence that structured support aids reintegration.
- **Extension (Stcrt. 2019, 28510, 28 May 2019):** application window extended from 31 December 2018 to **31 October 2020**; new annual ceilings **EUR 300,000 for 2019 and EUR 250,000 for 2020** (i.e., a maximum of 120 and 100 grants respectively). The toelichting states the ceiling was set lower than in 2018 "omdat de subsidie toen niet was uitgeput" (because the budget was not exhausted). https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stcrt-2019-28510.html
- **End date:** applications closed 31 October 2020; the regulation lapsed **1 January 2022** (wetten.overheid.nl status line).

**Uptake (verified):**
- "Vorig jaar hebben ruim 100 herintreders gebruikgemaakt van de subsidie" — **over 100 returners in 2018**; and "In 2019 kunnen 100 subsidies worden verstrekt en in 2020 nog eens 100" — **100 subsidies available in each of 2019 and 2020**. Source: OCW newsletter, "Subsidie voor herintreders", 3 July 2019, https://www.nieuwsbrievenminocw.nl/actueel/nieuws/2019/07/03/subsidie-voor-herintreders.
- "Sinds oktober 2017 hebben bijna 200 herintreders hiervan gebruik gemaakt" — **nearly 200 cumulative by autumn 2019**. Source: Kamerstukken II 2019/20, 35 300 VIII, nr. 15 (Verslag houdende een lijst van vragen en antwoorden, OCW budget 2020, 31 October 2019), answer to question 44, https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/kst-35300-VIII-15.html.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** Final grant counts for 2019 and 2020 individually. Tried: DUS-I website (herintreders page no longer exists; the old regional-approach URL 404s), OCW year reports via web search, Staatscourant amendments, aanpaklerarentekort.nl. The honest formulation is "up to 120 grants funded in 2019 and 100 in 2020; final uptake not published". **This corrects GAPS.md's "~100 grants 2019, ~100 in 2020", which conflates available slots with grants made.**
- **[UNVERIFIED]** No dedicated published evaluation of this regeling was found (searches: "evaluatie regeling tegemoetkoming herintreders primair onderwijs", DUS-I, rijksoverheid.nl). The Staatscourant text only reserves OCW's right to evaluate under the Kaderregeling subsidies OCW, SZW en VWS. State this as an evidence gap (fits the GAPS.md B2 "announced, rarely accounted for" pattern).

### 1.2 What was found — Instrument 2: RAL → RAP regional subsidy schemes

**RAL (predecessor):** Subsidieregeling regionale aanpak lerarentekort, *Staatscourant* 2018, No. 68805 (https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stcrt-2018-68805.html; consolidated: https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0041633). For 2019: **EUR 9 million** (EUR 4.5m primary; EUR 4.5m secondary/vocational), max **EUR 250,000 per region** (EUR 500,000 for joint po+vo plans); the four largest cities (G4) were funded separately via a decentralisation grant of **EUR 1 million per municipality** (VOS/ABB summary, https://www.vosabb.nl/subsidieregeling-regionale-aanpak-lerarentekort/). One school year (2019/20). Monitored by Oberon/Kohnstamm Instituut: *Monitor Regionale Aanpak Lerarentekort — Eindrapport 2020*, https://www.oberon.eu/media/k5pfm3o0/eindrapport-monitor-regionale-aanpak-lerarentekort-definitief.pdf (not text-mined here; cite as monitoring reference).

**RAP:** Subsidieregeling regionale aanpak personeelstekort onderwijs 2020 en 2021, effective 10 December 2019, consolidated at https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0042894 (BWBR0042894); **expired 1 August 2025**. Design (verified from consolidated text):
- Applicants are **regional partnerships of school boards plus at least one teacher-training institution**, with minimum-coverage thresholds (Art. 8: primary — ≥35% of boards, ≥800 FTE staff and ≥1/3 of regional sector personnel; secondary — ≥50% of boards, ≥1,200 FTE; Amsterdam, Rotterdam, The Hague and Utrecht excluded, again funded separately).
- Subsidised activities: recruiting and guiding side-entrants (*zij-instromers*), guidance of beginning teachers, a mandatory **regional information desk/loket** for prospective (re-)entrants, strategic HR planning, professionalisation, and **activating the silent reserve including tailored guidance of herintreders**.
- Amounts: first round base **EUR 385,000 per region** (+ up to EUR 192,500 supplements; +30% where MBO participates); total first-round budget **EUR 32.8 million** (EUR 18.3m po; EUR 14.5m vo); later rounds EUR 3.345m (2021/22), EUR 18.421m (2022/23) and EUR 7.726m (Aug–Dec 2023). Co-financing of at least one-third of eligible costs required.
- Coverage: **67 regions funded in the first (2020/21) round** (41 po, 25 vo, 1 combined; MBO participating in 21) — PO-Raad, https://www.poraad.nl/nieuws-en-achtergronden/subsidies-regionale-aanpak-personeelstekort-2020/2021-toegekend; **71 partnerships by spring 2022** (evaluation, p. 5).

**Evaluation (primary, text-mined):** De Jong, A., A. Suijkerbuijk, S. van der Ploeg, S. Weijers (Oberon), E. Stronkhorst and W. Schenke (Kohnstamm Instituut) (2022), *Evaluatieonderzoek Regionale Aanpak Personeelstekorten — Eindrapportage augustus 2022*, commissioned by OCW, 91 pp., https://www.aanpaklerarentekort.nl/site/binaries/site-content/collections/documents/2022/09/29/evaluatieonderzoek-rap-eindrapportage-augustus-2022/Evaluatieonderzoek+RAP+Eindrapportage+2022.pdf. Key findings:
- Scheme scale "€30 mln in totaal" for 2020/21–2021/22; 71 partnerships, most grown out of RAL; 10 new (management summary, pp. 5–6).
- Satisfaction with regional cooperation high; knowledge exchange the main perceived gain (pp. 5–6).
- Goals on **side-entry mostly met; pay and career-perspective goals least often met**; delays largely attributed to COVID-19 (p. 6).
- **Herintreders-specific:** activating the silent reserve was a standard goal category ("Activeren van stille reserves; een concreet doel hierbij is bijvoorbeeld een x aantal herintreders"), but in secondary/MBO regions "Vooral bij het maatwerk aanbieden aan herintreders lijkt de uitvoering minder goed geslaagd" — tailored support to returners was among the **least-realised activities** (p. 60, Figure 4.17).
- Notable unintended effect: "meer aanmeldingen van zij-instromers en herintreders … dan dat er plekken zijn binnen de regio" — **more side-entrant and returner applications than places** (Table 3.17, p. 37) — i.e., demand-side interest outran schools' absorption capacity.
- The evaluation is process-focused and self-reported; **no causal estimate of effects on shortages** is attempted.
- Succession: RAP was extended in April 2022 (2022/23 round) and folded into the *onderwijsregio's* structure from 2023/24; first interim evaluation by Berenschot, June 2025 (https://www.aanpaklerarentekort.nl/site/binaries/site-content/collections/documents/2025/06/24/eerste-tussenrapport-evaluatie-onderwijsregios/onderzoek-berenschot-eerste-tussenrapportage-evaluatie-onderwijsregio-s-2025.pdf).

### 1.3 Corrected/new text for the draft (Section 4.1, new Netherlands case)

> **Netherlands: a small returner subsidy inside a large regional infrastructure.** The Netherlands — the paper's benchmark case for measuring the reserve — has also run a dedicated activation instrument. Between November 2017 and October 2020, the Ministry of Education, Culture and Science (OCW) operated the *Regeling tegemoetkoming herintreders primair onderwijs*, a one-off subsidy of EUR 2,500 paid to primary school boards for each returning teacher they appointed for at least six months, provided the person had not taught for at least twelve consecutive months (Staatscourant 2017, No. 62002; administered by DUS-I). The subsidy financed the board's costs of induction and guidance rather than the returner's pay, reflecting lessons from the earlier "Terug voor de klas" project (1998–2002). The original budget of EUR 1.25 million provided for 500 returners, against an estimated silent reserve of at least 31,000 qualified persons in primary education alone. Take-up fell well short of even this modest ambition: just over 100 returners were subsidised in 2018 and nearly 200 cumulatively by late 2019 (Kamerstukken II 2019/20, 35 300 VIII, nr. 15), and when the scheme was extended in 2019 its annual ceilings were lowered — to at most 120 grants in 2019 and 100 in 2020 — explicitly because the budget "was not exhausted". The regulation lapsed on 1 January 2022 without a published evaluation. Returner activation was subsequently mainstreamed into regional labour-market schemes: the *Subsidieregeling regionale aanpak lerarentekort* (2019/20, EUR 9 million) and its successor, the *Subsidieregeling regionale aanpak personeelstekort onderwijs* (RAP, 2020–2023, c. EUR 30 million for the first two school years across 71 regional partnerships of school boards and teacher-training institutions), which required each region to run an information desk for prospective entrants and listed "activating the silent reserve" among fundable goals. The commissioned evaluation found high satisfaction with regional cooperation and largely met side-entry targets, but tailored support to returners was among the least-realised activities, and — tellingly — several regions reported more applications from side-entrants and returners than schools could place (De Jong et al., 2022, pp. 37, 60). No causal effect on shortages was estimated. The Dutch experience thus mirrors the pattern seen elsewhere in this chapter: the country with the best-measured reserve attached only a marginal, under-subscribed instrument to it, while absorption capacity in schools, not applicant interest, emerged as the binding constraint.

## 2. ITEM C4 NICE-TO-HAVE (a) — Japan: re-employment of retired teachers

**Sources found:**
- Nippon.com (2022), "Survey Reveals Teacher Shortage in Japan", 15 March 2022, https://www.nippon.com/en/japan-data/h01264/ — MEXT's **first national teacher-shortage survey**: shortage of **2,558 teachers (0.31% of the 836,079 required)** at **1,897 schools (5.8%)** in public elementary, junior-high and high schools at the start of AY2021.
- Nippon.com/Jiji (2026), "3,827 Teacher Positions Unfilled across Japan: 2025 Survey", https://www.nippon.com/en/news/yjj2026030500966/ — the follow-up MEXT survey found **3,827 unfilled positions in 2025**, up from 2,065 on the comparable start-of-year definition four years earlier. (Do not mix the 2,558 and 2,065 figures — different reference dates within AY2021.)
- Sakuma, A. and N. Shimazaki (2024), "'Why are We Running Short of Teachers Even as the Birthrate Declines?': A Case Study of the Teacher Shortage in Public Schools in X Prefecture in Japan", *Educational Studies in Japan: International Yearbook*, No. 18, March 2024, pp. 191–210, https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1433887.pdf (originally in *Kyoikugaku Kenkyu*, Vol. 88, No. 4, 2021). Verified from full text:
  - Age-based retirees "are expected to be rehired until they reach pension age, but there are now few (full-time) aspirants: only about 35% of age-based retirees in elementary schools and 55% in junior high schools" (p. 201).
  - Adjunct (part-time) hires in the prefecture's elementary and junior-high schools: "52.7% in their 60s and 7.1% in their 70s, meaning that retired teachers comprise about 60% of the whole" (p. 203, Figure 9).

**Draft paragraph (for 4.1, after China):**

> Japan illustrates re-employment of retirees as routine staffing rather than a branded programme. After MEXT's first national shortage survey found 2,558 unfilled teaching positions at 5.8% of public schools at the start of the 2021 school year — rising to 3,827 in the 2025 survey — attention turned to the system's heavy dependence on retired teachers (Nippon.com, 2022; 2026). Japanese boards of education conventionally re-employ teachers after mandatory retirement until pension age, and retirees dominate the substitute pool: in one prefecture studied in depth, 52.7% of part-time adjunct hires were in their sixties and a further 7.1% in their seventies, so that retirees made up roughly 60% of all adjuncts (Sakuma and Shimazaki, 2024, p. 203). The same study shows the model's fragility: only about 35% of age-based retirees in elementary schools (55% in junior high) now seek full-time re-employment, and ageing adjuncts increasingly decline the most demanding replacement work (pp. 201, 203) — a reminder that a reserve composed mainly of retirees depletes on its own schedule.

## 4. ITEM C5 NICE-TO-HAVE (c) — England: Teachers' Pension Scheme re-employment/abatement rules (for 4.2)

**Source (verified):** Teachers' Pensions (n.d.), "Return to work — Once retired", https://www.teacherspensions.co.uk/members/once-retired/return-to-work.aspx. Key verified rules:
- Abatement applies only to **final-salary Age or Premature retirement benefits**: "If you're receiving Age or Premature final salary benefits, your pension may be temporarily suspended, depending upon your earnings."
- Mechanics: "Your index-linked salary of reference minus your annual pension is the limit that you can earn in a tax year before your pension is affected" — exceed it and the final-salary pension is suspended for the rest of the scheme year.
- **Career-average (post-2015) benefits are not abated:** "If you're receiving career average benefits they will not be affected by re-employment unless you're in receipt of Ill-health Retirement benefits."
- **Actuarially-adjusted early retirement is not abated:** "If you took your final salary pension early (actuarially adjusted benefits)… your pension will not be abated."
- Monitoring via employer Monthly Data Collection; otherwise a **Certificate of Re-employment** must be filed; failure can create recoverable overpayments (see also Teachers' Pensions factsheet "Returning to work after claiming your benefits", June 2024, https://www.teacherspensions.co.uk/-/media/documents/member/factsheets/applying-for-retirement/returning-to-work-after-claiming-your-benefits.ashx).

**Draft paragraph (4.2, England):**

> England's regulatory posture toward returning pensioners is milder than the US states surveyed above, and softening by design of scheme succession. Under the Teachers' Pension Scheme, only final-salary Age or Premature retirement benefits are subject to abatement: a retiree may earn, in any tax year, up to their index-linked "salary of reference" minus their annual pension before the pension is suspended for the remainder of the scheme year. Benefits drawn early with actuarial reduction are never abated, and — crucially — pensions accrued in the career-average scheme that covers all service since 2015 are not abated at all, so the abatement constraint is confined to a shrinking cohort of final-salary entitlements and disappears as career-average membership matures (Teachers' Pensions, n.d.). Compliance runs through employers' monthly data returns or a Certificate of Re-employment, with overpaid pension recoverable.

**Supersedes:** nothing — adds the England paragraph GAPS.md C5 requested; complements `cases_england_nz` (4.1 material).
