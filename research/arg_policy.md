# The general theory of re-entry policy that the evidence implies

*Argument memo. Angle: what the whole body of international experience collectively teaches about how a re-entry policy must be built to work, reduced to a small number of principles a minister could act on without knowing any individual case. Evidence base: the verified research files in this directory; `fix_*.md` and `ch5_synthesis.md` govern on any conflict; `TEAM_INPUT.md` is binding. Complements rather than repeats `arg_concept.md` (what a former teacher is), `arg_mechanism.md` (where the pathway breaks) and `arg_comparative.md` (why systems differ); cross-references are flagged where the arguments touch.*

---

## 0. What a theory of re-entry policy has to explain

The corpus contains a pattern that no existing framing accounts for. Measures that cost almost nothing and were never designed as re-entry policy produced large, verifiable movements of people: North Carolina lifted one earnings cap and roughly 3,500 retirees came back over a decade, over a third of the retirement-eligible cohort returning to full-time work by 2009, with more than half of them going back to the school they had left (Jarrold-Grapes and Mahler, 2024). Measures that were designed as re-entry policy, budgeted, branded and launched, produced almost nobody: England's Return to Teaching pilot converted 5,729 expressions of interest into 27 permanent posts in the target subjects, at an estimated GBP 37,400 per additional returner, and its evaluators concluded it was "unlikely to represent good value for money" (Buchanan et al., 2018, pp. 56-58, 91). The Netherlands, the system that can see its reserve better than any other, offered school boards EUR 2,500 for each returner they inducted, budgeted for 500, subsidised just over 100 in 2018, and then *lowered* its own ceilings because the money was not being spent (Staatscourant 2017, No. 62002; Staatscourant 2019, No. 28510; Kamerstukken II 2019/20, 35 300 VIII, nr. 15).

Any theory worth the name must explain why effort and outcome are inversely related in this field, and it must do so without retreating to the claim that context differs. The claim advanced here is that effort and outcome are inversely related because most re-entry *programmes* act on the wrong object. They act on the willingness of former teachers, which policy cannot manufacture, while the results are produced by instruments that act on the price the state itself charges for coming back — a price most systems set inadvertently, through rules written for other purposes, and have never audited.

Five principles follow. They are stated in the order a minister should apply them.

---

## 1. Every system already has a re-entry policy. Almost none has written one

**The claim.** A system's re-entry policy is not the programme it launches; it is the aggregate price that its pension rules, service-crediting rules, licensure rules and salary schedule charge a qualified person for returning to a classroom. That price exists whether or not anyone has decided it, it is usually set by four instruments none of which was designed with returners in mind, and it is almost always higher than any government intended. The first act of re-entry policy is therefore not to build something but to read what is already there.

**The mechanism.** Each of the four instruments was written to solve a different problem. Pension abatement and earnings caps exist to prevent double-dipping and protect actuarial integrity. Service-crediting exclusions exist to stop an employer from re-hiring its own retiree cheaply, or to stop a period from generating both a pension and a salary increment. Licence lapse and refresh requirements exist for quality assurance. The seniority salary schedule exists to reward retention. None was designed to price return, but together they do, and their combined incidence falls on exactly the person a shortage-hit ministry wants to attract. Because no single official owns the aggregate, no one ever computes it.

**The evidence for.** The clearest demonstration is the pair of Austrian cases sitting inside one country. A teacher who retired as a civil servant is excluded by law from prior-service crediting for any period generating a pension, and therefore returns at the teacher starting salary; a teacher who retired from the contract scheme has that same service credited and broadly recovers pre-retirement pay (BMBWF, 2023, answer to Question 19, p. 18). Two people with identical careers face offers differing by most of a salary scale, on the basis of an employment status chosen decades earlier, and no one designed this as a re-entry incentive because it is not one — it is pension law with a side-effect. The audited national result is 1,062 returners across six school years against a workforce of roughly 123,000, with intakes peaking in 2021/22-2022/23 and falling thereafter (Rechnungshof, 2025, pp. 24-25).

Georgia supplies the same finding with an official price tag. HB 385 stacked three screens — thirty years of creditable service, a one-year break, and employment confined to one of three highest-need content areas in the employer's regional service agency — and the state audit its own statute mandated found 635 full-time rehired retirees across three fiscal years, about 350 in any given year, under 1% of a 125,000-strong workforce, against roughly 6,000 vacant or not-fully-certified positions. The decisive sentence in that audit is not the headcount: it is that 45% of surveyed school systems said the statutory provisions had blocked a hire they would otherwise have made (Georgia Department of Audits and Accounts, 2025, Report 24-20). The willingness existed, the vacancy existed, the match existed, and the state's own rule stopped it.

North Carolina is the mirror image and the strongest evidence in the corpus. The 1999-2009 policy contained no bonus, no campaign and no support offer. Its entire content was the removal of a penalty: retirees could return full-time and draw salary and pension concurrently, instead of being confined to part-time work capped at half their previous salary. Roughly 3,500 returned; over half of the schools in the sample hired at least one; they were disproportionately hired by schools with more economically disadvantaged and more Black students; and when the window closed in 2009 returns fell to zero (Jarrold-Grapes and Mahler, 2024). That instantaneous collapse is diagnostic. Inducements decay when withdrawn; penalties bite the moment they are reimposed. The pattern identifies what the policy was actually doing.

**The evidence against.** Three strains are real. First, removing a penalty is not costless, and the penalty sometimes exists for a defensible reason. Ontario is the documented case: the 95-day re-employment ceiling raised in 1990 during a shortage was, by 2008/09, associated with the province's ten largest boards spending CAD 108.3 million a year on retired supply teachers earning nearly double the new-graduate day rate — CAD 432.69 against CAD 227.36 — while roughly 12,774 newly certified teachers competed for about 6,500 openings; the cap was cut to 50 days from September 2012 (Hammer and Alphonso, 2010; OSSTF, n.d.). A penalty on returning is also a protection for entering, and the audit must price both sides.

Second, penalty removal is necessary but plainly not sufficient. New Mexico removed essentially every restriction — a 90-day layout period, then no limits on salary or hours for 36 months, extended to 60 months and a higher earnings threshold in 2025 — and its no-limits tier drew 154 users in its first year against roughly 48,000 potentially eligible retirees (New Mexico Educational Retirement Board, 2023; NM HED, 2022). Liberalising a rule for people who are not already at the door changes nothing, which is `arg_comparative.md`'s first proposition and is not disputed here.

Third, Austria removed nothing at federal level and still produced 1,062 returners, so friction is not the whole story; the Länder that produced them did something else, discussed in §3.

**What it implies.** The first instrument of re-entry policy is an audit, not a programme, and it can be commissioned in a week. The audit asks four questions with documentary answers: what happens to a returner's pension; what happens to their prior service in the salary schedule; what must they do to reactivate a licence, and on what timetable; and which categories of post are closed to them by eligibility screens. Each answer that imposes a cost must justify itself against the hires it blocks, with the Georgia finding as the standard of proof — a rule that 45% of employers say has stopped a hire they wanted is not a safeguard, it is a defect. This is the cheapest high-yield act available to a minister, it requires no budget line, and no country in the corpus has published such an audit as a routine exercise.

## 2. Instruments act on constraints, on motivation, or on the product — and money works only when it is the price of a friction

**The claim.** Re-entry instruments fall into three classes, not two. Constraint instruments remove a cost the state imposes on returning. Motivation instruments try to raise the attractiveness of returning. Product instruments change what post is being offered. The evidence is strong for constraint instruments, strong for product instruments, and close to empty for motivation instruments — with one apparent exception that, on inspection, is a constraint instrument wearing a bonus's clothing.

**The mechanism.** A former teacher compares a known current situation with an imagined returned one. Policy can lower the cost of the transition, change what the transition leads to, or attempt to make the destination more desirable in the abstract. The first two operate on quantities the person can verify; the third operates on a preference they have already resolved against, at a price they have already paid. The Australian panel evidence puts that price precisely: leavers' job satisfaction is 0.13 standard deviations higher one year after exit and 0.19 higher at three years, while their weekly wages are 6.2% and 9.4% lower (Cuervo and Vera-Toscano, 2025, per `fix_hilda_fleck.md`). English leavers accept roughly a 10% wage cut for shorter hours and higher satisfaction (Worth, Bamford and Durbin, 2015). Dutch inactive teachers rate several working conditions in their current jobs *more* favourably than active teachers rate theirs (Somers et al., 2024). A motivation instrument is asking these people to reverse a trade they made deliberately and profited from; a constraint instrument is merely offering not to charge them for changing their mind.

**The evidence for.** Portugal ran the cleanest test because a single decree deployed both an inducement and a constraint on adjacent populations. Article 5's returner route offered retirees their full pension plus compensation indexed to the first step of the career scale, pro-rated by hours; against a 200-place target it drew 79-80 applicants and 63 acceptances. Article 6 paid EUR 750 a month to teachers who postponed retirement; roughly 2,200 used it in 2025/26 (Decreto-Lei n.º 51/2024, Arts. 5-6; Diário de Notícias, 2024; RTP, 2026). The money was comparable in kind. What differed was that Article 6 asked people to defer a transition and Article 5 asked people to reverse one.

China looks at first like the counter-example — a genuine allowance producing genuine scale, over 20,000 retired teachers recruited since 2018 under the Silver Age schemes, with a corps of 120,000 targeted across five education strands (MOE and MOF, 2018; MOE et al., 2023; State Council, 2023). But the ¥20,000 per person per year is conditioned on service in counties recently lifted out of poverty, ethnic-minority counties, old revolutionary base counties and border counties, and host provinces additionally supply housing, once-a-semester return travel and insurance (MOE et al., 2023; People's Daily Online, 2025). That is not payment for a preference; it is compensation for relocation, priced against a friction that can be itemised. Read this way China stops contradicting Portugal and starts confirming the same rule from the other side.

Fitzpatrick (2015) supplies the mechanism's fine print: Illinois teachers offered the chance to buy additional pension benefits were willing to pay only about twenty cents per dollar of present-value pension wealth, valuing current cash roughly five to one over deferred. Money that reaches a returner as a deferred, complex or conditional entitlement is discounted almost to nothing. North Carolina's implicit premium — full salary alongside a pension, worth on the order of half as much again as a normal full-time salary — was current, simple and legible, which is why it moved people, and the underlying causal literature on incentives is consistent: pension enhancements that reward delayed retirement cut retirements by 4 to 10 percentage points (Fleck et al., 2025).

The product class is where the numbers quietly live. New Mexico's own tiers are the tidiest demonstration: alongside the 154 users of the unrestricted 36-month route, 686 retirees worked at a quarter time or less and 390 under the low-earnings threshold, within 2,792 working retirees overall (New Mexico Educational Retirement Board, 2023). The intensity of the post, not the generosity of the terms, sorted people. The same margin dominates elsewhere — New Zealand's twenty half-days of relief teaching without a practising certificate (Education and Training Act 2020, Sch. 3; Teaching Council, 2026), Ontario's fifty days, Ireland's annually suspended career-break substitution cap, Japan's adjunct pool in which retirees make up roughly 60% of hires in the prefecture studied in depth (Sakuma and Shimazaki, 2024, p. 203), and New South Wales's casual and temporary placements. `arg_comparative.md`'s second proposition develops the cross-country reading of this; the point for a theory of instruments is narrower and sharper: product is a policy variable, it is usually left unset, and where it is set low it out-recruits everything else in the same jurisdiction.

**The evidence against.** The taxonomy is cleaner than the world. England's advisory service is a hybrid — it reduces process and information costs, which is constraint work, but its content is encouragement — and at roughly GBP 5,400 per recorded returner it is an order of magnitude cheaper per head than the pilot it succeeded, with additionality unknown (UK Parliament, 2022; own calculation in `cases_england_nz.md` §2.3). More seriously, the whole classification rests on a base with a hole in it: no quasi-experimental study anywhere prices *re-entry* incentives. The causal evidence concerns retention and entry — GBP 2,000 a year cutting the probability of leaving by 23% in England's shortage subjects (Sims and Benhenda, 2022), Florida's loan forgiveness cutting attrition by 10.4% and 8.9% (Feng and Sass, 2018), a USD 1,800 North Carolina bonus cutting turnover by about 17% (Clotfelter et al., 2008) — and those elasticities cannot be borrowed for people who have already revealed a preference against current conditions (`lit_return_incentives.md` §C1, to be carried verbatim). The taxonomy is therefore an inference from take-up under different designs, not from identified effects.

**What it implies.** A minister should treat any proposal to pay former teachers to return as requiring an answer to one question: what specific, itemisable friction is this money the price of? Relocation to a remote county, the loss of a pension for the months worked, the cost of a refresher course, a certification fee, travel to a school no one will move to — all of these are answerable. "It will make teaching more attractive to them" is not an answer, because their revealed preference has already settled that question and the money on offer is never on the scale of the trade they made. Where no friction can be named, the money is deadweight, and Portugal's own government reached that conclusion about its stay-on supplement in July 2026, narrowing it to shortage zones on the ground that the blanket version "in many cases was not justified" (RTP, 2026).

## 3. The instruments that produced measurable results ride a register the state already maintains — and reach and accountability turn out to be the same property

**The claim.** Pension re-employment rules and employer-record outreach look like unrelated instruments — one legislative and central, the other administrative and local. They share the property that separates every result in this corpus from every announcement: each operates through a transactional register the state must keep anyway. That single property delivers two things at once that every purpose-built programme has to buy separately and usually buys only one of — it reaches named individuals at no discovery cost, and it counts itself.

**The mechanism.** A pension administrator holds a file on every retired teacher, pays them monthly, and must know whether they are working in order to pay them correctly. An employer holds a file on every person who resigned, with their subject, their school and their contact details. Both registers exist for reasons that have nothing to do with re-entry, and both are complete for the population that matters. An instrument attached to such a register addresses people rather than a public; and because the register's normal operation records every event, take-up appears in the accounts as a by-product rather than as an act of evaluation.

A campaign has neither property. It must attract an unknown population into a channel that does not yet exist, and it can only learn what happened by commissioning a study.

**The evidence for.** The reach half is visible in a direct comparison the corpus makes possible. New South Wales wrote to more than 1,500 named teachers who had resigned from its own system within the previous five years, packaged accreditation, approval to teach and background checks into a single contact, and placed 145 returners covering more than 600 schools, about 60% of them from retirement (NSW Government, 2024a; NSW Department of Education, 2024a, p. 14). That is a conversion of roughly one in ten of the people contacted. England, over the same kind of horizon, ran a national marketing campaign and a registration website, attracted 5,729 self-selected registrants, supported 354 and placed 27 in permanent posts in the target subjects (Buchanan et al., 2018). The instruments differ in almost every respect, but the decisive difference is that one addressed a list of individuals with a known recent tie and the other addressed a population.

Germany is the control that stops this from being a platitude about direct mail. Thirteen of sixteen Länder actively recruit pensioned teachers, Hesse wrote to some 7,000 of them in one year, and the national result is just over 5,000 retirees teaching — under 1% of a workforce of roughly 800,000 (Deutsches Schulportal, 2023). A register of retirees is a list, and it still failed, which locates the operative variable more precisely: the list must be recent and warm. NSW targeted the previous five years; Portugal's Article 5 restricted eligibility to those retired five years or less; 54% of North Carolina's returners went back to the school they had left; and the Austrian qualitative work finds that continuing contact with the former school is what converts availability into return (Resch, 2025). `arg_mechanism.md`'s third proposition draws the operational consequence — the unit of re-entry policy is the school and its exit protocol. The consequence for a theory of instruments is that the register has to be an employment register, not a pension register, if the object is working-age leavers.

The accountability half is where the argument becomes distinctive, because the same property explains a governance pattern usually blamed on political will. The only audited national take-up figure in Europe exists because employing a pensioner is an accounting fact that a supreme audit institution could reconstruct (Rechnungshof, 2025). New Mexico's tiered participation numbers exist because the Educational Retirement Board must know who is working in order not to pay them wrongly. Georgia's numbers exist because employers must notify the retirement system within thirty days and pay both employer and employee contributions — which is also why the audit could price the whole instrument at USD 11.1 million in contributions, under 1% of total contributions. Now set against these the instruments that generated no numbers at all: no uptake counts exist for Michigan's PA 184 of 2022 or PA 147 of 2023, Virginia's critical-shortage rehires, Louisiana's Acts 394 and 344, France's cumul emploi-retraite for teachers, New Zealand's Teacher Education Refresh waiver, England's post-2022 advisory phase, the Netherlands' final subsidy years, or China's progress toward 120,000 (`ch5_synthesis.md` Lesson 12; GAPS §B2). And NSW, the best funnel in the corpus, vanished from the Department's Annual Report the following year (NSW Department of Education, 2025).

**The evidence against.** The property is necessary, not sufficient, and two cases prove it. Portugal's Article 5 route ran through DGAE's electronic ranking pool — an administrative register — and produced 63 returners; riding a register guarantees a count, not a result. New Zealand's waiver runs through the Teaching Council's registration system and has produced no published count at all, so a regulatory register is not automatically a *counting* register. What the four exceptions have in common is narrower than "administrative": each attaches to a **money flow with an independent custodian** — a pension fund, an auditor, or a statutory reporting duty. Louisiana went further and legislated an actuarial report, but set the deadline before the 2034 session, which is honest about actuarial timescales and useless for any decision a minister will take this decade.

There is also a selection worry that should be stated rather than buried: instruments that count themselves are the only ones we can evaluate, so the apparent superiority of pension rules and record-based outreach may be partly an artefact of visibility. The corpus cannot fully dispose of this. What it can say is that the invisible instruments are invisible by construction, and an instrument that cannot in principle report its own take-up is not a candidate for renewal on evidence.

**What it implies.** Prefer instruments that ride an existing register; where the register is a pension fund the addressable population is retirees, and where it is an employment record the addressable population is working-age leavers, which is the more valuable of the two and the one most systems neglect. Where a new channel must be built, buy the measurement in the same act and from the same budget line, because it will not be bought later.

## 4. Accountability is a property of the instrument's birth, not of the ministry's diligence

**The claim.** The governance failure the corpus documents — measures announced with a press release and never accounted for again — is not a failure of will that better intentions would fix. It is the predictable equilibrium for an instrument whose political return is fully realised at announcement. It follows that the accounting obligation must be created by the same legal instrument that creates the measure, must name a counting body that is not the sponsor, and must be tied to a renewal decision that cannot proceed without the count.

**The mechanism.** A shortage-episode measure yields its political product — visible action — on the day it is announced. Every subsequent datum can only subtract. A sponsor therefore has no incentive to produce one, and in the absence of a duty, none is produced. This is why exhortation fails and why the OECD's own diagnosis, though correct, is incomplete: *Education Policy Outlook 2024* observes that "many education systems may lack accurate data on the number and characteristics of potential returnees and their motivations, which could limit their ability to capitalise on this opportunity strategically" (OECD, 2024, p. 51). True — but the corpus shows the data gap is not a capability gap. Systems that can count do not, unless required.

**The evidence for.** Georgia is the working model and it is worth stating precisely what it did: the statute contained its own sunset, 30 June 2026, and its own audit requirement, which produced Performance Audit Report 24-20 in April 2025 — the document that revealed both the small headcount and the 45%-blocked-hire finding — after which the legislature extended the programme with modifications through Senate Bill 150 in 2026 (Georgia Department of Audits and Accounts, 2025; Teachers Retirement System of Georgia, 2026). Sunset, count, arms-length review, informed renewal: the loop closed once, and it is the only place in the corpus where it did.

The counter-cases show each missing element separately. Michigan's PA 147 of 2023 carries a sunset — 10 October 2028 — with no count required, so the 2028 decision will be taken blind, and a sunset without a count is arguably worse than no sunset, because it manufactures a decision point with nothing to decide on. Ireland's suspension of career-break substitution restrictions has been renewed annually since at least 2021/22, most recently by Information Note TC/IN 0005/2025 replacing TC/IN 0009/2024, with no published uptake — a measure permanent in fact and temporary in form, which is the worst combination, since returners cannot plan around it and no one is obliged to ask whether it works. And Ontario's cap, the most consequential dial in the corpus, has been moved four times since 1990; the only downward move was triggered not by a review but by a newspaper investigation (Hammer and Alphonso, 2010), and the only refusal to move it up again came from a veto player with a different constituency — the Ontario Teachers' Federation declining a fourth extension in March 2024 on the grounds that "the Pension Plan is designed to provide an income to retired teachers; it is not a tool for addressing labour market challenges" (Alphonso, 2024; OCEOTA, 2024). Where accounting is absent, the correction arrives from journalism or from a union, which is not a system of government.

**The evidence against.** Accountability has a real cost and can itself deter. England's pilot is the field's best-evaluated instrument and its evaluation is what killed it; a system that evaluates rigorously will kill more measures than one that does not, and some of those will be measures worth keeping. The honest response is that this is the intended effect at the scale of spending re-entry justifies, and that the counting obligation proposed here is deliberately minimal — a headcount and a cost, not an impact evaluation. New Mexico's annual tiered participation report is the appropriate model of proportionate accounting; NFER-grade evaluation is appropriate only where a measure carries a budget comparable to teacher training.

**What it implies.** Three clauses in the founding instrument, drafted together: a mandated uptake count with a named custodian that is not the sponsoring ministry, published annually; a sunset short enough that the count matters, on the Georgia pattern rather than Louisiana's 2034; and, where an instrument rides a pension or payroll register, an explicit permission to link that register to school employment records, since the count is otherwise reconstructible only by an audit body acting on its own initiative. This costs a paragraph of drafting. Nothing else in this memo has a better ratio of value to cost.

## 5. Re-entry policy cannot deliver what returners ask for, and the redesign it can deliver is less job, not better job

**The claim.** What former teachers say would bring them back is a redesigned job — lower workload, control over hours, autonomy, relief from administrative load. A re-entry programme is structurally incapable of supplying that, for reasons of jurisdiction and of incentive compatibility. What it can supply is a smaller quantity of the same job. Once this is seen, re-entry policy stops being a defective supply programme and becomes recognisable as three other things, all of which it does well.

**The mechanism.** Three barriers stand between a re-entry programme and the redesigned job. The first is jurisdiction: workload, timetable, class size, administrative burden and leadership quality are set by school-level and system-level policy applying to everyone, and a recruitment channel has no authority over any of them. The second is incentive compatibility: a returner-only concession on conditions would make exit-and-return a strictly dominant strategy for every serving teacher with the same complaint, converting a recruitment instrument into a retention risk. Portugal's decree measured the gradient in a single document — 63 came back through the returner route while roughly 2,200 postponed retirement through the stay-on supplement — and a system that had made the returner offer materially better than the stayer offer would have moved that ratio in the direction it least wants. The third is that the redesign returners describe is expensive precisely because it is what serving teachers also want; if it were affordable for returners it would be affordable generally, and then it would be retention policy, not re-entry policy.

**The evidence for.** Every case in which returners were given a genuinely different deal received *less* work rather than better work. New South Wales offered casual and temporary placements. Ontario offers fifty days. New Zealand offers twenty half-days of relief without a certificate. New Mexico's low-intensity tiers out-recruited its unrestricted tier by more than four to one. Japan's adjunct pool is roughly 60% retirees. Portugal's Article 5 pro-rates pay by hours taught. England's most return-committed segment, the career breakers, returned at double the rate of other types and wanted part-time work that schools rarely advertise (Buchanan et al., 2018; Worth and Sharp, 2018). Not one system in the corpus lowered a returner's workload, protected their autonomy or reduced their administrative load; several reduced their hours.

Where the offer was full classroom return on unchanged conditions, the union diagnosis and the take-up agree. Portugal's returners were offered the same conditions as active staff, and FNE's secretary-general attributed the response to "accumulated fatigue" and an uninviting school environment rather than to pay (SÁBADO, 2024). Sixty-three people came.

The strongest form of the argument, though, is what the evidence shows former teachers doing well when the role itself is redesigned rather than merely shortened. Retired and substitute teachers delivering a structured tutoring protocol reproduced at scale an effect obtained in efficacy trials with graduate students: +0.34 on the TEMA-3, with 86 tutors, 994 at-risk first graders in 76 schools across four urban districts (Gersten et al., 2015; Rolfhus et al., 2012). Experience Corps produced reading effects of 0.13 to 0.16, concentrated among students receiving at least 35 sessions (Morrow-Howell et al., 2009; WWC, 2010), together with randomised evidence of physical and cognitive benefits for the older volunteers themselves (Fried et al., 2004; Carlson et al., 2015). Against a real mentoring gap — TALIS 2018 found only 22% of novice teachers have an assigned mentor and 38% participate in any induction activity (OECD, 2019) — this is where the case for former teachers stands on its own evidence rather than on shortage arithmetic.

**The evidence against.** The role-redesign case must not be oversold, and one result restrains it hard: the single randomised trial of retired teachers as mentors of new teachers meets What Works Clearinghouse standards without reservations and returns an indeterminate effect in every reviewed domain — reading, mathematics and teacher retention (DeCesare, McClelland and Randel, 2017; WWC, 2019, Study #88756). The trial's own point estimates were more encouraging than the WWC's reading of them, but the disciplined conclusion is that mentoring claims should be modest and dosage-conscious. It is also uncomfortable, and worth saying in the paper, that the evidence for what former teachers bring back is strongest where their accumulated judgement matters least — the tutoring protocol was scripted (`arg_concept.md` develops this).

A second strain: reduced-hours work is not obviously the socially preferred product. Ontario's own history shows the casual market can become a transfer to pensioners at roughly twice the new-graduate day rate, and Chinese commentary reports localities using silver-age recruits to delay hiring new full-time teachers (R.I.S.E. Magazine, 2025, secondary). "Less job" is the product re-entry can actually sell; it is not automatically the product a system should want to buy.

**What it implies — the reframing.** Re-entry policy should be understood as three things, none of which is a shortage remedy.

It is, first, **a diagnostic instrument with no rival**. What leavers demand as the price of return is the most precise statement a system can obtain of what is wrong with the job, because it is expressed as a price by people with no stake in the answer who have already paid for their own conclusion. `arg_concept.md` makes this the reserve's highest-value use, and the point is accepted here with one addition that belongs to policy rather than to concept: it follows that the leaver survey is a *retention* instrument, should be funded from the retention budget, and should be run against the serving-teacher survey rather than reported on its own. The Michigan and Dutch surveys are cheap, and the reason to run them is not to plan a re-entry campaign.

It is, second, **a friction-removal and matching function attached to a flow that already exists and that policy did not create**. England's returners are the second-largest category of entrant, running at roughly 17,000 a year and rising, with no returner-specific bursary in existence; re-entrants have supplied 37 to 49% of new hires in the United States; Japan's substitute system runs on retirees as routine practice. The programmes in this corpus are a rounding error on that flow. Policy's realistic task is to stop taxing it and to make the match faster.

It is, third, **a procurement channel for small quantities of specific scarce capacity** — relief cover on a named day, tutoring dosage in early reading and number, mentoring hours where induction is absent, presence in a school no one will relocate to. This is where the causal evidence lives, where China deliberately aimed, and where Australia was heading when *Education Policy Outlook 2024* recorded it exploring returns into "alternative roles beyond traditional classroom teaching, such as mentoring" (OECD, 2024, p. 51). The Experience Corps volunteer-health trials add a rationale that does not come from an education budget at all, which is a practical point as well as a rhetorical one.

## 6. The anomaly that should be in the paper: the one instrument that funded returner induction could not be given away

**The claim.** *Teachers Matter* recommended in 2005 that induction programmes be extended to returning teachers, not only to beginners (OECD, 2005, p. 121). England's evidence says the recommendation was well aimed: returners retain worse than novices, with 32% of the 2011 returner cohort out of service four years later against 29% of the 2011 newly-qualified cohort, and departmental analysis putting the odds of a returner leaving after their first year back at 60% above average (Buchanan et al., 2018, p. 57). The Netherlands is the only system in the corpus that funded that recommendation. It could not spend the money. That conjunction is the most informative single fact in the file about where the constraint really sits.

**The mechanism and the reasoning.** The *Regeling tegemoetkoming herintreders primair onderwijs* paid EUR 2,500 to a school board for each returner it appointed for at least six months, explicitly to cover the board's costs of induction and guidance rather than as a payment to the teacher (Staatscourant 2017, No. 62002, Arts. 1, 3, 4, 9). The design was intelligent — it recognised that the returner is a short-duration asset needing support, and it paid the party who bears that cost. But it was structurally a rebate on a hire that had already happened: a board could only claim it once it had found, selected and appointed a returner. It therefore did nothing about the stage that binds, and if the binding stage had been the cost of induction, the money would have been exhausted. It was not. Just over 100 returners were subsidised in 2018 and nearly 200 cumulatively by late 2019, against a budget explicitly sized for 500, and the 2019 extension *lowered* the annual ceilings to 120 and 100 on the stated ground that the budget had not been exhausted (Ministerie van OCW, 2019a; 2019b; Kamerstukken II 2019/20, 35 300 VIII, nr. 15). The regulation lapsed on 1 January 2022 with no published evaluation.

The successor evidence points the same way and is more explicit. The evaluation of the regional schemes into which returner activation was folded found tailored support to returners among the least-realised activities and, revealingly, that several regions reported more applications from returners and side-entrants than there were places for them (De Jong et al., 2022, pp. 37, 60).

**Why this matters for the theory.** It settles a question that a headcount cannot. In the system that measures its reserve better than any other — roughly 62,000 people holding a teaching qualification and working outside education against a combined shortage of 13,600 FTE, such that reactivating 22% of them would close the gap entirely (Somers, Groot and van Merode, 2024) — willingness was not scarce and induction money was not scarce. Placements were. This is `arg_comparative.md`'s absorption finding, and it converts a general suspicion into a design rule: an instrument that pays for a stage downstream of the binding one is deadweight by construction, however well the stage it pays for is chosen.

**The evidence against.** One should not over-read a single national scheme. The subsidy was small, primary-only, first-come-first-served, and required boards to know it existed; low take-up of a EUR 2,500 administrative rebate is also consistent with ordinary bureaucratic friction rather than absent demand. But the RAP evaluation's finding of more applicants than places is independent of the subsidy's administration and points the same way, and the Dutch registers make the willingness side unusually well measured.

**What it implies.** Before funding anything, name the stage believed to bind, and state what observation would show that it does. If the answer is "schools cannot place the returners they already have", the correct instrument is not a returner instrument at all — it is vacancy creation, timetable flexibility or absorption capacity, and no amount of re-entry policy will substitute for it.

## 7. Sequencing and proportionality: what to spend, what to stop, and when the answer is none

**The claim.** Re-entry deserves a permanent administrative function and almost no programme budget. The near-free instruments should be adopted unconditionally because they are legislative and administrative rather than budgetary; the one item worth real money is conversion support tied to identified vacancies; and there are four identifiable conditions under which the right expenditure is zero or negative.

**Sequencing.** At equal money, preventing an exit is easier than reversing one, and the ordering is not a matter of taste. Portugal's two articles are the direct observation (63 against roughly 2,200). The causal literature reinforces it from a different direction: retention incentives have identified effects — 23% in England's shortage subjects, 10.4% and 8.9% in Florida, about 17% in North Carolina, and 4 to 10 percentage points from pension enhancements rewarding delayed retirement — while re-entry incentives have none anywhere (Sims and Benhenda, 2022; Feng and Sass, 2018; Clotfelter et al., 2008; Fleck et al., 2025). The paper should state the ordering plainly: retention window first, re-entry second.

It should also state honestly why the Portuguese comparison flatters retention. The stay-on supplement paid a great many teachers who would have stayed regardless — the Portuguese government said as much when it narrowed the measure in July 2026 — whereas returning from retirement is never accidental, so the 63 are almost certainly additional and much of the 2,200 is not (`arg_comparative.md` makes this argument). The defensible ordering rule is therefore not "retention because retention is bigger" but the comparison stated in the right units: **cost per additional teacher-hour delivered where the hours are actually scarce**. Re-entry loses that comparison decisively for establishment posts. It can win it for intermittent cover, for tutoring dosage, and for places no one will move to — which is exactly the product it is capable of selling.

**What to spend.** The evidence supports spending at the scale of an administrative function rather than of a programme. Auditing and removing the state's own return penalties costs legislative time. Keeping a warm list of recent leavers and using it costs an HR process; New South Wales's funnel required no legislation whatever, only the Department's own staffing records. Making licence reinstatement a standing rule rather than an annually renewed waiver costs a regulatory amendment — New Zealand's effective mainstreaming of its Teacher Education Refresh exemption is the model, Ireland's eighth consecutive annual suspension the anti-model. Publishing a count costs a reporting line.

The one item that justifies a budget is conversion at the point of an actual vacancy. England learned this from its own failure and corrected it immediately: the successor Returners Engagement Programme tied funded places to real vacancies (NCTL, 2017). The cost ceiling should be set well below the cost of training a new entrant, not at parity — the pilot's GBP 37,400 per additional returner sat inside the GBP 33,300 to GBP 46,900 range for a trainee employed after a year, but bought an asset that leaves 60% more readily in its first year. The GBP 5,400 order of magnitude of the advisory service is the right scale; its unknown additionality is the reason to fund it against vacancies rather than against registrations.

**What to stop.** Three things, each with direct evidence. Stop national marketing to an anonymous population: England's own evaluation found national advertising played a small role relative to online search, and the campaign converted 27 people. Stop blanket payments untargeted by scarcity: Portugal reversed its own. And stop announcing measures without the count, which is the subject of §4 and the single most common failure in the corpus.

**When the answer is none.** Four conditions, each observable without knowing any other country.

*When the system cannot see its reserve.* Not because measurement is a ritual prerequisite, but because a system that cannot enumerate working-age leavers will default to the only population it can list — retirees — and retiree policy has a demographic ceiling and a crowding-out risk. Germany, Austria and France are the demonstration; near-universal outreach in Germany yields under 1% of the workforce, and Japan shows the ceiling arriving on its own schedule, with only about 35% of age-based retirees in elementary schools now seeking full-time re-employment (Sakuma and Shimazaki, 2024, p. 201). In such a system the correct first expenditure is the register, not the programme — which is also what *Education Policy Outlook 2024* recommends in effect at p. 51.

*When the entry pipeline is loose.* Ontario in 2012 is the case: newly certified teachers roughly double the annual openings while boards spent CAD 108.3 million a year on retired supply at nearly twice the new-graduate day rate. Under those conditions re-entry policy is a transfer from new entrants to pensioners, and the correct expenditure is negative — tighten the cap, as Ontario did.

*When the constraint is absorption rather than willingness.* §6. Paying for more applicants when schools cannot place the ones they have is waste by construction.

*When the shortage is a distribution problem rather than a level problem.* Selection into return runs against scarcity, because people return where their outside options are worst. England's advisory service produced 23 physics returners across two years from more than 111 physics leads given an adviser, against 56 in modern foreign languages and 49 in mathematics (UK Parliament, 2022; Walker, 2022). A system whose gap is concentrated in physics and mathematics should expect the reserve to be least useful exactly there, and should say so before it spends.

**A note on why the repertoire drifted.** *Teachers Matter* in 2005 recommended maintaining contact with former teachers, targeted retraining, part-time opportunities and leave schemes, induction for returners, and flexible re-entry pathways (OECD, 2005, pp. 88-89, 121, 165). Two decades later, those are precisely the instruments that remain rarest in practice, while the instruments systems actually enacted are pension re-employment rules and bonuses, which the 2005 report did not emphasise. The explanation is not that the 2005 advice was wrong. It is that a pension re-employment rule is a single statute administered by a single body with no budget line and no school-level implementation, whereas "maintain contact with former teachers" requires every employer to run an exit protocol permanently. Systems do the legislatively easy thing and neglect the organisationally hard one. New South Wales is the exception that shows how cheap the hard thing actually is — and its disappearance from the annual report the following year shows why cheap is not the same as durable.

## 8. What the evidence cannot settle, and what would settle it

Three questions are open, and in each case the data required already exist inside administrative systems. Saying so is itself a finding, because it converts "more research is needed" into a specific, costed request.

**Do re-entry rules have a causal effect, and how large?** No study anywhere identifies one. Yet the corpus contains at least five sharp discontinuities in populations with complete administrative records on both sides: North Carolina's window opening in 1999 and closing in 2009; Georgia's sunset and its 2026 extension; Ontario's cap moving in 1990, 2012, 2020 and 2023; Michigan's PA 147 loosening in 2023 and its 2028 sunset; New Mexico's 2025 extension from 36 to 60 months and from USD 15,000 to USD 25,000. Each is a natural experiment on a defined cohort. What is missing is not data but permission and instruction — a mandate to link pension-administrator records to school employment records around the date of a rule change. Jarrold-Grapes and Mahler (2024) came closest and were explicit that they could not compute effects on vacancies or achievement because the vacancy data did not exist.

**Is outreach additional?** New South Wales's funnel has no counterfactual, and neither does England's advisory service, which is why its GBP 5,400 per recorded returner cannot be compared with anything. This is the cheapest unresolved question in the field: the list already exists, the letter costs almost nothing, and a staggered or randomised rollout across regions would produce a defensible estimate within one hiring cycle at a cost close to zero. No system has done it. A single OECD member could settle for everyone whether employer-record outreach works, and the paper should say so in those words.

**Are returners better or worse than the alternative hire?** The only published comparison anywhere is England's retention comparison — 32% against 29% out of service at four years, and 60% higher odds of leaving after the first year back. No system has published a comparison of returners' and novices' student outcomes in comparable posts, and North Carolina's evaluators state plainly that they could not attempt one. Until such a comparison exists, the claim that former teachers bring back classroom craft rests on inference from the tutoring trials, where the protocol was structured, and is contradicted at the one point where it was tested directly on mentoring (WWC, 2019). The paper should carry the claim about what former teachers bring back as a proposition supported by role-specific trials, not as an established property of the population — and should note that the missing comparison is a school-workforce-census question, not a research-funding question.

## CANDIDATE PROPOSITIONS FOR THE PAPER

1. **Every system already has a re-entry policy, set inadvertently by its pension, service-crediting, licensure and salary rules, and the highest-return act available to a minister is to audit that aggregate price rather than to launch a programme** — which is why removing one earnings cap in North Carolina produced roughly 3,500 returners and its expiry produced none, while Georgia's own audit found 45% of school systems reporting that the statute had blocked a hire they wanted to make.

2. **Money moves former teachers only when it is the price of an itemisable friction, never when it is the price of a preference** — Portugal's single decree drew 63 returners with an inducement to reverse a decision and some 2,200 with money to defer one; China's allowance is conditioned on relocation to border and poverty-lifted counties and comes with housing and travel; and teachers discount deferred compensation at roughly five to one.

3. **The instruments that produced results ride a register the state already maintains, which gives them reach and self-measurement in a single property, while purpose-built programmes must buy both and in practice buy only reach** — New South Wales converted about one in ten of 1,500 named recent leavers from its own records, England converted 27 of 5,729 self-selected registrants, and the only audited national take-up figures in the corpus exist because a pension fund or an auditor had to produce them anyway.

4. **Because a re-entry measure's political return is realised at announcement, accountability has to be created in the founding instrument or it will not exist** — a mandated count with a custodian other than the sponsor, a sunset short enough to make the count matter, and a permission to link the pension and payroll registers, on the Georgia pattern that produced the field's only closed loop of sunset, audit and informed renewal.

5. **Re-entry policy is structurally incapable of delivering the redesigned job that returners ask for, and the redesign it can deliver is a smaller quantity of the same job — so it should be understood not as a supply remedy but as a diagnostic on the profession, a friction-removal function on a flow policy did not create, and a procurement channel for small quantities of scarce capacity such as relief cover, tutoring dosage and mentoring**, which is where the causal evidence actually sits and where former teachers earn their place on their own merits.

6. *(reserve proposition, if a sixth is wanted)* **Re-entry warrants a permanent administrative function and almost no programme budget, and the right expenditure is zero or negative under four observable conditions** — when the system cannot enumerate its working-age leavers and therefore defaults to retirees, when the entry pipeline is loose enough that rehiring is a transfer from new graduates to pensioners, when schools cannot absorb the returners they already attract, and when the gap is concentrated in the subjects where outside options are best and return is therefore rarest.
