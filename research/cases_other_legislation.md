## 1. Ireland — Career Break Scheme for registered teachers

### 1.1 Identification

- **Official name:** Career Break Scheme for Registered Teachers, set out in **Chapter 7 of Circular Letter 0054/2019**, *Leave Schemes for Registered Teachers Employed in Recognised Primary and Post-Primary Schools* (Department of Education, 2019).
- **Responsible body:** Department of Education and Youth (formerly Department of Education), Teacher/SNA Terms & Conditions Section, Athlone.
- **Legal instrument for the 2025/26 flexibility:** **Information Note TC/IN 0005/2025**, *Temporary changes to the Career Break Scheme for Registered Teachers employed in Recognised Primary and Post Primary Schools – 2025/26 School Year*, signed by James Walsh, Principal Officer, dated **26 June 2025**; it explicitly **replaces Information Note TC/IN 0009/2024** (Department of Education and Youth, 2025a — full text extracted from the official PDF).

### 1.2 Verified rules (from Circular 0054/2019 / gov.ie service page)

- A career break is **unpaid special leave of at least one school year**, extendable annually, up to **5 years at any one time** and a **maximum of 10 years over a teacher's career**; the teacher retains the right to return to teaching afterwards (Department of Education and Youth, 2025b).
- Approved purposes include voluntary service overseas, accompanying a spouse/partner on posting, education, public representation, family reasons, personal development and self-employment (Department of Education and Youth, 2025b).
- **Application deadline: 1 February** each year prior to the school year in which the career break starts or is extended; employer decision due by 1 March; withdrawal after 14 April requires employer approval (Department of Education and Youth, 2025b; St. Senan's Education Office, n.d.).

### 1.3 The 2025/26 change (and what it actually is)

- Under the scheme proper, a teacher on career break may not hold a publicly funded teaching post, and substitute work was capped (per St. Senan's Education Office (n.d.): 90 substitute days/year at primary, 300 hours at post-primary under earlier suspensions; the underlying restriction is Chapter 7, Paragraph 8.1 of Circular 54/2019).
- **TC/IN 0005/2025 suspends those restrictions for 2025/26**: a teacher on career break "may now be employed, in a substitute capacity only, without the restrictions imposed in the Career Break Scheme… The employment… can also include part-time hours"; pay is "at the same rate as a regular substitute" (Department of Education and Youth, 2025a).
- **This is an annual, temporary suspension that has been renewed for "the past number of school years"** (wording of TC/IN 0005/2025 itself), i.e. at least since Information Note TC 0015/2021 (per St. Senan's Education Office, n.d.) and via TC/IN 0009/2024 for 2024/25 (TUI, 2024). It is a teacher-supply measure, introduced in response to substitute shortages (context: 1,847 vacant teaching posts reported July 2025 — Irish Times headline in search results, article not opened [seen in search snippet only]).
- **Salary progression:** substitute days worked while on career break "count towards progression on the incremental salary scale and superannuation"; a career-break teacher substituting at primary level is paid on a casual basis for the first 40 days, then at their personal rate (St. Senan's Education Office, n.d.). Note: incremental credit for substitute service flows from Ireland's general incremental-credit rules, **not** from TC/IN 0005/2025, which is silent on salary progression.
- **Panel eligibility:** fixed-term/temporary (including substitute) and part-time teachers can gain access to the **Supplementary Redeployment Panel** for permanent posts. For 2026/27 this is governed by **Circular 0074/2025** (published 19 November 2025): eligibility requires having progressed **3 increments** on the salary scale (post-resignation increments if previously permanent/CID) and minimum 2025 gross earnings of EUR 36,750 (pre-2011 entrants) / EUR 33,400 (post-2011 entrants); application deadline 19 December 2025 (Department of Education and Youth, 2025c — details from official gov.ie circular listing and search results). So substitution during a career break can indeed help build panel eligibility — but indirectly, through these separate rules.

### 1.4 Scale, results, status (2026)

- **Status:** in force; restrictions suspended again for 2025/26 by TC/IN 0005/2025 (effective 26 June 2025). No announcement for 2026/27 located as of 4 August 2026.
- **Scale:** the Department publishes counts of teachers on career break (by year, sector, gender) in its *Teacher Statistics* series on gov.ie, but specific figures were not retrievable in this session [UNVERIFIED — data exists but numbers not obtained]. No published evaluation of the substitution flexibility was found.
- **Political context:** in December 2022 the Department considered "temporary and modest amendments" to leave schemes amid shortages; unions (ASTI) attacked any curtailment as "another cosmetic measure" that could push career-break teachers to resign outright (RTÉ News, 2022).

### 1.5 Corrections to the Excel

1. The Excel implies the lifting of restrictions is new "from the 2025/26 school year". **Wrong emphasis:** the suspension has been renewed annually since at least 2021/22; TC/IN 0005/2025 merely continues it for 2025/26.
2. "**supplementary supply panel**" conflates two distinct mechanisms: the **Supplementary Redeployment Panel** (priority access to permanent posts, Circular 0074/2025) and the **Substitute Teacher Supply Panels** (primary-level blocks of substitute cover). The correct referent here is the Supplementary Redeployment Panel.
3. Salary-progression and panel effects are **not** provisions of the career-break information note; they arise from separate incremental-credit and panel-access rules. The Excel's causal phrasing ("so time spent substituting now counts…") overstates what the 2025/26 change did.
4. The vague source "Career Break Scheme (gov.ie)" is resolved into the citations below (circular, information note, service page).

### 1.6 Filter verdict

**Keep-as-brief-mention** — fully verifiable in primary sources and a neat example of activating *temporary* leavers as a substitute pool (with increment/panel incentives preserved), but it is a leave-retention scheme rather than re-entry legislation for former or retired teachers, and no uptake figures or evaluation exist.

## 2. Canada (Ontario) — OTPP re-employment limit ("50-day rule")

### 2.1 Identification

- **Official basis:** re-employment rules for pensioners set out in **Schedule 1 to the Teachers' Pension Act, 1990** (R.S.O. 1990), administered by the **Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan (OTPP)**. Changes require agreement of the plan's **co-sponsors: the Ontario government and the Ontario Teachers' Federation (OTF)** (OTPP, 2026; Ontario Ministry of Education, 2022).
- Not a program but a standing pension rule whose ceiling has been moved with labour-market conditions.

### 2.2 The rule (verified)

- A retired member may work **50 days per school year** for a participating employer (directly or indirectly) without affecting the pension; they may finish the calendar month in which they exceed the limit, but are "not entitled to receive [the] pension for any month(s) [worked] after the month in which [they] exceed the limit… even if [they] work for only one day". Payments resume in the first month with no re-employment or on 1 September, whichever is earlier (OTPP, 2026).
- Retirees can always work beyond the limit if they accept temporary pension suspension; they are responsible for tracking their own days (OTPP, 2026; Ontario Ministry of Education, 2022).

### 2.3 History of the ceiling (verified — the Excel's "1990" is correct)

| Period | Limit |
|---|---|
| Pre-1990 | 20 days/year |
| 1990 – Aug 2012 | **95 days/year for the first three post-retirement working years, then 20 days/year** ("95/20 rule"), raised in 1990 "in the face of a dire teacher shortage" (Hammer and Alphonso, 2010; Stewart, 2007; OSSTF, n.d.) |
| From 1 Sept 2012 | Flat **50 days/year** — announced May 2010 after a Globe and Mail investigation into costs and crowding-out of new graduates (Hammer and Alphonso, 2010; OSSTF, n.d.) |
| 2020-21, 2021-22, 2022-23 school years | Temporarily **95 days** for retirees holding OCT certification working in publicly funded schools, by government–OTF agreement, expiring 30 June 2023 (Ontario Ministry of Education, 2022; OTF, 2023) |
| Since 1 July 2023 | Back to **50 days**; in **March 2024 the OTF refused** the Minister's request for a further 95-day extension (OCEOTA, 2024; Alphonso, 2024). "There's currently no change to the 50-day re-employment limit" (OTPP, 2026). |

### 2.4 Evidence, figures, union pushback

- **2010 Globe and Mail investigation** (basis for the 2012 tightening): Ontario's 10 largest boards spent **CAD 108.3 million** on retired teachers in 2008-09; retirees on supply earned nearly double new teachers (**CAD 432.69/day vs CAD 227.36/day**); ~12,774 newly certified teachers in 2008 chased ~6,500 annual openings; at one board retirees got 2,861 supply assignments vs 1,638 for new graduates (Hammer and Alphonso, 2010).
- **Pandemic increase (announced 10 January 2022** by Minister Stephen Lecce after agreement with OTF, for 2021-22, repeating the 2020-21 amendment): justified by staffing data showing almost 40% of boards reported a quarter of teacher absences going unfilled. OTF cautioned it did "not expect that many retirees are interested" — only **about 60 of 142,000 retired members** had expressed interest since September 2021 — and noted the average age of retirees was 72 (Global News, 2022). Boards had to file monthly reports on retiree hiring as an OTF condition (Ontario Ministry of Education, 2022).
- **Union pushback (March 2024):** OTF declined a fourth consecutive extension request, calling it a "band-aid" that masks systemic problems: "The Pension Plan is designed to provide an income to retired teachers; it is not a tool for addressing labour market challenges"; "Deflecting responsibility onto retired teachers is neither a sufficient nor a sustainable option" (OTF President Yves Durocher, quoted in Alphonso, 2024; OCEOTA, 2024). The 2010 reporting also documents the crowding-out concern (young graduates vs pension-drawing retirees) that motivated the *downward* move in 2012 — a rare documented case of a rehiring incentive being rolled back for equity reasons.

### 2.5 Corrections to the Excel

1. **Source attribution wrong:** "Ontario College of Teachers" is only the certification regulator. The rule lives in **Schedule 1 of the Teachers' Pension Act, 1990 / OTPP**, and changes are negotiated by the co-sponsors (Ontario government + OTF). Cite OTPP/OTF/Ministry memos instead.
2. The Excel says the plan "lets retired teachers work up to 50 days… a limit that… went as high as 95 days during a severe shortage in 1990". **Nuance:** the 1990 move raised the limit from **20** (not 50) to 95 days — and only for the first three post-retirement years, with 20 days thereafter. The flat 50-day cap dates only from **September 2012**, when the limit was *reduced* to protect new-graduate hiring.
3. Pandemic increase confirmed (2020-21 through 2022-23, expiring 30 June 2023); union pushback confirmed and citable to March 2024.

### 2.6 Filter verdict

**Keep** — the best-documented example in the file of a pension re-employment cap used as a two-way policy dial (raised in shortages 1990 and 2020-23, cut in surplus 2012), with hard numbers on costs, uptake and crowding-out, and explicit union-side arguments.

## 3. France — cumul emploi-retraite for retired teachers

### 3.1 Identification

- **Nature:** an enabling statutory framework, not a program. **Cumul emploi-retraite (CER)** lets retirees combine a pension with earnings; retired public-school teachers are re-hired by rectorats (education authorities of the académies) as **agents contractuels or vacataires**, typically for replacements and shortage subjects.
- **Legal basis (verified in sources opened):** Articles **L. 161-22 and L. 161-22-1** of the Code de la sécurité sociale; capped cumul and the six-month rule originate in the **Loi n° 2003-775 du 21 août 2003** (pension reform); the framework was liberalised by **Article 26 of Loi n° 2023-270 du 14 avril 2023** (LFRSS 2023 — which also created new pension rights for work resumed in retirement), implemented by **Décret n° 2023-753 du 10 août 2023** (Légifrance, 2023a; 2023b; Assemblée nationale, 2020; 2023).
- Rules for combining a civil-service pension with new *public-sector* pay also involve the Code des pensions civiles et militaires de retraite [UNVERIFIED — the relevant académie pages (ac-paris.fr, ac-montpellier.fr) and service-public.gouv.fr could not be opened in this session].

### 3.2 The six-month wait (verified, with an important qualification)

- The waiting period applies to **resuming work with one's last employer**: "a retired public agent, whether permanent or contractual, may resume a professional activity, including teaching duties, with their last establishment subject to a **six-month delay between the effective date of the pension and the resumption of activity**" (ministerial answer to Assemblée nationale QE n° 2072, 28 March 2023 — Assemblée nationale, 2023).
- Its purpose, per the Government's answer to QE n° 25142 (1 September 2020): to stop employers immediately re-hiring their own just-retired staff at lower pay; it attaches to **capped** cumul (cumul plafonné) under the 2003 law. That answer also mentioned a then-planned reduction to 3 months for post-1975 cohorts — [UNVERIFIED whether ever enacted] (Assemblée nationale, 2020).
- **Correction of nuance for the Excel:** the wait is not a blanket "six months before any teaching"; it bites because a teacher returning to the Éducation nationale is returning to their **last employer**. Retirees meeting full-cumul conditions (all pensions liquidated, full rate/age) face a more liberal regime.

### 3.3 National Assembly debate (the Excel's "National Assembly Question" resolved)

- **QE n° 7305 (16th legislature), Mme Béatrice Bellamy (Horizons, Vendée), submitted 18 April 2023** (renewed 19 September 2023), to the Ministry of National Education and Youth: citing millions of teaching hours lost to unreplaced absences, she asked — by analogy with retired-doctor policies — which regulatory obstacles (CER restrictions) should be lifted to let willing retired teachers do substitute work. **The question was never answered; it was closed on 11 June 2024 at the end of the legislature** (Assemblée nationale, 2024). This is the most on-point citation for "debated in the National Assembly".
- **QE n° 2072 (16th), M. Robin Reda (Renaissance, Essonne)**, submitted 11 October 2022, answered 28 March 2023: about retired teachers as *vacataires* in higher education; the answer contains the Government's clearest statement of the six-month rule and confirms the cessation-of-activity requirement in L. 161-22 was abolished (Assemblée nationale, 2023).
- **QE n° 11063 (17th), M. Julien Odoul (RN, Yonne)**, submitted 18 November 2025, answered 12 May 2026: argues CER conditions (all pensions liquidated, 172 quarters, age 63.5) discriminate against teachers who left before the 2015 reform with early pension rights, capping their combined income (~EUR 1,500 vs ~EUR 2,600 + full salary for complete careers); the Government defended the contributory logic and pointed to 2026 legislation to harmonise cumul conditions (Assemblée nationale, 2026).

### 3.4 Use, scale, results

- **No official aggregate figures on retired teachers returning under CER were found**, consistent with the Excel's claim; the ministerial answers cited above give none (Assemblée nationale, 2023; 2026).
- Anecdotal use by académies: in December 2021 the **rectorat de Toulouse wrote to recently retired teachers** inviting them back as reinforcements amid the Omicron-wave substitute crisis; the rector conceded the initiative had "not had the desired impact" (France Bleu Occitanie, January 2022) [seen in search snippet only — article returned HTTP 403].
- Context for policy salience: ~**330,000 teacher retirements expected by 2030** (VousNousIls, 26 March 2025, citing labour-ministry data) [seen in search snippet only].

### 3.5 Corrections to the Excel

1. "after a six-month waiting period from the date they retired" — **qualify**: the six-month délai runs from the pension's effective date and applies to re-employment **with the last employer** (which is what returning to the Éducation nationale entails); it stems from the 2003 reform, not from teacher-specific rules.
2. "**Boards** use it to fill posts" — France has no school boards; recruitment of retired teachers as contractuels/vacataires is done by **rectorats/académies**.
3. "debated in the National Assembly" — verified, but note the flagship question (Bellamy, QE 7305) **went unanswered** and lapsed in June 2024; the live 2026 debate (Odoul, QE 11063) is about CER *income caps* penalising pre-2015 leavers, a barrier angle useful for the paper.
4. Since the 2023 reform (Loi 2023-270, art. 26), retirees in full cumul **accrue new pension rights** from resumed work — an incentive the Excel omits.

### 3.6 Filter verdict

**Keep-as-brief-mention** — a verifiable enabling framework and a well-documented *barrier* case (six-month wait, income caps, unanswered parliamentary pleas), but with no uptake data or evaluation it cannot carry more than a contrast paragraph.
