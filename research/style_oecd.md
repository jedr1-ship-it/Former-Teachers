### 1.2 Body structure

Two observed variants:

- **Single-chapter variant (WP 342)**: one chapter title equal to the paper title, then 7 unnumbered first-level sections: *Introduction; Key concepts: …; Drivers of …; Towards a framework for …; Ensuring the quality of …; Enabling system conditions: How policies shape …; Towards coherence and quality in …* (the last is the conclusion — note it is phrased as a forward-looking claim, not "Conclusion"). Boxes/figures/tables numbered flat (Box 1…Box 10, Figure 1…5, Table 1–2).
- **Numbered-chapter variant (WP 213, WP 328)**: `1 Introduction`, `2 Results…` (new template drops the dot: "1 Introduction"; old template used "1. …" with sub-sections 1.1, 1.2, and even 1.1.1). Figures then number per chapter (`Figure 1.1`, `Figure 2.3` in WP 328). **For our ~40–60 page paper with distinct chapters (barriers → incentives → policy lessons), use the numbered-chapter variant with per-chapter figure/table numbering; boxes may still number flat (WP 328 does: "Box 1.") — but "Box 3.1" style is what OECD full reports use, and is also acceptable.**

**Opening apparatus of the body (signature move of the project's papers)**: WP 342 opens the body with a full-page bordered box headed **"Key Take-Aways"**, containing 3 bold mini-headings (*Potential benefits… / Key challenges to consider / Enabling system conditions*) each followed by 4–5 bullets of the form "**Bold claim label:** one-sentence elaboration." Reproduce exactly this device (an alternative label used elsewhere is "Key messages"; *Executive summary* belongs to the Policy Perspectives series, not the working papers).

### 1.3 Boxes ("Box 3.1. Country example" pattern)

- Box title: `Box 1. Subject Specialist Accelerated Academic Pathway: The STEP Programme from Finland` — i.e. **Box N. [Category label]: [Programme name] + country**. Title in blue bold on the box's first line (no separate title bar).
- WP 342's country boxes follow a fixed 5-part internal template with **bold run-in leads**: `Purpose and Origins:` / `Structure and Delivery:` / `Entry and Participants:` / `Certification and Progression:` / `Outcomes, impacts and challenges:` — each a justified paragraph with normal author-year citations inside. This maps perfectly onto our programme entries (e.g. NSW Teachers Re-Engage, China Silver Age, Portugal's pension top-up): rename leads as needed (e.g. *Purpose and origins / Design and incentives / Take-up and results / Challenges*).
- Boxes are cross-referenced from the running text before they appear: "…implemented in Germany and Sweden (see Box 5)." / "Finland (depicted in Box 1)".
- One box ≈ ⅔–1 page; WP 342 has 10 boxes in 40 body pages (~1 per 4 pages).
- A methodological box is also idiomatic (WP 328's "Box 1. Key findings of the Policy Survey…"; WP 342's "Box 10. How to Use the Framework – Guidance for Different Audiences" with sub-leads "For policy makers", "For researchers"…).

### 1.4 Figures and tables

- Caption **above** the graphic: `Figure 1. Priorities for attracting teachers by policy area for 2025-30` in blue bold, followed by an *explanatory subtitle line* in smaller roman type stating the measure: "Percentage of education systems specifying policy areas of "high" or "very high" importance in at least one level of education."
- Below the graphic, in small type:
  - `Note: A policy area of "high importance" is considered…` (definitions, sample restrictions, significance conventions: "'**' indicates differences significant at 5%. '***' indicates differences significant at 1%.")
  - `Source: OECD (2024[8]), Education Policy Outlook 2024: Reshaping Teaching into a Thriving Profession from ABCs to AI, https://doi.org/10.1787/dd5140e4-en, Chapter 2 - Figure 2.1.` — i.e. the Source line repeats the in-text citation *with* its bracketed reference number, full title in italics, DOI, and pinpoint. Database sources: `Source: Based on OECD, TALIS 2024 Database (OECD, 2024[9]) - https://www.oecd.org/en/data/datasets/talis-2024-database.html.` Interview-based boxes: `Source: Interview with Sarah Stewart and Alison Glover (Open University); Glover, A. and S. Stewart (2023[70]); …`
- Tables: title `Table 1. Criteria and categories of alternative pathways` in blue bold; header row shaded light grey (#D2D2D2 measured), thin rules, no vertical gridlines dominant; same Note:/Source: apparatus.

## 2. Prose style

Quantitative fingerprints below are measured on WP 342's ~22,200-word body text.

- **Register**: impersonal, institutional, present-tense. The paper itself is the agent: "This working paper provides…", "The paper introduces…", "This section examines…", "It concludes by identifying…". No "I"/"we" in WP 342's analytical text. No rhetorical questions in body prose (the Policy Perspectives briefs do use numbered guiding questions in their Executive summary).
- **Sentence length**: mean ≈ 25 words, median ≈ 23 (841 sentences sampled). Long but controlled; typically one subordinate clause plus a parenthetical country list or citation cluster. Paragraphs 3–6 sentences, justified.
- **Hedging economy (counts per ~22,000 words)**: *can* 82, *may* 58, *often* 48, *must* 27, *likely* 13, *should* only 12, *could* 5. That ratio is the voice: **possibility and capacity ("can", "may") dominate; obligation ("should"/"must") is reserved for design principles**, e.g. "Quality assurance mechanisms must ensure that alternative routes uphold equivalent standards". *tend to* (10) softens empirical generalisations: "The most effective examples tend to be those that integrate structured coursework, supervised practice and ongoing mentoring".
- **Contrast scaffolding**: *while* 76, *however* 18 — nearly every evaluative paragraph runs "benefit + however/while + risk + condition under which benefit holds". Archetype (WP 342 conclusion): "it also shows that these benefits are neither universal, automatic nor evenly distributed. They depend heavily on how systems design, regulate and support these routes."
- **"Systems" vs "countries"**: *systems* 83 (of which "education systems" 24) vs *countries* 51. **"Education systems" is the actor/unit of analysis** ("6 and 7 out of ten education systems with data identify…", "systems must remain responsive and inclusive"); **"countries" is geographic** ("across OECD countries", "Such programmes are present across several OECD countries", "countries face the challenge of ensuring…"). WP 328/Policy Perspectives also use "jurisdictions" for survey respondents; WP 342 never does. Follow WP 342: systems act, countries locate.
- **How country examples are woven in** — three registers, always escalating: (1) parenthetical enumeration in running text: "– notably in Luxembourg, Sweden, the Netherlands, Norway, Finland (depicted in Box 1) and New Zealand –"; (2) one-sentence cameo with citation: "Such programmes have been implemented in Germany and Sweden (see Box 5)."; (3) full box (see §1.3). Never more than a couple of sentences on one country in running text — depth goes in boxes.
- **Policy implications phrasing** (verbatim patterns from WP 342's final section, to imitate): "For policymakers, the key implication is that implementing alternative pathways is not a quick fix…"; "Policymakers therefore…"; "Systems should systematically collect and analyse data on programme effectiveness…"; "By [collecting and] analysing data, governments can identify which pathways are most effective…"; "Looking ahead, countries face the challenge of ensuring that diversification leads to coherence rather than fragmentation."; "For researchers, this underscores the need for more systematic evidence on…"; closing turn: "Ultimately, the question is not whether to expand or restrict alternative pathways, but how to design them so that flexibility coexists with quality." Recommendations are conditions, trade-offs and implications — never imperatives, never numbered "Recommendation 1".
- **Spelling and mechanics**: British/OECD spelling with -ise (*recognise, prioritised, co-ordination* with hyphen in OECD house style, *programme, labour*); "%" with figures; spaced en dashes for asides ("– such as expanding teacher supply and recognising prior experience –"); serial comma generally absent ("governments, providers and schools") though not perfectly consistent; numbers: words below 10 in prose, figures with units/percentages; decade spans as "2025-30".
- **Section-opening move**: each section starts with a one-paragraph framing claim anchored on an OECD flagship citation (Education Policy Outlook, TALIS, Education at a Glance), then narrows: "In this context, alternative pathways into teaching have gained renewed policy attention as education systems seek new ways to respond to workforce challenges."

## 3. Citation and bibliography format

**In-text (OECD house style — author-year plus bracketed reference number, rendered as subscript in print):**
- Parenthetical: `(OECD, 2025[1])` — the `[1]` is the entry's number in the reference list, typeset subscript.
- Clusters, semicolon-separated, no alphabetical requirement: `(OECD, 2020[4]; Darling-Hammond, 2017[5]; OECD, 2018[6])`.
- Narrative: `Chetty, Friedman and Rockoff (2014[2])` — three authors all named; four or more: first author + et al.
- The same `(Author, year[n])` form is used inside boxes, figure Source lines and annex tables.
- In Word, produce this with Zotero/EndNote "OECD style" if available; a faithful manual fallback is author-year with subscripted bracket numbers keyed to the bibliography order. (Dropping the `[n]` markers and using plain author-year is a legible simplification, but the brackets are the single most recognisable OECD tic.)

**Reference list**: headed "References", alphabetical by author, hanging indent, each entry with its `[n]` right-aligned on the first line. Journal titles and book/report titles italic; article/working-paper titles in double quotes; volume/issue as `Vol. 21/2`; DOIs as full URLs; `(accessed on DD Month YYYY)` for web sources. Author format: first author "Surname, Initial."; later authors "Initial. Surname"; joined by "and" (no ampersand): `Boeskens, L., D. Nusche and M. Yurita (2020)`.

**Three concrete worked examples (copied from WP 342's reference list, opened and verified):**

1. Journal article:
   > Bengtsson, A. and L. Mickwitz (2021), "The complexity of professional integration: An investigation of newly arrived teachers' initial process of establishing themselves as teachers in Sweden", *European Educational Research Journal*, Vol. 21/2, pp. 214-229, https://doi.org/10.1177/14749041211009421. `[80]`
2. OECD report:
   > OECD (2024), *Education Policy Outlook 2024: Reshaping Teaching into a Thriving Profession from ABCs to AI*, OECD Publishing, Paris, https://doi.org/10.1787/dd5140e4-en. `[8]`
3. OECD working paper (canonical form; note WP 342's own list renders the series number as "Vol. 235" — a reference-manager artefact; prefer the canonical "No. 235"):
   > Boeskens, L., D. Nusche and M. Yurita (2020), "Policies to support teachers' continuing professional learning: A conceptual framework and mapping of OECD data", *OECD Education Working Papers*, No. 235, OECD Publishing, Paris, https://doi.org/10.1787/247b7c4d-en.

Also observed and useful for our source types:
- Government/programme webpage: `Australian Government Department of Education and Training (2025), High Achieving Teachers (HAT) Program, https://www.education.gov.au/... (accessed on 18 June 2025). [72]`
- Book chapter: `Alfonso, M., M. Bassi and A. Santiago (2012), "Enseña Chile, EnseñaPerú y Enseñá por Argentina", in M. Cabrol & M. Székely (ed.), Educación para la transformación, Inter-American Development Bank, https://doi.org/10.18235/0012493 (accessed on 29 July 2025). [84]`
- Undated web source: `All, T. (n.d.), Teach For All, https://teachforall.org/ (accessed on 10 June 2025). [85]` (n.d. = no date).

### 4.2 Typography hierarchy mapped to Garamond

OECD's current template is Arial-based (embedded fonts verified: Arial/Arial Bold body + headings, Arial Narrow for figure notes; the 2020 template used Times New Roman body + Arial headings — so a serif body is not un-OECD). Since the deliverable is Garamond, keep OECD's *hierarchy, colours and spacing* and let Garamond carry all text; sizes below compensate for Garamond's small x-height (~1–1.5 pt above the Arial original, which is ~10 pt body; sizes are recommendations, not OECD measurements).

| Element | Word style spec (Garamond) |
|---|---|
| Chapter title (H1) | 20 pt bold, #4E81BD, space after 18 pt; numbered "1 " (no dot) or unnumbered for a single-chapter paper |
| Section (H2) | 14 pt bold, #4E81BD, space before 14 pt / after 6 pt (e.g. "Introduction", "Drivers of re-entry…") |
| Sub-section (H3) | 12 pt bold, #4E81BD (or 11.5 pt bold italic for H4) |
| Body | 11.5–12 pt, black, **justified**, single spacing, 6 pt after paragraph, no first-line indent |
| Key Take-Aways / box body | 10.5–11 pt; bold run-in leads; bullets as small squares/round, indented |
| Box title | 11–11.5 pt bold, #4E81BD, inside the box, first line |
| Box container | Paragraph shading #EEECE1; box border ½ pt, #4E81BD; internal padding ~0.2 cm; keep-with-next so boxes don't split badly |
| Figure/Table caption | 11 pt bold #4E81BD ("Figure 2.1. …"); subtitle line 10 pt regular black below it |
| Note:/Source: lines | 8.5–9 pt (Garamond or Arial Narrow for authenticity), flush left, "Note:"/"Source:" not bolded |
| Table header row | Bold, 9.5–10 pt, shading #D2D2D2; table body 9.5–10 pt; horizontal rules only (top, under header, bottom), ½ pt |
| Running footer | 8 pt, ALL CAPS/small caps, centred: `FORMER TEACHERS: BARRIERS, INCENTIVES AND POLICY LESSONS FOR RE-ENTRY © [INSTITUTION] 2026` |
| Page numbers | Top outer corner with pipe: verso `20 |`, recto `| 21` (mirror margins) |
| TOC | Entries in the heading blue for headings, black for figure/table lists; blocks headed FIGURES / TABLES / BOXES in blue caps |
| Charts (Excel/Word) | Bars/lines #156082 (primary) and #4F81BD/#A7B9E3 (secondary), gridlines #D9D9D9, no chart border, title carried by the Word caption not the chart, axis labels ~9 pt |

Margins ~2.5 cm; A4. Colour discipline: **blue is structural only** (headings, captions, box frames) — body prose, emphasis and tables stay black/grey; emphasis via bold run-in leads, not italics-heavy text.

### 4.3 What NOT to copy
- OECD logo, cover art, the "PUBE" stamp, the cote (EDU/WKP…), the verbatim series disclaimer, and any implication the paper *is* an OECD publication. Style imitation ≠ impersonation: the cover should carry our own institutional identity, with an OECD-blue/green-inspired geometric motif at most.

## 5. Typical length and balance (for a ~40–60 page working paper)

Measured distributions:
- **WP 342 (61 pp.)**: front matter 5 pp.; body 40 pp. (~22,200 words); References 10 pp. (~130 entries); Annexes 5 pp. Inside the body: Introduction 2 pp.; Key concepts 3 pp.; Drivers 4 pp.; **core framework + country boxes 20 pp.**; Quality evidence 5 pp.; Enabling system conditions 5 pp.; forward-looking conclusion 1–2 pp. Density: 10 boxes, 5 figures, 2 tables in 40 body pages — roughly one display item per 2.5 pages, one box per 4 pages.
- **WP 213 (82 pp.)**: front matter 7 pp.; body 40 pp. across 6 numbered chapters (context 11 pp.; framework 4 pp.; three analytical chapters 5–11 pp. each; conclusion 2 pp.); Annexes 19 pp.; References 16 pp.
- **WP 328 (99 pp.)**: body only 32 pp. — the annex (58 pp. of country tables) carries the evidence. A model if we want a country-by-country annex table of re-entry programmes/legislation (our Excel maps directly onto an "Annex Table A A.1. Overview of identified re-entry programmes across countries").

**Recommended allocation for our ~50-page paper** (mirroring WP 342's proportions):
| Component | Pages |
|---|---|
| Cover, series-style page, Acknowledgements, Abstract (+Résumé), TOC | 5–6 |
| Key take-aways box | 1–1.5 |
| 1 Introduction (context, shortages, why re-entry) | 2–3 |
| 2 Key concepts & the leaver/retiree pool (hidden reserve evidence) | 4–5 |
| 3 Why teachers leave — barriers to return | 6–8 |
| 4 Programmes and incentives for re-entry (framework + country boxes — the core) | 12–15 |
| 5 Retirees as mentors/tutors (second core strand) | 5–6 |
| 6 Enabling system conditions & policy lessons | 5–6 |
| 7 Towards … (forward-looking conclusion, unnumbered feel) | 1.5–2 |
| References (~100–140 entries) | 8–11 |
| Annexes (programme/legislation overview tables) | 3–6 |
Target ~20,000–24,000 body words; 8–12 boxes (one per country programme worth a deep dive), 4–6 figures, 2–3 body tables.

## 6. Flags on the team's Excel (style/citation-relevant)

The structured Excel (`excel_structured.json`) is usable for content but **fails OECD citation standards as-is** — every claim will need a real reference built to §3 format:
- Sources are non-citable labels: "2019 Paper", "2018 NFER Evaluation Report", "News in ORF", "2025 Case Study", "Government Notice", "Education Hub", "KLFY.com" — no authors, titles, publishers or URLs anywhere in the file.
- Two sources are personal communications ("John Rice, WestEd (email via Matthew Soldner)"; "Amy Feygin, AIR (email via Matthew Soldner)") — OECD practice would be an interview/communication Source line (cf. WP 342: "Source: Interview with …"), and they are marked as unpublished/anecdotal.
- Typos to fix before quoting: "NSW Goverment Announcement" (Government); "ChatGTP" in a quality comment; "US (4 states)" program has an empty `name`.
- Truncations/incomplete rows: the last research entry has `country: "International: USA,"` cut mid-list with **empty `short` and `long`** fields (only "2025 Paper", quality 4, comment ending "…however.." mid-sentence) — the underlying study is unidentifiable from the file alone; pepe's comment on that row and the phrase "the model model is quite simplistic" are visibly truncated/garbled. `quality_jose`/`comments_jose` are empty throughout.
- Editorial-voice mismatches to neutralise when drafting in OECD register: phrases like "one of the few return programs anywhere that publishes its full funnel", "with little deadweight", "called the expectation of their return an illusion" are punchier than OECD prose — keep the content, hedge the voice (see §2), and put program detail into boxes.
