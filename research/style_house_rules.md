# House style rules (binding, from the lead author)

These rules OVERRIDE any conflicting habit. They apply to every chapter, box, note and summary of the paper.

1. **The prose must not read as AI-generated.** Write like an experienced OECD analyst, not like a language model. Concretely, avoid the tics that give machine text away:
   - No "pausa y dos puntos" constructions ("The problem: ...", "El resultado: ...", "One caveat: ..."). Colons are allowed occasionally where genuinely natural (introducing a list, a ratio, a formal definition), but never as a rhetorical device, and never twice on the same page.
   - Em dashes (—) only sparingly. At most one every few paragraphs, and never two in the same sentence. Prefer commas, parentheses or splitting the sentence.
   - No triadic filler ("clear, concise, and compelling"), no "not only X but also Y" chains, no "It is important to note that", "crucially", "moreover" carpets, no paragraph-opening "Overall," everywhere.
   - No bullet lists inside body prose except where an OECD paper would genuinely use one (key messages, recommendations). Chapters are written in full paragraphs.
   - Vary sentence length and paragraph rhythm. Some short sentences. Some long ones that carry a whole argument before landing.
   - No section that opens by announcing what the section will do ("This section discusses..."), except the one place OECD papers conventionally do it (end of the introduction, roadmap paragraph).
2. Hedge like a researcher (evidence "suggests", "is consistent with"), not like a marketer ("powerful", "transformative", "game-changing" are banned).
3. Every number carries its source and year in the text. No unattributed statistics.
4. British English spelling (OECD convention): programme, labour, behaviour, organisation.
5. Spanish is not used in the paper body; the paper is written in English throughout.
