# NARRATIVE REFORM BRIEF — binding for the v4 rewrite

## Why the v3 narrative was rejected

The lead author rejected v3's narrative outright. The verdict: no soul, no substance,
reads as machine-written, does not surface the points that matter. The diagnosis:

- **The protagonist of v3 is bookkeeping.** Registers, netted series, counting duties,
  audits and "measurement gaps" carry the thesis; teachers and governments are scenery.
  Chapter climaxes are instructions about data hygiene.
- **The findings are buried under their own methodology.** The paper's genuinely
  dramatic discoveries (27 placements vs 3 000 returns; two identical careers a salary
  scale apart by accident; a pension fund telling a ministry its plan "is not a tool";
  a state auditing itself and finding its own rules blocking hires; a third of returners
  gone again in four years) appear as evidence for measurement claims instead of AS the story.
- **Template rhythm.** Every section performs the same bolded-lede + qualification dance;
  every take-away was a meta-statement about counting. The register was compliant and dead.

## The story the verified evidence actually tells (the v4 spine)

**Thesis: states are asking their former teachers to come back while their own laws
charge them for returning. The decisive re-entry policy of a state is not its
recruitment programme; it is the price its rules put on a return — a price written
mostly by accident, in pension and civil-service law, that no ministry has ever
totalled. Repair the deal, do not advertise it.**

The arc, in order:

1. **The people.** Every year teachers walk out of classrooms and keep their licences.
   They did not vanish: most stay close, most stay qualified, and states talk constantly
   about this "reserve" while never having honestly counted it. Who are they? Three
   groups (life-event leavers, mid-career resigners, retirees), and the distinctions
   that matter for policy (served/never-served, the pension position).
2. **The deal they made.** Leaving was a purchase: they gave up earnings to get their
   time back. That is why they return for days and part-time contracts, not full-time
   posts — and why the survey answers (salary) and the observed behaviour (conditions)
   split. What they can do when back: the only deployment ever shown to work was a
   defined job at a specified dose; twenty years of craft may deserve a premium, but
   only a specified purchase has ever been priced.
3. **The record of attempts (the centre).** Governments under pressure bought campaigns;
   campaigns returned teachers by the dozen. The mass returns on record were not asked
   for: a rule changed, the price fell, teachers came back through ordinary hiring
   (North Carolina). The price is written in four places (pay, exclusion, intensity,
   licence) — four dials, almost all owned outside education ministries, almost all set
   by accident (Austria's crediting rule). The fights over the dials reveal who owns
   them (Ontario's pension co-sponsor refusing: "not a tool for addressing labour
   market challenges"). One state audited its own scheme and found its own rules
   blocking the hires it wanted (Georgia).
4. **Repair the deal.** The instructions, in the order the evidence supports them,
   told as repairs: total the bill your own state presents to a returner and take it
   to the departments that wrote it; publish an honest count of the people you could
   recall; sell the hours they want to buy; buy defined work and evaluate at purchase;
   check who stayed (a third of the one followed cohort did not); and know when the
   right spend is zero. None of this is a campaign. Almost all of it is cheap.

## LEAD AUTHOR'S SIX THREADS (added after review — these OVERRIDE the old shortage rule)

The lead author has ordered six ideas woven through the narrative, in roughly this
causal order. They form the paper's motivation layer and its claim to a contribution:

1. **Governments have long run active policies to re-attract teachers.** This is not a
   new fashion; the record compiled here spans decades of programmes, statutes and
   campaigns. Say so early.
2. **This paper compiles and analyses them.** That compilation IS the contribution:
   nobody had put the international record of re-entry policy side by side and asked
   what actually moved people.
3. **A teacher shortage exists in many countries.** NAME IT as motivation, honestly and
   without drama, using only facts already in the verified research base. The old rule
   ("shortages only in passing, unquantified") is REVOKED as a gag; what survives of it
   is only the analytical point that re-entry alone cannot close a shortage and should
   not be sold as the fix.
4. **The teaching workforce is ageing.** Retirement waves are feeding the reserve and
   raising the stakes; use the age/seniority material already in the base.
5. **Retirees and experienced leavers carry experience that is valuable in itself —
   the endogenous growth of schools.** Experienced colleagues raise the quality of the
   teachers around them (the peer-spillover evidence already in Chapter 2); recovering
   them is recovering capability the system itself paid to build, not just filling a slot.
6. **Re-attraction efforts are partly shortage cover — say so.** The honest framing:
   systems knock on former teachers' doors partly because vacancies hurt, and the paper
   respects that motive while showing that the returns worth having (volume, retention,
   experience) come from repairing the deal, not from the knocking.

7. **The paper's core mass is the description of the policies themselves, grouped.**
   The lead author's instruction: the largest share of the paper describes what
   governments actually did, organised into policy families. The pattern to follow:
   "in multiple countries, efforts have been made to re-attract retired teachers to
   classrooms, seeking X and Y... to that end, several routes are enabled... in China,
   for example...; in [country], for example...". For each family: the objectives
   governments declared, the routes they enabled, and generous country-by-country
   description of the concrete schemes, with their detail told in prose. This
   deliberately RELAXES the old "argue, never enumerate / no catalogues" rule:
   descriptive texture with named country examples is now wanted as the paper's body,
   with the analysis (campaigns versus rules, the four dials, what verifiably happened)
   as the frame around it rather than a replacement for it. Chapter 3 carries this and
   should be clearly the largest chapter.

These threads do not replace the "repair the deal" spine; they motivate it. The arc
becomes: shortage and ageing → governments have tried re-attraction for decades → we
compile and analyse that record → campaigns return dozens, repaired rules return
thousands → and the prize is more than cover, it is experience that improves schools
from within.

## Binding style instructions for the rewrite

- Same facts, same citations, same tables and boxes, same propositions P1-P6 underneath.
  NOTHING in the evidence base changes. What changes is who the story is about.
- Stakes first. Sections open on what is at issue for teachers, schools or budgets —
  never on a data source, a register, or the paper's own apparatus.
- Findings are told as discoveries, with their drama intact, not as support for claims
  about measurement. The Austria accident, the Ontario refusal, the Georgia audit, the
  27-vs-3 000 arithmetic, the third-gone-in-four-years are protagonists.
- Measurement material survives as the SERVANT of the story (how we know; what a
  minister should demand next), compressed, mostly in Chapter 4 and never as a section's
  emotional climax except in the counting-duty section where the Georgia loop IS the story.
- Kill the template rhythm: vary paragraph attack, vary sentence length, let a strong
  finding land in a short sentence. No formulaic bolded-lede dance; keep bolded ledes
  where the house format wants them but write each as a real sentence a human would say.
- Keep the OECD register (measured, evidenced, British spelling, house rules: no
  rhetorical colons, no em dashes in body prose, no triads) — the corpus's best papers
  drive hard while staying in register. Affirmative grammar per TEAM_INPUT §7 still binds.
- Shortages: still only in passing, unquantified (§8). Former teachers matter per se.
- P4 stays a measurement gap, never a verdict on craft (§6). The reserve is badly
  measured, never non-existent.
