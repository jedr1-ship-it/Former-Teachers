## 1. "10 mirrors" — Universidad de Zaragoza, Spain

### Identification
- **Official name:** *"10 mirrors"* (the project title is in English in the Spanish original). Full chapter title: *"10 mirrors: El acompañamiento y la orientación formativa de los estudiantes de los grados en magisterio desde la experiencia profesional"* (Lizalde Gil and Cortés Pascual, 2019).
- **Responsible body:** Faculty of Education (Facultad de Educación), Universidad de Zaragoza — the project sits inside the Faculty's University Orientation Plan (Plan de Orientación Universitaria, part of the university-wide POUZ) (Lizalde Gil and Cortés Pascual, 2019).
- **Launch date:** ran in the period before the September 2018 Congreso Internacional de Orientación Universitaria (CIOU, Zaragoza, 5–7 September 2018), where it was presented; the proceedings were published in 2019. Exact academic year of operation (likely 2017/18) [UNVERIFIED].
- **Legal instrument:** none — an internal university guidance/innovation project, not a government programme.

### Verified content
- **Design:** 10 retired teachers ("mentors") with recognised professional track records were paired with **57 education-degree students** (36 Primary Education, 21 Early Childhood Education) to provide accompaniment and formative/professional orientation "from the contrasted experience of retired teachers" (Lizalde Gil and Cortés Pascual, 2019, abstract via Dialnet).
- **Dosage:** three in-person sessions, covering teaching vocation, professional development and the teaching career — **not** continuous mentoring "throughout their training" as the Excel states.
- **Incentives:** none documented for mentors (voluntary participation implied) [UNVERIFIED — no compensation information in the abstract].
- **Evaluation:** pre/during/post questionnaires on student expectations only. Reported results: 37.50% of participants valued improvements to their training and 56.25% valued the professional guidance received; the authors conclude a "positive relationship … between training and professional reality" (Lizalde Gil and Cortés Pascual, 2019). No control group, no learning or labour-market outcomes.
- **Status 2026:** no evidence of continuation. The Faculty of Education's current "Programa Mentor" is peer mentoring by senior *students*, not retirees (Facultad de Educación, Universidad de Zaragoza, n.d.). [UNVERIFIED whether "10 mirrors" was repeated after 2018.]

### Corrections to the Excel
1. Source "2019 Paper" is actually a **2019 book chapter in conference proceedings** (CIOU 2018), not a journal article: Lizalde Gil, M. and M. P. A. Cortés Pascual (2019), in P. Allueva Torres (coord.), *Orientación y calidad educativa universitarias*, Servicio de Publicaciones, Universidad de Zaragoza, pp. 373–384, ISBN 978-84-16723-62-1.
2. "Mentoring and support **throughout their training**" overstates the intervention: the documented pilot consisted of **three sessions** in one academic year.
3. Scale should be added: 10 mentors, 57 students — a one-off micro-pilot.

### FILTER VERDICT: **keep-as-brief-mention**
One of very few documented Spanish examples of retirees mentoring *pre-service* teachers and now fully citable, but a tiny one-off pilot evaluated only with satisfaction questionnaires — usable as an illustration, not as evidence.

## 2. Retired Mentors for New Teachers — Aurora Public Schools, Colorado (US)

### Identification
- **Official name:** Retired Mentors for New Teachers program.
- **Responsible body:** Aurora Public Schools (APS), Colorado — fifth-largest district in Colorado (64 schools, >40,000 students at study time). Evaluated by Regional Educational Laboratory (REL) Central (administered by Marzano Research) under IES contract ED-IES-12-C-0007 (DeCesare, McClelland and Randel, 2017).
- **Launch date:** developed by APS before the 2013/14 school year; the RCT covers 2013/14–2014/15. Exact programme start year not stated in the report [UNVERIFIED].
- **Legal instrument:** none — district programme.

### Verified programme design (DeCesare, McClelland and Randel, 2017)
- **Mentors:** master educators (teachers *and principals*) recently retired from APS; eligibility ≥5 years teaching in the district (in practice the mentor team averaged 22 years in APS high-need schools; most had 10+ years in district, 20+ in education) plus a record of excellence (student achievement, performance reviews, leadership); screened by interview with the district head of professional development. Mentors were **paid USD 41/hour** (national comparison rate USD 51/hour); caseload of 3–7 mentees (typically 3–4 per school) in 1–2 elementary schools.
- **Mentees:** probationary teachers (first three years with the district, regardless of prior experience — actual prior experience ranged 0–15 years) in high-need (Title I, ≥80% FRL) elementary schools.
- **Dosage:** two years; weekly one-on-one meetings plus monthly school-level mentee-cohort meetings; mentors had no role in evaluation. Programme mentees also kept the district's business-as-usual "buddy" mentor (a colleague paid a USD 500/year stipend, ~15 contact hours/year) — so the RCT estimates the *added* value of the retired mentor.

### Verified results
- **RCT:** 77 probationary teachers in 11 Title I elementary schools, randomly assigned within school (38 programme, 39 business-as-usual); analytic samples 1,189 students/70 teachers (year 1), 1,155/66 (year 2 reading), 1,105/62 (year 2 math).
- **Math:** year 1 +1.4 MAP points, effect size 0.064, p = .045 — significant at .05 but **not** after the report's own Benjamini–Hochberg multiple-comparison adjustment (threshold .025); year 2 ES 0.061, n.s.
- **Reading:** ES 0.014 (year 1) and 0.066 (year 2), both n.s.
- **Teacher evaluation ratings:** no effect (point estimate negative, n.s.).
- **Retention:** no significant effect (year-2 leavers: 11 programme vs 5 control). Exploratory (non-causal, programme group only): each additional 10 hours of mentoring associated with a doubling of the odds of staying (p = .004).
- **Cost:** ~USD 171 per student per year in local prices (USD 237 at national prices); exploratory ROI: projected lifetime-earnings gains (via Chetty, Friedman and Rockoff estimates) could repay annual cost >15 times. Implementation fidelity met on all components except summer access to mentors.

### What Works Clearinghouse review — precise verdict (WWC, 2017, Study #88756)
- **Rating:** *Meets WWC group design standards **without** reservations* — reviewed as a cluster RCT with low cluster-level attrition and low individual-level non-response, under the WWC **Review of Individual Studies Protocol, version 4.0, and Standards 4.0**.
- **Outcome domains reviewed:** (i) reading achievement (NWEA MAP), (ii) mathematics achievement (NWEA MAP), (iii) teacher retention in the school district.
- **WWC characterisation:** **indeterminate effects — no statistically significant findings in any of the three domains, in either year** (the WWC's characterisation applies its own corrections, under which the report's year-1 math result is not significant).

### Corrections to the Excel
1. The Excel sentence is broadly right but conflates two sources: the **REL report itself** headlines a *statistically significant positive year-1 math effect* (which disappears under multiple-comparison adjustment), while the **WWC review** characterises all reviewed outcomes (reading, math, retention) as null. Cite both; note the WWC did not list teacher *evaluation ratings* among the reviewed domains shown on the study page.
2. Worth adding: this is a *high-internal-validity* null/weak-positive — "Meets WWC standards without reservations" — and one of the only RCTs anywhere on retirees mentoring new teachers, with cost data (USD 171/student/year).
3. "2017 Report" → DeCesare, McClelland and Randel (2017), REL 2017–225; "WWC Review" → WWC (2017), Study #88756.
4. **Status 2026:** no public evidence the programme still operates in APS [UNVERIFIED].

### FILTER VERDICT: **keep**
The single most rigorous causal test of retirees as mentors for early-career teachers, with cost and ROI data — central to the paper's "retirees as mentors" section regardless of the mostly-null findings.

## 3. "United States (4 states)" — Number Rockets scale-up (retired/substitute teachers as math tutors)

### Identification
- The unnamed entry is the **Number Rockets** scale-up replication trial run by REL Southwest: the "2015 Paper" is **Gersten et al. (2015)** in *American Educational Research Journal*; the same study was first released as an NCEE report (Rolfhus et al., 2012, NCEE 2012-4007).
- **Intervention:** Number Rockets, a scripted Tier-2 small-group (2–3 pupils) tutoring programme for grade 1 students at risk in math; ~40-minute sessions, ~3×/week for 17 weeks (~30 hours total on top of classroom instruction) (Gersten et al., 2015; Evidence for ESSA, n.d.).
- **The earlier programme it replicated:** Fuchs et al. (2005), a Vanderbilt University efficacy trial (564 first graders screened; 127 at-risk students randomised; tutoring 3×/week for 16 weeks) that found significant positive effects on computation and concepts/applications (not fact fluency). In that original trial **the tutors were the authors' graduate students** — the reason Evidence for ESSA excludes it and the replication with a realistic workforce mattered (Fuchs et al., 2005; Evidence for ESSA, n.d.). (Note: Gersten et al. describe the original sample as 139; the JEP abstract says 127 randomised.)

### Verified replication facts (Gersten et al., 2015; Rolfhus et al., 2012; WWC Study #86426)
- **Scale:** 994 at-risk first graders in 76 schools across four urban districts in **four of the five REL Southwest states** (the region comprises Arkansas, Louisiana, New Mexico, Oklahoma and Texas; the four study states are not named in the public record).
- **Tutors:** **86 tutors recruited from local pools of retired and substitute teachers** — i.e., retirees *and* substitutes, hired and trained for the study (one-day initial training plus two 2-hour follow-ups), not regular district hires.
- **Results:** intervention students scored significantly higher on the TEMA-3 general math proficiency test (+4.28 points; **effect size +0.34**); exploratory interaction analyses n.s.
- **Evidence ratings:** WWC — *Meets WWC group design standards **with** reservations* (cluster RCT with high individual non-response; baseline equivalence satisfied), statistically significant positive effect on general mathematics achievement, Tier 2/moderate evidence (WWC, n.d., Study #86426). Evidence for ESSA — "Strong" category.

### Corrections to the Excel
1. **Name the programme**: Number Rockets (currently disseminated with support from the American Institutes for Research; materials cost ~USD 95–120 per tutor set, plus tutor salaries) (Evidence for ESSA, n.d.).
2. Tutors were **retired *and substitute* teachers**, not only retirees.
3. The pension sentence ("Hiring retirees through tutoring programs, rather than school districts, can also avoid pension-related complications") **is not in the 2015 paper** — it needs a different source or deletion. Closest verifiable support: Texas school-board guidance explaining that the TRS pension surcharge on district-rehired retirees does not apply if the retiree works half-time or less, which makes part-time tutoring a low-friction re-entry route (TASB, 2018). As a general claim across states it remains [UNVERIFIED].
4. In the trial, tutors were engaged **by the study team**, not by the districts — consistent with, but not proof of, the "avoid pension complications" argument.

### FILTER VERDICT: **keep**
Large multi-state RCT showing that a tutor workforce of retired and substitute teachers can reproduce (at somewhat smaller effect size) results originally obtained with graduate students — directly relevant to using retirees productively without full re-employment.

## 4. AARP Foundation Experience Corps — United States (national)

### Identification
- **Official name:** AARP Foundation Experience Corps.
- **Responsible body:** AARP Foundation (programme joined AARP in late 2011 and formally became part of AARP Foundation in 2015; before that run by Civic Ventures and local partners) (Wikipedia, 2026; Columbia Mailman School of Public Health, 2025).
- **Launch:** concept paper by John W. Gardner (1988); pilot designed 1993–95 by Marc Freedman and Dr. Linda P. Fried; **first volunteers entered schools in early 1996** in 12 schools across five cities (Philadelphia, South Bronx NYC, Minneapolis, Portland OR, Port Arthur TX) (Wikipedia, 2026). AARP and Columbia both date the launch to 1995 (AARP, 2025; Columbia, 2025) — the Excel's "since 1996" is defensible for classroom operation.
- **Legal instrument:** none — nonprofit programme; some sites use AmeriCorps/CNCS funding streams (historically, stipended slots up to USD 278/month) (WWC, 2010).

### Verified programme model
- **Volunteers:** adults **aged 50+** (55+ in the early research era); minimum high-school diploma; ≥20 hours initial training (15–32 hours across sites in the RCT era) plus background screening. **Being a former teacher is *not* an eligibility requirement** — the Excel's "former teachers volunteers" framing overstates; volunteers are older adults, *some* of whom are retired educators (share [UNVERIFIED]) (ProvenTutoring, n.d.; WWC, 2010; AARP, n.d.).
- **Students:** grades K–3, reading 1–1.5 years behind; sessions typically two 30-minute sessions/week (2–4×/week, 25–45 min across sites), one-on-one or groups ≤3 (ProvenTutoring, n.d.; WWC, 2010).
- **Scale and status 2026:** active — celebrated its 30th anniversary in 2025. In school year 2023/24: **1,284 volunteers tutored 3,659 students in 18 US communities**; among 2,573 students with ≥2 test scores, 95.3% improved on at least one metric (AARP, 2025; AARP, n.d.). (Columbia (2025) says "16 cities" — minor discrepancy; prefer AARP's own 18-community figure.)

### Verified evidence — both RCTs
- **Student reading RCT** (the Excel's "2009 Paper"): Morrow-Howell et al. (2009), Washington University in St. Louis CSD Report 09-01 — 881 students in grades 1–3 (430 T / 451 C) in 23 schools in Boston, New York City and Port Arthur (TX), 174 volunteers, school years 2006/07–2007/08. Significant gains on Woodcock–Johnson passage comprehension (**ES 0.13**) and teacher-assessed grade-specific reading skills (**ES 0.16**) — roughly a 50th→56th percentile move; **no** significant effects on receptive vocabulary or phonemic awareness. The **WWC Quick Review (February 2010)** rated the research "**consistent with WWC evidence standards**" — "a well-implemented randomized controlled trial". Peer-reviewed version: Lee et al. (2012), *Education and Urban Society* 44(1), 97–118.
- **Volunteer health RCTs (Johns Hopkins, Baltimore):** pilot RCT of 128 volunteers aged 60–86 (Fried et al., 2004) found increased physical, cognitive and social activity; the full **Baltimore Experience Corps Trial** (design: Fried et al., 2013) found causal increases in physical activity/walking (Varma et al., 2016, *Am J Prev Med*), increases in generativity (Gruenewald et al., 2016, *J Gerontol B*), and — in a neuroimaging subsample of 111 — 0.7–1.6% *gains* in cortical and hippocampal volumes over two years in men, against age-typical declines in controls (Carlson et al., 2015, *Alzheimer's & Dementia*).
- The Excel's "one of the few programs rigorously shown to benefit both sides of the exchange" is **supported**.

### Corrections to the Excel
1. "Former teachers volunteers" → "volunteers aged 50+, many but not all retired professionals; retired educators are a subset" (share undocumented).
2. Launch: classroom operation from early 1996, programme launched/designed 1995 — both dates appear in official histories.
3. "18 US communities" confirmed (AARP, 2023/24 data); note Columbia's 2025 "16 cities" figure.
4. Resolve "Program Research Page" → AARP Experience Corps impact page (aarp.org/experience-corps/); "2009 Paper" → Morrow-Howell et al. (2009), plus WWC (2010) quick review and Lee et al. (2012) journal version.

### FILTER VERDICT: **keep**
The best-evidenced deployment of older/retired adults in schools anywhere — dual RCT evidence (student reading + volunteer health), 30 years of operation, current scale data — but reframe as an *older-volunteer* programme in which former teachers participate rather than a former-teacher programme per se.

## 5. "United States (unspecified)" — pension-related savings funding mentor stipends (Next Education Workforce link)

### Identification attempt
- **Claim in Excel:** some states rehire retirees who keep their pensions, so the position carries lower benefits costs; savings are redirected to stipends for resident/apprentice teachers matched with veteran/retired mentors; reportedly used by the **Next Education Workforce** initiative (Arizona State University) in New Mexico or Arizona. Source: personal communication (John Rice, WestEd, e-mail via Matthew Soldner).
- **What could be verified:**
  - The **Next Education Workforce (NEW)** initiative exists at Arizona State University's Mary Lou Fulton College; it builds team-based strategic staffing models and reached ~150 schools in 17 states in school year 2024/25 (ASU Next Education Workforce, n.d.; K-12 Dive, 2025).
  - NEW works **in New Mexico**: Albuquerque Public Schools is partnering with NEW to redesign up to 20 middle schools around team-based models, and ASU has hired a New Mexico-based NEW implementation coach (CRPE, n.d.; ASU job posting JR103045).
  - **New Mexico's enabling law exists** (and is already a separate Excel legislation entry): the Educational Retirees Returning to Work Act (House Bill 73, 2022) lets retired educators return for up to three years with no salary/hours limits while keeping pension benefits (NM Higher Education Department, 2022).
- **What could NOT be verified:** any public NEW, WestEd, state or district document describing the specific mechanism "pension-related benefits savings → stipends for residents/apprentices matched with retired mentors". Extensive searching found nothing. **[UNVERIFIED — rests entirely on the personal communication, as the Excel itself concedes ("No published studies on this specific approach were identified").]**
- **Substantive caveat to the premise:** the assumption that a rehired retiree's position "doesn't need to cover benefits costs" is state-dependent — several pension systems levy employer surcharges or contributions on rehired retirees (e.g., Texas TRS surcharge when a rehired retiree works more than half-time; TASB, 2018), which can erase the claimed savings. The Excel's own caveats (offset if retiree is high on the salary scale; mixed mentoring experiences, generational gaps, currency of retirees' skills) are anecdotal from the same e-mail.

### Corrections to the Excel
1. The entry cannot be attributed to Next Education Workforce on the basis of any public source; if retained, it must be cited as *J. Rice, WestEd, personal communication via M. Soldner (2026?)* — with the year of the e-mail added by the team.
2. NEW's verifiable New Mexico activity (Albuquerque Public Schools partnership) and NM's HB 73 (2022) can be cited as the enabling context, but neither documents the funding mechanism.

### FILTER VERDICT: **drop** (as a standalone case)
The core mechanism is unverifiable in any public source and rests on a single second-hand personal communication; at most, fold a one-sentence acknowledgement of the idea into the paper's funding discussion, cited explicitly as personal communication, next to the verifiable New Mexico rehiring law.
