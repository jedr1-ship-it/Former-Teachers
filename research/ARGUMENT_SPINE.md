## 1. THE THESIS

Former teachers are a distinct policy population — qualified, once in service, now out of it — and the registers that already name them can count that population to a specification any ministry could publish within the year: served rather than never served; certificate valid, restorable or lapsed; last taught within five years or beyond; subject and phase against vacancies; age against pensionable age; region — six cuts that every register in this corpus already supports, each of which moves the count downward, so that the netted series is an honest upper bound on the pool while the headline figures now in circulation describe the rule of the register that produced them. What governs whether that population comes back is a price, and the state sets most of it outside education: pension, service-crediting and salary-scale rules fix the step a returner lands on and what they forfeit for standing on it, and because the price is charged to people who left deliberately, having bought fewer hours and more control at a wage cost they were willing to accept, the offers that recruit former teachers in numbers are offers of days rather than posts — the exception being the one system that filled establishment posts at scale by stacking a full pension on a full salary. Re-entry policy is therefore a standing administrative function of government: it audits and removes the charges the state levies on its own former employees, it procures small quantities of specific capacity that former teachers and almost nobody else can supply, it moves numbers by changing an institution rather than by sending a message, and it rests on a measurement — a netted reserve, a returner linked to the classes and colleagues they work with — that a ministry can build within the year on data it already holds; a modest margin of the answer to staffing, and one that can be staffed, costed and judged.

A reader can disagree with all three sentences. They can hold that published reserve figures already measure an available labour supply awaiting better marketing; that what governs return is information and licensure rather than price and product; and that re-entry scales into an answer to shortages. The paper's job is to show, from the record, why each of those positions is harder to hold than it looks — and, where the record is silent rather than adverse, to name the measurement that would end the argument.

## 2. WHY THE THESIS IS WORTH A PAPER

Take a reader who currently thinks of former teachers as a staffing reserve. That reader has in mind a number, an intention and an instrument: a register count of qualified people not teaching, a plan to convert some share of them, and a programme with a budget, a target group and a campaign. The paper hands that reader three things they do not yet have.

**A countable population.** The paper supplies the specification that turns a register count into a measurement, and three systems have published enough of their own internals to show what each cut does. England's headline pool of roughly 366 000 contains 106 000 people who have never taught, already held as a separate series. Michigan's 61 252 contains 44 to 49 per cent holding expired certificates, against 6 per cent among serving teachers. The Netherlands produces a reserve of about 62 000 or of about 98 000 in the same year depending on which register rule is applied, and a share of the qualified stock that reads 22 per cent for primary and 43 per cent for secondary and vocational. Every deduction those registers can compute runs in the same direction, which is why the netted figure is an honest upper bound and the headline figure is a register design. A ministry that has been quoting a reserve is a definition and a query away from quoting a measurement, on data it already holds.

**An auditable price.** What a government charges a returner is a sum it can total and publish. The variable that most plausibly separates the corpus's one mass return from its many small ones is the salary step a returner is placed on, and in most systems that step is set inadvertently, in pension and civil-service law, by a department with no interest in teacher supply — which is why the price is the ministry's to audit and largely somebody else's to set. The audit that would reveal it costs a week; no country in the corpus has published one, and any country could.

**A function a ministry can staff and judge.** The evidence supports a permanent administrative function: it removes the state's own charges, sells hours rather than posts, buys defined roles at a specified dose and measures what it buys, and reports destination and survival. That is a smaller ask than a recruitment programme and a more defensible one, it has a home in an organisation chart and a line in an estimate, and it is the kind of thing a minister can actually do.

What the reader gains, then, is a re-entry policy that can be judged, and a specification for the two measurements that would let them judge it better still: a netted reserve, and a returner linked to the classes and colleagues they work with. What it displaces along the way is small — an unnetted headline figure, and a campaign that was never the instrument.

## 3. THE SUPPORTING PROPOSITIONS

Six propositions, ordered so that each opens the next. The first defines the population and states the count that measures it, the second the price it faces, the third the offer that matches it, the fourth what it can be asked to do and what has been measured about that, the fifth what re-entry policy delivers, and the sixth what the published record measures and where a counting duty is most needed.

### P1. The actionable count of former teachers is a netted series that a register can already produce — served rather than never served; certificate valid, restorable or lapsed; last taught within five years or beyond; subject and phase against vacancies; age against pensionable age; region — six cuts that every register in the corpus already supports, each of which moves the number downward, so the netted figure is an honest upper bound on the population, the published headline describes the register rule that made it, and producing the series is a definitional and administrative task rather than a research programme.

**Evidence that carries it.** The cuts are specified from what registers demonstrably hold, and three systems have published enough of their own internals to show what each one does. England's headline pool of roughly 366 000 is 260 000 qualified teachers of working age out of service *plus* 106 000 who have never been in service (Worth, 2023, citing DfE): 29 per cent of the quoted pool has nothing to return to, and the two series are already held separately. Michigan, which links certificates to employment records, shows what sits inside a single register: of 61 252 certified teachers not teaching, 70 per cent had not taught in a public school at any point in the previous five years, 44 to 49 per cent held expired certificates against 6 per cent of active teachers, and among respondents who had never taught, 53 per cent said they had been unable to obtain a full-time position (Lindsay, Gnedko-Berry and Wan, 2021). The Netherlands produces about 62 000 or about 98 000 in the same year depending on which register rule is applied, and a share of the qualified stock that reads 22 per cent for primary against 43 per cent for secondary and vocational — a divergence in the measurement, not in the population. It also wrote the confusion into law: Article 1 of the *Regeling tegemoetkoming herintreders primair onderwijs* defines a returner as a teacher who has interrupted "or has not begun" a career in primary education (Staatscourant 2017, No. 62002).

**Strongest objection.** Three, and they stack. That registers are not comparable is a thirty-year-old observation in comparative education policy. From a pure supply standpoint the never-served are convertible people, so excluding them is a definitional convenience. And a netted figure would still not be an available supply, since willingness, subject, geography and price all bind after the register does — so netting may buy less clarity than the paper promises.

**How the paper answers.** It concedes the supply point and refuses it under its own premise: people who never taught have no craft, no curriculum experience and no mentoring capacity to bring back, and under the premise this paper is written on they are not members of the population, whatever they are for a supply model. It concedes the third objection outright and turns it into the proposition's boundary — a netted reserve is an upper bound honestly stated, not a forecast, and the paper claims only that a defensible upper bound beats an indefensible one. What it does not concede is that the claim is the tired one about registers differing. The claim is that the deductions are computable and unpublished: England, the Netherlands, Michigan and Australia each hold last-taught date, certificate status, service history, subject and phase, and age against pensionable age, and no system in the corpus publishes a reserve net of any of them. So the paper states the netting specification rather than the complaint — served versus never served; certificate valid, restorable or lapsed; last taught within five years or beyond; subject and phase against vacancies; age against pensionable age; region — and observes that every one of those cuts the number and none of them raises it. That is why the direction of the error is known while its magnitude is not, and why the first recommendation in this paper costs a query rather than a budget line. Publishing the netted series is a contribution nobody in the field has made.

### P2. A system's re-entry policy is the aggregate price its own rules charge for coming back, and the single most consequential number in that price is the salary step a returner is placed on — set, in most systems, inadvertently, in pension law, by a ministry that does not answer to education.

**Evidence that carries it.** Austria excludes from prior-service crediting any period that already generates a pension, so a returning former civil-servant teacher draws a full pension plus the teacher *starting* salary while a former contract teacher, whose service is credited, broadly recovers pre-retirement pay (BMBWF, 2023, answer to Question 19, p. 18). Two identical careers, offers differing by most of a salary scale, on the basis of an employment status chosen decades earlier, and nobody designed it as a re-entry incentive because it is not one. The audited national result is 1 062 returners across six school years against a workforce of about 123 000 (Rechnungshof, 2025, pp. 24-25). Portugal then wrote the Austrian setting deliberately into its returner offer, indexing Article 5 pay to the *first step* of the career scale and pro-rating it by teaching hours (Decreto-Lei n.º 51/2024, Art. 5), and accepted 63 people against a 200-place quota. North Carolina set the same dial the other way, letting retirees draw full salary alongside a full pension, on the order of half as much again as a regular full-time teacher's income, and roughly 3 500 returned across a decade, averaging 31 years of experience, hired by more than half the schools in the sample (Jarrold-Grapes and Mahler, 2024). The governance half is equally documented: Ontario's cap can be moved only by agreement of the pension plan's co-sponsors, one of which refused a fourth extension in March 2024 on the ground that "the Pension Plan is designed to provide an income to retired teachers; it is not a tool for addressing labour market challenges" (Alphonso, 2024; OCEOTA, 2024); England's abatement bites only on final-salary Age and Premature benefits and will dissolve as career-average membership matures, without any ministerial decision (Teachers' Pensions, n.d.); France's six-month waiting period is a social-security rule whose stated purpose is to stop employers re-hiring their own just-retired staff more cheaply (Assemblée nationale, 2020).

**Strongest objection.** These instruments trade in permission rather than price, and the corpus is full of liberalisations that produced nothing. Ontario raised its cap from 50 to 95 days for the pandemic years; New Mexico removed salary and hours limits altogether and its flagship tier drew 154 users in its first year against roughly 48 000 potentially eligible retirees (NMERB, 2023; NM HED, 2022). Worse for the proposition, the one employer-side dataset in the corpus was asked what stopped it hiring returners and did not say salary: Georgia's audit names the thirty-year service rule, the one-year break and the high-need-subject methodology, with 45 per cent of 163 surveyed school systems reporting a blocked hire, while its rehired retirees earned an average of USD 78 487, which is a veteran wage that schools were evidently willing to pay (Georgia DOAA, 2025).

**How the paper answers.** By narrowing the claim until it is exactly true and then pressing it. The proposition is about the *level of pay a returner ends up on*, not about the removal of a limit, which is precisely why it survives Ontario and New Mexico: neither raised the money, and neither moved anybody. The Ontario figure most often quoted against permissiveness (about 60 of 142 000 members expressing interest) is a union forecast published on the day the change was announced and measures appetite for a forty-five-day increment, not appetite for returning; the corpus contains no post-liberalisation Ontario take-up figure at all, and the paper says so rather than borrowing the number. Georgia is conceded in full and folded in: the price has at least two dials, the pay dial and the exclusion dial, and Georgia is the cleanest measurement of the second. What unites them is the proposition's real payload, which is jurisdictional. Every dial that has ever been shown to move re-entry volume sits outside the education ministry. A re-entry strategy written inside an education ministry is a strategy over the least powerful instruments in the system.

### P3. Former teachers bought fewer hours and more control when they left, at a wage cost they were willing to accept, so an offer of days is the offer that matches the trade they made — which is why every system that recruits them at any scale sells hours rather than posts.

**Evidence that carries it.** Australian panel evidence puts leavers' job satisfaction 0.13 standard deviations above stayers' one year after exit and 0.19 higher after three, while weekly wages fall 6.2 per cent and 9.4 per cent and hourly wages stay lower across the following decade (Cuervo and Vera-Toscano, 2025). English leavers accept roughly a 10 per cent wage cut in exchange for shorter hours and higher satisfaction (Worth, Bamford and Durbin, 2015). Dutch inactive teachers rate several working conditions in their current jobs more favourably than active teachers rate theirs, and name lower work pressure, more control over hours and more autonomy ahead of salary (Somers et al., 2024). Very few American leavers take jobs paying more than teaching did (Scafidi, Sjoquist and Stinebrickner, 2006). Against that, look at what systems actually sell. New Zealand's standing on-ramp is twenty half-days a year of relief teaching without a current practising certificate (Education and Training Act 2020, Sch. 3). Ontario sells fifty days. Ireland's supply measure is the annual suspension of the cap on substitute work by teachers already on career break. In the Japanese prefecture studied in depth, retirees are roughly 60 per cent of part-time adjunct hires (Sakuma and Shimazaki, 2024, p. 203). New South Wales placed its 145 returners into casual and temporary work. Portugal pro-rates by hours. And NFER's typology found the segment that converted best was the smallest, career breakers at 11 per cent of registrants, returning at about double the rate of the other four types and wanting part-time work that schools rarely advertise (Buchanan et al., 2018; Worth and Sharp, 2018). The pattern long predates the current programmes: *Teachers Matter* recorded returners as about 25 per cent of the full-time inflow into UK maintained schools in 2000/01 but more than half of the part-time inflow (OECD, 2005, p. 68).

**Strongest objection.** The stated-preference evidence is split, and the split runs against the proposition in the one large survey that asked the question directly. Michigan's certificate-holders led with money: 33 per cent put a higher salary among their three most important reasons for not teaching, and a salary increase was the most frequently selected incentive to return (Lindsay, Gnedko-Berry and Wan, 2021). Separately, New Mexico's largest re-employment tier is defined by a long layout period rather than by low hours, with 1 716 of 2 792 working retirees in it, so the tier ranking cannot carry an intensity law.

**How the paper answers.** It prints the split as a finding with numbers, as `TEAM_INPUT` §2 requires, and it applies the same scepticism to both arms rather than discounting the one it dislikes. The conditions-first reading rests on revealed post-exit trajectories in three systems; the money-first reading rests on one survey with a 30 per cent response rate that its own authors describe as not representative, and whose incentive list is dominated by financial items. It also carries the honest caveat that the Australian multivariate result is estimated on a sample excluding those who left permanently and never returned, which makes it strongest evidence about temporary exit and, usefully, explains why career breakers convert best. New Mexico is conceded and re-used: what its 2 792 working retirees show is not an intensity ranking but that unprogrammed re-employment under long-standing rules dwarfs the flagship new route, which is P5's business. The operational payoff survives all of it. Advertise the hours, not the post; and tie any funded conversion support to identified vacancies, which is England's own correction after its pilot (NCTL, 2017) and the design New Zealand reached independently by making the refresh requirement contingent on *not* having a job offer (Teaching Council, 2026).

### P4. What former teachers can be asked to do is a designed role at a specified dose, and where a system has specified the role it has also measured it: structured tutoring at a stated protocol and dose carries trial evidence at scale, while the mentoring role has one small trial that set no dose and was rated indeterminate — so the craft rationale stands as an open question with a price attached, and the asymmetry between the two is one of measurement effort.

**Evidence that carries it.** The two deployments differ in how tightly the role was drawn, and the evidence tracks that difference exactly. At the low-discretion end the design was specified and the specification was tested: a scripted Tier-2 mathematics protocol, forty minutes three times a week for seventeen weeks, delivered by 86 tutors recruited from local pools of retired and substitute teachers, reproduced at scale — 994 at-risk grade 1 students in 76 schools across four urban districts — an effect first obtained with graduate students, +0.34 on the TEMA-3 (Gersten et al., 2015; Rolfhus et al., 2012). At the high-discretion end the design was left to the mentors and the trial was small: recently retired master educators averaging twenty-two years in the district's high-need schools, screened by interview for excellence, paid USD 41 an hour and deliberately excluded from evaluating their mentees, produced a study that the What Works Clearinghouse rated as meeting standards *without reservations* and characterised as indeterminate in all three reviewed domains — reading, mathematics and teacher retention (DeCesare, McClelland and Randel, 2017; WWC, 2019, Study #88756). The year-one mathematics effect of 0.064 did not survive the report's own multiple-comparison adjustment and the retention point estimates ran the wrong way; but the trial is 77 teachers in 11 schools of a single district over two years, it set no dose, and its own exploratory analysis — non-causal, and reported as such — associates each additional ten hours of mentoring with a doubling of the odds that a mentee stays. That is one trial of an unspecified dose against one trial of a specified one. Availability is amply sufficient for either design: the thresholds in this literature are in the tens of hours while the caps that constrain retirees are in the hundreds, so where retiree deployment underperforms it does so on role design, financing and measurement.

**Strongest objection.** A measurement gap is a weaker thing to put in front of a minister than a finding, and "unevaluated" reads as a licence to buy whatever the ministry already wanted. Nor does the positive trial belong cleanly to former teachers: its tutors were retired *and substitute* teachers with no published split, and substitutes are working casual teachers, not former ones; the tutoring meta-analysis distinguishes teacher, paraprofessional, volunteer and parent tutors but does not isolate retired educators (Nickow, Oreopoulos and Quan, 2024); and Experience Corps is evidence about older volunteers, since being a former teacher is not an eligibility requirement.

**How the paper answers.** It concedes every attribution problem in its own voice, once, early, and then states the hole plainly: no study anywhere in this evidence base links a returner to a student outcome or to a colleague's, and the authors of the largest re-entry episode say explicitly that they could not attempt it, having no vacancy data and no counterfactual (Jarrold-Grapes and Mahler, 2024). What it refuses is the inference that this settles anything about craft. A single underpowered trial that set no dose is not a verdict on twenty-two years of practice, and "indeterminate" under Standards 4.0 is a statement about evidential power rather than a negative result; the one deployment that was specified and dosed is also the one that was properly measured, which is a fact about research design before it is a fact about former teachers. So the per-se case is carried in the meantime by what the evidence does carry — a valid or restorable licence; immediate deployability that consumes no initial teacher education place; and institutional fluency combined with standing outside the school's evaluation hierarchy, the trust advantage Aurora's own report identifies and that neither a new graduate nor a serving head of department can offer — and the craft rationale is carried as an open question with a price attached. The paper names the measurement that would close it: link returner records to the classes they teach and the colleagues they work beside, in the systems that already hold both, and trial designed roles at a specified dose the way the tutoring trials did rather than buying seniority and hoping. Experience Corps then earns its place for a different reason, the randomised evidence of physical and cognitive benefit to the older volunteers themselves, which lets a health ministry's budget into a conversation an education ministry cannot afford alone.

### P5. What re-entry policy delivers is destination and survival — where returners land and how long they stay — and it delivers volume only where a government changes an institution rather than a message: unprogrammed return already supplies a third to a half of new entrants under standing rules, every programmed instrument in the corpus moves a fraction of one per cent of its eligible pool in a year, and the two that broke that band did so by stacking a full pension on a full salary and by making re-employment the default staffing route.

**Evidence that carries it.** The band is narrow and consistently measured across very different systems. Germany has 457 000 retired teachers, thirteen of sixteen Länder actively recruiting them, one Land writing to some 7 000 in a single school year, and just over 5 000 pensioners teaching nationwide, under 1 per cent of a workforce of roughly 800 000 (Deutsches Schulportal, 2023); Hesse's own handbook attributes about 550 filled posts to a package combining retiree recruitment with service extensions and part-time top-ups, not to the letters alone (Hessisches Ministerium, 2024, p. 8). Austria audited 1 062 returners across six school years against about 123 000 teachers (Rechnungshof, 2025). Georgia produced 635 full-time rehires across three fiscal years, about 350 in any year, against a workforce of some 125 000 and roughly 6 000 vacant or not-fully-certified posts, with its own auditors calling the effect on the shortage minimal (Georgia DOAA, 2025). Portugal accepted 63. The Netherlands budgeted for 500 and cut its ceilings twice because the money was not being spent (Staatscourant 2017, No. 62002; Staatscourant 2019, No. 28510). Set against that band, roughly 17 000 teachers return to English state schools each year, about a third of all new entrants (DfE, 2023); re-entrants have been 37 to 49 per cent of new hires in the United States (Sutcher, Darling-Hammond and Carver-Thomas, 2016); and New Mexico recorded 2 792 working retirees across four routes in a single year, of which the flagship new tier was the smallest at 154 (NMERB, 2023). The two cases that break the band broke an institution to do it: North Carolina stacked a full pension on a full salary, and Japan makes re-employment after mandatory retirement the default staffing route.

**Strongest objection.** The counts are not commensurable. The denominators differ (workforce, eligible retirees, measured reserve, vacancies), most are first- or second-year figures, and the outcomes are four different goods: Georgia's full-time rehires, New Mexico's quarter-timers, New South Wales's casual placements and England's permanent shortage-subject secondary posts cannot be ranked against one another.

**How the paper answers.** By making the incommensurability a rule rather than a caveat. Every returner count in this paper carries the intensity of the post and the denominator it is measured against, and the funnel table is built to enforce it. The proposition then does its real work by converting a volume claim the evidence will not support into two claims it will. Destination: North Carolina's returners went to schools with 3.71 percentage points more economically disadvantaged and 4.86 percentage points more Black students than average with no mandate after June 2000, though 54 per cent returned to the school they had left, so the paper states the gradient as modest and possibly inherited rather than as a movement of experience; China conditions its allowance on service in poverty-lifted, minority and border counties; Portugal narrowed its supplement to shortage zones in 2026 after conceding the blanket version "in many cases was not justified" (RTP, 2026); and Georgia shows the counter-case, having tried to steer by prohibition and found the screens blocked hires instead of redirecting them. Survival: England is the only system with the linked data, and it finds 32 per cent of its 2011 returner cohort out of service four years later plus 2 per cent retired against 29 per cent of the 2011 newly qualified cohort, with the odds of a returner leaving after the first year back running 60 per cent above average (Buchanan et al., 2018, p. 57). The paper uses that to establish the denominator that belongs in the account — cost per retained teacher-year, measured by survival at one and three years, rather than cost per hire — and observes that England alone holds the linked data to report it; the survival gap itself is small and the cost ranges overlap, so it settles the denominator rather than the value of returners.

### P6. The published record measures exactly what an independent body has a standing duty to count: every re-entry measure in the corpus that produces recurring numbers does so under a counting obligation owed outside the sponsor, which makes that obligation the drafting instrument that creates evidence — and which makes the entry-level supply market, where any displacement of new graduates would show, the place a counting duty most needs to reach next.

**Evidence that carries it.** Three instruments in the corpus produce recurring numbers, and in each case the obligation sits outside the sponsor: Georgia's statute wrote in its own sunset and its own audit, producing Performance Audit Report 24-20 and then an informed renewal through Senate Bill 150 in 2026, the only closed loop anywhere; Austria's Court of Audit produced Europe's only audited national take-up figure on its own mandate; New Mexico's Educational Retirement Board reports tiered participation annually. Against them, Michigan's PA 184 and PA 147, Virginia's critical-shortage rehires, Louisiana's Acts 394 and 344 and France's *cumul emploi-retraite* all sit inside pension systems holding exactly the same data and publish nothing. An independent duty is therefore what predicts being counted; riding a register, which all of them do, predicts nothing. The consequence for the evidence base is a selection effect the paper should state as a finding: the three most rigorous products in the corpus, NFER's independent evaluation, Georgia's mandated audit and the Austrian Court of Audit, all return negative or modest verdicts, while the cases that report success publish no evaluation at all. New South Wales's funnel appeared once, in a May 2024 release, and had vanished from the following Annual Report. A synthesis of published *evidence* is therefore biased downward and a synthesis of published *claims* upward, and nothing in the corpus permits a correction. The harm sits in the same blind spot. Ontario is the only jurisdiction that has ever wound a re-employment rule back, and it did so after a newspaper investigation found its ten largest boards spending CAD 108.3 million a year on retired supply teachers at roughly 1.9 times the new-graduate day rate while some 12 774 newly certified teachers competed for about 6 500 openings, and one board gave retirees 2 861 supply assignments against 1 638 for new graduates (Hammer and Alphonso, 2010). It is also the only rule in the corpus whose amendment requires the consent of a body that represents new entrants as well as pensioners.

**Strongest objection.** The correlation between counting and unflattering verdicts may be an artefact of when systems commission audits, since governments investigate what they already suspect. And the higher day rate is what a seniority grid does when one substitute has thirty years of credited service, not a subsidy; crowding out further assumes boards would otherwise have hired graduates for those days.

**How the paper answers.** It concedes both and narrows to what is unassailable. The claim about counting becomes a drafting instruction with a testable form: put the count inside the standing duty of a body that does not answer to the sponsor, and if that cannot be done, expect no evidence and budget accordingly. The claim about crowding out becomes a claim about denominators, which is stronger than the version it replaces. Re-entry can be immaterial at the national workforce denominator and decisive in the casual and supply market at the same time, because those are different denominators, which is why the guardrail belongs where the effect is: publish new-graduate hiring rates for casual and supply pools alongside retiree re-employment counts. Two independent corroborations are noted with their status attached, Chinese commentary that localities use silver-age recruits to delay creating formal posts (secondary source) and the Bahamian union's observation that retired educators were already working full-time in supply roles before the reservist scheme named the practice.

## 4. THE TENSIONS THE PAPER KEEPS OPEN

These are not caveats and they are not "it depends". Each is a place where the evidence genuinely fails to settle a question the paper has posed, and each gets a sentence that says so and says what would settle it.

**A. The channel that absorbs former teachers is the channel that asks least of what they carry.** Relief, casual and adjunct work is where returners reliably land, and it buys presence in a classroom rather than continuity with a class, induction or a development function. The sentence: *the record shows where former teachers are absorbed and has never been asked what is gained or lost by absorbing them there, so this paper treats the relief channel as the observed route and the designed role as the barely measured one — one trial of a designed role exists, it set no dose, and it was rated indeterminate — and it notes that the comparison is not expensive but unattempted, being available to any system that linked returner records to the classes they teach and the colleagues they work beside.*

**B. Whether pay or conditions is the price of return.** Conditions lead in Dutch, Australian and English evidence; salary leads in the one large American survey. The sentence: *the split is a finding and the paper reports it with numbers, noting that the conditions-first evidence is revealed and the pay-first evidence is stated, on a sample its own authors call unrepresentative, from a questionnaire whose options were mostly financial.*

**C. Whether willingness or absorption binds.** The country with the best-measured reserve reported both an unspent returner subsidy and regions receiving more applications than places. The sentence: *the Dutch record supports the direction of an absorption constraint and not its magnitude, because the finding is a single self-reported row about side-entrants and returners jointly, in a process evaluation of schemes covering a different sector from the subsidy, and the regional partnerships hold the numbers that would settle it.*

**D. Yield against displacement.** Yield and displacement are measured on different denominators, so a claim that re-entry is a meaningful supply lever and a claim that it puts new entrants at risk answer different questions. The sentence: *the paper asserts both in their proper places, immaterial against a national workforce and potentially decisive in an entry-level supply market, and it holds each to the denominator that produced it.*

**E. Emergency valve or structural channel.** New Zealand mainstreamed its waiver, Ireland has renewed its suspension annually for several years, most American instruments carry sunsets. The sentence: *the paper forces the choice rather than answering it on the policymaker's behalf, since an instrument justified as barrier removal should be permanent and evaluated as regulation while one justified as crisis cover should be explicitly sunset and counted, and what no system should do is run a crisis instrument on annual renewal for five years while describing it as structural reform.*

**F. Design failure or demographic ceiling.** Japan reports only about 35 per cent of age-based elementary retirees now seeking full-time re-employment, Austria's audited intakes peaked and fell, and every retiree pool ages. The sentence: *where the addressable population is retirees, part of what reads as policy failure is a stock that is old and self-limiting, and this paper does not attribute to design what demography may explain.*

## 5. THE NARRATIVE ARC

Four chapters plus front matter, 12 000 to 15 000 words, with Chapter 3 taking the largest single share as `TEAM_INPUT` §3 requires. Every chapter is argued. None is a survey, and no chapter is organised by country.

**Front matter (about 900 words).** Abstract, Résumé, Key take-aways. The abstract's rule is narrower than a prohibition, and the OECD voice study is what narrows it: four of twelve OECD abstracts do open on a supply or recruitment pressure, including both of the publications closest to this one, so recruitment pressure is not forbidden in our first sentence. What is forbidden is what those abstracts never do. They never let the pressure stand alone — it is always one term in a compound of drivers, three abreast; they never quantify it, since no first sentence in the corpus carries a numeral and no working-paper abstract carries a substantive statistic at all; and they never make the deficit the grammatical subject, which is always the education systems and what they are doing about it. Our abstract may therefore name recruitment difficulty as one driver among changing career patterns and what this workforce carries, but it is motivated by the population and the price, it quantifies nothing, and it names no country. The take-aways are five or six propositions, not nine statistics. This is a hard constraint and the single most visible signal to the lead author that the diagnosis has been absorbed.

**Chapter 1. Introduction: a population and the count that measures it (about 1 800 words).**
*Proposition established:* P1, and the paper's per-se ground.
*Opening question:* every system holds a register of people it has qualified to teach and does not currently employ; what would it take to turn that register into a count a ministry can act on?
*Evidence deployed:* the English served and never-served stocks; Michigan's internal deductions; the two Dutch reserve figures in one year; the Dutch legal definition of a returner; the netting specification the chapter asks ministries to publish, stated as six cuts every register already supports; and the *Education at a Glance 2025* Indicator D8 split between resignations and retirements as the diagnostic that decides which instrument is even relevant, with the average of 51 per cent and the two tails named.
*Cases used as evidence, and for what:* none as cases. Registers appear as evidence about registers.
*What it does that the rejected draft did not:* it names the population and states the count that measures it, in the paper's own voice and early — a netted series any register can produce, an honest upper bound, publishable within the year, against a headline figure that describes a register design and runs above it. It says in the same breath that re-entry is a modest margin; that the link between a returner and a student outcome is the measurement this field still owes itself, which is a gap in what has been measured rather than a verdict on what returners are worth; and that the per-se claim rests on a distinct policy population defined by legally created barriers together with what its members carry — so it forfeits nothing later by having overclaimed here. It also delivers the three descriptive groups the structure requires (retirees; people out of the labour market; job switchers) in a paragraph, while making the analytically live distinctions the served/never-served split and the pension position.
*Handed to Chapter 2:* a defined population, and the question of what its members bought when they left.

**Chapter 2. What former teachers bought, and what they can be asked to do (about 3 200 words).**
*Propositions established:* P3 and P4.
*Opening question:* what did the people who left actually get, and at what price did they get it?
*Evidence deployed:* the Australian panel trajectories with their sample caveat; the English wage-for-conditions trade; the Dutch inactive-teacher ratings; American destination and earnings data; Stinebrickner's life-event leavers; the Michigan survey printed as the other arm of the split; NFER's typology with career breakers correctly at 11 per cent and converting at double; the Australian willingness item used once, to make the point that a costless signal is not a supply estimate; the *Education at a Glance* seniority distribution of resigners, stated with all seven systems so that "one in three under five years" is not allowed to imply a craft-thin margin everywhere. Then the trial evidence, presented as an asymmetry in what has been measured rather than as a ranking of what former teachers can do: Number Rockets with its protocol and its dose both stated; Aurora under WWC Standards 4.0, with the scale of the trial (77 teachers, 11 schools, one district, no dose set) reported in the same breath as the indeterminate rating, and its exploratory hours association flagged as non-causal; Experience Corps with its eligibility caveat and its volunteer-health arm; the tutoring meta-analysis gradient and its failure to isolate retired educators; Jackson and Bruegmann on peer effects as the mode of transfer that visiting mentors do not reproduce; and the induction trial's one-year null alongside its year-three positive after two years of treatment. The chapter closes on a measurement request rather than a verdict: the craft rationale stands as an open question with a price attached, and what would settle it is a linkage between returner records and the classes and colleagues they work with, plus trials of designed roles at a specified dose.
*Cases used as evidence, and for what:* the three trials are evidence about role design and about the state of measurement, not about countries. Japan, Ontario, New Zealand and Ireland appear only as one sentence each, as the market's answer to the question this chapter asks.
*Handed to Chapter 3:* a specification of the product former teachers will buy — hours, a defined role, a specified dose — against which each country's offer can be read as an answer or a mismatch, and a standing question about the designed role that Chapter 4 will convert into a request.

**Chapter 3. Re-entry policy in practice: international experience (about 6 000 words). The centre of the paper.**
*Propositions established:* P2 and P5.
*Opening question:* governments have been offering former teachers a job; what price did they attach to it, and who set that price?

*3.1 Programmes (about 2 800 words),* argued around three questions asked of every programme rather than country by country: who was addressed, what was offered, and what verifiably happened. England carries the argument that the binding constraints were operational and were named as such by its own evaluators — timing relative to the hiring cycle, recruitment, participant suitability and the availability of practical teaching opportunities — with registrant interest abundant throughout, and that the fix its successor programme found was to tie funded places to actual vacancies. New South Wales carries the argument that outreach is a channel that transmits a changed offer rather than creating one, since the letters went out weeks after the October 2023 pay settlement and the department's own returner page still leads with the pay rise, and that bundling accreditation, approval to teach and background checks into a single contact is the cheapest documented instrument in the corpus — sold on its cheapness, not on a conversion rate the evidence cannot establish. The Netherlands carries the argument that an instrument paying for a stage downstream of the binding one is deadweight by construction, and supplies Tension C. Austria and Germany carry the salary-step artefact and the ceiling on near-universal retiree outreach. China carries the design point that money conditioned on an itemisable friction, relocation to named county types with housing and travel supplied, is a different instrument from money offered against a preference, with its unaudited status flagged in the same sentence. The tutoring and mentoring programmes carry role design.
*3.2 Regulation (about 3 200 words),* organised by four dials rather than by jurisdiction. The **pay dial**: salary-step placement and pension-salary concurrency, evidenced by Austria, Portugal and North Carolina, and by Fitzpatrick's finding that teachers value current cash roughly five to one over deferred entitlement, which explains why the cheap versions fail. The **exclusion dial**: service thresholds, waiting periods and subject or region screens, evidenced by Georgia's audit and by its 2026 devolution of the high-need determination, which is the cleanest natural test the field will get. The **intensity dial**: day caps and FTE ceilings, evidenced by Ontario, New Mexico's four routes reported in full, New Zealand's statutory relief allowance and Ireland's career-break suspension. The **licence dial**: lapse, refresh and waiver, evidenced by Michigan's expired certificates, New Zealand's mainstreamed waiver and Virginia's fast-track, with the honest statement that this is the most commonly recommended fix and the least evidenced one anywhere.
*Handed to Chapter 4:* an audit template, a demonstration that every effective dial sits outside the education ministry, and the two outcomes policy can actually deliver.

**Chapter 4. Lessons and recommendations (about 2 600 words).**
*Proposition established:* P6, and the conversion of P1 to P5 into instructions.
*Opening question:* what should a minister do on Monday, and in what order does the evidence support doing it?
*Structure:* recommendations in the order the evidence supports them — publish the reserve netted to a stated specification, and quote no reserve figure that is not; audit the return price, beginning with the salary step; advertise hours rather than posts and tie any funded conversion to identified vacancies; buy defined roles at a specified dose rather than buying seniority, and instrument what is bought, so that the next paper is not written on the same absence of evidence as this one; put the count inside the standing duty of a body that does not answer to the sponsor, with a sunset short enough for the count to matter and new-graduate hiring rates published alongside; report survival at one and three years and destination, never placements alone. Then the section the rejected draft lacked entirely, on when the right expenditure is zero or negative: where the entry pipeline is loose enough that rehiring is a transfer from new graduates to pensioners, where schools cannot absorb the returners they already attract, and where the only enumerable population is retirees. Then the research agenda stated as a costed request rather than as a plea, and carrying the two measurements on which the paper's own open questions turn. The netting request comes first, because it is the cheapest: any register holding last-taught date, certificate status, subject and phase, and age against pensionable age can publish a netted reserve series and a headline that is an honest upper bound, and none does. The craft request comes second and is the one that would settle P4: link returner records to the classes they teach and to the colleagues they work beside — a school-workforce-census question in England, an NCERDC question in North Carolina, not a research-funding one — and commission trials of designed roles at a specified dose, since the only role that was specified and dosed is also the only one that was measured. Then the rest: England can publish cost per additional returner teacher-year tomorrow from data it already links; DUO's annual reserve series spans the 2017-2020 Dutch subsidy window and would give the field its first estimate of what share of a *measured* reserve an instrument can activate; a lottery among 5 729 registrants would have cost nothing and produced the causal estimate the field still lacks; and Georgia's 2026 devolution is a live natural test.

## 6. WHAT GETS CUT, AND WHY

**The organising principle of the rejected Chapter 3.** Section 3.1's four "design families", its country-by-country narration and its three boxes (England, the Netherlands, Austria) go as an architecture. Cases become evidence inside argued sections. The test the lead author set is that the argument must be legible without any single case; the current chapter is illegible without all of them.

**Every claim a critic demolished, by name.** The England-versus-New South Wales conversion ranking in any form, including the "nine to one" and the implied cost ranking, because completing England's own arithmetic (about 4 per cent of roughly 5 375 non-supported registrants also returned) collapses the ratio and because the two outcomes are a casual placement and a permanent shortage-subject secondary post. Portugal's "thirty-five to one" as a natural experiment or a price test, because the two articles address disjoint populations by law, the returner arm was capped at 200 places, the eligibility window was five years, the application window was twelve days, and Article 5 was a pay cut against Article 6's supplement on top of a full career-step salary. "Returns fell to zero" as an elasticity, because the category ceased to exist. The claim that no system has extended induction to returners, because the Dutch subsidy was exactly that and went unclaimed. The recommendation of school-held exit registers as the instrument with the best odds, because no such instrument is observed anywhere in the corpus. The craft-versus-availability "bind by construction", because North Carolina returned about 3 500 people averaging 31 years of experience into full-time posts. Selection against shortage via outside options, because England's advisers converted physics leads at about the same rate as mathematics and languages leads and what differed was the number of leads. The stage-attribution table, because England is assigned four different binding stages across four sections. The reserve as a pricing instrument, because nothing in the corpus elicits a price. "What a system can see determines whom it addresses", because Michigan, England and four American states have registers and legislate for retirees anyway.

**Numbers with no argumentative job.** UNESCO's 44 million teachers needed by 2030, which is a global advocacy figure that moves nothing here. The 26 per cent to 47 per cent series on principals reporting instruction hindered by staff shortages, retained at most as a single clause and preferably not at all, since the abstract and introduction may not be motivated by shortages. The exact grant lines: GBP 586 900 in pilot grants, the adviser service's costs to the penny, the EUR 1.25 million Dutch budget. The Austrian Länder disaggregation (Vienna's 300, Salzburg's contacting to age 67, Vorarlberg's 42, Upper Austria's 227, Tyrol's 49), which survives only as the audited national 1 062 plus Hesse's 7 000 letters against about 550 posts filled by a combined package. The Portuguese subject breakdown of returners, which was doing work only in a proposition that collapsed. China's 120 000 target and the 7 000 sought in the 2025 round as headline numbers, since no audited progress count exists; the design survives, the target does not. Ontario's day rates to the cent, replaced by the ratio.

**Whole passages.** Table 1.1 keeps its three descriptive groups because the agreed structure requires them, but loses its status as the paper's analytic axis, since destination is the one property of a former teacher that predicts nothing; the served/never-served split and the pension position take that role. The nine statistic-laden key take-aways. The nine separate paragraphs saying that a given measure published nothing, consolidated into one table and one argument. The Bahamas as a case with a section, reduced to one sentence of evidence about a programme naming an existing dependence. The Rice/WestEd personal communication, dropped entirely as unpublished. The Toulouse rectorat letter, which the corpus flags as a search snippet from a page that returned HTTP 403, dropped as load-bearing. Ireland's removal from the "publishes nothing" list, since the figures exist in the *Teacher Statistics* series and were simply not obtained. The New Zealand Teacher Bonding Scheme, which is not a re-entry instrument.

## 7. WHERE THE NUMBERS GO

**In prose, because they size a mechanism.** The salary-step trio, which is P2's engine: Austria's starting-salary rule against credited pay; Portugal's first-step indexation pro-rated by hours; North Carolina's concurrent salary and pension at roughly half as much again as a normal full-time income, against about 3 500 returners averaging 31 years. The trade P3 turns on: +0.13 and +0.19 standard deviations of satisfaction against 6.2 and 9.4 per cent of weekly wages, and the English 10 per cent. The two trial results that define P4's measurement asymmetry, each carried with the size of the trial that produced it: +0.34 on the TEMA-3 from a stated protocol at a stated dose across 76 schools, and an indeterminate rating across reading, mathematics and teacher retention under WWC Standards 4.0 from 77 teachers in 11 schools of one district with no dose set. Georgia's 45 per cent of 163 surveyed systems reporting a blocked hire, which is the corpus's only employer-side counterfactual. England's survival gap and its 60 per cent higher first-year odds, used to argue for cost per retained teacher-year as the denominator that belongs in the account. The unprogrammed flow, at about 17 000 English returns a year and roughly a third of entrants, and 37 to 49 per cent of American new hires. At most four yield ratios, one per system, in P5.

**In tables.** Four, and each earns its place by replacing prose that would otherwise enumerate. A rebuilt funnel table with a mandatory column for the intensity of the post and another for the denominator, and the outcome definition stated in each row, retitled so that it cannot be read as a league table. A regulatory table of the four dials by system, replacing the country-by-country regulation narrative. An accountability inventory naming, for each measure, the counting body, whether that body answers to the sponsor, what was published and when publication stopped. A reserve table showing each headline figure alongside the deductions its own register can compute, with a column for the netted figure where the arithmetic is publishable and an explicit "not computed by the publisher" where it is not — the point of the table being that every deduction runs downward, so the netted column is the honest upper bound and the headline column records a register rule.

**In boxes, at most two.** North Carolina, because it is the single institutional change that broke the band and the only place where a price and a response are both observed. Georgia's audit loop, because sunset, audit and informed renewal closed exactly once in the entire record and the paper's accountability recommendation is a request to reproduce it.

**Nowhere.** UNESCO's 44 million. The New Mexico tier ranking presented as a finding, replaced by the total of 2 792 working retirees against roughly 48 000 and the observation that the flagship new route was the smallest. The Australian 50-per-cent-would-consider against 5-per-cent-actively-looking as a ratio or a law, reduced to one sentence about costless signals. Every take-up figure quoted without its denominator and the intensity of the post, which under the rule stated in P5 simply cannot appear.

## 8. THE RISKS, AND WHAT WOULD FALSIFY THE ARGUMENT

**Risk 1. The salary-step proposition rests on three cases in which pay level is confounded with everything else.** Austria, Portugal and North Carolina differ in pension regime, age profile, product, era and administrative ceiling as well as in the step a returner lands on, and the corpus contains no instance in which that step was varied while anything else was held constant. A well-briefed official will say that North Carolina worked because it was America in the 2000s and Portugal failed because it offered a twelve-day window for a January start to people averaging 68. *What would falsify it:* Austria disaggregating its 1 062 returners by former employment status and finding no difference between the group forced onto the starting salary and the group whose service was credited, or any system that resets returners to the entry step and nonetheless recruits at scale. The paper must state that the Austrian split compares two non-randomly-assigned employment cohorts and would therefore price the pay rule together with everything else that distinguishes a career civil servant from a contract teacher, which is why a null there would be more informative than a positive.

**Risk 2. "Hours, not posts" may be an artefact of the menu rather than a property of the population.** No system in the corpus has ever offered a full-time post on genuinely changed conditions, with a protected timetable and a capped administrative load, so the observed preference for small quantities may be the only preference the market permitted anyone to express. The Michigan survey stands as contrary evidence throughout, and the paper carries it rather than burying it. *What would falsify it:* a system advertising guaranteed part-time contracts with protected timetables and finding no differential take-up against full-time posts, or a large-scale full-time return outside a pension-stacking regime. NFER identified the scarcity of advertised part-time posts as the binding constraint for the most return-committed segment in 2018, and in the eight years since, nobody has tested the fix.

**Risk 3. The per-se case is unevaluated at the level the paper needs, and an unevaluated premise is a premise a hostile reader can push either way.** No study anywhere links a returner to a student outcome or to a colleague's; the one head-on test of retirees transferring expertise to other adults is an indeterminate rating on 77 teachers in 11 schools with no dose set; and the strongest positive trial cannot separate retired teachers from substitutes. The paper's exposure is symmetrical and it should say so: a linked-data comparison could find that returners in comparable posts outperform novices, which would strengthen the premise the paper has declined to assert, or that they underperform them, in which case the justification narrows to a valid licence, deployability without a training place and institutional fluency — a supply argument, and therefore the framing the team has ruled out. *What would settle it:* England's School Workforce Census linked to the National Pupil Database, or North Carolina's NCERDC, comparing returner-staffed with novice-staffed classrooms; and a trial of a designed mentoring or induction role at a specified dose, powered for the effect the tutoring literature suggests is available. The paper's defence is that it names this hole in its own voice, early, and prices the measurement that would fill it, rather than resting a premise on an OECD assertion that returners bring "diverse career experiences, which can enhance teaching quality and drive innovation" — an assertion advanced as a proposition rather than a finding, and one that no study in this evidence base has tested in either direction.

**Risk 4. The netting claim can be answered by netting.** P1 asserts that a netted series is computable in every register in the corpus and that netting moves the headline materially downward; both halves are checkable, and a ministry that resents the charge can dispose of it in a fortnight. If a register produced a netted series whose headline barely moved — if the never-served, the long-lapsed and the retirement-aged turned out to be a small share of the pool in systems other than England and Michigan — the proposition would collapse to a complaint about two registers. The paper's protection is that it states the direction, not the magnitude, and that the two systems where the arithmetic has been done both lose large shares. *What would falsify it:* a published netted reserve materially unchanged from its headline, or evidence that the deductions the paper lists are not in fact held in linkable form. *What would confirm it in the strongest way available:* any ministry publishing the series at all, since the claim that nobody does is the part a single counter-example settles.

A further exposure is worth naming even though it is not a risk to the argument so much as a property of it. Four propositions rest on a single observation each: England on cost, Portugal on the two margins, Ontario on displacement, North Carolina on price. In each instance that observation is the only one of its kind in the world. The paper should be candid that its comparative architecture is built on a handful of measured cases rather than on a literature, and should note that this is exactly what P6 predicts.
