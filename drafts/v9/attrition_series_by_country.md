# Teacher leaving-rate series beyond the US, England, Sweden and Australia

Research notes for the OECD-style policy paper. All sources accessed 28 August 2026. Countries already documented elsewhere (United States TFS 1988-2022; England SWC 2010-2024; Sweden IFAU registers 1985-2016; Australia e61 tax records 2009-2019) are excluded. Items marked [secondary] rely on press or academic summaries of official data; items marked [figure-only] have the series published as a chart without a machine-readable table.

## 1. Norway (register data, 1975-2013): early-career attrition FALLING, early retirement RISING

- Source: With, M. L. (2017), "Are Teachers Increasingly Leaving the Profession?", Professions and Professionalism 7(2), doi 10.7577/pp.1723, Norwegian administrative registers. URL: https://journals.oslomet.no/index.php/pp/article/view/1723 (full text PDF: https://journals.oslomet.no/pp/article/download/1723/1774/6334)
- Cohort analysis (graduation cohorts 1975-2003, N = 57,025; "attrition" = not employed in the school sector, so profession exit including exits to non-employment):
  - Bachelor-level PGCE graduates not in the school sector 20 years after graduation: 19 per cent (1975-79 cohort), 22-25 per cent (1980s cohorts), somewhat lower for later cohorts; at 10 years, 21 per cent (early 1980s cohort) vs 25 per cent (late 1980s), declining thereafter.
  - Master-level PGCE graduates: same pattern; 10-year attrition fell from 31 per cent to 23 per cent across cohorts.
  - General teacher education (GTE) graduates: lowest attrition, 9-16 per cent; rose somewhat from the 1970s cohorts, then flattened.
- Annual attrition analysis (teachers observed 1992-2008, N = 97,415; three windows 1992-97, 1998-2002, 2003-07):
  - Exits to other employment (school-level exit, includes moves to another school level): early-career teachers approx. 7 per cent per year (primary/lower secondary) and 8-10 per cent (upper secondary); mid-career 2-4 per cent; late-career 1-2.5 per cent. Slight, statistically significant decline in the latest window.
  - Exits to non-employment (retirement-exits): sharp rise after the early-retirement (AFP) age was lowered in 1997/98. At age 63, exits rose from 2 per cent (1992-97) to about 25 per cent (primary/lower secondary) and 15 per cent (upper secondary) in 1998-2002 and 2003-07; at age 64, from 4-6 per cent to 15-20 per cent.
- Definition: profession/sector exit measured in registers; retirement captured separately as exit to non-employment (income below NOK 60,000 in 2011 prices counts as not employed).
- Verdict: genuine three-decade evidence, but published as cohort and period contrasts, not a single annual series. Early-career profession exit stable to falling; total exits pushed up by earlier retirement after 1998.
- Statistics Norway publishes teacher workforce statistics ("Ansatte i barnehage og skole", register-based, from 2015) but no published annual leaving-rate series was located: https://www.ssb.no/utdanning/barnehager/statistikk/ansatte-i-barnehage-og-skole

## 2. New Zealand (Ministry of Education payroll data, 2004-2024): broadly STABLE, recently FALLING

- Source: Education Counts, "Teacher turnover" statistics page (annual series from 2004, state and state-integrated schools, from the school payroll): https://www.educationcounts.govt.nz/statistics/teacher-turnover and "Entering and leaving teaching": https://www.educationcounts.govt.nz/statistics/entering-and-leaving-teaching
- Leaving rate (teachers leaving the state teaching workforce, i.e. sector exit including retirement): 7.3 per cent in 2024, down from 7.5 per cent in 2023; the 2024 figure is the lowest since the series began in 2004 apart from the COVID dip to 6.9 per cent in 2020. Total turnover (school moves plus exits) fell from 18.5 per cent (2023) to 17.3 per cent (2024), the lowest since 2012 except 2020.
- Longer-run pattern: Ministry analysis (He Whakaaro: Teacher turnover in New Zealand schools, 2019) shows about half of turnover is school moves and half is exits from regular teaching; primary turnover ran about 5 percentage points above secondary over 2010-2017; retention of new teachers has been improving (year-5 retention 71 per cent for the 2004 cohort, 74 per cent for 2011, 78 per cent for 2014). URL: https://www.educationcounts.govt.nz/publications/series/he-whakaaro/he-whakaaro-teacher-turnover-in-new-zealand-schools
- Definition: exit from the state schools payroll (sector exit, retirements included); re-entrants are common. Year-by-year workbooks are downloadable from the statistics page (site is Cloudflare-protected; figures above are as reported by the Ministry pages and summaries).
- Verdict: genuine annual series 2004-2024; fluctuates in roughly the 7-9 per cent band, no sustained rise, at a series low in 2024.

## 3. Netherlands (DUO payroll data, annual editions circa 2013-2024): STABLE then RISING, easing in 2024

- Source: OCW, Trendrapportage Arbeidsmarkt Leraren po, vo en mbo (annual; DUO salary administration). 2025 edition: https://open.overheid.nl/documenten/c0734b61-05a1-4480-be61-fa76d3b238f1/file ; 2022 edition: https://open.overheid.nl/documenten/ronl-18202bb854ec7f706ee1e63087fc2b3158a173cc/pdf ; theme page: https://www.ocwincijfers.nl/themas/arbeidsmarkt-en-leraren
- 2022 edition: "De uitstroom van leraren uit de onderwijssectoren is al jaren vrij constant" at about 6 per cent in po (primary), somewhat higher in vo and mbo (average of the 2017-18 to 2020-21 transitions; figure 9.7) [figure-only].
- 2025 edition: outflow "is de laatste jaren gestegen" to about 7 per cent in po, 8.1 per cent in vo and 9.6 per cent in mbo (mbo down 1.5 points on the year before); all three sectors rose in recent years and then dipped slightly in the 2023-24 transition (figure 13.1, averages of 2020-21 to 2023-24 transitions) [figure-only].
- Composition: teachers aged 60+ leave at about 21-22 per cent per year (largely pension); other age groups 4-5 per cent in po.
- Definition: sector exit from government-funded po/vo/mbo (leaving for higher education, the inspectorate or the ministry counts as exit; retirement included; returns after a gap year are known to occur). Cross-sector moves (0.7-1.9 per cent per year) are shown separately.
- Verdict: genuine annual administrative series, published per edition mostly as charts; level about 6-8 per cent in po, 8-10 per cent in vo/mbo; stable through circa 2021, rising to 2023, easing 2024.

## 4. France (DEPP, 2008-09 to 2022-23): very low level, clearly RISING

- Sources: DEPP, Panorama statistique des personnels de l'enseignement scolaire 2024-2025: https://www.education.gouv.fr/depp/panorama-statistique-des-personnels-de-l-enseignement-scolaire-2024-2025-452859 (PDF: https://www.education.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/document/panorama-statistique-des-personnels-de-l-enseignement-scolaire-2024-2025-475342.pdf); Senate finance committee report on the 2024 budget (reproduces the DEPP series 2008 to 2022-23): https://www.senat.fr/rap/l23-128-314/l23-128-314_mono.html ; DEPP Note d'information 19.54 (December 2019) on resignations: https://www.education.gouv.fr/media/19976/download
- Series (voluntary definitive departures of tenured public-sector teachers, i.e. resignations plus, from 2020, ruptures conventionnelles; retirements excluded): about 0.06 per cent in 2008, about 0.08 per cent in 2013-14, about 0.20 per cent in 2017-18, 2,836 departures or about 0.4 per cent in 2021-22, and 2,473 departures or 36 per 10,000 (0.36 per cent) in 2022-23. The Senate report describes a rise of 0.34 points in ten years and a near sevenfold increase in departures since 2008. [Senate figures are the DEPP's own series as reproduced in the report.]
- Trainee teachers (stagiaires) resign at much higher rates: 0.9 per cent (2008), 2.6 per cent (2017), 3.75 per cent (2021-22).
- Definition: employer exit (leaving the education nationale), voluntary only; retirements counted separately in the Panorama (bilans sociaux). Not comparable in level with sector-exit series elsewhere; worth reporting honestly as a rising series from a very low base.
- Verdict: genuine annual series circa 2008-2023; RISING from about 0.06 to about 0.36-0.40 per cent of tenured teachers.

## 5. Germany (Destatis pension statistics, 1993-2024): retirements ROSE then FELL; incapacity share collapsed

- Sources: Destatis press releases: 2015 record ("Zahl der Pensionierungen von Lehrkräften bleibt 2015 auf Höchstwert"): https://www.destatis.de/DE/Presse/Pressemitteilungen/2016/12/PD16_455_742.html ; first decline 2016: https://www.destatis.de/DE/Presse/Pressemitteilungen/2017/12/PD17_460_742.html ; 2024 figures: https://www.destatis.de/DE/Presse/Pressemitteilungen/2025/12/PD25_455_742.html ; chart page: https://www.destatis.de/DE/Themen/Staat/Oeffentlicher-Dienst/_Grafik/_Statisch/Versorgungsempfaenger-Schuldienst.html
- Series (newly retired tenured (verbeamtete) teachers, statistics since 1993): peak of about 27,900 in both 2014 and 2015; declining since 2016; about 14,400 new retirements in 2024, the fewest since 2003.
- Incapacity (Dienstunfähigkeit): more than half of teacher retirements each year in 1993-2001 were on incapacity grounds; by 2015 only about 12 per cent (88 per cent at or after the age limit). This extends the 2001-2017 incapacity series already documented.
- Definition: retirement events among civil-servant teachers only; no national series on total profession exits (voluntary quits by employee-status teachers are not captured here). KMK publishes recruitment-need models but no exit-rate series was located.
- Verdict: genuine annual retirement series 1993-2024 (rise to 2014-15, fall since); no series for non-retirement exits.

## 6. Flanders, Belgium (labour-market registers, 2014-2021): STABLE around 8 per cent

- Source: De Witte, K., De Cort, W., Tobback, I., Van Belle, J., Schelfhout, W., Tanghe, E., Smet, M., Vansteenkiste, S. (2024/2025), De in- en uitstroom in het lerarenberoep vanuit vergelijkend perspectief, Departement Onderwijs en Vorming. Report PDF: https://data-onderwijs.vlaanderen.be/documenten/bestanden/Instroom_uitstroom_Lerarenberoep.pdf (site refused automated download on access date); companion presentation with the key table: https://feb.kuleuven.be/eme/leuven/downloads-studiedagen-leraren/studiedag-deel-1.pdf
- Figures: average annual outflow from the education sector 8.0 per cent over 2014-2021 (inflow 9.5 per cent; retention 91.2 per cent; 237,851 jobs), which makes education one of the most stable sectors in the Flemish economy (all-sector average outflow 16.7 per cent). Of leavers, about 75 per cent go to non-employment (largely pension) and 25 per cent to other sectors [secondary, from the report's summaries].
- Definition: sector exit measured in social-security (RSZ) data; retirements included. Published as a period average; a public year-by-year table was not located, so treat as a period figure rather than a series.
- Verdict: multi-year administrative evidence 2014-2021; level about 8 per cent, no sign of a strong trend; not (yet) a published annual series.

## 7. Denmark: no annual official leaving-rate series located; cohort attrition RISING, EAG level HIGH

- AE (Arbejderbevaegelsens Erhvervsraad, June 2024), register analysis of new folkeskole teachers: nearly 1 in 5 newly qualified teachers leaves the folkeskole within three years and almost 1 in 4 within five; the 2013 graduation cohort took five years to lose a fifth, the 2018 cohort only three. URL: https://www.ae.dk/analyse/2024-06-mange-nye-folkeskolelaerere-forlader-folkeskolen-paa-faa-aar [cohort measure, school-sector exit, not an annual rate]
- Share of all teacher-trained Danes teaching in the folkeskole fell from 55.8 per cent (2010) to 48.4 per cent (2023). URL: https://www.ae.dk/analyse/2025-09-kortlaegning-af-laereruddannedes-beskaeftigelse-i-og-uden-for-folkeskolen
- OECD EAG 2025 reports Danish attrition above 10 per cent in 2022/23 (see item 14), far above the cohort-study impression; definitional care needed (EAG counts all leavers including retirement across pre-primary to upper secondary).
- Verdict: no genuine long annual series found; cohort attrition rising.

## 8. Finland: NO published leaving-rate series

- The official teacher data collection (Ministry of Education and Culture with Statistics Finland, since 1999; results in the Vipunen service from 2010: https://vipunen.fi/fi-fi and https://www.oph.fi/fi/tilastot/tilastoja-opettajista-ja-rehtoreista) publishes qualification and staffing statistics but no annual profession-exit rate. No official attrition series was located; Finland is best reported as a data gap.

## 9. Japan (MEXT School Teachers Survey, triennial, FY2012-FY2021): total separations FALLING, non-retirement exits RISING

- Source: MEXT, FY2022 School Teachers Survey (Gakko Kyoin Tokei Chosa), confirmed results summary, 27 March 2024: https://www.mext.go.jp/content/20240321-mxt_chousa01-000030586_1.pdf ; survey portal: https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/toukei/chousa01/kyouin/1268573.htm ; detailed tables on e-Stat: https://www.e-stat.go.jp/stat-search/files?tstat=000001016172
- Public elementary schools, teachers leaving during the fiscal year (retirements included): FY2012 18,020; FY2015 17,649; FY2018 16,619; FY2021 15,030. The mandatory/encouraged retirement share fell from 66.6 to 53.3 per cent, so non-retirement exits rose from 6,015 (FY2012) to 7,024 (FY2021); within these, job changes (tenshoku) rose from 1,318 to 2,098 and exits for illness from 589 to 753 (of which mental illness 350 to 569).
- Public lower secondary: 8,700 (FY2012), 8,280 (FY2015), 9,059 (FY2018), 8,477 (FY2021). Public upper secondary: 6,331, 5,340, 5,246, 5,601.
- As a share of the roughly 415,000 full-time public elementary teachers, total separations are about 3.5-4 per cent per year, and non-retirement separations about 1.5-1.7 per cent (shares computed from MEXT counts and School Basic Survey headcounts; MEXT itself publishes counts, not rates). Secondary analyses put the voluntary (jiko-tsugo) exit rate at about 1-1.2 per cent.
- Definition: separation from full-time teaching posts (school-sector exit), retirement included but itemised; moves between schools excluded (a university-sector exception is noted in the source but does not apply to schools).
- Verdict: genuine triennial series (waves every three years, here FY2012-FY2021; earlier waves back to the 1970s exist on e-Stat); total separations falling as the retirement bulge passes, voluntary and illness exits rising.

## 10. Korea (KESS/MOE data, 2019-2023 plus early-retirement series 2005-2021): mid-career exits RISING

- Mid-career (pre-retirement-age) separations of public school teachers: 6,151 (2019), 6,512 (2020), 6,642 (2021), 6,774 (2022), 7,626 (2023, a record high after the Seo2 (Seoi) Elementary incident). Separation rates by level in 2023: elementary 1.50 per cent, middle 2.43 per cent, high 1.93 per cent. Source [secondary, from Korean Educational Statistics Service data]: Seoul Economic Daily: https://www.sedaily.com/article/13958781 ; primary data: Korean Educational Statistics Service (KESS): https://kess.kedi.re.kr
- This complements the already documented rising honorary/early retirement (myeongye toejik) series 2005-2021.
- Definition: mid-career separation (leaving the teaching service before pension age); ordinary retirements excluded in the counts above.
- Verdict: short but genuine annual series, clearly rising 2019-2023.

## 11. Scotland (GTCS register and Teacher Census): roughly STABLE 4-6 per cent; post-probation wastage RISING

- GTCS register exits: over the five years to 2024, between 4.11 and 5.69 per cent of registrants left the register annually [secondary, Tes citing GTCS]: https://www.tes.com/magazine/news/general/what-proportion-teachers-are-leaving-profession
- Scottish Government FOI release (teacher retention and turnover, 2024) points to wastage/leaver rates in workforce-planning papers 2018-2023 and to Teacher Census Supplementary Statistics (Table 1.6, leavers, from 2020): https://www.gov.scot/publications/foi-202400434716/ ; census tables: https://www.gov.scot/publications/teacher-census-supplementary-statistics/
- Post-probationer wastage (not teaching in a publicly funded school the year after induction): 19 per cent (2021) rising to 27 per cent (2023) for primary [secondary, Tes]: https://www.tes.com/magazine/news/general/scotland-drop-off-rates-following-teacher-induction-very-high
- Definition: register exit (profession exit, retirements included) vs census leaver (public-sector school exit); the two differ.
- Verdict: multi-year but short published series; overall exit roughly stable at 4-6 per cent, early-career wastage rising sharply.

## 12. Ireland: very LOW attrition, no long published series

- OECD EAG 2025: attrition of fully qualified teachers under 3 per cent in 2022/23, among the lowest of 19 countries (see item 14). Department of Education parliamentary-question data show resignations at less than 0.5 per cent of allocated teachers per year: https://www.oireachtas.ie/en/debates/question/2023-06-29/93/
- Verdict: point estimates only; low and apparently stable; no annual series located.

## 13. Canada, Chile, Israel

- Ontario: the Ontario College of Teachers ran the annual Transition to Teaching survey 2002-2022 (final report 2023); it tracks early-career employment and register exits (about 30 per cent five-year attrition from the College register for 2013-2017 entrants, higher for French-language teachers) rather than an annual profession-exit rate. Reports: https://www.oct.ca/en-ca/news-and-resources/focus-on-teaching/transition-to-teaching (e.g. 2022 report: https://www.oct.ca/-/media/PDF/transition%20to%20teaching/2022/T2T_2022_EN_PUBLISH.pdf). No BC series located.
- Chile: administrative studies of the national teacher registry report average annual exit ("desercion docente") of 9.4 per cent over 2004-2021, ranging from 6.1 per cent (2020) to 15.0 per cent (2010); public-sector exit stabilised around 3-4 per cent after the 2016/2018 teacher-career law. Sources [academic, using MINEDUC administrative data]: https://www.scielo.cl/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S0718-51622023000300267 and https://www.redalyc.org/journal/2431/243178931009/html/ ; a University of Chile (CIAE) study of public-sector teachers: https://uchile.cl/noticias/225369/estudio-ciae-aborda-abandono-docente-en-la-educacion-publica-
- Israel: CBS data show first-year attrition rising from 5.8 per cent (2010 entry cohort) to 10.8 per cent (2018 cohort), five-year cohort attrition 16.5 per cent, and 21 per cent of teachers with under five years' experience leaving in 2021 vs 13 per cent in 2018 [secondary, from a 2025 policy analysis citing CBS: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02607476.2025.2563153 and Haaretz on the August 2026 CBS report: https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/2026-08-13/ty-article/.premium/israel-sees-a-drop-in-teacher-quality-and-retention-statistics-bureau-says/0000019f-f667-d929-afff-f6ef2d690000]. Note the clash with EAG 2025, which puts Israeli attrition under 3 per cent in 2022/23 (different definition: fully qualified teachers, all levels). CBS cohort series is genuine but cohort-based, RISING.
- Spain, Italy, Portugal: no official leaving-rate series located; exits are dominated by retirement statistics in civil-service systems and are not published as attrition series.

## 14. OECD cross-country points: a two-point comparison is possible

- EAG 2021, indicator D7 (new indicator, reference year 2016): teacher attrition rates, pre-primary to upper secondary, indirect measure of teachers leaving the profession, headcounts. In primary to secondary public institutions attrition ranged from 3.3 to 11.7 per cent across countries. Tables D7.1 (by gender and age) and D7.2 (by level): https://www.oecd-ilibrary.org/education/teacher-attrition-rates-by-level-of-education-2016_47694778-en and https://www.oecd-ilibrary.org/education/teacher-attrition-rates-in-pre-primary-to-upper-secondary-education-by-gender-and-age-group-2016_11aac764-en
- EAG 2025 (reference year 2022/23): average attrition of fully qualified teachers 6.5 per cent across 19 countries; under 3 per cent in France, Greece, Ireland and Israel; over 10 per cent in Denmark, Estonia and Lithuania; 51 per cent of leavers resigned on average; pre-primary 7.3, primary 5.8, secondary 5.9 per cent. Chapter: https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2025/09/education-at-a-glance-2025_c58fc9ae/full-report/how-severe-are-teacher-shortages-across-countries_781f4a97.html
- The EAG 2014/15-vintage column the paper already uses, plus D7 2016 and EAG 2025 2022/23, would give a two- to three-point OECD comparison for overlapping countries, but the 2021 measure is "indirect" (workforce accounting) and the 2025 measure covers fully qualified teachers only, so levels are not strictly comparable across editions; treat as separate cross-sections.

## Definitional caveats blocking comparison

- Profession exit (Norway registers, GTCS register, EAG) vs sector exit (Netherlands DUO, Flanders RSZ, New Zealand payroll, Japan MEXT, Chile) vs employer exit (France education nationale) give systematically different levels: France's 0.36 per cent (voluntary, tenured only, no retirement) cannot be set against New Zealand's 7.3 per cent (all exits including retirement).
- Retirement treatment is the single largest wedge: Germany and Korea publish retirement-only or retirement-excluded series; the Netherlands, Flanders, New Zealand and Japan include it; Norway and Japan itemise it.
- Register data count exits that are later reversed (Netherlands and New Zealand both document substantial re-entry), so annual exit rates overstate permanent loss.
- Cohort attrition (Denmark, Israel, Ontario, Scotland post-probation) is not convertible to an annual leaving rate without strong assumptions.
