## 2. Survey population

- Web survey "Lärarutbildades karriärvägar", fielded October 2022 – January 2023; results published May 2023.
- Population: c. 4,750 persons (unweighted respondents; response rate 62%, results weighted to represent the full frame of 4,748) who in 2022 were **employed, but not as teachers in schools**; aged at most 65; held a Swedish teaching licence (lärarlegitimation); and had worked at least two years as a teacher at some point in 2012–2020.
- **School forms covered:** compulsory school (grundskolan), upper secondary school (gymnasieskolan) and municipal adult education (komvux). **Not covered:** preschool-only teachers; people who stayed in schools in non-teaching roles (e.g. became principals/heads).
- So: "former teachers who left the profession within roughly the past decade", not the whole teaching workforce. SCB itself cautions the answers refer only to leavers (p. 7).

Notes:
- Report headline (p. 14): "Hög arbetsbelastning det vanligaste skälet för att lämna yrket" — high workload is the single most common reason contributing fully or to a large extent (p. 16: "Arbetsbelastning är den enskilt vanligaste anledningen som bidrog helt eller till stor del till att lämna läraryrket").
- Workload and psychological strain were reported by a higher share of women than men; administrative tasks by a somewhat higher share of men (within grundskolan and gymnasiet).
- Compared with 2016, the shares citing workload, stress/parental expectations, administrative tasks, lack of control over the work situation, and oversized student groups were all higher in 2022 (p. 18).
- "Vårdnadshavare" is literally "guardians" (SCB's term); "parents" is an acceptable translation in OECD prose, "parents/guardians" is safest.

## 4. Verdict on versions (a) and (b)

- **Version (a) — "workload/stress ranked first among Swedish leavers": CORRECT and safest.** Work environment (stress/workload) is the top broad reason (68%), and excessive workload is the top specific factor (64%).
- **Version (b) — "workload and parental expectations first, administrative tasks and lack of control over one's own work following": ROUGHLY right but imprecise as written.** Strictly, workload alone ranks first (64%); "psychologically demanding work, e.g. stress and expectations from parents/guardians" is a *separate* item ranking second (58%); administrative tasks third (57%) and lack of control fourth (54%). Parental expectations are only an illustrative example within the second item, not paired with workload in the first. If version (b) is kept, it must be reworded to reflect this ordering.
- Neither version should imply the four items are from the same question as the 68% "work environment" figure — they are the detailed factor battery.

## 5. Safe sentence (English)

> Among Swedish teachers who left the profession, excessive workload was the single most commonly cited factor contributing fully or to a large extent to their decision to leave (64%), followed by the psychologically demanding nature of the work, such as stress and parents' expectations (58%), too many administrative tasks (57%) and limited control over one's own work situation (54%) (Statistics Sweden, 2023).

Shorter alternative:

> Work environment factors — above all workload and stress — ranked first among the reasons Swedish leavers gave for having left teaching, cited by 68% of former teachers, ahead of terms of employment such as pay and working hours (39%) (Statistics Sweden, 2023).
