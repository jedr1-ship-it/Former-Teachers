# Sweep D: Anglophone countries (Canada, UK beyond England, Australia, New Zealand, US states)

All sources accessed 28 August 2026. Question per country: does ANY multi-year evidence exist on the evolution of the teacher leaving rate (exits from the profession, or nearest equivalent)?

---

## Canada (federal)

**Verdict: NO national leaving-rate series.**

1. **Statistics Canada, Pan-Canadian Education Indicators Program (81-582-X)** - [Education Indicators in Canada, March 2026](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/81-582-x/81-582-x2026001-eng.htm). Contains educator counts, age structure and pupil-teacher ratios by province, but no teacher attrition or leaving-rate indicator. Not a leaving rate.
2. **Academic confirmation of the gap**: Kutsyuruba, Godden and Tregunna (2014), [Curbing Early-Career Teacher Attrition: A Pan-Canadian Document Analysis](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1035357.pdf), CJEAP 161, states explicitly that "no pan-Canadian statistics or comprehensive reviews are available" on teacher attrition.
3. **Canadian Teachers' Federation** - [Parachute survey series](https://www.ctf-fce.ca/one-foot-out-the-door-students-remain-key-reason-why-most-teachers-choose-to-stay/) (2023-2025): repeated national surveys of leaving intentions only, not actual exits. Secondary, intentions-based.

Searched: StatCan 81-582-X catalogue and PCEIP tables; "Statistics Canada teacher attrition longitudinal"; CTF publications. Evidence in Canada is provincial (below).

## Canada - Ontario

**Verdict: YES - a real register-based early-career attrition series, annual 2001-2022 (discontinued).**

- **Source**: Ontario College of Teachers, *Transition to Teaching* annual study, 2001 to 2022 (final report published 2023): [T2T 2022 report (PDF)](https://cdn-ca.aglty.io/f2c1d1f3/t2t/pdf/transitions22_en.pdf); series landing page: [OCT Transition to Teaching](https://www.oct.ca/en-ca/news-and-resources/focus-on-teaching/transition-to-teaching); older editions in OCT annual reports (e.g. [2020 annual report statistics](https://reports.oct.ca/en/2020/Statistics/Transition-to-Teaching)).
- **Span**: annual since 2001; attrition measured by cohort from College membership records (register-based), standard measure is attrition after five years.
- **Level and range**: 2022 analysis of membership records: on average 4 per cent of a new cohort do not renew membership within 1 year, 7 per cent within 2 years, 11 per cent within 3 years, 14 per cent within 4 years; five-year attrition 17 per cent for English-speaking members versus 30 per cent for French-speaking members (latest complete cohorts, certified 2013-2017).
- **Trend verdict**: non-renewal rose during the oversupply years (roughly 2009-2015) and the reports note retention strengthening again under the post-2017 shortage ("Given the shortage, stronger teacher retention will likely continue"). French-language attrition persistently about double the English rate from 2017 forward.
- **Definition**: non-renewal of Ontario College of Teachers certification by years since initial certification. Nearest equivalent rather than a pure leaving rate: lapsing includes teachers moving to other provinces or countries as well as genuine profession exits. Register-based, published findings, microdata closed.

## Canada - Quebec

**Verdict: PARTIAL - ministry departure counts 2013-14 to 2022-23 (FOI, counts not rates) plus academic estimates; no official rate series.**

1. **Ministère de l'Éducation du Québec, FOI response 24-658 (27 January 2025)** - [PDF](https://cdn-contenu.quebec.ca/cdn-contenu/adm/min/education/publications-adm/education/Acces-information/reponses-transmises/2025/Janvier-mars/24-658_Diffusion.pdf). Primary source (PERCOS payroll system). Departures of teaching staff, all causes, all school service centres/boards including Cree and Kativik: 2013-14: 2,137; 2014-15: 2,315; 2015-16: 2,281; 2016-17: 2,421; 2017-18: 2,682; 2018-19: 3,267; 2019-20: 3,510; 2020-21: 3,230; 2021-22: 3,892; 2022-23: 4,519. Trend: more than doubled over ten years. Counts, not rates; excludes contract endings and resignations of non-regular (precarious-status) staff; includes retirements. Not a proper leaving rate but a genuine multi-year administrative series.
2. **Le Devoir** (2025), [Deux fois plus de départs d'enseignants en 10 ans au Québec](https://www.ledevoir.com/actualites/education/848017/deux-fois-plus-departs-enseignants-10-ans-quebec-toujours-peu-suivi): journalistic analysis of the same ministry data; resignations (démissions) 2,029 in 2022-23 versus 1,953 the year before. Secondary.
3. **Karsenti et al. (décrochage enseignant literature)**: Karsenti, Collin and Dumouchel (2013), [Le décrochage enseignant: état des connaissances](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11159-013-9367-z), International Review of Education: Quebec attrition estimated at 15-20 per cent (up to 25-30 per cent in some analyses) within the first five years. Academic estimate, abstract-only for exact framing; not a time series. Karsenti's FRQ-funded reports: [summary](https://frq.gouv.qc.ca/app/uploads/2021/08/prs_karsentit_resume_decrochage-nouveaux-enseignants.pdf).
4. Context: [Radio-Canada, June 2025](https://ici.radio-canada.ca/nouvelle/2261434/demissions-denseignants-cueillette-donnees-obligatoire-rentree) reports that collection of resignation data becomes mandatory from the 2025 school year, confirming no systematic tracking to date.

## Canada - British Columbia

**Verdict: PARTIAL/THIN - only FOI-based departure counts 2018-19 to 2023-24 via media; no published rate series.**

- **The Tyee (6 December 2024)**, [Teacher Resignations Are on the Rise in BC](https://thetyee.ca/News/2024/12/06/Teacher-Resignations-Rise-BC/): data compiled by the BC Public School Employers' Association from all 60 districts, 2018-19 to 2023-24. Teachers leaving districts for all reasons peaked at 1,684 in 2021-22, falling to 1,310 in 2023-24; resignations rose 37 per cent from about 472 (2018-19) to 647 (2023-24); retirements roughly stable around 250-300 a year. Secondary (media, FOI-derived), counts not rates, and measures leaving a district, not the profession.
- Searched: BC Teachers' Council annual reports (certificate issuance and discipline statistics only, no attrition series); BC Ministry of Education workforce publications; "British Columbia teacher attrition rate series". Nothing register-based and published on profession exits over time.

## Canada - Alberta

**Verdict: PARTIAL - one-off ministry cohort analysis (register-based, cohorts 2004/05-2006/07); no ongoing published series.**

- **Alberta Education (2013)**, [A Transformation in Progress: Alberta's K-12 Education Workforce 2012/2013 (PDF)](https://open.alberta.ca/dataset/b9f67a91-e513-4c9b-92ed-835f8b699485/resource/08422626-c422-4855-b7b5-359724e51eba/download/6084401-2013-02-transformation-in-progress-february-2013-final.pdf), using the Workforce Planning Datamart (administrative). Early-career attrition for the 2004/05 to 2006/07 hiring cohorts: about 10 per cent leave in the first year, a further 6.4 per cent in year two, 5.9 per cent in year three; the five-year cohort attrition rate is above 25 per cent. Definition: "leaving the education system" means no longer employed by any Alberta school jurisdiction and not returning within five full years - a proper exit measure at provincial level, but published once, not a maintained series.
- **Alberta Teachers' Association** surveys (e.g. [CTV coverage, 2022](https://edmonton.ctvnews.ca/disrespected-and-demoralized-survey-shows-37-of-alberta-teachers-may-leave-in-the-next-five-years-1.5757753): 37 per cent intend to leave within five years) are intentions, not exits. Secondary.

## United Kingdom - Scotland

**Verdict: PARTIAL - register and workforce-planning leaver rates exist for recent years (roughly 2016 onwards) but no long published exit-rate series; new academic survey study 2026.**

1. **GTC Scotland register leavers** (via Tes FOI analysis): [What proportion of teachers are leaving the profession?](https://www.tes.com/magazine/news/general/what-proportion-teachers-are-leaving-profession) - between March 2016 and March 2020, 3,054 to 4,367 teachers left the GTCS register each year, 4.11 to 5.69 per cent of registrants. All-reason register exits (lapsing, fitness-to-teach removals, non-engagement); not a pure leaving rate because the register includes non-practising teachers and lapsing does not always equal leaving teaching. Secondary (Tes, from GTCS data). GTC Scotland now publishes annual [Registration Snapshot reports](https://www.gtcs.org.uk/news/news-and-updates/gtc-scotland-publishes-registration-report) (from 2023-24) which could seed a future series.
2. **Scottish Government workforce-planning leaver ("wastage") rates**: [FOI 202400434716, Teacher retention and turnover data](https://www.gov.scot/publications/foi-202400434716/) - leaver/wastage and returner rates for permanent, fixed-term and supply teachers are held in Teacher Workforce Planning Advisory Group modelling documents (December 2018 to November 2023) and post-probationer dashboards (2020 onwards); rates are computed as five-year averages, not published as an annual series. See also the [2022 exercise statistical model](https://www.gov.scot/publications/teacher-workforce-planning-advisory-group-2022-exercise-october-2021-statistical-model/).
3. **Probationer wastage**: [FOI 202200311782](https://www.gov.scot/publications/foi-202200311782/) - Teacher Induction Scheme wastage (five-year averages): primary 19 per cent, secondary 16 per cent; for the 2020/21 primary probationer cohort, 31 per cent of FTE was not employed in a publicly funded school at the September 2021 census. Cohort-based, early-career only.
4. **Academic**: Hulme et al. (2026), [Teacher turnover in Scotland: reluctant leavers, downscaling, and the limits of voluntary exit](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/03054985.2026.2709882), Oxford Review of Education (open access): 69.4 per cent of actual leavers in the sample were "reluctant leavers". Survey-based typology, not a rate series; abstract-only consulted (full text blocked to automated access).

Searched additionally: Scottish Government teacher census supplementary statistics (workbooks 2010-2025 contain leaver tables only from 2020 onwards, per FOI); school education statistics collection.

## United Kingdom - Wales

**Verdict: YES but SHORT - official leaving-the-profession data 2020/21 to 2024/25 (SWAC); too new for a long trend.**

- **Source**: Welsh Government School Workforce Annual Census (SWAC): [School Workforce Census results, November 2024](https://www.gov.wales/school-workforce-census-results-november-2024-official-statistics-html); StatsWales dataset [Teacher leavers by subject, years of experience, destination and local authority](https://stats.gov.wales/en-GB/11b2a0b1-91d8-4f52-a882-6c15bbc19895/start) (span September 2020 to August 2025).
- **Level**: 1,465 teachers left the profession during 2023/24, of whom 455 (31.0 per cent) retired, against roughly 25,700 teachers in post in November 2023, implying a leaving rate of about 5.7 per cent. The release presents leavers-by-destination trends from 2020-21 to 2023-24.
- **Trend verdict**: series too short for a robust trend; headcount fell 2.7 per cent (2022 to 2023) and 2.4 per cent (2023 to 2024), partly attributed to the end of temporary RRRS pandemic funding.
- **Definition**: a proper leaving-the-profession measure - SWAC records leavers by destination (employed outside education, non-teaching capacity in education, another school, retirement, early retirement), so profession exits are separable from school moves. EWC [education workforce statistics](https://www.ewc.wales/site/index.php/en/about-us/policy-and-research/workforce-statistics) provide register stocks as complement.

## United Kingdom - Northern Ireland

## Australia

**Verdict: YES - the administrative tax-data series now runs 2009 to 2021 (e61), showing a FALL; ATWD registration data add recent levels.**

1. **e61 Institute** - Buckley and Griselda (2025), *Who Stays, Who Goes? A new look at teacher attrition using administrative data*: [e61 page](https://e61.in/who-stays-who-goes-a-new-look-at-teacher-attrition-using-administrative-data/); [report PDF](https://e61.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/teacher_attrition_final-20250604.pdf) (site blocks automated access; figures confirmed via [APO record](https://apo.org.au/node/330924) and [The Educator summary](https://www.theeducatoronline.com/k12/news/australias-teacher-attrition-may-be-easing-new-study-suggests/287310)). Population-wide administrative tax data (ALife), 2009 to 2021: the share of school teachers quitting the profession each year FELL from 5.9 per cent (2009) to 5.1 per cent (2021); early-career attrition nearly halved from 4 per cent to 2.3 per cent; attrition now lower than in nursing, medicine or policing. This extends the earlier 2009-2019 result by two years. Proper leaving rate (occupation exit in tax records).
2. **AITSL/ATWD**: [National Trends: Teacher Workforce June 2025](https://www.aitsl.edu.au/research/australian-teacher-workforce-data/atwd-reports/national-trends-teacher-workforce-jun2025) and [June 2026](https://www.aitsl.edu.au/research/australian-teacher-workforce-data/atwd-reports/national-trends-teacher-workforce-june-2026). Registration-linked data (from 2018): about 95 per cent of 2022 ITE graduates still registered in 2023, and roughly 1.25 per cent of early-career registrants discontinue registration per year; registration-based attrition around 4.6 per cent a year has been cited against a HILDA-based leaving rate of 11.6 per cent (2022). Intentions data (39 per cent intend to leave before retirement, 2023, up from 31 per cent in 2021) are survey-based and distinct from actual exits. Registration lapse is a nearest equivalent, not a pure leaving rate.

## New Zealand

**Verdict: YES - VERIFIED official payroll-based leaving-rate series 2004-2024; latest years are the LOWEST on record.**

- **Source**: Ministry of Education, Education Counts - [Entering and leaving teaching](https://www.educationcounts.govt.nz/statistics/entering-and-leaving-teaching) (with downloadable workbooks "Time series: Leaving workforce rates 2004-2024" and "Teacher turnover 2004-2024"); companion [Teacher turnover page](https://www.educationcounts.govt.nz/statistics/teacher-turnover). Note: the site blocks automated fetching (Cloudflare); values below were verified from the live page text via search capture on 28 August 2026.
- **Span**: 2004 to 2024, annual; payroll data (Novopay), state and state-integrated schools.
- **Level and range**: leaving rate 7.3 per cent in 2024, down from 7.5 per cent in 2023; pandemic dip to 6.9 per cent in 2020; apart from that dip, 2024 is the LOWEST leaving rate since the series began in 2004 (series has generally run in the 7-9 per cent band).
- **Trend verdict**: broadly flat to declining over two decades, with a 2020 trough, a modest post-Covid rebound, and renewed decline to a series low by 2024. Supporting analysis: [He Whakaaro: Teacher turnover in New Zealand schools](https://www.educationcounts.govt.nz/publications/series/he-whakaaro/he-whakaaro-teacher-turnover-in-new-zealand-schools) (2019) and [He Whakaaro: New teacher retention](https://www.educationcounts.govt.nz/publications/series/he-whakaaro/he-whakaaro-new-teacher-retention-in-new-zealand-schools): year-5 retention of new teachers improved from 71 per cent (2004 cohort) to 78 per cent (2014 cohort); in 2017, 21 per cent of teachers moved schools or left, flat over the preceding five years.
- **Definition**: teachers leaving the state-sector teaching workforce (not observed in teaching payroll the following year) - a proper leaving rate for the public system; exits to private schools or ECE are counted as leaving the schooling workforce.

## United States - state-level long series (beyond the national TFS)

### Washington (CALDER, 39-year series)

- **Sources**: Goldhaber and Theobald (2022), [Teacher Attrition and Mobility Over Time](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3102/0013189X211060840), Educational Researcher (abstract-only for the journal version); full findings in the open CALDER briefs: [Flash Brief 23-0421 (PDF)](http://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED612299.pdf) and [Policy Brief 30-0322 (PDF)](https://caldercenter.org/sites/default/files/2024-11/CALDER%20Policy%20Brief%2030-0322.pdf); recent extension in [Supply and Demand in the Postpandemic Teacher Labor Market (WP 313-0325)](https://caldercenter.org/sites/default/files/2025-03/CALDER-WP-313-0325_0.pdf) (S-275 database now spans 39 years).
- **Span**: Washington S-275 administrative data, 1984-85 through 2021-22 and onwards.
- **Level and range (long-run shape)**: annual attrition from the state public school workforce has stayed within 5 to 8 per cent across the entire 35-39 year window; attrition plus school moves ranged 14 to 19 per cent; year-to-year changes never exceeded 1.6 percentage points; attrition is strongly counter-cyclical (falls when unemployment rises). Around the pandemic: leaving public school teaching positions was about 8.4 per cent after 2019-20 versus 9-10 per cent in the prior five years; workforce attrition rose from 6.4 per cent after 2019-20 to 7.3 per cent after 2020-21.
- **Definition**: leaving the Washington public school workforce entirely (separately: moves to non-teaching roles and school moves). A proper exit series at state level; leavers may still teach in private schools or other states.

### Texas

- **Source**: Texas Education Agency, [Teacher Employment, Attrition, and Hiring (April 2026, PDF)](https://tea.texas.gov/texas-educators/superintendents/teacher-employment-attrition-and-hiring-published-april-16-2026.pdf); historical reports on [TEA Historical Data - Teacher Studies](https://tea.texas.gov/reports-and-data/educator-data/historical-data-teacher-studies).
- **Span and values**: annual attrition of employed teachers, 2011-12 to 2024-25: 10.6, 10.6, 10.5, 10.3, 10.4, 10.3, 10.4, 10.4, 10.2, 9.3 (2020-21 trough), 11.6, 13.5 (2022-23 peak), 12.2, 12.9 per cent.
- **Trend verdict**: flat at 10-11 per cent through the 2010s, Covid dip to 9.3 per cent, sharp rise to a 13.5 per cent peak in 2022-23, partial easing since but still well above pre-pandemic levels.
- **Definition**: teachers not employed as public school teachers in Texas the following year - includes moves to non-teaching education roles, private schools or out of state, so a near-proper leaving rate for the state system.

### North Carolina

- **Source**: NC DPI, *State of the Teaching Profession* annual report to the General Assembly: [2024-25 press release](https://www.dpi.nc.gov/news/press-releases/2026/03/04/report-shows-strong-principal-retention-teacher-attrition-rates-hold-steady-2024-25-school-year); [2022-23 report](https://www.dpi.nc.gov/documents/files/state-teaching-profession-2022-2023/download); [April 2025 report (PDF)](https://www.ednc.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/state-of-teaching-profession-report-released-april-2025.pdf).
- **Span and values**: annual legislatively mandated attrition reporting since the 2010s (definitions changed after 2015-16, so earlier years are not comparable). Recent series: 7.8 per cent (2021-22), 11.5 per cent (2022-23 peak), 9.88 per cent (2023-24), 10.11 per cent (2024-25). Beginning teachers (0-5 years) left at 14-18 per cent.
- **Trend verdict**: post-Covid spike then levelling off at about 10 per cent, still clearly above the pre-pandemic norm.
- **Definition**: teachers no longer employed in North Carolina public schools (March-to-March window) - state-system attrition, not pure profession exit.

### New York

**Verdict: PARTIAL - annual statewide turnover indicator exists, but it is turnover, not profession exit, and no long published exit series was found.**

- **Source**: NYSED Data Site, Student and Educator Reports (annual): [2023 statewide report](https://data.nysed.gov/studenteducator.php?year=2023&state=yes) (editions back to at least 2016 via [report cards](https://data.nysed.gov/reportcard.php?instid=800000081568&year=2016&createreport=1&teacherqual=1&teacherturnover=1)). Statewide teacher turnover: 2016: 11 per cent (all teachers), 21 per cent (under five years' experience); 2023: 14 per cent and 22 per cent respectively.
- **Caveat**: NYSED "turnover" counts teachers leaving their school/district, not the profession, so this is not a proper leaving rate. Supplementary: [Ed Trust NY teacher retention analyses](https://newyork.edtrust.org/teacher-retention-in-new-york-state/) (2018-19 to 2021-22, FOI-derived, retention by race/region). Searched additionally for a register-based statewide profession-exit series (NYSED educator diversity report 2019, comptroller briefs); none published.
