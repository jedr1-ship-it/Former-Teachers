# Sweep C: Nordics, Baltics, Ireland, Luxembourg, Belgium (French Community)

Systematic country sweep (group C of 4) for the OECD policy paper. Question per country: does any multi-year evidence exist on the evolution of the teacher leaving rate (exits from the teaching profession, or nearest equivalent)? All sources accessed 28 August 2026. Secondary or press sources are marked as such. Where relevant, the cross-country anchor is the OECD's new attrition indicator in Education at a Glance 2025 (EAG 2025), which reports the share of fully qualified teachers who left the profession by resigning or retiring in 2022/23 for 19 countries, with a comparison against 2013/14 for a subset: range from under 3 per cent (France, Greece, Ireland, Israel) to over 10 per cent (Denmark, Estonia, Lithuania) ([OECD EAG 2025, teacher shortages chapter](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2025/09/education-at-a-glance-2025_c58fc9ae/full-report/how-severe-are-teacher-shortages-across-countries_781f4a97.html); full chapter page is Cloudflare-gated, figures verified via the Lithuania and Estonia country notes cited below).

## Denmark (deepen)

**Verdict: YES, strong multi-year evidence; cohort attrition RISING, annual sector-exit rate rose after the 2013/14 reforms.**

1. **AE (Arbejderbevaegelsens Erhvervsraad), Klarskov, Jensen and Lykketoft, "Mange nye folkeskolelaerere forlader folkeskolen paa faa aar", 7 June 2024** ([analysis](https://www.ae.dk/analyse/2024-06-mange-nye-folkeskolelaerere-forlader-folkeskolen-paa-faa-aar)). Register-based (BFL wage-earner employment register), tracking graduation cohorts annually each August.
   - Span: graduation cohorts 2013 to 2018, followed one to five years.
   - Level and range: of the 2018 cohort, ~10 per cent had left folkeskolen after 1 year, ~19 per cent after 3 years, ~23 per cent (23.7 per cent) after 5 years. For the 2013 cohort, ~18 per cent had left after 5 years.
   - Trend: RISING and accelerating; the one-in-five threshold took 5 years for the 2013 cohort but only 3 years for the 2018 cohort. Of leavers within 3 years, roughly half leave teaching entirely (30 to 40 per cent go to private schools, 10 to 20 per cent teach elsewhere).
   - Definition: exit from folkeskole employment (sector exit, not profession exit); AE decomposes destinations, so a profession-exit share can be derived. Companion piece: [AE debate article, July 2024](https://www.ae.dk/debatindlaeg/2024-07-hver-fjerde-nyuddannede-laerer-har-forladt-folkeskolen-efter-fem-aar-det-er-en); earlier stock analysis: [AE 2022, "Kun 6 ud af 10 laereruddannede arbejder i folkeskolen"](https://www.ae.dk/analyse/2022-04-nyuddannede-kun-6-ud-af-10-laereruddannede-arbejder-i-folkeskolen).

2. **Epinion for the Ministry of Education (UVM), "Laereruddannedes mobilitet paa arbejdsmarkedet", April 2017** ([PDF](https://uvm.dk/media/qoqfp2b5/170406-epinion-laereruddannedes-mobilitet-paa-arbejdsmarkedet-rapport.pdf)). Register-based, includes annexes on entries to and exits from folkeskolen over time.
   - Key figures: in 2014, 2,501 trained teachers moved out of folkeskole jobs against 2,386 moving in; exit levels in 2014 comparable with the previous peak in 2010; total mobility judged modest relative to the stock.
   - The report also synthesises **Danmarks Statistik, DST Analyse, 16 November 2016, "Laerere soeger oftere job efter nye arbejdstidsregler og folkeskolereform"** (cited at note 6 of the Epinion report): 11 per cent of teachers employed in folkeskolen in August 2014 were no longer employed there a year later, an increase of about 2 to 3 percentage points on the years before the 2013 working-time rules and the 2014 folkeskole reform. KRL payroll data (via Ritzau/TV2, secondary): 5,406 teachers left folkeskolen in 2014/15 and 5,180 in 2015/16, a rise of 25 to 31 per cent on 2012/13.
   - Definition: exits from folkeskole employment (sector exit); annual rate, so a proper leaving-rate series in the sector-exit sense.

3. **OECD EAG 2025**: Denmark is among the three countries with attrition above 10 per cent in 2022/23 (profession-exit definition as reported by Denmark) ([chapter](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2025/09/education-at-a-glance-2025_c58fc9ae/full-report/how-severe-are-teacher-shortages-across-countries_781f4a97.html)).

4. Secondary corroboration of the cohort trend turning: [Danske Kommuner, 10 June 2024](https://danskekommuner.dk/artikler/nyheder/2024/juni/10/hver-femte-nyuddannede-laerer-forlader-folkeskolen-inden-tre-ar-men-kurven-gaar-nu-den-rigtige-vej) reports the three-year cohort exit curve has recently begun to improve. AE has also published a 2025 projection ([Fremskrivning af arbejdsmarkedet for folkeskolelaerere, September 2025](https://www.ae.dk/analyse/2025-09-fremskrivning-af-arbejdsmarkedet-for-folkeskolelaerere)).

## Finland

**Verdict: NO official leaving-rate series; multi-year evidence is limited to turnover-intention surveys. A statistical gap acknowledged domestically.**

1. **What exists.** The triennial national teacher data collection (Opettajatiedonkeruu, Statistics Finland for the Ministry of Education and Culture, run since 1998; published as the "Opettajat ja rehtorit Suomessa" series edited by Timo Kumpulainen and successors) tracks qualification rates, age structure and group sizes, NOT exits from the profession. Verified in **Opetushallitus, "Opettajat ja rehtorit Suomessa 2019: Opettajatarpeet nyt ja tulevaisuudessa" (2020)** ([PDF](https://www.oph.fi/sites/default/files/documents/opettajat_ja_rehtorit_suomessa_2019_opettajatarpeet_nyt_ja_tulevaisuudessa.pdf)): mobility out of teaching and retirement are listed only as forecasting inputs; no exit rates are published. The last collection with published results is 2019 ([OPH statistics page](https://www.oph.fi/fi/tilastot/tilastoja-opettajista-ja-rehtoreista)).
2. **The gap is documented.** The teachers' union magazine reports that statistical knowledge of teachers has "sunk into obscurity" since 2019 and that a national teacher register has been proposed but not yet decided ([Opettaja.fi, "Tilastotieto opettajista vajosi hamaryyteen"](https://www.opettaja.fi/tyossa/tilastotieto-opettajista-menee-metsaan/), secondary; [OAJ on the register, 2023](https://www.oaj.fi/ajankohtaista/uutiset-ja-tiedotteet/2023/opettajarekisterin-toteutukseen-selkeytta--tulevalta-hallitukselta-tarvitaan-paatos/)).
3. **Intention evidence (not exits), multi-year.** Raesaenen, Pietarinen, Pyhaelto, Soini and Vaeisaenen (2020, Social Psychology of Education) found 32 per cent of 1,064 basic-education teachers reported persistent turnover intentions over a five-year follow-up (2010 to 2016) (abstract-only via [ResearchGate/Frontiers citation trail](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02671522.2022.2125054)); a 2023 University of Eastern Finland doctoral study (Katariina Raesaenen) reported every second teacher considering a change ([UEF press release](https://www.uef.fi/fi/artikkeli/fm-katariina-rasanen-vaitos-13102023-joka-toinen-opettaja-harkitsee-alanvaihtoa-taustalla-usein)); OAJ surveys report intention shares rising from about two in ten (2013) to six in ten ([OAJ/STT release](https://www.sttinfo.fi/tiedote/69920059/dramaattinen-muutos-jo-kuusi-kymmenesta-opettajasta-harkitsee-alanvaihtoa?publisherId=69817356), secondary). These are intentions, not a leaving rate.
4. **Searched before concluding absence**: opettajien alanvaihto/ammatinvaihto tilasto; opettajien pysyvyys ammatissa rekisteritutkimus; kuinka moni opettaja vaihtaa alaa Tilastokeskus; Vipunen opettajat; Opettajat Suomessa editions 2013 and 2019 (exit tracking absent); "teacher attrition Finland register-based longitudinal". Finland does not appear in the EAG 2025 19-country attrition set. Conclusion: no published multi-year profession-exit series.

## Iceland

**Verdict: YES, one register-based academic series covering 1998 to 2020; level ~7.5 per cent a year, broadly STABLE with a counter-cyclical dip.**

1. **Amalia Bjoernsdottir and Boerkur Hansen, "Starfsmannavelta medal kennara i grunnskolum arin 1998 til 2020", Netla (Serrit 2024, Menntakvika), University of Iceland, 2024**, DOI [10.24270/serritnetla.2024.6](https://doi.org/10.24270/serritnetla.2024.6), article page: [ejournals.is/index.php/netla/article/view/4021](https://ejournals.is/index.php/netla/article/view/4021).
   - Span: 1998 to 2020, register data from Hagstofa Islands (Statistics Iceland).
   - Level and range: on average about 7.5 per cent of compulsory-school (grunnskoli) teachers employed in a given year 1998 to 2018 were not employed in teaching the following year; the rate fell to about 6.3 per cent in the post-crisis years 2009 to 2013.
   - Trend: broadly STABLE across two decades, dipping after the 2008 financial crisis; turnover higher in smaller schools; a substantial share leave before retirement age.
   - Definition: not employed as a grunnskoli teacher the following year (sector exit from compulsory-school teaching, including retirements); an annual leaving rate in the sector sense.
2. **Helgi Eirikur Eyjolfsson and Stefan Hrafn Jonsson (2017)**, report on recruitment and retention of grunnskoli teachers: recruitment does not compensate for age-related exits; related survey evidence indicates 69 per cent of those who begin teaching are still teaching seven years later (via search summaries of [opinvisindi.is holdings](https://opinvisindi.is/server/api/core/bitstreams/7fdb96ff-a48a-45f7-9ac4-d2f4fc2eb550/content); secondary, not independently verified page by page).
3. Context: Hagstofa Islands reports the number of licensed teachers at a record high ([Hagstofa news](https://hagstofa.is/utgafur/frettasafn/menntun/kennarar-med-kennslurettindi-hafa-aldrei-verid-fleiri/)). A 2025 parliamentary report on the teaching workforce exists (Althingi, 156th session, thingskjal 311) but the site blocks automated access ([blocked URL](https://www.althingi.is/altext/pdf/156/s/0311.pdf)); older analysis reported staff attrition of 14.9 per cent (2010 to 2011) against 13.2 per cent the year before, but that figure includes non-licensed staff (whose attrition was 39.0 per cent, against 12.9 per cent for qualified teachers), so it is not comparable with the Netla series.

## Norway (extend beyond With 2017)

**Verdict: YES, extended; SSB register evidence through 2024; leaving is gradual and the picture is broadly STABLE, with net outflow exceeding returns.**

1. **SSB, Aamodt and Naesheim, "Vanligere aa forsvinne fra laereryrket enn aa komme tilbake dit", 18 June 2019** ([article](https://www.ssb.no/utdanning/artikler-og-publikasjoner/vanligere-a-forsvinne-fra-laereryrket-enn-a-komme-tilbake-dit)).
   - Span: 2008 to 2018. Level: 20 per cent of teachers employed in skoleverket (primary and secondary schools) in 2008 had left by 2018 (81 per cent ten-year retention); 8,100 left for other industries while only 3,700 returned (14 per cent of leavers; 16 per cent of women, 10 per cent of men). Exits concentrated among teachers aged 50 plus.
   - Definition: exit from the school sector (skoleverket), distinguished from the wider teaching industry; decadal cohort survival rather than an annual rate.
2. **SSB, "Overgangen fra laererutdanning til laereryrke", 27 March 2025** ([article](https://www.ssb.no/utdanning/hoyere-utdanning/artikler/overgangen-fra-laererutdanning-til-laereryrke)): of the 2016 graduating cohort (6,628 completers), 84 per cent of primary teachers (grades 1 to 7) were employed in schools seven years after graduation; about 9 in 10 grunnskole-trained teachers work in schools after two years. Widely reported as "most teachers stay for many years" ([Kommunal Rapport](https://www.kommunal-rapport.no/nyheter/ssb-over-8-av-10-laerere-blir-i-yrket-i-mange-ar/810151), secondary).
3. **SSB, Gilje, "Grunnskolelaerere blir lengst i skolesektoren", 19 August 2025** ([article](https://www.ssb.no/utdanning/barnehager/statistikk/ansatte-i-barnehage-og-skole/artikler/grunnskolelaerere-blir-lengst-i-skolesektoren)): stock analysis as of November 2024; 77 per cent of teacher-trained people 0 to 10 years after graduation work in the school sector, declining to 53 per cent 30 to 39 years after graduation; 87 per cent of grunnskole-trained teachers work in the school sector in the first ten years.
4. Stock complement: **SSB 2021/2022 articles**, "Vel 1 av 3 med skolerettet laererutdanning er ikke i skoleverket" ([article](https://www.ssb.no/utdanning/artikler-og-publikasjoner/vel-1-av-3-med-skolerettet-laererutdanning-er-ikke-i-skoleverket)) and "8 av 10 laererutdannede jobber i skolen eller andre relevante naeringer", 29 August 2022 ([article](https://www.ssb.no/utdanning/barnehager/statistikk/ansatte-i-barnehage-og-skole/artikler/8-av-10-laererutdannede-jobber-i-skolen-eller-andre-relevante-naeringer)). Forward-looking: **LAERERMOD 2024-2060**, November 2025 ([article](https://www.ssb.no/arbeid-og-lonn/sysselsetting/artikler/faerre-barnehagelaerere-men-flere-grunnskolelaerere-frem-mot-2060); [LAERERMOD 2020-2040 report PDF](https://www.ssb.no/arbeid-og-lonn/sysselsetting/artikler/laerermod-2020-2040/_/attachment/inline/88e4a930-a958-4dcf-8e60-a6b5a346a131:bb13eb2cac67aecc1a4b5b001a849a835a56a2b9/RAPP2023-13.pdf)).
5. Assessment: taken with With (2017), the SSB series show no marked worsening of Norwegian teacher attrition; exits are dominated by older teachers, and cohort retention of newly qualified teachers remains high (84 to 90 per cent in schools two to seven years out). Trend verdict: STABLE. Note these are sector-exit, not strict profession-exit, definitions; no single annual leaving-rate series is published.

## Estonia

**Verdict: YES; register-based national evidence; RISING sharply since about 2019/20; among the highest attrition in the OECD.**

1. **Eneli Kindsiko, "Opetajate ametist lahkumine", Arenguseire Keskus (Foresight Centre, Estonian Parliament), 2023** ([report page](https://arenguseire.ee/raportid/opetajate-ametist-lahkumine/)). EHIS register data.
   - Span: school years 2005/06 to 2021/22.
   - Level and range: 1,200 to 1,900 general-education teachers leave each year (average about 1,459), equal to 8 to 12 per cent of the teaching workforce; 1,704 left temporarily or permanently in 2021/22.
   - Trend: RISING; departures in 2021/22 were about one third higher than in 2019/20. 76 per cent of leavers were of active working age (46 per cent under 40; only 24 per cent aged 60 plus). About one third of beginning teachers leave within their first three years.
   - Definition: left the school where they taught, temporarily or permanently (school/sector exit including temporary leave), so it overstates a strict profession-exit rate; the report is explicit about this.
2. **OECD EAG 2025, Estonia country note** ([page](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/education-at-a-glance-2025_1a3543e2-en/estonia_9f1f41d3-en.html)): in 2022/23, 2.2 per cent of teachers retired and 9.4 per cent resigned (total attrition about 11.6 per cent, among the highest); the chapter records that Estonia's attrition rose by more than 2 percentage points between 2013/14 and 2022/23 (with Austria the only other such case). This is the international confirmation of the national doubling the project holds.
3. **Ministry of Education and Research (HTM), "Opetajate jarelkasv. Kirjanduse ulevaade" (literature review, 2023)** ([PDF](https://www.hm.ee/sites/default/files/documents/2023-05/%C3%95petajate%20j%C3%A4relkasv.%20Kirjanduse%20%C3%BClevaade_0.pdf)): synthesises EHIS-based analyses of teacher supply, entry and exit, including the finding that a third of beginning teachers leave within three years.
4. Secondary press analyses of EHIS data: [Postimees, "STATISTIKA Kuhu kaovad noored opetajad?"](https://haridus.postimees.ee/7619712/statistika-kuhu-kaovad-noored-opetajad) and [Lounapostimees on experienced teachers leaving](https://lounapostimees.postimees.ee/7851833/raport-opetajate-jarelkasvu-probleemi-suvendab-kogenud-opetajate-lahkumine).
5. Why the rise: the Foresight Centre attributes the post-2019 acceleration to workload and burnout (aggravated during COVID-19), pay competitiveness, and the age structure, with exits concentrated in Estonian language, mathematics and English teachers.

## Latvia

**Verdict: PARTIAL; cohort-based audit evidence exists (one third of new teachers leave education within five years), but no published multi-year leaving-rate series was found.**

1. **Valsts kontrole (State Audit Office of Latvia), audit report "Have the actions taken ... to ensure regeneration and persistence in the teaching profession of general education institutions been effective?", 3 July 2024** ([English summary](https://www.lrvk.gov.lv/en/news/sao-latvia-much-greater-efforts-must-be-made-to-ensure-regeneration-and-persistence-of-teachers-in-the-profession)).
   - Findings: about 33 per cent of young teachers exit the education sector within their first five years; more than 1,000 teacher-training graduates a year, of whom 81 per cent start work in the sector. Cohort-based (VIIS and sector registers via audited institutions), single snapshot rather than a series.
   - Definition: exit from the education sector within five years of entry (sector exit, early-career cohort), not an annual profession-exit rate.
2. **European Commission, Education and Training Monitor 2025, Latvia chapter** ([page](https://op.europa.eu/webpub/eac/education-and-training-monitor/en/country-reports/latvia.html)): repeats the one-third-within-five-years figure and the ageing profile (half of teachers over 50). Secondary compilation.
3. Intention evidence (not exits): TALIS 2024 via [OECD Education GPS Latvia](https://gpseducation.oecd.org/CountryProfile?primaryCountry=LVA&treshold=10&topic=TA) (over 30 per cent of non-retiring teachers, and 53 per cent of under-30s, intend to leave within five years); national surveys reported by LSM ([44 per cent doubt staying, 2021](https://www.lsm.lv/raksts/zinas/latvija/petijums-44-skolotaju-saubas-par-paliksanu-profesija.a456315/); [2023 follow-up](https://www.lsm.lv/raksts/dzive--stils/vecaki-un-berni/18.05.2023-petijums-pedagogi-latvija-jutas-nenoverteti-demotiveti-gatavi-pamest-izglitibas-nozari.a509144/), both secondary).
4. **Searched before concluding no series**: skolotaju aiziesana no profesijas IZM; pedagogu mainiba statistika; pedagogu izdegsana aiziesana LIZDA; VIIS teacher exit data; OECD School Resources Review Latvia; EAG 2025 (Latvia does not appear among the countries quoted in the attrition discussion; its country note emphasises unfilled positions, 1.1 per cent, rather than attrition). Conclusion: no annual leaving-rate series published by IZM or CSP; the SAO audit is the best national evidence.

## Lithuania

**Verdict: YES; national register-based evidence now exists; level ~7 per cent a year on average 2020/21 to 2024/25 and RISING; OECD reports the highest attrition rate (about 12.5 per cent in 2022/23).**

1. **Nacionaline svietimo agentura (NSA), "Pedagogu poreikio prognozavimas: atnaujintas modelis ir rezultatai", 2025** ([PDF](https://www.nsa.smsm.lt/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Pedagogu-poreikio-prognozavimas-2025.pdf)). Register of Pedagogues and SVIS data.
   - Span: 2020/21 to 2024/25 observed; forecasts to 2030/31.
   - Level: on average 7 per cent of working pedagogues left pedagogical work each year over 2020/21 to 2024/25 (section 5.6, "Auga is darbo iseinanciu pedagogu dalis", the share leaving work is growing); for pre-school teachers and social pedagogues the observed rate already reaches about 10 per cent.
   - Trend: RISING; the model's central scenario assumes the leaving share climbs by one percentage point a year to 10 per cent, driven by age structure (45 per cent of teachers aged 55 plus by 2025) and early-career exits; under that scenario the five-year need for new subject teachers rises 67 per cent (6,700 to 11,300).
   - Definition: leaving pedagogical work for any reason (retirement, other work, death, emigration, parental leave); the report states reasons for leaving are not systematically collected, so this is a gross exit rate from teaching employment rather than a clean profession-exit rate.
2. **OECD EAG 2025, Lithuania country note** ([PDF](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/09/education-at-a-glance-2025-country-notes_9749f4ff/lithuania_9552f981/b4b81fd6-en.pdf)): Lithuania among the high-turnover countries (above 10 per cent in 2022/23); no retirement/resignation breakdown available. The 12.5 per cent figure held by the project is from the underlying EAG data (Figure 6); the country note text confirms the high placement without printing the number.
3. **NSA, Svietimo bukles apzvalga 2026** (State of Education Review 2026, announced 26 August 2026, with a dedicated focus on teachers) ([announcement](https://www.nsa.smsm.lt/2026/08/26/2026-metu-svietimo-bukles-apzvalgoje-isskirtinis-demesys-mokytojams/)): reports the growing shortfall (150 unfilled teacher posts in 2023, 350 in 2024, 600 in 2025) and a projected shortage of about 6,000 teachers over five years. Precursor modelling: **STRATA (2018), "Forecasting the Teaching Workforce in Lithuania"** ([PDF](https://strata.gov.lt/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/2018-11-07-Forecasting-the-Teaching-Workforce-inLithuania.pdf)).
4. Intention evidence: TALIS 2024, nearly half of Lithuanian teachers under 30 consider leaving within five years (via [NSA/press](https://77.lt/oecd-tyrimas-lietuvos-mokytojai-sensta-jauni-mokytojai-bega-is-mokyklu), secondary).

## Ireland

**Verdict: YES but thin; payroll-based estimates and the OECD indicator show attrition LOW and broadly STABLE (about 2 to 3 per cent, half of it retirements); no long published national series.**

1. **Department of Education, "Developing a Teacher Demand and Supply Model for Ireland 2021-2038", technical report, 2021** ([PDF](https://assets.gov.ie/136246/69e07296-94b9-4ebd-8e6c-4bd789161893.pdf); [collection page for the 2020-2036 edition](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-education/publications/developing-a-teacher-demand-and-supply-model-for-ireland-2020-2036/)).
   - Span: payroll data 2014 to 2018/19.
   - Level: estimated primary-level resignations (teachers on payroll one year, never on payroll again, career breaks excluded) averaged 210 a year over 2014 to 2016, plus about 92 a year not returning from career break, giving roughly 302 resignations a year against a primary teaching stock of well over 35,000, well under 1 per cent a year. Retirements are the dominant exit route.
   - Definition: profession exit proxy (permanent disappearance from the public payroll), pre-retirement resignations only; a proper leaving-rate construction but published as model inputs, not as a statistical series.
2. **OECD EAG 2025** ([chapter](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2025/09/education-at-a-glance-2025_c58fc9ae/full-report/how-severe-are-teacher-shortages-across-countries_781f4a97.html); [Ireland country note](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/education-at-a-glance-2025_1a3543e2-en/ireland_1ed5ff68-en.html)): Ireland is in the under-3-per-cent attrition group in 2022/23, with 1.4 per cent of teachers retiring and 0.9 per cent resigning (about 2.3 per cent total); retirements account for more than half of departures.
3. **Searched further**: Department of Education statistical bulletins and Teacher Statistics pages ([education statistics](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-education/publications/education-statistics/); the [Retention collection](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-education/collections/retention/) covers pupil retention, not teachers); Teaching Council annual reports ([2024/25](https://www.teachingcouncil.ie/news/the-teaching-council-publishes-its-annual-report-2024-2025/)) publish registration counts, not exit rates; ESRI and academic work focuses on supply shortages rather than exits (Harford and O'Doherty and others, ["Teacher supply in Ireland: anatomy of a crisis", Irish Educational Studies, 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/03323315.2023.2222709), abstract-only). Conclusion: attrition is low and stable, but Ireland publishes no continuous leaving-rate series; the OECD submission and the demand-supply model are the evidence base.

## Luxembourg

**Verdict: NO published evidence found; a January 2026 parliamentary question explicitly requests ten years of turnover data, indicating no public series exists.**

1. **Parliamentary question by deputy David Wagner (dei Lenk), 23 January 2026, "Turnover, postes vacants et facteurs d'epuisement professionnel dans l'enseignement fondamental"** ([text](https://dei-lenk.lu/en/2026/01/23/turnover-postes-vacants-et-facteurs-depuisement-professionnel-dans-lenseignement-fondamental/)): asks the ministers of Education and of the Civil Service for ten years of departures by reason (resignation, dismissal or end of contract, retirement, death, change of administration) by regional directorate, plus sick leave and burnout indicators. The fact that this is being requested, with no figures cited, is itself evidence that no published series exists. A ministerial answer with statistics had not been located at the time of access.
2. **Searched before concluding absence**: MENJE statistical publications "L'enseignement au Luxembourg en chiffres" ([2022/23](https://men.public.lu/fr/publications/statistiques-etudes/themes-transversaux/22-23-enseignement-chiffres.html), [2021/22](https://men.public.lu/fr/publications/statistiques-etudes/themes-transversaux/21-22-enseignement-chiffres.html)) and secondary-education statistics ([ESG 2020/21 PDF](https://men.public.lu/dam-assets/catalogue-publications/statistiques-etudes/secondaire/20-21-esg-statistiques.pdf)): staff counts only, no exits; the Edustat key-figures portal ([edustat.script.lu](https://edustat.script.lu/fr/public-data/key-figure)); Eurydice Luxembourg teachers chapter ([page](https://eurydice.eacea.ec.europa.eu/fr/national-education-systems/luxembourg/enseignants-et-personnel-de-leducation)); parliamentary question archives on teacher shortage ([QP 8022, June 2023](https://men.public.lu/fr/actualites/questions-parlementaires/2023/06/qp-8022.html)); searches in French for "demissions d'enseignants Luxembourg", "enseignants quittent l'enseignement MENJE". Luxembourg is not in the EAG 2025 attrition set. Conclusion: no multi-year leaving-rate evidence, official or academic, was found.

## Belgium, French Community (Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles)

**Verdict: YES; two register-based cohort studies a decade apart; early-career exit around one third within five years, essentially STABLE (35 per cent for 2005-2011 entrants; 33.7 per cent for the 2017/18 cohort).**

1. **Delvaux, Desmarez, Dupriez, Lothaire and Veinstein (2013), "Les enseignants debutants en Belgique francophone: trajectoires, conditions d'emploi et positions sur le marche du travail", Les Cahiers de recherche du GIRSEF n. 92, UCLouvain**, and companion papers (Lothaire, Dupriez and Dumay 2012; Dupriez, Delvaux and Lothaire 2016). Administrative payroll database of all beginning teachers in fondamental and secondaire, January 2005 to December 2011.
   - Level: 35 per cent of beginning teachers left the profession within their first five years; about one in five left within the first year; the five-year exit rate was 21 per cent for holders of a pedagogical qualification against 61 per cent for those without; leaving probability strongly tied to months worked in year one (58.2 per cent exit for those employed 2 to 5 months in year one, against 19 per cent for those employed 10 months). Convenient synthesis with the figures: **Lothaire, S. (2021), "Le recrutement des enseignants en Federation Wallonie-Bruxelles: un processus impactant les parcours professionnels", Cereq Echanges** ([PDF](https://www.cereq.fr/sites/default/files/2021-02/29-Le%20recrutement%20des%20enseignants%20en%20F%C3%A9d%C3%A9ration%20Wallonie-Bruxelles%20%20un%20processus%20impactant%20les%20parcours%20professionnels.pdf)); contemporary press summary: [RTBF, 2 May 2013](https://www.rtbf.be/article/un-jeune-enseignant-sur-cinq-quitte-la-profession-des-sa-premiere-annee-7986043) (secondary).
   - Definition: exit from teaching employment in the FWB system within five years of entry (profession exit within the Community's schools); proper cohort leaving rates.
2. **FWB and Universite de Liege cohort follow-up (reported 2025)**: 66.3 per cent of teachers who entered in 2017/18 were still active in the profession five years later, so 33.7 per cent had left; exits again markedly higher among entrants without pedagogical credentials ([BX1 report](https://bx1.be/categories/news/etre-enseignant-en-federation-wallonie-bruxelles-ca-attire-toujours/), secondary reporting of the study; primary study not located online).
3. **Administration generale de l'Enseignement, "Les indicateurs de l'enseignement"** annual series, 18th edition 2023 ([PDF](http://www.enseignement.be/public/docs/les-indicateurs-de-l-enseignement-2023-m-j-du-05-03-2024-.pdf); [index page](http://www.enseignement.be/index.php?page=0&navi=2264); 19th edition published 31 March 2025, [press release](https://belgashare.be/fr/newsrooms/200/press-releases/8000/)). The brochure carries staff indicators; the 2025 press release headlines student indicators, and the personnel exit indicator could not be verified page by page here (PDF gated behind a redirect at access time), so the brochure is flagged as a source to verify in print.
4. Trend reading: with c. 35 per cent (entrants 2005 to 2011) and 33.7 per cent (entrants 2017/18) leaving within five years, FWB early-career attrition is high but essentially stable across a decade; recruitment of unqualified entrants (10 per cent of new recruits in 2016 to 25 per cent in 2024, per [Cereq/BX1 reporting](https://bx1.be/categories/news/etre-enseignant-en-federation-wallonie-bruxelles-ca-attire-toujours/), secondary) mechanically pushes aggregate exits up because their five-year exit rate is about three times higher.

## Cross-cutting notes for the paper

- Definitions vary systematically: Denmark (AE, DST) and Norway (SSB) measure sector exits (folkeskolen, skoleverket); Iceland (Netla) measures exit from compulsory-school teaching; Estonia (EHIS) and Lithuania (Register of Pedagogues) count all leavers including temporary ones; Ireland isolates pre-retirement resignations; FWB measures profession exit of entrant cohorts. Only the OECD EAG 2025 indicator imposes a common (self-reported) definition, and only for 2022/23 with a 2013/14 comparison for some countries.
- Trend summary for group C: rising in Denmark, Estonia and Lithuania; stable in Norway, Iceland, Ireland and FWB (at a high level for FWB entrants); no usable exit series in Finland, Latvia (cohort snapshot only) and Luxembourg (nothing).
