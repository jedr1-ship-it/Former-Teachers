# Where does pay rank among teachers' STATED reasons for leaving?

Verification memo for the claim: *"teachers rarely name pay as the main reason for leaving, yet observed decisions respond strongly to pay variation."*
All sources accessed **26 August 2026**. Figures below were read directly from the primary PDFs/pages unless marked otherwise. Actual-leaver evidence and intention-to-leave evidence are kept strictly separate throughout.

---

## 1. United States

### 1.1 ACTUAL LEAVERS — NCES Teacher Follow-up Survey (TFS) 2021–22 (primary, verified from PDF)

Design: nationally representative follow-up of 2020–21 NTPS teachers who left K–12 teaching by 2021–22. Voluntary leavers chose **one single "most important reason"** from a detailed pick-list (Table A-7).

Top single most-important reasons, all leavers:

| Rank | Reason | % |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Retirement / retirement benefits | 16.1 |
| 2 | Other personal life reasons (health, pregnancy/childcare, caring for family) | 15.0 |
| 3 | Decided to pursue a position other than K–12 teacher | 13.1 |
| **4** | **"Because I wanted or needed a higher salary"** | **9.5** |
| 5 | Dissatisfied with teaching as a career | 7.3 |
| 6 | Other factors | 7.4 |

Public-school leavers only: 9.2% named higher salary. "Needed better benefits" did not meet reporting standards (too few cases). So in the flagship US survey of *actual* leavers, **pay is 4th and under 10% as the main reason** — but note the grouped "personal life factors" and the single-choice design compress everything; no single item exceeds 16%.

- Taie, S., & Lewis, L. (2023, December). *Teacher Attrition and Mobility: Results From the 2021–22 Teacher Follow-up Survey to the National Teacher and Principal Survey* (First Look, NCES 2024-039), Table A-7. https://nces.ed.gov/pubs2024/2024039M.pdf
- Corresponding NCES online table: https://nces.ed.gov/surveys/ntps/estable/table/ntps/tfs2122_srt05_f12n

### 1.2 ACTUAL LEAVERS — RAND survey of ~1,000 former public-school teachers (Dec 2020; verified from PDF)

Diliberti, Schwartz & Grant (2021), RR-A1121-2. Multi-select "What were the reasons you left teaching?" (voluntary early leavers, N=723, Table B.8):

- Stress and disappointments of teaching weren't worth it — **43%**
- Didn't like the way things were run at my school — 32%
- Not enough support from district/school — 29%
- **"My pay was not sufficient" — 24%** (≈4th)
- Better education-related job — 23%

Report text: pre-pandemic leavers cited stress (48%) "nearly twice as often" as insufficient pay (27%). **Important nuance:** among the subset who left *because of COVID* (N=109), the item "the pay wasn't sufficient to merit the **risks or stress**" was the top multi-select reason (64%) and the top "single biggest reason" (21%) — but that item explicitly bundles pay with risk/stress, so it cannot be read as pay-alone ranking first.

- Diliberti, M. K., Schwartz, H. L., & Grant, D. (2021). *Stress Topped the Reasons Why Public School Teachers Quit, Even Before COVID-19.* RAND, RR-A1121-2. https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA1121-2.html (PDF: https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/research_reports/RRA1100/RRA1121-2/RAND_RRA1121-2.pdf)

### 1.3 INTENTIONS — RAND State of the American Teacher (SoT) surveys 2023–2025 (verified from PDFs)

**2023 (Doan, Steiner, Pandey & Woo, RR-A1108-8, Figure 6).** Teachers likely to leave ranked their top-five factors: stress/disappointments not worth it **70%**, **salary 60% (rank #2)**, hours worked outside the school day 54%, total hours 50%. Subgroups where salary rose to/near the top: 72% of Black teachers (vs 57% White); **98% of teachers with 0–5 years of experience** selected low salary as a top reason. Salary was also a top reason to *stay* for 48% of stayers.
https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA1108-8.html

**2024 (RR-A1108-12).** No leave-reasons ranking; "my salary is too low" was a top-ranked *source of job-related stress* for 37% of teachers (up from 2023). https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA1108-12.html

**2025 (Steiner, Levine, Doan & Woo, RR-A1108-16).** Low salary was the **second most commonly ranked top-3 source of stress (39%)**, behind managing student behavior (50%). https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA1108-16.html

**Bottom line US:** among *intenders*, pay is #1–#2; among *actual leavers*, pay is ~4th at 9.5–24% depending on design.

### 1.4 Michigan (the lead author's memory) — mostly INTENTIONS, and context-specific

- **EPIC / Harbatkin et al. 2023** (*Teacher Mobility in Turnaround Schools*, EPIC/MSU, Aug 2023; verified from PDF, Figure 4): among teachers in Michigan **Partnership (lowest-performing, high-poverty) districts** who reported *plans to leave* their district (N=218, 2020–21/2021–22 surveys), **pay and workload were the top-cited factors in Cohort 2 (~60% each rating them major/primary)**, ahead of school leadership (42%). EPIC's own 2024 Shortage Study summarizes this as "teachers who planned to leave their jobs cited workload and pay as the top reasons." This is (a) intentions, not exits, and (b) a low-pay, high-need district sample — exactly the context where pay rises to the top.
  https://epicedpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Partnership_TeacherMobilityReport_Aug2023.pdf
- **Michigan Educator Workforce Initiative / Impact Research poll (Aug 2026)**: representative poll of 300 Michigan teachers (May 29–Jun 30, 2026; MoE ±5.7pp): ~25% considering leaving within a year; **inadequate compensation named among the top three reasons for considering leaving**; only 32% strongly agree they are fairly compensated. Small n; considerers, not leavers. Reported by Chalkbeat Detroit: https://www.chalkbeat.org/detroit/2026/08/25/michigan-teachers-considering-leaving-the-profession-in-a-year/ *(press account; underlying tabulations not located — secondary)*
- **EPIC Michigan Teacher Shortage Study 2026** (Jan 2026, verified from PDF): administrative data, no leaver survey; notes nationally that teachers report "salary as a top source of job-related stress (Steiner et al., 2025) and low salary as a top reason for leaving the profession (NCES, 2023)" — note the NCES attribution is loose relative to Table A-7 above. https://epicedpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Teacher-Shortage-Report-Combined-Jan2026.pdf

**So the lead author's Michigan memory is real but is an intentions finding, concentrated in low-paid/low-performing districts and in a small 2026 considerers poll — not a finding about actual leavers' main stated reason.**

## 2. England

### 2.1 ACTUAL LEAVERS — DfE Working Lives of Teachers and Leaders (WLTL), waves 3–4 (verified from PDFs)

Multi-select "how important were the following factors in your decision to leave the state education sector" (leavers, n=520 in wave 3):

Wave 3 (left 2023→2024): high workload **84%**, stress/poor wellbeing **75%**, views not valued by policymakers 67%, Ofsted/outcomes pressure 64%, poor mental/physical health 62%, funding 58%, staff shortages 52%, lack of support from superiors 51%, government initiatives 47%, mismanagement 46%, personal reasons 44%, pupil behaviour 44%, lack of flexible working 39%, **dissatisfaction with pay 34% (≈14th of 17 items)**, progression 28%, bullying/harassment 27%, public perceptions 23%.

Wave 4 (left 2024→2025): workload 74%, stress 73%; **dissatisfaction with pay fell to 25%** (from 34% in 2024, 39% in 2023). Pay was more salient for ex-teachers (37%) than ex-leaders (14%) and for early-career/younger leavers.

### 2.2 INTENTIONS — same survey, considerers (wave 3, n=3,316)

Stress and workload both **90%**; not valued by policymakers 79%; funding 78%; **dissatisfaction with pay 56% (≈9th)**, down from 63% in 2023. Note the leaver–considerer gap: pay 56% among considerers vs 34% among the same year's actual leavers.

- DfE (2025). *Working lives of teachers and leaders: wave 3 research report* (IFF Research/IoE), Figs 12.1, 13.1. https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68f11c49e8e4040c38a3cfbf/Working_lives_of_teachers_and_leaders_wave_3_-_research_report.pdf
- DfE (2025). *Working lives of teachers and leaders: wave 4 summary report*, Fig 11.1. https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/693bdc426a12691d48491e9b/Working_lives_of_teachers_and_leaders_wave_4_summary_report.pdf
- Corroboration: NFER *Teacher Labour Market in England Annual Report 2025* — 90% of teachers considering leaving in 2023/24 cited high workload. https://www.nfer.ac.uk/publications/teacher-labour-market-in-england-annual-report-2025/ *(secondary synthesis of the above)*

## 3. Other countries

### 3.1 Netherlands — Van Casteren et al. 2023 (VERIFIED from PDF; the paper's "pay sixth, 22%" checks out)

ResearchNed for OCW: Van Casteren, W., Lodewick, J., Lommertzen, J., Luyten, E., & Van Mensvoort, C. (Dec 2023). *Vertrekredenen leraren en docenten in het po, vo en mbo.* Method: interviews with 184 recent leavers/switchers + 129 written accounts (actual leavers, open/qualitative coding into ten motives — modest sample). Ranked motives: werkdruk (workload/stress) **47%**; lack of challenge/professional development 41%; dissatisfaction with leadership/board 37%; lack of mentoring/team 27%; educational vision/autonomy 25%; **#6 "Onvrede over (toepassing van) de arbeidsvoorwaarden (salaris)" — 22%** (tied with #7, frustration over own impact, 22%); work-life 14%. Caveat: the 22% item is "employment conditions (salary)" — broader than salary alone. 80% said they could have been retained had conditions differed.
PDF: https://www.voion.nl/wp-content/uploads/media/pfxfeukc/vertrekredenen-leraren-en-docenten-in-het-po-vo-en-mbo-december-2023.pdf (landing: https://www.researchned.nl/2023/12/vertrekredenen-leraren/)

### 3.2 Poland — ZNP / University of Warsaw study, June 2026 (intentions/stressors; "pay first" only partially verifiable)

"Ogólnopolskie badanie nt. ryzyka psychologicznego i stresu w zawodzie nauczyciela," fieldwork Feb–Apr 2026 by a University of Warsaw team (Żytko et al.), ~1,750–1,800 education workers, commissioned by the ZNP union. Public materials: **low pay ("inadequate to professional responsibility") is presented first among barriers to wellbeing / the most burdensome of 20 stressors, 13 of which are "systemic"**; 72% cannot clearly commit to staying (22% thinking of leaving, 19% would leave if they could, 11% actively leaving, 17% near retirement); only 28% sure they will stay. **No published ranked table of "reasons for leaving" with percentages was located; commissioning body is a union (advocacy risk); treat "pay ranks first in Poland" as supported for *stressors among serving teachers*, not verified for leavers.**
- ZNP results page: https://znp.edu.pl/wyniki-ogolnopolskiego-badania-nt-ryzyka-psychologicznego-i-stresu-w-zawodzie-nauczyciela/
- Press: https://www.prawo.pl/oswiata/co-czwarty-nauczyciel-chce-odejsc-ze-szkoly-dane-znp,1547006.html *(secondary/press summaries; full technical report not located — mark as partially verified)*
- Context: TALIS 2024 (IBE Poland) — only 20% of Polish teachers satisfied with salary vs 37% EU average. https://znp.edu.pl/kondycja-polskich-nauczycieli-i-nauczycielek/

### 3.3 Australia — ATWD/AITSL (INTENTIONS; verified for 2023, 2024 partially)

Australian Teacher Workforce Survey, reasons for intending to leave the profession before retirement (AITSL National Trends: Teacher Workforce, June 2025 release, Table 18; 2023 data): workload **75%**, work/life balance 69%, stress/mental health 68%, administrative workload 63%, **insufficient pay 52% (5th)**, professional regulation demands 50%. Insufficient pay has risen steadily: 36% (2019) → 32% (2020) → 42% (2021) → 50% (2022) → 52% (2023). The June 2026 release reports workload/work-life/stress again as the top three (69–75% in 2024), with parent behaviour 48% and student behaviour 47% rising. No national stated-reason survey of *actual* leavers.
- https://www.aitsl.edu.au/research/australian-teacher-workforce-data/atwd-reports/national-trends-teacher-workforce-jun2025
- https://www.aitsl.edu.au/research/australian-teacher-workforce-data/atwd-reports/national-trends-teacher-workforce-june-2026 *(2024 figures read from summary text/search capture; verify against final PDF before citing — partially verified)*

## 4. Verdict

**"Teachers rarely name pay as the main reason for leaving" is defensible only if it is explicitly restricted to surveys of actual leavers — and even then it needs the design caveat.** The pattern in every actual-leaver survey verified here: US TFS 2021–22 (pay 4th, 9.5% as the single most-important reason), RAND 2021 (pay 24% vs stress 43%), England WLTL (pay 34%→25%, ~14th, vs workload 74–84%), Netherlands (pay/conditions 6th, 22%, vs workload 47%). No verified actual-leaver survey puts pay first.

**But the lead author is also right**, in three specific senses: (1) in *intention* surveys pay is at or near the top — RAND SoT 2023 has salary #2 at 60% (and #1 for Black and early-career teachers, the latter at 98%); Michigan Partnership-district intended leavers put pay joint-top (~60%); Poland's 2026 study puts pay first among stressors; Australia's pay share has climbed to >50%; (2) pay's rank climbs in low-pay systems and segments; (3) rankings are artifacts of design — single-most-important pick-lists (TFS) suppress pay relative to multi-select importance ratings (WLTL, ATWD), and open-response coding (NL) sits in between. "Main reason" claims are therefore only meaningful within one design.

### Claim the paper CAN make (draft):

> "Teachers who have actually left the profession rarely name pay as their single most important reason: in follow-up surveys of leavers in the United States, England and the Netherlands, pay ranks between fourth and sixth — cited by roughly 10–35% — well behind workload, stress, and personal or career factors. Dissatisfaction with pay does, however, move to or near the top of stated reasons when serving teachers are asked why they are *considering* leaving, and among early-career teachers and in low-paid segments and systems — so the stated–revealed contrast is sharpest, and safest to draw, for realized exits."

A one-sentence variant: *"Among teachers who actually leave, pay is rarely the top-stated reason (typically fourth to sixth, behind workload, stress and personal factors, in US, English and Dutch leaver surveys), although it ranks first or second in many intention-to-leave surveys and in low-pay contexts — whereas observed exit decisions respond strongly to pay variation."*

**Do not write:** "teachers rarely cite pay" without the leaver/considerer distinction — the RAND 2023, Michigan and Polish intention data flatly contradict the unqualified version.
