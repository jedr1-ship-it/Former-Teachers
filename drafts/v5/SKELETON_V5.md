# SKELETON V5 — binding contract for the three-section restructure

The lead author has ordered a restructure of the paper into THREE SECTIONS. The v4
narrative spine ("repair the deal"), the six threads, all verified facts, displays and
citations carry over; the ARCHITECTURE changes. OECD Policy Papers presentation: Key
messages up front, short sections, displays introduced by their conclusion.

## Section 1 — Introduction (~1,200 words; Intro + Section 2 must fit 6-7 pages ≈ 3,900 words combined)
Motivation and context, compressed from v4 ch1: shortage and ageing (verified figures),
governments' decades of active re-attraction policy, the reserve that is real but never
honestly counted, who former teachers are (three exit profiles), the paper's
contribution (compiling and analysing the international record) and its thesis:
campaigns returned dozens, repaired rules returned thousands; the state's own price of
return is the decisive policy. Roadmap sentence for the three sections.

## Section 2 — Why teachers leave, where they go, what they can bring back (~2,600 words)
From v4 ch2, recast around THE FOUR-CATEGORY MOTIVES TABLE (the lead author's taxonomy,
mapped to Nguyen et al. 2020's meta-analytic grouping):
1. Intrinsic (burnout, perceptions of the job) — weighs most under hard accountability climates.
2. Environmental (school conditions, workload/hours, poor leadership, collegial support)
   — weighs most where part-time markets are thin.
3. Economic (salary vs alternatives, pension design, contract duration) — weighs most
   where the pay gap with alternative careers is wide.
4. Life-cycle (family and care, youth exploring other careers, retirement) — transversal;
   defines the three exit profiles.
TABLE 2.1 columns: Category | Motive | What the evidence shows (citation) | Where it
weighs most | Policy family that addresses it (→ Section 3 letter). The last column is
the hinge to Section 3.
Then: WHERE THEY GO (linked-records evidence: most stay near education, licences
survive) and WHAT THEY CAN BRING BACK (peer-spillover/endogenous capability; the
defined-role tutoring evidence; P4 measurement gap intact). Purchase-of-time thesis
retained as the section's argument.

## Section 3 — The policies (~10,000-11,000 words; THE BODY)
PRIMARY AXIS: the instrument. Five families, ORDERED BY DOCUMENTED SCALE OF RETURN
(the hierarchy is the finding):
- A. Pension and employment rules that set the price of return (thousands moved):
  North Carolina, Georgia, New Mexico, Michigan, Virginia, Louisiana, Ontario, France,
  Austria crediting.
- B. Certification and licensing routes (cheap, least measured): New Zealand
  (fees paid, refresh tied to job offer), fast-track relicensing, Louisiana folding,
  licence-survival facts.
- C. Money attached to the return (hundreds at most; underspends): Netherlands (school
  subsidy), Portugal (indexation route), China (rural allowance — with its research
  learnings), Hesse supplements.
- D. Active recruitment and accompaniment (dozens to hundreds): England pilot and
  successor corrections, Austria outreach, NSW Teachers Re-Engage, advisers.
- E. Return as a defined role (the only family with measured student effects):
  tutoring trials (Number Rockets), Aurora mentoring, Japan re-employment, Bahamas
  reservists.
SECONDARY AXIS: beneficiary (the returner / the school / students / new teachers).
NAVIGATION MAP at the section's head: a table crossing family × beneficiary × flagship
example × headline learning, introduced by its conclusion.
PER-POLICY FICHE (in flowing prose, not bullets): what is done → published scale (only
where it exists, never forced) → learnings (the associated research where it exists:
Georgia audit, England evaluation, China research, NC study, Dutch evaluation).
CLOSING SUBSECTION (~1,500 words): "What the record teaches" — absorbs v4 ch4's
essentials as operational lessons: total and publish the price; publish the netted
count; advertise hours tied to vacancies; buy defined roles with evaluation attached;
report destination and survival; when zero is the right budget; the counting duty.
Existing displays redistribute: Tables 3.1, 3.2, Boxes 3.1, 3.2 into Section 3;
Table 4.1 into the closing subsection; Table 1.1 in Section 1 or dropped to fit pages;
ch2's tables stay in Section 2. Renumber displays to the new section numbers and fix
every cross-reference.
