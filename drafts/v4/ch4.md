# 4. Repairing the deal: lessons and recommendations for policy makers

The governments now asking their former teachers to come back are, for the most part, the governments whose own rules charge those teachers for returning. They ask, in part, because vacancies hurt. The record compiled here respects that motive, and it shows that the returns worth having came from repairing the deal rather than from the asking. The preceding chapters priced that charge and found its authors. It is written mostly in pension and civil-service law, mostly by accident, and no ministry has ever totalled it. The one mass return on record followed a rule change that lowered the price, with teachers coming back through ordinary hiring, while the campaigns that advertised over the price returned teachers by the dozen (Chapter 3). A state in this position does not need a recruitment drive. It needs to repair a deal its own laws broke. This chapter sets out the repairs in the order of the evidence behind them, which runs from strong to thin, and almost every one of them is cheap. Only the first requires powers an education ministry does not hold.

## 4.1. Repair the terms of return

**Total the bill your own state presents to a returner, and carry it to the departments that wrote it.** What a system charges a returner is a sum its own rules fix, and the salary step a returner is placed on is the dial that most plausibly separates the record's one mass return from its many token ones (Chapter 3). The difficulty is ownership. Most of the rules that set the price sit in pension and civil-service law, drafted by departments that never answer for teacher supply, so an education ministry can total the bill and publish it but usually cannot change it alone. That is an argument for the audit, not against it. A re-entry strategy drafted inside the education ministry alone is a strategy confined to the weakest dials in the system, and the audit is the instrument that carries the bill to the desks where the strongest ones are kept. No system examined in this paper has published one. Any of them could, in weeks rather than years.

**Publish an honest count of the people you could recall.** Every register examined in Chapter 1 already holds the fields such a count requires, and the specification is stated there. Each deduction moves the figure downward, so a netted series is an honest upper bound on the recallable population, while a headline total describes the rule of the register that produced it (Table 1.1). The OECD has observed that missing data on potential returners may limit systems' ability to use this channel strategically (OECD, 2024), and the remedy is administrative rather than scientific. A ministry that keeps quoting an unnetted figure is describing its register, and should say so.

**Sell the hours returners want to buy, and tie every funded offer to a named vacancy.** Former teachers bought shorter hours and more control when they left, and the segment most committed to returning wants the part-time posts schools rarely advertise (Buchanan et al., 2018; Worth and Sharp, 2018). The repair is a retail one. Put the hours in the advertisement. Release conversion support only against an identified vacancy, the correction England's successor programme made after its pilot (NCTL, 2017). Spare returners who already hold a job offer any refresh requirement, the design New Zealand reached independently (Teaching Council of Aotearoa New Zealand, 2026). The scarce product has been identified for years. Stocking it costs the price of an advertisement.

**Buy defined work at a specified dose, and write the evaluation into the contract of purchase.** The one deployment of former teachers that stated its protocol and its dose was measured at scale and worked; the role left to the practitioner's discretion was measured once, in a small trial that set no dose, and settled nothing (Gersten et al., 2015; DeCesare, McClelland and Randel, 2017; Chapter 2). A government that wants tutoring, induction support or mentoring from its former teachers can specify the role and the dose the way the tutoring trials did, and can make the evaluation a term of the purchase rather than a commission raised after the money is spent. Buying seniority and hoping is what the record mostly shows. It is also why the next review of this field would otherwise be written on the same absence of evidence as this one.

**Check who stayed, and price the programme on the years it kept.** A placement is the friendliest number a programme owns, and the least informative, because the goods being counted range from a casual day to a permanent post. The account that can carry a decision is cost per retained teacher-year, built from survival at one and three years and from where returners land, and the one cohort anyone could follow shows why. Close to a third of it was out of service again within four years, and the odds of leaving after the first year back sat well above the workforce average (Buchanan et al., 2018; see Chapter 3). The record's largest return episode moved returners modestly towards harder-to-staff schools, most often the ones they had left (Jarrold-Grapes and Mahler, 2024). Neither fact is visible in a placement count. Both belong in the price.

## 4.2. When the right expenditure is zero

**A repair schedule includes the discipline to spend nothing, and the record identifies three conditions under which zero, or the withdrawal of an instrument, is the defensible budget.**

The first is a loose entry pipeline. Where newly qualified teachers outnumber the openings available to them, premium-priced re-employment of retirees operates as a transfer from new entrants to pensioners, and the harm lands in the casual market where new careers begin. That is the configuration reporters found in Ontario before the rule was tightened, with the largest school boards spending over a hundred million Canadian dollars a year on retired supply teachers at close to twice the new-graduate day rate, in years when newly certified teachers outnumbered annual openings roughly two to one (Hammer and Alphonso, 2010; see Chapter 3). Commentary on the Chinese programmes points the same way, suggesting that some localities use retiree recruitment to defer creating formal posts, although this is secondary reporting rather than an official series (R.I.S.E. Magazine, 2025). Under this condition the right expenditure is negative, in the strict sense that withdrawing the instrument is the action the evidence supports. The dependence can also run ahead of any policy. In the Bahamas, the teachers' union reported retired educators already working full-time in supply roles before the reservist scheme of 2026 gave the practice a name, a union statement rather than an official count (Munnings, 2026).

The second is an absorption constraint. Where schools cannot place the returners they already attract, further recruitment spending buys a queue. In the Netherlands, regional partnerships reported receiving more applications from side-entrants and returners than schools could place, in the same window in which the national returner subsidy went underspent and had its annual ceilings lowered on extension (De Jong et al., 2022; Ministerie van OCW, 2019a). As Chapter 3 set out, the finding establishes the direction of the constraint rather than its size, and the direction alone is enough to move money from recruitment towards part-time posts, timetabling and hiring capacity.

The third is a population consisting only of retirees. A retiree pool is old and self-limiting. In the system where re-employment after retirement is the default staffing route, only about a third of age-based leavers from primary schools now seek full-time re-employment, and the one audited national intake peaked and fell within its own audit window (Sakuma and Shimazaki, 2024; Rechnungshof, 2025; see Chapter 3). Part of what reads as programme failure in this record is demography, and this paper does not attribute to design what demography may explain. A ministry can hold the channel open at low standing cost. What it should not do is fund an ageing stock as if it were a growth market.

None of the three conditions is permanent, and each is observable in data a ministry already holds. Zero is a budget line to be revisited, never a verdict on the population.

## 4.3. The clause that creates the evidence

**Write the duty to count into the instrument itself, and lodge it with a body that does not answer to the sponsor.** This is the one instruction in the chapter that is about measurement, and it earns the place because measurement is where the whole record was decided. Wherever a re-entry measure produced recurring numbers, somebody outside the sponsoring ministry held a standing duty to count; wherever nobody did, no numbers exist, however loudly the measure was announced. Evidence in this field is created at the drafting stage or not at all, and a minister who wants the next review to know more than this one can arrange it in a single clause.

Three instruments in this record produce audited or recurring numbers, and in each the obligation is owed elsewhere. In Georgia the statute wrote its own sunset and its own audit. In Austria the national court of audit counted on its own mandate. In New Mexico the pension board reports participation to a legislative committee every year. Instruments of the same family in several other systems sit inside pension administrations that hold exactly the same records and publish nothing (Table 4.1). What predicts being counted is an independent duty; riding a register, which every one of these instruments does, predicts nothing.

The consequence is a selection effect that deserves to be stated as a finding about the literature. The most rigorous products in this record, an independent programme evaluation, a mandated state audit and a court of audit report, all return negative or modest verdicts, while the measures that report success publish no evaluation at all. The one funnel published by a sponsoring department appeared in full only in a single media release six months into the programme (May 2024), with a one-line outcome in one annual report, and was gone from the next (NSW Government, 2024; NSW Department of Education, 2024; 2025). A synthesis of published evidence in this field is therefore biased downward, a synthesis of published claims biased upward, and nothing in the record permits a correction. Part of the scepticism re-entry attracts is an artefact of who was obliged to count. The same selection marks this paper's own architecture, since four of its propositions each rest on a single observation that is the only one of its kind, the English cost estimate, the Portuguese price test, the Ontario displacement investigation and the North Carolina pay experiment. That is exactly the literature a field in which evidence follows the counting duty should be expected to produce.

The objection writes itself, and it is partly right. Governments tend to audit what they already suspect, so the association between counting and unflattering verdicts cannot be read as an effect of counting. The claim this paper retains is narrower and proof against the objection, because it is a drafting instruction rather than a correlation. Place the count inside the standing duty of a body that does not answer to the sponsor, and where that cannot be arranged, expect no evidence and budget accordingly. The full loop of sunset, audit and informed renewal has closed exactly once in this record, in the statute that wrote its own audit (Box 3.2). The clause also enforces a distinction between two honest designs, provided the sunset falls while the count can still change the decision. An instrument justified as the removal of a barrier can be permanent and evaluated as regulation, while one justified as crisis cover can be explicitly temporary and counted. What no system should do is run a crisis instrument on annual renewal for half a decade while describing it as structural reform.

The place the duty is needed next is the entry-level supply market, because that is where any harm would show. Re-entry can be immaterial against a national workforce and decisive in the casual and supply market at the same time; those are different denominators, and every returner count needs to carry the denominator and the intensity of post it was measured against. The record makes the need concrete. The one wind-back it contains followed a newspaper investigation rather than an official series, the Ontario case of the previous section, and the rule in question is the only one whose amendment requires the consent of a body speaking for serving and future teachers as well as pensioners. That body has since refused a further loosening, restating that a pension plan exists to provide retirement income rather than to manage the labour market (Alphonso, 2024; OCEOTA, 2024; see Chapter 3). A higher day rate is partly what any seniority grid produces when a substitute carries decades of credited service, and a displacement reading assumes the boards would otherwise have hired graduates for those days. Neither side of that argument can currently be settled from a published series, which argues for the guardrail rather than against it. Education systems should therefore publish new-graduate hiring rates for their casual and supply pools alongside retiree re-employment counts, and treat movement in the first as the trigger for reviewing the second.

## 4.4. The measurements worth paying for

**The research agenda this paper hands on is a costed request, and its two largest items are cheap because the data already exist.** Governments have been trying to re-attract their former teachers for decades, and the compilation of that record in the preceding chapters is what lets the next review of this field start ahead of this one. The first item costs a definition and a query. Any register that holds a last-taught date, a certificate status, subject and phase, and age against pensionable age can publish a netted reserve series beside its headline, and none of the registers examined in this paper does. The claim that no publisher nets its series is the kind a single counter-example settles, and the paper would welcome the correction. Until it comes, every reserve figure in circulation describes a register rule.

The second carries the open question of Chapter 2. Linking returner records to the classes they teach and the colleagues they work beside is, in systems that maintain workforce censuses and pupil databases, a matter of administrative permission rather than research funding, and it would show whether what returners carry back reaches students and colleagues. Its complement is a trial of a designed role at a specified dose, powered for effects of the size the tutoring literature suggests are available, since the only role in this record that was specified and dosed is also the only one that was properly measured. Both measurements expose the paper's own premise to refutation, which is what makes them worth buying.

The remaining items are cheaper still, and each sits with a system that already holds the data. England can publish the cost of a retained returner teacher-year from records it already links (Buchanan et al., 2018). The annual Dutch reserve series now spans the years in which the national returner subsidy ran, so the field's first estimate of what share of a measured reserve an instrument activates is a desk exercise (DUO, 2026; Ministerie van OCW, 2017). Any oversubscribed programme can allocate its support by lottery among registrants, which costs nothing and yields the causal estimate this field has never produced. One natural test is already under way, since the renewal in Georgia devolved the designation of high-need areas to school systems while keeping the service threshold, and the coming counts will show what the central screen was doing (Teachers Retirement System of Georgia, 2026).

Former teachers will keep returning through whatever channel exists, because unprogrammed return is already a routine part of every measured inflow. What a government decides is the deal that meets them, and that deal is written in its own law, administered by its own registers and priced, so far, by nobody. What this paper asks of a ministry is modest. Total the bill and take it to its authors. Publish the honest count. Advertise the hours. Buy defined work and evaluate it at purchase. Follow the people, and be willing to spend nothing. None of this is a campaign, and almost all of it is cheap. The prize is larger than a staffed classroom. Experienced returners raise the quality of the teaching around them, as the evidence on learning from colleagues in Chapter 2 suggests, so a repaired deal recovers capability the system has already paid to build. The state wrote the terms on which its teachers left and the price they face on the way back. The deal is its own to repair.
