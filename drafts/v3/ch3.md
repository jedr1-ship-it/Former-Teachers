# 3. Re-entry policy in practice: international experience

Governments have been offering former teachers a job, in one form or another, for at least two decades. This chapter asks of every such offer the questions Chapter 2 prepared. Who was addressed, what exactly was on the table, and what price did the state's own rules attach to accepting it? The last question matters most, because the price of return is rarely written where the offer is. It sits in pension law, civil-service crediting rules and salary scales, and most of it was set by departments with no responsibility for teacher supply.

Read this way, the international record is less a gallery of programmes than a ledger of charges. The first section reads the programmes, asking of each who it addressed, what it offered and what verifiably happened. The second reads the rules, as four dials that together price a return, and asks who holds each dial. The two sections reach the same place from opposite ends. Interest from former teachers was rarely the scarce resource anywhere; what varied was whether anything behind the offer had actually changed.

## 3.1. Programmes: who was addressed, what was offered and what verifiably happened

**What re-entry programmes deliver is a destination and a survival rate, and they deliver volume only where a government changed an institution rather than a message.**

Return is already a standing channel before any programme touches it. Roughly 17 000 teachers return to English state schools in a year, about a third of all new entrants (Department for Education, 2023), and re-entrants have made up between roughly a third and a half of new hires in the United States (Sutcher, Darling-Hammond and Carver-Thomas, 2016). This unprogrammed flow, produced by standing rules and ordinary hiring, is the baseline against which every branded initiative has to be judged. A programme does not create the channel; it bids for a margin on top of it.

The programmes in this record addressed one of three populations, and the choice shaped the product more than the outcome. Some wrote to recent leavers held in the employer's own records, an approach that permits a personal offer because the system knows whom it is addressing. Some invited the whole register-visible qualified stock, which permits scale but not selection. Some addressed retirees through pension rolls, the population most systems can see even without a register. Each choice fixes what can credibly be offered; none of them, on this record, fixed how many came back.

What was offered falls into three families. The first is information and process, an adviser, a bundled re-licensure route, a single point of contact. The second is money, usually small and usually attached to something other than the returner's pay, a subsidy to the hiring school or an allowance against a named friction. The third is a redesigned role, tutoring or mentoring rather than a full class. What almost no programme offered was a change in the price of return itself, which stayed where the rules of the next section had set it.

What verifiably happened is assembled in Table 3.1, and its outcome definitions differ in every row, which is why this paper reproduces each count only with the intensity of the post and the denominator its publisher measured against. Within that discipline, one regularity survives. Wherever a full account exists, a programmed instrument moved a fraction of one per cent of its eligible pool in a year. The audited Austrian count is 1 062 returned retirees across six school years, in a workforce of roughly 123 000, with intakes that peaked and then fell (Rechnungshof, 2025). Near-universal outreach does not escape the band. With thirteen of sixteen German Länder actively recruiting pensioners, just over 5 000 were teaching nationwide, under 1% of the workforce, and the one Land that wrote to some 7 000 retirees in a school year attributes about 550 filled posts to its whole short-term package of retiree recruitment, service extensions and part-time top-ups, not to the letters alone (Deutsches Schulportal, 2023; Hessisches Ministerium für Kultus, Bildung und Chancen, 2024).

Two cases broke the band, and both did so by changing an institution. One state let retirees draw a full salary on top of a full pension and staffed permanent posts in the thousands over a decade (Box 3.1). One system makes re-employment after mandatory retirement the default staffing route, and in the prefecture studied in depth retirees supply roughly 60% of part-time adjunct hires (Sakuma and Shimazaki, 2024). The second case also shows the ceiling on any retiree-based channel, since only about 35% of age-based elementary retirees there now seek full-time re-employment, and every retiree pool ages on its own schedule. Where the addressable population is retirees, part of what reads as policy failure is a stock that is old and self-limiting, and this paper does not attribute to design what demography may explain.

Where a programme was independently evaluated, the binding constraints turned out to be operational, and they were named as such by the evaluators. In England, the one pilot in this record with a full independent evaluation, registrations ran to nearly double the target while placements ran to a small fraction of it, and the evaluation attributes the gap to the timing of support relative to the hiring cycle, to recruitment difficulties, to the suitability of some participants and to the scarcity of practical teaching opportunities in partner schools (Buchanan et al., 2018). Interest, in other words, was abundant throughout; what the programme could not manufacture was a vacancy at the moment the returner was ready. The successor programme drew the right conclusion and tied every funded place to a school with an identified vacancy (NCTL, 2017), which remains the most direct fix in the record for the most commonly documented failure.

Outreach, meanwhile, is a channel that transmits a changed offer rather than an instrument that creates one. The cheapest documented approach anywhere in this record is direct contact from the employer's own records with accreditation, approval to teach and background checks bundled into a single conversation, and in New South Wales that approach placed its returners into casual and temporary work within months (NSW Government, 2024). The letters went out weeks after a major pay settlement, and the department's standing page for returners still leads with the pay rise (NSW Department of Education, 2026), so the outreach is best read as the messenger of a repriced offer, and it is sold here on its cheapness, not on a conversion rate the evidence cannot establish. Its funnel was published once, in a May 2024 release, and does not appear in the following annual report (NSW Department of Education, 2025).

An instrument that pays for a stage downstream of the binding one is deadweight by construction. The Dutch returner subsidy illustrates the mechanism cleanly, because it paid school boards for the induction and guidance of returners they had already appointed, which is the stage after the constraint. The budget provided for 500 returners against a silent reserve estimated in the tens of thousands, the money went substantially unspent, the annual ceilings were lowered on extension explicitly because the budget was not being exhausted, and the scheme lapsed without a published evaluation (Ministerie van OCW, 2017; 2019a; Tweede Kamer, 2019). The regional successor schemes then recorded the constraint from the other side, with several regions reporting more applications from side-entrants and returners than schools could place (De Jong et al., 2022). The Dutch record supports the direction of an absorption constraint but not its magnitude, because the finding is a single self-reported row covering side-entrants and returners jointly, in a process evaluation of schemes spanning a different sector from the subsidy, and the regional partnerships hold the numbers that would settle it.

Money can also be pointed at a friction rather than at a preference, and the distinction separates two instruments that look alike. An allowance paid to a retiree for relocating to a named category of county, with housing and travel supplied in kind, compensates an itemisable cost of the specific service being bought; the Chinese silver-age schemes are built this way, conditioning an annual allowance of CNY 20 000 on service in poverty-lifted, ethnic-minority and border counties and attaching demonstration and mentoring duties to the role, though their cumulative recruit counts are self-reported and no independent audit exists (MOE and MOF, 2018; MOE et al., 2023; State Council, 2023). Money offered flat against a revealed preference for not returning is a different instrument, and Chapter 2 set out why the preference usually wins. The programmes that specified a role and a dose, the tutoring deployments of Chapter 2, are in turn the only ones in this record whose product was ever measured against a comparison group; specification is what converts a programme from an announcement into a measurable purchase.

Destination is the first of the two outcomes a programme can actually deliver, and the record on it is thin but consistent. Where the pension penalty was removed without any targeting mandate, returners went disproportionately to schools with more disadvantaged intakes, though the gradient was modest and more than half returned to the school they had left, so part of it is inherited attachment rather than steered movement (Jarrold-Grapes and Mahler, 2024; Box 3.1). Targeting by condition attaches money to the destination itself, as the Chinese county conditions do; targeting by narrowing came later elsewhere, as when the Portuguese government restricted the stay-on supplement paid under the same decree as its returner route to shortage zones, conceding that the blanket version "in many cases was not justified" (RTP, 2026). Targeting by prohibition is the one variant with an audited failure, examined in the next section, where screens meant to steer hires blocked them instead.

Survival is the second deliverable, and only one system can currently report it. Linked English data show 32% of one returner cohort out of service again four years later, with a further 2% retired, against 29% of the same year's newly qualified cohort, and the odds of a returner leaving after the first year back run 60% above average (Buchanan et al., 2018). The gap is small and the cost ranges overlap, so the finding does not price returners against novices. What it settles is the denominator, since a hire that does not survive its first year buys very little, and the honest unit of account for any programme in Table 3.1 is cost per retained teacher-year, at one and three years, rather than cost per placement.

The programmes, then, deliver what they were designed to touch, a contact, a stage of conversion, occasionally a destination, and their volumes stay inside a narrow band unless something institutional moves behind them. What moves behind them is the subject of the next section, and none of it is administered by the people who run the programmes.

## 3.2. Regulation: the four dials that price a return

**A system's re-entry policy is the sum of the charges its own rules levy on a returner, and the most consequential charge, the salary step a returner lands on, is set in most systems inadvertently, in pension and civil-service law, by departments that do not answer for teacher supply.**

Four dials do the pricing. The pay dial fixes the step a returner is placed on and whether pension and salary may be drawn together. The exclusion dial decides who may return at all, through service thresholds, waiting periods and subject or region screens. The intensity dial caps how much teaching may be sold before the pension reprices. The licence dial governs lapse, refresh and waiver. Table 3.2 sets out where each dial sits in thirteen systems, and the striking regularity of the table is jurisdictional, since almost none of these settings lives in education law.

**The pay dial is the one dial that separates the record's one mass return from its many small ones.** The clearest natural setting is Austrian, and it was never designed as re-entry policy, which is precisely the point. Federal law excludes from prior-service crediting any period that already generates a pension, so a teacher who retired as a civil servant returns on a full pension plus the profession's starting salary, while a former contract teacher, whose service is credited, broadly recovers pre-retirement pay (BMBWF, 2023). Two identical classroom careers thus attract offers most of a salary scale apart, on the basis of an employment status chosen decades earlier, because a crediting rule written for the whole federal service happens to fall on teachers too. In England the same dial is being moved by nobody at all, since abatement attaches only to final-salary retirement benefits and will dissolve of its own accord as career-average membership matures (Teachers' Pensions, n.d.).

One government wrote the low setting deliberately. The Portuguese returner route lets a teacher retired five years or less come back on a fixed-term contract, keeping the pension, with additional pay indexed to the first step of the career scale and pro-rated by teaching hours (Decreto-Lei n.º 51/2024, Art. 5). Sixty-three people accepted against a quota of 200 (Diário de Notícias, 2024; Renascença, 2024). The price cannot be read entirely cleanly, because the application window lasted twelve days, placements began in January, and the applicants averaged close to seventy years of age, so process and demography share the explanation with pay. What can be said is that the offer asked veterans to return at the price of novices, and very few did.

The opposite setting produced the only large-scale return in the record. Where retirees could hold a full-time post at full salary while drawing a full pension, on the order of half as much again as a regular full-time teacher's income, roughly 3 500 returned over a decade, more than half the schools in the sample hired at least one, and the returners averaged over thirty years of experience (Jarrold-Grapes and Mahler, 2024; Box 3.1). The mechanism that separates the settings is not mysterious. Teachers have been shown to value current cash at roughly five times deferred pension wealth (Fitzpatrick, 2015), so an offer that protects the pension while resetting current pay to the entry step withholds exactly the currency returners respond to, and preserves the one they discount.

**Box 3.1. Removing the pension-salary trade-off entirely: North Carolina's return-to-work window, 1999-2009**

Three cases carry the pay dial, and honesty requires saying what they cannot carry. They differ in pension regime, era, product and age profile as well as in the step a returner lands on, and nowhere in this record has the step been varied while anything else was held constant. The discriminating test exists and is cheap. The Austrian audit could disaggregate its returners by former employment status, comparing the cohort forced onto the starting salary with the cohort whose service was credited; even that comparison would price the pay rule together with everything else that distinguishes a career civil servant from a contract teacher, which is why a null result there would be more informative than a positive one.

**The exclusion dial has the corpus's cleanest measurement, because one legislature ordered an audit of its own screens.** Eligibility conditions are additive in law and multiplicative in effect: a service threshold, a mandatory break and a subject-and-region test must all be cleared, each is set independently of the others, and the population that survives all three can be a small fraction of the population any one of them would pass. In Georgia, where the rehire statute stacked a 30-year service requirement, a one-year break and a centrally determined list of the three highest-need subjects per region, full-time rehires stayed below one per cent of the workforce while 45% of 163 surveyed school systems reported at least one hire the statutory provisions had blocked (Georgia Department of Audits and Accounts, 2025). Box 3.2 traces the loop from statute to audit to renewal.

The audit is also the only employer-side counterfactual in this record, and what employers named as the obstacle was not money. The retirees they did rehire earned an average of USD 78 487, a veteran wage the systems were evidently willing to pay; what stopped further hires were the service rule, the waiting year and the subject methodology (Georgia Department of Audits and Accounts, 2025). The pay dial and the exclusion dial are therefore genuinely distinct charges, and a system can set one generously while the other quietly does the rationing.

Waiting periods, the most widespread exclusion, sit in pension-integrity law rather than education policy. The French six-month delay before a retired public servant may resume work with the last employer exists to stop employers re-hiring their own just-retired staff more cheaply (Assemblée nationale, 2020); the American waits, ninety days in New Mexico, six months in Michigan and Virginia after earlier longer periods, a year in Louisiana and Georgia, descend from tax-law tests of bona fide retirement (New Mexico Legislature, 2022; Michigan Office of Retirement Services, 2023; Commonwealth of Virginia, 2023; Teachers' Retirement System of Louisiana, 2024). Each guards a pension system's integrity, and each is, incidentally, a charge on teacher re-entry levied by a body with no teacher-supply mandate. The most recent movement on this dial is Georgia's renewal of its rehire law, which extended the programme to the end of the decade, kept the 30-year rule, and devolved the high-need determination to school systems (Teachers Retirement System of Georgia, 2026). The screen that was relaxed is the locally held one, the screen that stayed is the centrally set one, and the take-up series that follows will be the cleanest natural test of the exclusion dial the field is likely to get.

**The intensity dial has been moved in both directions by the same system, which makes it the best-documented dial in the record.** A day cap defines how much teaching a retiree may sell before the pension reprices, and it is a genuine two-way valve. The Ontario cap was raised from 20 to 95 days in a shortage, cut to a flat 50 from 2012 after a newspaper investigation documented heavy board spending on retired supply teachers at nearly twice the new-graduate day rate while newly certified teachers went short of assignments, restored for the pandemic years, and cut back when the teachers' federation that co-sponsors the pension plan refused a further extension (Hammer and Alphonso, 2010; Ontario Ministry of Education, 2022; OCEOTA, 2024; Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan, 2026). The refusal came with the record's most quotable statement of where this dial lives: "the Pension Plan is designed to provide an income to retired teachers; it is not a tool for addressing labour market challenges" (OCEOTA, 2024; Alphonso, 2024). The cap can be moved only by agreement of the plan's co-sponsors, one of which answers to serving and newly certified teachers as well as to pensioners.

What moving the cap achieved is, strikingly, not known. No post-liberalisation take-up figure for the pandemic increase was ever published; the number most often quoted against permissiveness is a union estimate of member interest released on the day the change was announced, which measures appetite for a forty-five-day increment rather than appetite for returning (Global News, 2022), and this paper declines to borrow it. The fuller lesson comes from the system that removed intensity limits altogether. When New Mexico opened a route with no salary or hours caps, the new route drew 154 users in its first year while 2 792 retirees in total were already working under the standing quarter-time, low-earnings and layout routes, against roughly 48 000 potentially eligible (New Mexico Educational Retirement Board, 2023). Permission is not price. Liberalising a cap moves nobody by itself, and unprogrammed re-employment under long-standing rules dwarfs the flagship new route it is meant to showcase.

The intensity dial is also where systems set their standing on-ramps, and here the record poses a choice rather than answering one. A statutory allowance of twenty half-days of relief teaching without a current certificate is permanent law in one system (New Zealand Legislation, 2020); the annual suspension of substitute-work limits for teachers on career break has been renewed year by year for what its own text calls the past number of school years in another (Department of Education and Youth, 2025); several of the American instruments carry sunsets. An instrument justified as barrier removal should be permanent and evaluated as regulation, while one justified as crisis cover should be explicitly sunset and counted; what no system should do is run a crisis instrument on annual renewal for five years while describing it as structural reform.

**The licence dial is the cheapest to move and, after moving, the least measured.** The scale of the barrier is register-visible, since close to half of the certified non-teachers in the one register that links certificates to employment held expired certificates, against a small minority of serving teachers (Lindsay, Gnedko-Berry and Wan, 2021). The designs on offer are sensible. One system ties its refresh requirement to the absence of a job offer, so a returner a school actually wants is never delayed by the rule, and currently pays practising-certificate fees on teachers' behalf (Teaching Council of Aotearoa New Zealand, 2026; New Zealand Ministry of Education, 2026); another fast-tracked lapsed licences by executive directive (Will, 2022); others folded relicensing into simplified working-after-retirement rules (Michigan Office of Retirement Services, 2022; Teachers' Retirement System of Louisiana, 2026). Yet no jurisdiction publishes take-up for a licensure waiver, so the dial most often recommended in policy commentary is the one whose effect the record cannot yet show, and claims for it should be made in the register of promise rather than of evidence.

The payload of the four dials is jurisdictional, and it can be stated as a single finding. Every dial that has demonstrably moved re-entry volume in this record sits outside the education ministry: the crediting rule in civil-service and pension law, pension-salary concurrency in state retirement statutes, the day cap with a pension plan's co-sponsors, the waiting period in social-security and tax law. A re-entry strategy written wholly inside an education ministry is therefore a strategy over the least powerful instruments in the system, which is a large part of why the programmes of Section 3.1 moved so little while costing so little.

What an education ministry can do, on its own authority and within a week, is audit the price. The charges a state levies on its own former teachers are a finite list that can be totalled, published, and taken to the departments that own each dial, beginning with the salary step; no country in this record has published such an audit, and any of them could. The next chapter turns that observation, together with the two outcomes a programme can genuinely deliver, into an ordered set of recommendations, and into the counting duty without which none of them can be judged.
