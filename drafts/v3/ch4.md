# 4. Lessons and recommendations for policymakers

What should a ministry do first, and in what order does the record support doing it? The preceding chapters have answered the questions that come before that one. A population a register can count, a price the state largely sets itself and an offer of hours rather than posts together define a function that a ministry can staff, cost and judge, and this chapter converts them into instructions. The order of the instructions is the order of the evidence behind them, which runs from strong to thin.

One property of the record governs everything that follows, and it is the first lesson rather than a caveat. Wherever a re-entry measure produces recurring numbers, somebody outside the sponsoring ministry held a standing duty to count; wherever nobody did, no numbers exist, however loudly the measure was announced. Evidence in this field is created at the drafting stage or not at all. A minister who wants the next review to know more than this one can arrange it in a single clause.

## 4.1. Why does the record look the way it does?

**Published evidence follows the counting duty, and in every instrument that has produced a recurring series the duty sits outside the sponsor.**

Three instruments in this record produce audited or recurring numbers, and in each the obligation is owed elsewhere. In Georgia, for example, the statute wrote its own sunset and its own audit; in Austria, the national court of audit counted on its own constitutional mandate; in New Mexico, the pension board reports participation to a legislative committee every year. Instruments of the same family in several other systems sit inside pension administrations that hold exactly the same records and publish nothing (Table 4.1). What predicts being counted is an independent duty; riding a register, which every one of these instruments does, predicts nothing.

The consequence is a selection effect that deserves to be stated as a finding about the literature rather than left as background noise. The most rigorous products in this record, an independent programme evaluation, a mandated state audit and a court of audit report, all return negative or modest verdicts, while the measures that report success publish no evaluation at all; the one funnel published by a sponsoring department appeared in a launch-week release and a single annual report and was gone from the next (NSW Government, 2024; NSW Department of Education, 2024; 2025). A synthesis of published evidence in this field is therefore biased downward, a synthesis of published claims biased upward, and nothing in the record permits a correction. Part of the scepticism re-entry attracts is an artefact of who was obliged to count.

The objection writes itself, and it is partly right. Governments tend to audit what they already suspect, so the association between counting and unflattering verdicts cannot be read as an effect of counting. The claim this paper retains is narrower and proof against the objection, because it is a drafting instruction rather than a correlation. Policy makers can place the count inside the standing duty of a body that does not answer to the sponsor, and where that cannot be arranged they should expect no evidence and budget accordingly. The loop of sunset, audit and informed renewal has closed exactly once in this record (Box 4.1).

:::box
**Box 4.1. A counting duty that closed the loop: the return-to-work audit in Georgia (United States)**

House Bill 385 of 2022, which admits retired teachers to full-time posts on full pension in designated high-need areas, wrote both a sunset and a performance audit into its own text. The audit appeared on schedule (Report No. 24-20, April 2025). It counted 635 full-time rehires across fiscal years 2023 to 2025, about 350 in any one year against a workforce of some 125 000 and roughly 6 000 vacant or not-fully-certified posts, and it surveyed employers: 45% of 163 responding school systems reported a hire the statutory screens had prevented, naming the thirty-year service rule, the one-year break and the centrally determined high-need methodology as the main barriers. Sunset, audit and informed renewal closed exactly once in the record this paper assembles, and this is where.

The place the duty is needed next is the entry-level supply market, because that is where any harm would show. Re-entry can be immaterial against a national workforce and decisive in the casual and supply market at the same time; those are different denominators, and every returner count must carry the denominator and the intensity of post it was measured against. The one wind-back in the record followed a newspaper investigation rather than an official series. In Ontario, reporters found the largest school boards spending over a hundred million Canadian dollars a year on retired supply teachers at close to twice the new-graduate day rate, in years when newly certified teachers outnumbered annual openings roughly two to one (Hammer and Alphonso, 2010); the rule is also the only one in this record whose amendment requires the consent of a body speaking for serving and future teachers as well as pensioners, and that body has since refused a further loosening on the ground that a pension plan "is designed to provide an income to retired teachers; it is not a tool for addressing labour market challenges" (Alphonso, 2024; OCEOTA, 2024).

A higher day rate is partly what any seniority grid produces when a substitute carries decades of credited service, and a displacement reading assumes the boards would otherwise have hired graduates for those days. Neither side of that argument can currently be settled from a published series, which argues for the guardrail rather than against it. Education systems should publish new-graduate hiring rates for their casual and supply pools alongside retiree re-employment counts, and treat movement in the first as the trigger for reviewing the second.

## 4.2. What policy makers can do now

**The record supports a sequence of six actions, ordered by the strength of the evidence behind them, and all but one lie within powers education ministries already hold.**

**Publish the reserve as a netted series, and quote no figure that is not.** The specification is stated in Chapter 1, and every register examined there already holds the fields it requires. Each deduction moves the count downward, so the netted series is an honest upper bound on the population while a headline total describes the rule of the register that produced it (Table 1.1). The OECD has identified missing data on potential returners as what limits systems' ability to use this channel strategically (OECD, 2024), and the remedy is administrative rather than scientific. A ministry that keeps quoting an unnetted figure is describing its register, and needs to say so.

**Audit the price of return, beginning with the salary step.** What a system charges a returner is a sum its own rules fix, and the step a returner is placed on is the dial that most plausibly separates the record's one mass return from its many token ones (Chapter 3). Most of the relevant rules are written in pension and civil-service law by departments that do not answer for teacher supply, so an education ministry can total and publish the price but usually cannot change it alone; a re-entry strategy drafted without the pensions and finance departments is a strategy over the weakest instruments in the system. No system in this record has published such an audit. Any of them could, in weeks rather than years.

**Advertise hours, and tie funded support to a named vacancy.** Former teachers bought shorter hours and more control when they left, and the segment most committed to returning wants the part-time posts schools rarely advertise (Buchanan et al., 2018; Worth and Sharp, 2018). Education systems can put the hours in the advertisement, can release conversion support only against an identified vacancy, the correction England's successor programme made after its pilot (NCTL, 2017), and can spare returners who already hold a job offer any refresh requirement, the design New Zealand reached independently (Teaching Council of Aotearoa New Zealand, 2026). The scarce product has been identified for years; stocking it costs the price of an advertisement.

**Buy defined roles at a specified dose, and attach the measurement at purchase.** The deployment that stated its protocol and dose was measured at scale and worked, while the role left to the practitioner's discretion was measured once, in a small trial that set no dose, and settled nothing (Gersten et al., 2015; DeCesare, McClelland and Randel, 2017; Chapter 2). Education systems that want tutoring, induction support or mentoring from former teachers can specify the role and the dose the way the tutoring trials did, and can write the evaluation into the contract rather than commissioning it afterwards. Buying seniority and hoping is what the record mostly shows; it is also why the next review of this field would otherwise be written on the same absence of evidence as this one.

**Write the count into the instrument, and match the sunset to the decision it serves.** The duty belongs, as the previous section argues, with a body that does not answer to the sponsor, and the sunset needs to fall while the count can still change the decision. The count also enforces a distinction between two honest designs. An instrument justified as the removal of a barrier can be permanent and evaluated as regulation, while one justified as crisis cover can be explicitly temporary and counted; what no system should do is run a crisis instrument on annual renewal for half a decade while describing it as structural reform.

**Report destination and survival, never placements alone.** A placement is the cheapest outcome a programme can report and the least informative, because the goods being counted range from a casual day to a permanent post. The account that supports decisions is cost per retained teacher-year, built from survival at one and three years and from where returners land. Where linked data exist they change the picture: close to a third of one returner cohort was out of service again within four years, with the odds of leaving after the first year back well above the workforce average (Buchanan et al., 2018), and the record's largest return episode moved returners modestly towards harder-to-staff schools, most often the ones they had left (Jarrold-Grapes and Mahler, 2024). Neither fact is visible in a placement count, and both belong in the price.

## 4.3. When is the right expenditure zero?

**A standing re-entry function includes the discipline to spend nothing, and the record identifies three conditions under which zero, or the withdrawal of an instrument, is the defensible budget.**

The first condition is a loose entry pipeline. Where newly qualified teachers outnumber the openings available to them, premium-priced re-employment of retirees operates as a transfer from new entrants to pensioners, and the harm concentrates in the casual market where new careers begin; that is the configuration the Ontario investigation documented before the rule was tightened (Hammer and Alphonso, 2010). Commentary on the Chinese programmes points the same way, suggesting that some localities use retiree recruitment to defer creating formal posts, although this is secondary reporting rather than an official series (R.I.S.E. Magazine, 2025). Under this condition the right expenditure is negative, in the strict sense that withdrawing the instrument is the action the evidence supports.

The second condition is an absorption constraint. Where schools cannot place the returners they already attract, further recruitment spending buys a queue. In the Netherlands, regional partnerships reported receiving more applications from side-entrants and returners than schools could place, in the same window in which the national returner subsidy went underspent and had its ceilings lowered twice (De Jong et al., 2022; Ministerie van OCW, 2019a). The finding is self-reported and does not separate returners from side-entrants, so it establishes the direction of the constraint rather than its size; the direction alone is enough to move money from recruitment towards part-time posts, timetabling and hiring capacity.

The third condition is a population consisting only of retirees. A retiree pool is old and self-limiting: in the system where re-employment after retirement is the default staffing route, only about a third of age-based leavers from primary schools now seek full-time re-employment, and the one audited national intake peaked and fell within its own audit window (Sakuma and Shimazaki, 2024; Rechnungshof, 2025). Part of what reads as programme failure in this record is demography, and this paper does not attribute to design what demography may explain. A ministry can hold the channel open at low standing cost; what it should not do is fund an ageing stock as if it were a growth market.

None of the three conditions is permanent, and each is observable in data a ministry already holds. Zero is a budget line to be revisited, not a verdict on the population.

## 4.4. The measurements worth paying for

**The research agenda this paper hands on is a costed request, and its two largest items are cheap because the data already exist.**

The first request costs a definition and a query. Any register that holds a last-taught date, a certificate status, subject and phase, and age against pensionable age can publish a netted reserve series beside its headline, and none of the registers examined in this paper does. The claim that no publisher nets its series is the kind a single counter-example settles, and the paper would welcome the correction; until it comes, every reserve figure in circulation describes a register rule.

The second request carries the open question of Chapter 2. Linking returner records to the classes they teach and the colleagues they work beside is, in the systems that maintain workforce censuses and pupil databases, a matter of administrative permission rather than research funding, and it would show whether what returners carry back reaches students and colleagues. Its complement is a trial of a designed role at a specified dose, powered for effects of the size the tutoring literature suggests are available, since the only role in this record that was specified and dosed is also the only one that was properly measured. Both measurements expose the paper's own premise to refutation, which is what makes them worth buying.

The remaining items are cheaper still, and each sits with a system that already holds the data. England can publish the cost of a retained returner teacher-year from records it already links (Buchanan et al., 2018); the annual Dutch reserve series now spans the years in which the national returner subsidy ran, so the field's first estimate of what share of a measured reserve an instrument activates is a desk exercise (DUO, 2026; Ministerie van OCW, 2017); and any oversubscribed programme can allocate its support by lottery among registrants, which costs nothing and yields the causal estimate this field has never produced. One natural test is already under way, since the renewal in Georgia devolved the designation of high-need areas to school systems while keeping the service threshold, and the coming counts will show what the central screen was doing (Teachers Retirement System of Georgia, 2026). The field's next paper can be written on measurements rather than announcements, and every instrument for making them is already in a ministry's hands.

Former teachers will keep returning through whatever channel exists, because unprogrammed return is already a routine part of every measured inflow. What policy makers can decide is whether that channel runs priced and counted, under a duty that outlasts the announcement. The measurements that would make it so are the cheapest this paper has asked anyone to make, and the population they describe is one the state itself qualified and can now count.
