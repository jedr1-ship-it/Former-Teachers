### England — Return to Teaching pilot (2015-16), Returners Engagement Programme (2016-18), Return to Teaching Adviser service (2015-present)

**CONTEXT.** The NCTL established the Return to Teaching pilot in 2015 to help secondary schools with recruitment shortfalls in EBacc subjects attract qualified teachers not teaching in the English public sector. The evaluation frames the policy problem precisely: the number of qualified teachers out of service aged under 60 rose from 16,010 in 2011 to 17,230 in 2016, against 243,900 qualified out-of-service teachers under 60 in total (VERIFIED — NFER report, lines 531-534 of the PDF text; https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5b33aeef40f0b67f6744de9d/Evaluation_of_the_Return_to_Teaching_pilot_programme.pdf). The RTT pilot ran in parallel with a second, separate instrument inside the Maths and Physics Teacher Supply Package: the Return to Teaching Adviser (RTTA) service, which is one-to-one advice rather than school-based support (VERIFIED — DfE research brief, March 2017).

**DESIGN.**
- *Pilot (2015-16).* Application rounds September-December 2015; in-school support January-October 2016 (extended in March 2016 from end-March to mid-July, with autumn-term continuation). Grant-funded partnerships led by School Direct lead schools; **60 schools took part**. No payments to returners. Judged against the November 2016 School Workforce Census (VERIFIED — NFER report, lines 217-229, 625-632; gov.uk guidance "supporting returning teachers").
- *Targets.* ~3,000 registrations of interest, of whom ~650 would receive support and return to an EBacc subject on a permanent contract in a state-funded secondary by autumn 2016 (VERIFIED — NFER, lines 334-338). **This target is absent from our fichas and is the sharpest available statement of what the scheme promised.**
- *Successor — Returners Engagement Programme.* Published 18 November 2016, updated 27 January 2017, **withdrawn 14 March 2018**; restricted to the North West and South East and to maths, physics and MFL. Payments: £2,500 to the lead school for retraining, £1,500 on employment of the returner, a further £1,500 in the South East only for part-time/flexible placements (up to £5,000); and, to returners themselves, **up to £600 for a month-long course and £150 a week toward childcare/time off**. Two intakes, February and May 2017; 2-4 weeks of training before an employment offer (VERIFIED — https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/returners-engagement-programme; Schools Week, 27 November 2016, https://schoolsweek.co.uk/schools-can-get-5k-for-getting-returner-teachers-back-into-classrooms/).
- *RTTA service.* Free one-to-one advice from a named adviser: applications, personal statements, interview preparation, signposting to refresher courses (including Teacher Subject Specialism Training) and vacancies, themed webinars, a returners' Facebook group; contact by phone, text or email at the returner's chosen frequency. Originally maths/physics/MFL only; now open to anyone who previously taught or trained to teach in the UK, primary or any secondary subject, plus QTS holders with a term of paid UK experience and a TRN (VERIFIED — https://teaching-vacancies.service.gov.uk/jobseeker-guides/return-to-teaching-in-england/return-to-teaching, page last updated 28 August 2025).

**TAKE-UP AND CONVERSION.** The full funnel, with denominators, is now longer than the one in our fichas:

| Stage | Number | Denominator/definition |
|---|---|---|
| Registrations of interest on the RTT website | 5,729 (by summer 2016) | vs target 3,000 — 191% of target |
| Applied for a pilot place by end-March 2016 | ~574 applicants | Schools Week FOI |
| Recruited / commenced training | 426 (FOI Feb 2017) / 428 "trained" (Schools Week) / **412 started training** (NCTL cost data used by NFER) | three sponsor-sourced counts that do not agree |
| Actually supported Jan-Sep 2016 | **354** | net of withdrawals and non-starters (72 withdrew or failed to start) |
| In any state-funded teaching post, Nov 2016 | **62 (18%)** | of 354 supported |
| In a permanent EBacc post in a state-funded secondary, Nov 2016 | **27 (8%)** | of 354 supported; vs target 650 (4% of target) |
| Comparison: non-supported registrants who returned to the state sector anyway | **~4%** | NFER's own comparison, explicitly *not* a valid additionality estimate |

(VERIFIED — NFER report lines 354-372, 789-818, 2239-2244; Schools Week, 30 December 2016 and 28 June 2018.)

**Cost.** Grant payments to schools £586,900; **average grant cost per participant £1,425** (over the 412 who started training). Cost per supported returner employed in a permanent EBacc post £17,800. Cost per *additional* returner, under scenarios spanning 1-8 percentage points of additionality: **£142,500 (1pp) … £37,400 (4pp, i.e. 50% deadweight) … £17,800 (8pp, zero deadweight)** (VERIFIED — NFER Table 34, lines 4063-4076). Comparator: average cost of ITT per trainee still teaching after one year, £33,300-£46,900 (Allen et al., 2016, as cited).

**RTTA conversion, 2015/16 (NEW, and it changes the picture).** Two RTTAs in post in 2015/16 **supported 541 eligible returners; 63 of them had secured teaching jobs in state-funded schools by end-September 2016, above the strand's target of 50 — a conversion rate of 11.6%** (VERIFIED — Straw et al., DfE research brief DFE-RB683, March 2017, pp. 7-8: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/624770/Maths_and_physics_teacher_supply_package-brief.pdf).
**RTTA cost and recorded returns, 2019-21.** £361,344.69 (2019/20) and £325,562.73 (2020/21); returns recorded in the three eligible subjects: maths 25 then 24, physics 8 then 15, MFL 29 then 27 — 128 over two years, ≈£5,366 per recorded return. The Minister's answer states explicitly that returners to other subjects and unreported returns are not captured (VERIFIED — Munira Wilson to Jonathan Gullis, W/A 54668, answered 2022, mirrored at https://www.theyworkforyou.com/wrans/?id=2022-09-22.54668.h).

**EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION.**
- *Pilot.* Independent evaluation by NFER for DfE, published 28 June 2018 (Buchanan, Sharp, Worth, Martin, Bamford, Des Clayes). Mixed method: survey of 577 non-supported registrants (44% of a 1,300 sample, with an embedded incentives experiment), survey of supported returners (818 respondents), interviews with returners and school coordinators, and School Workforce Census / Database of Teacher Records linkage. **No valid counterfactual**: NFER states the supported/non-supported comparison "is likely to over-estimate the impact" because supported returners were already more certain of returning. Verdict: "unlikely to represent good value for money", with the failure attributed to *timing* (few posts available for those supported after May), *continuing support needs*, *suitability of some participants*, and *lack of part-time work* (VERIFIED).
- *Retention.* Of returners who returned to a secondary school in 2011, 32% were out of service four years later plus 2% retired, against 29% of the 2011 NQT cohort; and DfE (2017c) put the odds of a returner leaving after their first year back at 60% above average (VERIFIED — NFER lines 2288-2296).
- *Age gradient of spontaneous return (NEW and highly usable).* NFER Table 17, from DTR/SWC linkage: annual return rate of qualified out-of-service secondary teachers in 2015 was **14-15% at ages 20-29, 8-10% at 30-39, 5-6% at 40-49 and 3% at 50-59**, with almost no male/female difference within bands (VERIFIED — NFER lines 2214-2231). This is the base rate any outreach scheme must beat, and it collapses with age.
- *Group with the best odds.* Career breakers (chiefly child-rearing) had double the return rate of other returner groups, and NFER recommends targeting them with part-time offers (VERIFIED — NFER lines 383-386, 3670-3676).
- *RTTA.* Process evaluation only (NFER + Sheffield Hallam Institute of Education, commissioned March 2016 by DfE and NCTL). One adviser and ten participants interviewed; participants praised prompt contact and careers advice but "struggled to obtain classroom experience or mentoring support from schools"; one of ten had returned to a permanent post, two to temporary posts. Recommendations: provide placements, integrate classroom experience and a reference into courses, add a brokering role with schools, and manage expectations in marketing (VERIFIED — Straw et al., 2017, pp. 7-8).
- **CORRECTION to fichas:** "No independent evaluation of the RTTA has been published" is wrong. A DfE-commissioned, independently authored process evaluation of the RTTA strand exists (Straw et al., 2017) and supplies the only published adviser-service conversion rate (63/541 = 11.6%). It is a process, not impact, evaluation, and it covers only 2015/16 — but it is an evaluation.
- **NOT FOUND:** any evaluation of the Returners Engagement Programme (2016-18); any additionality estimate for the RTTA at any date; any published RTTA conversion figure after 2020/21.

**STATUS 2026.** The RTTA service is live and unrestricted by subject or phase. The Government's *6,500 additional teachers delivery plan*, published **27 April 2026**, lists the returner offer among its measures: a step-by-step guide supplementing the advisory service, content aimed at parents returning after an extended break, and extension of the service to teachers moving into further education. Context figure quoted in that plan: **17,274 teachers returned to the classroom, the highest in a decade** (VERIFIED — https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/every-child-achieving-and-thriving/6500-additional-teachers-delivery-plan-html-version). The underlying series: 16,985 returners in 2023-24 and 17,274 in 2024-25, the highest return rate since 2014-15, out of ~41,700 joiners (VERIFIED — Schools Week, 5 June 2025). The November 2025 census release (published 4 June 2026) reports ~41,000 FTE joiners, 1 in 11 of all qualified teachers, with returners continuing to rise since 2021/22 and NQT entrants falling to 16,300 (40% of entrants) (VERIFIED — https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/school-workforce-in-england-november-2025).

**CONTESTATION.** Schools Week ran the evaluation as "Return to Teaching pilot was a flop, new data reveals" (28 June 2018) and, earlier, "Pilot to return teachers into classrooms failed to fill chemistry and physics spaces" (30 December 2016), reporting from FOI that no lead school came close to its 40-place cap, that only three schools exceeded 20 enrolments, and a lopsided subject distribution — English 106, languages 100, computer science 27, physics 18, chemistry 17 — in a programme sold as a science-and-maths fix (VERIFIED).

**WHAT IT CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** (i) That England is the only system with a published, audited, end-to-end returner funnel *and* a cost-per-additional-returner range, and that the range straddles and often exceeds the cost of training a new teacher; (ii) that interest is abundant (191% of registration target) while conversion is not (4% of the placement target); (iii) that the base rate of spontaneous return falls from ~15% a year at 20-29 to 3% at 50-59, so schemes aimed at older leavers are fishing in a pool that barely moves; (iv) that a low-cost adviser service converted 11.6% of eligible supported returners in its first year, an order of magnitude better than the pilot's 8% on permanent EBacc posts and at roughly a third of the per-head grant, though on a looser outcome definition (any state-funded teaching job); (v) that England has kept the cheap instrument (advisers) and dropped the expensive ones (grants to schools), which is the honest policy lesson.

**WHAT IT CANNOT SUPPORT.** (i) Any additionality claim for the RTTA — none has ever been estimated; (ii) reading £37,400 as *the* cost per additional returner (it is the mid-scenario of a range running to £142,500); (iii) attributing the rise in returners to 17,274 to any programme — the delivery plan asserts no such link and the DfE cannot see returns that go unreported to advisers; (iv) treating 426/428/412/354 as interchangeable counts of "returners supported".

### Austria — Bildungsdirektion outreach to retirees (Vienna, Styria, Salzburg, Vorarlberg, Upper Austria, Tyrol)

**CONTEXT.** No national programme, no legal instrument: nine regional directorates acting ad hoc under the federal *Klasse Job* strategy launched in autumn 2022. The Court of Audit is blunt that the ministry had forecast a shortage from 2018/19 as early as 2009 and set no systematic counter-measures until *Klasse Job*, bridging the gap instead with overtime, out-of-field teaching and *Sonderverträge* for people without completed teacher education — a practice it criticises for lack of sustainability (VERIFIED — Rechnungshof, Bericht 2025/18 *Lehrpersonaleinsatz*, published 23 May 2025, pp. 7, 24-25: https://www.rechnungshof.gv.at/rh/home/home/2025_18_Lehrpersonaleinsatz.pdf).

**DESIGN.** Verified regional detail (APA/ORF, 11 March 2023):
- **Vienna** wrote to ~300 already-retired teachers in December 2022 and instructed principals to speak to staff nearing retirement.
- **Styria** instructed school leaders to ask retirement-age teachers about continuing, including at reduced hours, and contacted retirees; "gar nicht so wenige" agreed.
- **Salzburg** wrote to all retirees up to age 67, adding transport job tickets and higher premiums, and flagged prior-service crediting (*Vordienstzeiten*) as a problem requiring federal legislation.
- **Vorarlberg** has recruited retirees "for some time"; a project post created a year earlier informs principals about re-employment options, and re-entry information goes out with the retirement paperwork.
- **Upper Austria** reported 36 retirees in classrooms and no new measures; **Tyrol** contacted retirees only during the Ukraine influx and had 10 active.
(VERIFIED — https://oesterreich.orf.at/stories/3198263/ and https://www.salzburg24.at/news/oesterreich/wie-pensionisten-den-lehrermangel-verringern-sollen-135350821.)

**TAKE-UP AND CONVERSION — the gap our fichas flags is now closed, twice over.**
1. **Vienna: ~300 letters (December 2022) → 3 returns.** The directorate then changed tack at the end of February 2023, instructing principals to check individually whether staff could be kept beyond 65: 46 teachers deferred their pension the previous year and 20 more had done so by late March 2023. The same recall campaign in Vienna's hospitals produced 23 returns in 2021 and 3 the following year; the transport operator recalled 6 of ~40 contacted (VERIFIED — wien.ORF.at, "Pensionisten kehren kaum in Job zurück", 29 March 2023, https://wien.orf.at/stories/3200562/).
2. **Nationwide: more than 3,000 retired teachers received the 2022 letter; around 45 accepted** — i.e. roughly 1.5% (VERIFIED via the article's indexed text — Resch, K. (2025), "Retired teachers return to school to tackle teacher shortages in Austrian schools. An untapped reserve or emergency response?", *European Journal of Teacher Education*, published online July 2025, https://doi.org/10.1080/02619768.2025.2531930. **Caveat: the full text returned HTTP 403; the two figures were corroborated from two independent index snapshots of the article body, and should be re-checked against the PDF before print.**)
3. **Audited stock: 1,062 teachers already in pension or retirement resumed teaching duties across school years 2018/19-2023/24; Upper Austria 227, Tyrol 49; concentrated in 2021/22 and 2022/23, "dieser Trend setzte sich nicht fort".** The Court of Audit credits the ministry and directorates with the 1,062 but warns of "den vorübergehenden Charakter dieser Maßnahme" (VERIFIED — Rechnungshof 2025/18, TZ 2, pp. 24-25, German text confirmed verbatim).
4. **March 2023 snapshot (different definitions):** ~72 retirees teaching nationwide — 42 in compulsory schools and ~30 in federal schools — against ~123,000 teachers.

**DISCREPANCY (must be resolved before print).** Two readings of the same APA/ORF wire disagree on whether the number 42 refers to *Vorarlberg's* retirees in service or to *all compulsory schools nationwide*. One rendering gives "Vorarlberg: 42 pensionierte Lehrpersonen im Einsatz"; the other gives "42 in compulsory schools nationwide plus ~30 at federal schools". Our fichas uses the second. Given that Upper Austria is separately reported at 36 and Tyrol at 10, the nationwide-42 reading is arithmetically doubtful. **Recommendation: re-read the German original and, if unresolved, drop the 42/30 snapshot and rely on the audited 1,062 series, which is the stronger number anyway.**

**EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION.** No impact evaluation of any Austrian outreach exists. The only research is Resch's qualitative study (nine interviews with retired teachers, six returners and three non-returners, embedded in a wider 2024-25 study of teacher shortages in Upper Austria; a companion count of 14 interviews including experts appears in the abstract), which frames post-retirement return as a biographical decision shaped by individual, family, school and system factors, with continuing contact with the former school the key facilitator. The Court of Audit's contribution is arithmetic, not causal: it counts, and it warns about durability.

**STATUS 2026.** Pressure easing but not gone: for 2026/27 more than 6,500 teaching posts were advertised nationally, ~2,100 in Vienna alone; directorates reported a 17% rise in applicants for autumn 2025 and residual gaps in special education, languages and creative subjects, filled where necessary with student teachers, career-changers and overtime — retirees are not mentioned as a current lever (VERIFIED — vienna.at, "Teacher Shortage: Situation Eases According to Education Directorates", August 2025, https://www.vienna.at/teacher-shortage-situation-eases-according-to-education-directorates/9595358; BMB main advertisement round 2026/27, https://www.bmb.gv.at/service/juk/ausschr/hauptausschreibung_2026.html).

**CONTESTATION.** The Rechnungshof itself is the contestation: it treats retiree re-employment as a stop-gap and recommends the ministry and directorates pursue *Klasse Job* systematically so that need is met "rechtzeitig und zielgerichtet" instead.

**WHAT IT CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** That Austria is now the best-documented failure of mass personalised outreach in the corpus: **>3,000 letters → ~45 acceptances nationally (~1.5%), and 300 letters → 3 returns in Vienna (1%)**, while the audited six-year total of returns (1,062) both dwarfs the letter yield and peaks in the two years of maximum emergency rather than of maximum mailing. That the instrument that visibly moved people in Vienna was not the letter but the principal's individual conversation about deferring retirement (46 + 20 deferrals versus 3 returns).

**WHAT IT CANNOT SUPPORT.** Attributing the 1,062 to the letters (the series begins four years before them). Any pay-effect claim: the *Vordienstzeiten* rule is documented, its behavioural effect is not. Any claim that Austria has stopped: the directorates simply moved from mailing to individual retention conversations.

### Germany — Hesse's 7,000 letters inside a 13-of-16-Länder pattern (plus Lower Saxony's pre-retirement mailing)

**CONTEXT.** No register of qualified teachers outside schools; 457,000 retired or pensioned teachers (2021) against a workforce of ~800,000. Deutsches Schulportal's exclusive survey of all sixteen ministries (published 2 August 2023) found **13 of 16 Länder actively approaching retired or near-retirement teachers**; Brandenburg, Rhineland-Palatinate and Saarland do not (VERIFIED — https://deutsches-schulportal.de/bildungswesen/laenderueberblick-umfrage-lehrermangel-pensionierte-lehrer/).

**DESIGN.** Hesse wrote to ~7,000 retired teachers in 2022/23 (retirees have been contacted since 2020). The standard instrument set across Länder: raised earnings thresholds before pension deduction, ~10% non-pensionable supplements, reduced teaching loads with equivalent pension credit, streamlined hiring, service-time crediting — and **no Land imposes an age ceiling on employed retirees** (VERIFIED — Schulportal, 2 August 2023). Lower Saxony runs the pre-retirement variant: the education minister writes personally to teachers shortly before retirement setting out two options — immediate re-hire on a temporary collective-agreement contract (open to both civil servants and salaried staff) or voluntary deferral of retirement by up to three years on timely request (VERIFIED — Niedersächsisches Kultusministerium, "Umfangreicher Erfahrungsschatz", https://www.mk.niedersachsen.de/startseite/aktuelles/kultusministerin_hamburg_schreibt_an_angehende_pensionare/umfangreicher-erfahrungsschatz-kultusministerin-hamburg-informiert-lehrkrafte-kurz-vor-dem-ruhestand-uber-moglichkeiten-der-weiterbeschaftigung-227231.html — **no date recoverable from the page; do not date-cite it**).

**TAKE-UP AND CONVERSION.**
- Nationwide: **just over 5,000 retirees teaching, under 1% of the ~800,000 workforce** (Länder self-reports compiled by Schulportal, 2023).
- **Hesse: ~7,000 contacted → "typically up to 200 posts" gained a year** — i.e. an implied conversion of roughly 3% (VERIFIED — Schulportal, 2 August 2023). **This closes the second half of our fichas' "no letters-to-returns rate is published anywhere" gap.**
- Hesse 2024/25: **around 200 retired teachers returned to school service** at the start of the year, alongside service extensions, a 50% expansion of primary teacher-education places, financial incentives for MINT and health-science master's students, and lateral entry (VERIFIED — fuldainfo, 4 October 2024, reporting the Hessisches Kultusministerium, https://www.fuldainfo.de/hessen-hat-200-pensionierte-lehrkraefte-fuer-den-schuldienst-gewonnen/).
- Hesse trend by age: teachers aged 66+ in Hessian primary schools rose from 130 (2018) to 282 (2024), with parallel rises in comprehensive, special and grammar schools; ~67,000 teachers in Hesse in total (VERIFIED — news4teachers, 7 April 2025, reporting Minister Armin Schwarz's answers to Green Party questions, https://www.news4teachers.de/2025/04/immer-mehr-eigentlich-pensionierte-lehrer-arbeiten-im-schuldienst/).
- Per-Land counts (2022/23, Schulportal): NRW teachers aged 66+ **1,228**; Schleswig-Holstein 390; Bavaria ~690 (October 2022); Thuringia ~220; Baden-Württemberg 531; Berlin ~400; Hamburg 285.
  **DISCREPANCY:** our fichas gives NRW as "1,304 past pension age (2022)". The retrieved Schulportal rendering gives 1,228 for teachers aged 66+ in 2022/23. These may be two different cuts (past-pension-age vs 66+; calendar vs school year). **Re-read the Schulportal table and state which definition is being used, or drop the NRW figure.**

**CONTEXT.** Launched November 2023 by the NSW Department of Education, alongside the October 2023 pay settlement (the largest teacher pay rise in three decades) and workload measures.

**DESIGN.** Outreach from the Department's own staffing records to teachers who resigned in the previous five years, with the re-entry friction bundled into one contact: support for NESA accreditation reactivation, approval to teach, and the Working with Children Check; placement into casual and temporary work, with permanent posts and small-group tutoring roles also open. ### Ireland — national campaign to bring retired teachers back, plus the abatement waiver (2021-2027) — **NEW CASE**

**CONTEXT.** Persistent substitute-supply difficulty, especially in Dublin and in shortage subjects, in a system where a retired public servant's pension is normally *abated* if pension plus new salary exceeds pre-retirement salary — a direct financial bar to substitute work.

**DESIGN — two instruments, deliberately paired.**
1. **Price.** An **abatement waiver**: the first **50 days worked in a calendar year** are exempt from abatement, each working day counting as one day regardless of hours. Introduced by Circular 0003/2021 (29 January 2021), extended by Circular 0004/2024 (20 September 2024) for 2024 and 2025, and extended again by **Circular 17/2026 for calendar years 2026 and 2027**. Conditions: the school must confirm it is impracticable to fill the post otherwise, and the waiver does not apply to contracts running beyond 50 days. Retired teachers must maintain or renew Teaching Council registration to be paid (VERIFIED — ASTI, "Teaching in Retirement and Abatement", https://www.asti.ie/your-employment/teaching-in-retirement-and-abatement/; RTAI, "Extension to the Waiver of Abatement of Teachers' Pensions for 2024 and 2025", 10 January 2024, https://www.rtaireland.ie/2024/01/10/extension-to-the-waiver-of-abatement-of-teachers-pensions-for-2024-and-2025/).
2. **Outreach.** "A new communications campaign began earlier this year to encourage retired teachers to return to teaching", stated by the Minister for Education in a written answer of 30 April 2024, alongside reduced restrictions on job-sharing teachers and teachers on career break working as substitutes (VERIFIED — Dáil written answers, 30 April 2024, https://www.kildarestreet.com/wrans/?id=2024-04-30a.699).

**TAKE-UP AND CONVERSION.**
- **Outcome measure attached to the campaign: a 49% increase in teaching days worked by retired teachers between 2021/22 and 2022/23** (VERIFIED — same written answer). Note this pre-dates the 2024 campaign and therefore measures the *waiver*, not the campaign; the answer bundles them.
- **Stock: as of end-2023, 1,388 retired teachers (permanent and fixed-term) had returned to work in the primary sector, of whom only 2.88% fell to be abated after the waiver** (VERIFIED — reported from the Department's figures by RTAI/ASTI channels; **flagged as union-relayed rather than read from the circular itself — verify against Circular 0004/2024 before print**).
- **NOT FOUND:** the campaign's name, budget, channel or contact volume; any post-primary equivalent of the 1,388; any figure after 2023.

**EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION.** NOT FOUND — no evaluation of the waiver or the campaign. The 49% rise in teaching days is a pre/post comparison with no counterfactual, and 2021/22 is a COVID-depressed base.

**STATUS 2026.** Live and extended through 2027 (Circular 17/2026), which makes Ireland the only system in the corpus that has renewed a returner-price instrument three times.

**WHAT IT CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** That Ireland is the cleanest example of the paper's price-plus-outreach pairing: it removed the abatement bar *first* and then advertised, and it is the only case where the outcome is measured in **teaching days delivered** rather than heads — a better unit for a reserve that works casually. That the waiver's design (50 days, a day is a day) is deliberately capped so that returners supplement rather than displace, and that only 2.88% of returning primary teachers were still abated afterwards, i.e. the bar was almost entirely removed for the target population.

**WHAT IT CANNOT SUPPORT.** Any causal claim for either instrument. Any claim about the campaign's reach or cost. Any transfer of the 1,388 to post-primary.

### United States — Number Rockets: retired and substitute teachers as structured maths tutors (REL Southwest, 2008-09)

**CONTEXT.** The efficacy trial behind the protocol (Fuchs et al., 2005) used graduate-student tutors; the open question was whether a realistic, hireable workforce could reproduce the effect.

**DESIGN.** Scripted Tier-2 small-group (2-3 students) maths intervention for at-risk grade 1 students: 40-minute sessions, three times a week, 17 weeks, target minimum 45 lessons (63 sessions in the ESSA summary). Tutor training: one 8-hour initial day plus two 2-hour follow-ups, with weekly coaching. **Tutors were recruited and hired through a nationwide temporary services company that handled all HR functions for the study team**; candidates had to be certified teachers and/or have K-5 experience, with preference for small-group experience (VERIFIED — Rolfhus et al., NCEE 2012-4007, pp. 46-58, https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED529429.pdf).

**WHO THE TUTORS ACTUALLY WERE (NEW — this is the detail our fichas says does not exist).** Of the final tutor sample: **77.3% reported being retired teachers and/or working as substitute teachers**; 85.3% female; 46.7% White, 42.7% Black, 8.0% Hispanic; 32.0% held a master's degree or higher; and **44.0% reported 0-5 years of teaching experience**. Eighty-four tutors were trained before implementation and **27 more were hired mid-stream to replace tutors released or withdrawn**; the final count was 86 (82 active, 4 alternates), averaging 4.3 per school (VERIFIED — same report, "Description of tutors" and "Tutor attrition", pp. 46-50).

**COST (NEW).** Study staff estimated the annual average cost of Number Rockets as implemented at **approximately $10,000 per school and $700 per student**, including materials, coaches, tutors and ongoing support — a first-year figure, exclusive of screening (VERIFIED — same report, "Cost of implementation", p. 58).

**TAKE-UP AND CONVERSION.** Not applicable (a trial, not a recruitment scheme). Sample: **994 at-risk grade 1 students in 76 schools across four urban districts in four of the five REL Southwest states**.

**EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION.** Cluster RCT. **Effect on TEMA-3 = 0.34 SD, p < .001 (Hedges' g), robust across six sensitivity analyses**; no spillover to non-participating at-risk students in treatment schools (0.08 SD, p = .564) and none to non-at-risk students (−0.01 SD, p = .913). WWC: "meets standards with reservations". Evidence for ESSA: **Strong** (VERIFIED — Rolfhus et al., 2012, pp. 498-508; https://www.evidenceforessa.org/program/number-rockets/).

**STATUS 2026.** The protocol is in circulation (materials sold by Vanderbilt; listed by Evidence for ESSA, Blueprints and ProvenTutoring); there is no ongoing programme of that name staffed by retirees.

**WHAT IT CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** That a tutor workforce drawn overwhelmingly (77.3%) from retired and substitute teachers, hired through a temp agency and trained in a single day plus boosters, reproduced at scale an effect first obtained with graduate students — and did so at about $700 per student. That the *role*, not the *seniority*, carried the effect: 44% of these tutors had five years' experience or less.

**WHAT IT CANNOT SUPPORT.** Attribution to retirees specifically — the 77.3% is a combined "retired and/or substitute" self-report with no published split. Any claim that a retiree tutor workforce is stable: 27 replacement hires against 84 trained is a ~32% mid-year churn rate, and that number belongs in any costing.

**SOURCES.** Rolfhus et al. (2012); Gersten et al. (2015); Fuchs et al. (2005) [all in references.md]. **[NEW]** Evidence for ESSA, "Number Rockets", https://www.evidenceforessa.org/program/number-rockets/ (accessed 19 August 2026).

### The tutoring evidence base — Nickow, Oreopoulos and Quan (2024) **and the at-scale correction** (Kraft, Schueler and Falken, 2024) — **NEW and load-bearing**

**WHAT THE BASE ALREADY HAS.** Nickow, Oreopoulos and Quan (2024), *American Educational Research Journal* 61(1), pp. 74-107, "The Promise of Tutoring for PreK-12 Learning": pooled 0.288 SD; effects largest for teacher and paraprofessional tutors, in earlier grades, at ≥3 days a week, during the school day (VERIFIED — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3102/00028312231208687; NBER w27476).

**WHAT IS MISSING AND MATTERS.** Kraft, M. A., B. E. Schueler and G. Falken (2024), "What Impacts Should We Expect from Tutoring at Scale? Exploring Meta-Analytic Generalizability", EdWorkingPaper 24-1031, October 2024, https://doi.org/10.26300/zygj-m525. An expanded meta-analysis of **265 RCTs**. Findings (all VERIFIED from the PDF text):
- Full-sample pooled effect **0.42 SD**; 95% prediction interval **−0.31 to 1.16 SD**.
- Effects decline **almost linearly with programme scale**: **0.55 SD** for programmes serving fewer than 100 students, **0.32 SD** for 100-399, **0.25 SD** for 400-999, **0.14 SD** for 1,000+.
- Preferred, target-equivalent estimates (US programmes, third-party standardised tests, at scale): **0.21 SD (400-999 students) and 0.16 SD (1,000+)** — "between a third and a half as large as the pooled estimate using our full meta-analytic sample".
- By publication era: 0.45 SD (pre-2000), 0.58 (2000-09), 0.38 (2010-19), **0.27 (2020 or later)**.
- Researcher-made tests inflate effects by **0.22 SD** relative to third-party standardised tests; tutoring outside the school day is worth **−0.19 SD**; unspecified tutor-student ratio **−0.19 SD**; **tutor types outside the main categories — "mostly community members and/or volunteers" — are worth −0.16 SD relative to a teacher**.
- Large-scale public programmes cited: the **UK National Tutoring Programme** at 0.06 SD in maths and 0.03 SD in English at Key Stage 2, and null at Key Stage 4 (maths −0.01, English −0.00) (Moore et al., 2024, Government Social Research); the **Victorian Tutor Learning Initiative** with no significant effects (VAGO, 2024); and multi-district US analyses finding statistically insignificant 0.03 SD maths / 0.07 SD reading across programmes jointly serving 12,000+ students (Carbonari, DeArmond et al., 2024).

**WHAT IT CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** That the strongest argument for the defined-role family — "tutoring works" — must be quoted at its at-scale value (0.16-0.21 SD), not its headline value (0.29-0.42 SD); and that the penalty for using community volunteers rather than teachers (−0.16 SD) is precisely the reason to define a former-teacher tutor role rather than a general volunteer one. This is the single most useful new finding in this file for Family E's argument, because it simultaneously deflates the promise and strengthens the case for *who* should do the tutoring.

**WHAT IT CANNOT SUPPORT.** Any claim that retired teachers specifically are or are not in the "teacher" category of these meta-analyses — none of them codes retirement status.

**SOURCES.** **[NEW]** Kraft, M. A., B. E. Schueler and G. Falken (2024), *What Impacts Should We Expect from Tutoring at Scale? Exploring Meta-Analytic Generalizability*, EdWorkingPaper 24-1031, Annenberg Institute at Brown University, October 2024, https://doi.org/10.26300/zygj-m525 (accessed 19 August 2026). *Note: the base flags "Kraft and Falken (2021)" as a missing key used only in lit files; this 2024 paper supersedes it for our purposes.*

### United States (Colorado) — Retired Mentors for New Teachers, Aurora Public Schools

**DESIGN.** Recently retired master educators of the district, screened for excellence, paid **USD 41/hour**, mentoring probationary teachers in Title I elementary schools over two years: weekly one-to-ones plus monthly cohort meetings, caseloads of 3-7, no evaluative role, layered on top of the district's business-as-usual buddy mentor.

**TAKE-UP AND CONVERSION.** 77 probationary teachers in 11 schools, randomised within school (38/39).

**EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION.** Randomised controlled trial by DeCesare, McClelland and Randel (REL Central, April 2017), https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED573097.pdf. **WWC review August 2019 under Standards 4.0 and Review Protocol 4.0: "meets WWC standards without reservations"** (cluster RCT, low cluster-level attrition and individual non-response), with **all three reviewed domains indeterminate and no statistically significant positive findings**:
- Reading comprehension (NWEA MAP): n.s. in year 1 (**1,156 students**) and year 2 (**964 students**).
- Mathematics (NWEA MAP): n.s. in year 1 (**1,189 students**) and year 2 (**1,105 students**).
- Teacher retention: n.s. after 1 year (**77 teachers**) and 2 years (**74 teachers**).
No improvement indices were computed because nothing reached significance. WWC adds the standard note that the intervention "may achieve a higher tier when combined with the full body of evidence" (VERIFIED — https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Study/88756).
The report's own year-1 maths finding (+1.4 MAP points, ES 0.064, p = .045) does not survive either the report's multiple-comparison adjustment or WWC's review; the exploratory dose-response (odds of staying roughly doubling per additional 10 mentoring hours) is non-causal.

**STATUS 2026.** NOT FOUND — no evidence the Aurora programme continues, and no replication anywhere.

**WHAT IT CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** That the one rigorous causal test of retirees mentoring novices is a clean, well-executed, **indeterminate** trial — and that "indeterminate" here has a precise, citable WWC meaning, with student samples above 1,100 and teacher samples of 74-77 (i.e. the student side was adequately powered; the teacher-retention side was not).

**WHAT IT CANNOT SUPPORT.** A verdict against retiree mentoring, or for it. Nor any claim that mentoring improves mentee retention: the point estimate ran the wrong way (11 programme leavers vs 5 control).

**SOURCES.** DeCesare, McClelland and Randel (2017); What Works Clearinghouse (2019) [both in references.md]. **[NEW]** direct WWC study record: https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Study/88756 (accessed 19 August 2026).

### Japan — post-retirement re-employment as the default staffing route, and its visible exhaustion

**CONTEXT AND STATUS 2026 (major update).** MEXT published the Reiwa 7 (2025) teacher-shortage survey on **5 March 2026**: as of 1 May 2025 the national shortfall was **3,827 teachers, a shortage rate of 0.45%**, against **2,065 (0.25%)** in the comparable 2021 survey — an increase, with 43 authorities worse and 23 better, and only 8 prefectures reporting no shortage. The count explicitly includes regular teachers, temporary appointees, part-time lecturers/fiscal-year appointees **and re-employed (再任用) teachers, full-time and short-hours** (VERIFIED — https://www.mext.go.jp/content/20260302-mxt_kyoikujinzai01-000047773_1_1.pdf, pp. 1-3; release page https://www.mext.go.jp/a_menu/shotou/kyoin/mext_00018.html).

**THE FINDING THAT MATTERS (NEW).** MEXT asked every board of education to rate, on a five-point scale, a list of candidate causes of the shortage. Two items are about the retiree reserve, and roughly 68 boards answered each:
- "Compared with when new-hire numbers were decided, **the number of mandatory retirees wishing to be re-employed fell short of expectations**": 4 "strongly applies" + 25 "somewhat applies" = **29 of ~68 (43%)**.
- "Compared with when new-hire numbers were decided, **the number of already re-employed staff wishing to continue fell short of expectations**": 2 + 25 = **27 of ~68 (40%)**.
(For calibration, the top-rated cause — "more retirements than forecast" — scored 14 + 34 = 48.) (VERIFIED — same PDF, "「教師不足」の要因に対する各教育委員会の認識(2/2)".)

**POLICY RESPONSE 2026 (NEW).** MEXT's stated responses include "creating further opportunities for experienced retired teachers"; promoting 再任用 by flexibly redistributing non-teaching duties in view of physical demands and by giving retirees roles in **developing young teachers**; piloting and rolling out a **"Japanese-style supply teacher" system** and other flexible appointment forms to find new entrants; building **licence-holder deployment systems (人材バンク / talent banks)** in authorities receiving hands-on MEXT support; and a "travel teacher" model combined with relocation policy (VERIFIED — same PDF, pp. 5 and the response slides).

**DESIGN AND SCALE.** Still no branded national programme: boards conventionally re-employ teachers after mandatory retirement, and retirees dominate the substitute/adjunct pool. In the one prefecture studied in depth, 52.7% of part-time adjunct hires were in their sixties and 7.1% in their seventies — retirees roughly 60% of adjuncts; only about 35% of age-based retirees in elementary schools (55% in junior high) now seek full-time re-employment (Sakuma and Shimazaki, 2024, pp. 201-203, already in the base). **NOT FOUND: any current national count of re-employed teachers.** MEXT's own 再任用 statistical tables at https://www.mext.go.jp/a_menu/shotou/jinji/1331020.htm are the 2010 (Heisei 22) series and are useless for 2026 — they show 7,709 re-employed 教諭 in that year, which must **not** be quoted as current.

**THE STRUCTURAL CHANGE (NEW).** Japan's civil-service retirement age is being raised from 60 to 65 in one-year steps every two years from 2023, reaching 65 as the national standard by 2031 (62 in 2025), with a pre-retirement short-hours re-employment option of 15h30-31h a week for those choosing to leave at 60. This dissolves the very gap that post-retirement re-employment was invented to fill: as the retirement age rises, the "re-employed retiree" category is progressively converted back into ordinary employment.

**EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION.** NOT FOUND — no impact evaluation of re-employment in Japan; the MEXT survey is a perception survey of boards plus an establishment count.

**WHAT IT CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** That Japan is the case where the retiree reserve was institutionalised rather than campaigned for, and that its **national statistical agency now records the depletion of that reserve as a cause of shortage** — around 40% of boards attributing shortage in part to fewer retirees wanting re-employment or wanting to continue. That is the strongest available evidence anywhere for tension F (a retiree-based reserve depletes on its own schedule), and it is official.

**WHAT IT CANNOT SUPPORT.** Any national headcount of re-employed teachers in the 2020s. Any claim that MEXT instructs boards to recruit retirees — the 2026 document *encourages* creating roles for them, which is weaker and should be quoted as such.

### Bahamas — Teacher Reservist Program

**CONTEXT.** A shortfall of about 300 teachers across 162 public schools on 24 islands, driven by retirements, expiring contracts and new specialised subjects (special education, technology, financial and digital literacy, entrepreneurship).

**DESIGN.** A standing, registered reserve built on the platform of the existing Supply Teacher Programme: retired educators, trained teachers who left for other professions, and recent University of The Bahamas graduates enlist once and are called on by schools; a multi-agency Teacher Recruitment Task Force fast-tracks vetting, credential assessment and Public Service Commission processing. No reservist-specific financial incentive published (VERIFIED — ZNS Bahamas, "Teacher Recruitment Task Force Launched" and "The Ministry of Education Aiming to Attract More Bahamian Teachers"; the bahamas.gov.bs mirror returned 403 on 19 August 2026).

**TAKE-UP AND CONVERSION — updated to August 2026.**
- 17 July 2026: the government sought **up to 100 Bahamians** before September, with "between 60 and 100" being enlisted and **42 applications submitted to the Public Service Commission** (The Tribune, "Govt seeks 100 Bahamians to plug teacher shortage", 17 July 2026).
- August 2026: **about 100 Bahamian teachers have been enlisted to enter classrooms in September 2026**, with the ministry targeting recent UB graduates, retired teachers and others through the teacher reserve and supply-teacher programmes (VERIFIED — The Tribune, 11 August 2026, https://www.tribune242.com/news/2026/aug/11/govt-moves-to-expand-special-education-to-address-island-waitlist/).
- **NOT FOUND:** any enrolment count for the reservist register itself, any retiree share, any cost.

**EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION.** None; the programme is weeks old.

**CONTESTATION (NEW).** The recruitment drive is politically contested, though on the foreign-recruitment flank rather than the reservist one: the teachers' union president publicly attacked a reported plan to recruit 300 Ghanaian teachers and warned of industrial action, and the Deputy Prime Minister responded that Bahamians get priority (The Tribune, 13 and 15 July 2026; Eyewitness News). The domestic reserve is, in effect, the government's answer to that criticism — which is a motive worth naming.

**WHAT IT CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** That the design innovation is institutional — a register former teachers join once, rather than a campaign that must find them again each year — and that by August 2026 the drive as a whole (not the reservist pool alone) had reached about 100 enlistments against a ~300 gap.
**WHAT IT CANNOT SUPPORT.** Any effectiveness claim; any reservist-specific number; any retiree share.

**SOURCES.** Cooper (2026); ZNS Bahamas (2026) [in references.md]. **[NEW]** The Tribune (2026), "Govt seeks 100 Bahamians to plug teacher shortage", 17 July 2026, https://www.tribune242.com/news/2026/jul/17/govt-seeks-100-bahamians-to-plug-teacher-shortage/; **[NEW]** The Tribune (2026), "Govt moves to expand special education to address island waitlist", 11 August 2026, https://www.tribune242.com/news/2026/aug/11/govt-moves-to-expand-special-education-to-address-island-waitlist/; **[NEW]** The Tribune (2026), "Union blasts plan to hire 300 Ghanaian teachers", 13 July 2026 (all accessed 19 August 2026).

### United States — AARP Foundation Experience Corps

**DESIGN.** Volunteers aged 50+ tutor K-3 students reading 1-1.5 years behind, typically two 30-minute sessions a week, one-to-one or in groups of three or fewer, after at least 20 hours of initial training; some slots historically stipended through AmeriCorps streams. **Being a former teacher is not an eligibility requirement and the retired-educator share is unpublished.**

**EVIDENCE.** Student RCT: 883 students in grades 1-3 randomised across 23 schools in Boston, New York City and Port Arthur, Texas (analytic 881; 430 treatment / 451 control; 822 with posttests); 174 volunteers. Significant gains in passage comprehension (**ES 0.13**) and teacher-rated grade-specific reading skills (**ES 0.16**); no effects on vocabulary or phonemic awareness; gains larger at ≥35 sessions. WWC Quick Review, February 2010, "consistent with WWC evidence standards". Volunteer-side RCTs: Fried et al. (2004, n=128), Varma et al. (2016), Carlson et al. (2015, n=111; 0.7-1.6% two-year gains in cortical and hippocampal volumes, significant in men only).
**Journal version identified (resolves a [KEY MISSING] flag):** Lee, Y. S., N. Morrow-Howell, M. Jonson-Reid and S. McCrary (2012), "The effect of the Experience Corps® program on student reading outcomes", *Education and Urban Society*, Vol. 44/1, pp. 97-118 (published online 2010) (VERIFIED — https://openscholarship.wustl.edu/csd_research/866/).

**STATUS 2026.** Operating and expanding. Active in 18 communities; Experience Corps Mesa expands to 12 Mesa Public Schools sites for 2026-27 with 70+ volunteers and a projected 250 students in K-4; Phoenix ran information and training sessions in August 2026 (VERIFIED — https://www.aarp.org/experience-corps/locations/; Signals AZ; City of Phoenix events calendar).

**WHAT IT CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** The dual-benefit argument — modest, dose-dependent student reading gains plus RCT-documented health and activity benefits for the older volunteers — which lets re-entry policy borrow a healthy-ageing rationale and a second ministry's budget. And, read against Kraft/Schueler/Falken, it illustrates the volunteer penalty: 0.13-0.16 SD is exactly the band the at-scale meta-analysis predicts for non-teacher tutors.
**WHAT IT CANNOT SUPPORT.** Anything about former teachers specifically.

**SOURCES.** Morrow-Howell et al. (2009); What Works Clearinghouse (2010); Fried et al. (2004); Carlson et al. (2015); Varma et al. (2016); AARP (2025) [all in references.md]. **[NEW]** Lee, Y. S. et al. (2012), *Education and Urban Society* 44(1), pp. 97-118, https://openscholarship.wustl.edu/csd_research/866/ (accessed 19 August 2026).

### Australia (Victoria) — Tutor Learning Initiative, 2021-2025 — **NEW CASE, and the largest in the family**

**CONTEXT.** Announced 13 October 2020 as COVID learning-recovery: an initial **$250 million package — $209.6m for 3,500 tutors in government schools, $30m for 600 tutors in non-government schools, $8.6m for family engagement** — later extended to a total **$1.2 billion over 2021-2025** (VERIFIED — https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/thousands-tutors-bring-students-speed, 13 October 2020).

**DESIGN — and why it belongs in Family E.** The initiative was explicitly staffed from the out-of-service teacher pool: it "called on **pre-service teachers, teachers on leave, retired teachers and casual relief teachers** to get back in the classroom". Schools employ tutors to deliver targeted small-group instruction (2-5 students) during school hours; tutors are qualified teachers (VERIFIED — Premier's release, 13 October 2020; VAGO report).

**TAKE-UP AND SCALE.** More than **4,100 tutors in 2021** and more than **5,150 in 2022** in government schools; **over 127,000 government school students tutored in 2023** (over 170,000 including non-government), across more than 1,500 government and 600 non-government schools. **NOT FOUND: the retired-teacher share of the tutor workforce at any date** — this is the case's central evidentiary hole and must be stated as such.

**EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION.** Victorian Auditor-General's Office, *Effectiveness of the Tutor Learning Initiative*, tabled **19 June 2024**. Method: matched comparison of learning gains of tutored and non-tutored students with similar baseline scores, plus assessment of school practice. Findings: **no significant improvement** — the median year 3 tutored student gained 7.1 in reading against 7.4 for a similarly-skilled non-tutored student; **fewer than one-third of schools reached "fully effective practice"** on targeting, appropriateness and fit by 2023, though 96% delivered tutoring on time; primary outperformed secondary; departmental support "had not been enough to drive sustained and significant improvement in schools' practices" since 2021. Three recommendations on data, measurable goals and piloting system-wide interventions (VERIFIED — https://www.audit.vic.gov.au/report/effectiveness-tutor-learning-initiative).

**CONTESTATION.** Grattan Institute (Hunter and Haywood, The Conversation, 20 June 2024) argue the failure is implementation, not tutoring: poor targeting, weak links to classroom teaching, and no systematic use of school-level data. Kraft, Schueler and Falken (2024) cite VAGO as one of the two flagship at-scale nulls in high-income systems.

**STATUS 2026.** The $1.2bn commitment ran to 2025. **NOT FOUND: confirmation of continuation into 2026** — do not assert either way.

**WHAT IT CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** That the largest deployment anywhere of an out-of-service teacher pool into a defined tutoring role — a billion-dollar programme explicitly recruiting retired teachers, teachers on leave and CRTs — was independently audited and found not to have improved learning; and that the audit locates the failure in role specification (targeting, fit, connection to classroom work), which is exactly the variable Family E's argument turns on. This is the strongest available counterweight to reading Number Rockets as a promise.
**WHAT IT CANNOT SUPPORT.** Any claim about retirees specifically — the tutor composition is unpublished. Nor a verdict that tutoring does not work: VAGO evaluated an implementation, not a protocol.

**SOURCES.**
- **[NEW]** Victorian Auditor-General's Office (2024), *Effectiveness of the Tutor Learning Initiative*, tabled 19 June 2024, https://www.audit.vic.gov.au/report/effectiveness-tutor-learning-initiative (accessed 19 August 2026).
- **[NEW]** Premier of Victoria (2020), "Thousands Of Tutors To Bring Students Up To Speed", 13 October 2020, https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/thousands-tutors-bring-students-speed (accessed 19 August 2026).
- **[NEW]** Hunter, J. and A. Haywood (2024), "Victoria's $1.2 billion school tutoring program has not 'significantly improved' learning. How could it work better?", *The Conversation*, 20 June 2024, https://theconversation.com/victorias-1-2-billion-school-tutoring-program-has-not-significantly-improved-learning-how-could-it-work-better-232832 (accessed 19 August 2026).

### England — National Tutoring Programme (for the at-scale comparison only) — **NEW, brief**

Cited here solely because it is the other high-income at-scale tutoring evaluation and because it disciplines any claim built on Number Rockets. Government Social Research (Moore et al., 2024) found, in the programme's third year and using matched comparisons with baseline test scores, **0.06 SD in maths and 0.03 SD in English at Key Stage 2, and null effects at Key Stage 4 (maths −0.01, English −0.00)** (VERIFIED as reported in Kraft, Schueler and Falken, 2024, p. 20-21). **NOT FOUND in this pass: the primary GSR report and any statement of who the NTP tutors were.** Do not attribute NTP tutoring to former teachers.

### Spain (Andalusia) — *Profesorado emérito colaborador* and the *Red Comparte Experiencia*, Decreto 12/2026 — **NEW CASE, and the corpus's first substantial Spanish one**

**CONTEXT.** Until now the corpus's only Spanish case was a one-off micro-pilot (Zaragoza, "10 mirrors", 10 retired mentors and 57 students). Andalusia has now created a standing legal figure.

**DESIGN.** **Decreto 12/2026, de 4 de febrero**, approved by the Consejo de Gobierno on 4 February 2026, regulates the figure of *profesorado emérito colaborador* in publicly funded non-university schools and creates the **Red Comparte Experiencia**.
- **Eligibility:** retired for any reason except permanent disability; **under 75**; at least **15 years of service** in public or publicly funded non-university centres in Andalusia.
- **Functions:** reading and library promotion; lectures and workshops; support for complementary and extracurricular activities; **mentoring new teachers**; supporting university student interns; conflict mediation; academic and careers guidance advice.
- **Explicit exclusions:** the role confers no right to occupy a post, no active-teacher functions, and **no remuneration**.
- **Compensation:** accident, illness and civil-liability insurance; annual certification; free access to cultural institutions on the same terms as serving teachers.
- **Volume and duration:** a maximum of **2,500 collaborators a year**; annual appointment, renewable, within a maximum of three years; applications from schools and teachers each year between **1 and 30 June**.
(VERIFIED — Junta de Andalucía, "Hasta 2.500 docentes jubilados podrán seguir aportando su experiencia en las aulas andaluzas", 4 February 2026, https://www.juntadeandalucia.es/educacion/portales/web/actualidad/w/hasta-2.500-docentes-jubilados-podran-seguir-aportando-su-experiencia-en-las-aulas-andaluzas; decree text summarised at https://noticias.juridicas.com/base_datos/CCAA/987224-d-desarrollo-educativo-12-2026-de-4-feb-ca-andalucia-regula-figura-y-actividades.html.)

**TAKE-UP AND CONVERSION.** None yet — the first application window is June 2026 and no enrolment figure has been published. **The 2,500 is a ceiling, not a count. Any use of it must say so.**

**EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION.** None, and none planned as far as can be established.

**CONTESTATION.** The unpaid design is the flashpoint. National coverage framed it as "the Junta asks retired teachers back to school: free, of course" (Xataka, 20 March 2026) and El Confidencial Digital as "proposes to 2,500 retired teachers that they return to the classroom to advise without pay" (23 March 2026). The Andalusian teaching unions (CCOO, UGT, ANPE, CSIF) have been mobilised in the same months, but on other decrees (compensatory education zones, teacher mobility); **I did not find a formal union statement specifically on Decreto 12/2026 — do not attribute one.**

**WHAT IT CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** That the defined-role logic has now produced a legal instrument in a Spanish region: a named status, an age and service test, an enumerated function list including mentoring novices, a capped volume, an annual window — and zero pay. That it is the family's purest test of whether role definition alone, without money, mobilises former teachers; and that it deliberately fences the role off from the establishment ("no right to occupy posts, no active-teacher functions"), which is the design choice that makes it politically survivable and simultaneously caps what it can deliver.
**WHAT IT CANNOT SUPPORT.** Any take-up, cost or effectiveness claim whatever. It is four months old at the time of writing and must always be dated "February 2026, first window June 2026".

### United States — post-COVID district and state tutoring corps (context only)

Retired teachers appear as one recruitment source among several in the ESSER-funded tutoring build-out, never as the defining feature: 37 state education agencies supported tutoring in their ESSER plans, with statewide tutoring corps in Tennessee and Arkansas; Guilford County (North Carolina), the most-cited district case, recruited over 600 tutors at peak — "retired teachers, accomplished professionals, and aspiring educators" — reaching more than 17,000 students in 2023-24 in partnership with NC Ed Corps and local universities. The evidence is the at-scale null band already reported above (Carbonari, DeArmond et al., 2024: 0.03 SD maths, 0.07 SD reading, n.s., across programmes jointly serving 12,000+ students), and the funding cliff arrived with ESSER's expiry in 2024-25. **No US programme isolates retired educators as a tutor class, and none reports their share.**

**SOURCES.** **[NEW]** FutureEd, "An Analysis of Local School Districts' Ambitious Post-Covid Tutoring Plans" and "A Primer on Sustaining High-Impact Tutoring After ESSER", https://www.future-ed.org/ (accessed 19 August 2026); **[NEW]** Education Week (2025), "Tutoring Programs Are in Limbo After the Expiration of COVID Relief Aid", April 2025, https://www.edweek.org/leadership/tutoring-programs-are-in-limbo-after-the-expiration-of-covid-relief-aid/2025/04 (accessed 19 August 2026).

## SUMMARY OF CORRECTIONS TO EXISTING MATERIAL

1. **England / RTTA:** "No independent evaluation of the RTTA has been published" is **wrong** — Straw et al. (2017), DFE-RB683, is a DfE-commissioned, NFER/Sheffield Hallam process evaluation of the RTTA strand, and it publishes the only adviser conversion rate we have (**63 of 541 eligible returners placed by end-September 2016 = 11.6%, above a target of 50**).
2. **England / successor programme:** the Returners Engagement Programme was announced **18 November 2016** (not "from 2017"), was **region-restricted to the North West and South East**, and **did pay returners** (up to £600 for a course, £150/week childcare) as well as schools (up to £5,000, of which £1,500 conditional on employment). The fichas' "no payments to returners" applies only to the 2015-16 pilot.
3. **England / cost:** £37,400 is the **midpoint of a scenario range £17,800-£142,500** (NFER Table 34); the fichas presents it as though it were the estimate. Add the pilot's targets (3,000 registrations, 650 permanent EBacc placements) and the comparison-group return rate (~4%) whenever the funnel is quoted.
4. **Austria and Hesse / conversion rates:** compiler note 3's claim that "no outreach scheme publishes a letters→returns conversion rate (Austria, Hesse)" is **wrong on both counts**. Austria: >3,000 letters → ~45 acceptances nationally (Resch, 2025); ~300 letters → 3 returns in Vienna (ORF Wien, 29 March 2023). Hesse: ~7,000 contacted → "up to 200" posts a year (Deutsches Schulportal, 2023).
5. **Hesse / the "~200" figure:** the fichas says it must not be used without its press source. Two sources now exist — Deutsches Schulportal (2023) for the annual norm and fuldainfo, 4 October 2024, reporting the Kultusministerium, for the 2024/25 instance.
6. **Number Rockets / tutor composition:** the fichas says there is "no published split" for retirees versus substitutes. There is no split, but there **is** a composition statistic: **77.3% of tutors reported being retired teachers and/or substitutes**, 44.0% had 0-5 years' experience, and 27 replacement tutors were hired against 84 trained. Add the cost figures ($10,000 per school, $700 per student), which the fichas lacks entirely.
7. **Tutoring evidence:** the 0.288 SD pooled estimate must be paired with Kraft, Schueler and Falken (2024) — **0.16-0.21 SD at scale**, a third to a half of the headline — otherwise the paper's Family E claim overstates the evidence it is built on.
8. **Japan / figures:** the 2021 comparator in MEXT's own series is **2,065 (0.25%)**, and the 2025 figure is **3,827 (0.45%)** as of 1 May 2025, published 5 March 2026. The base's caution about not mixing 2,558 and 2,065 is right and should now cite the 2026 survey directly. Also add: Japan is raising the retirement age from 60 to 65 by 2031, which structurally shrinks the post-retirement re-employment category.
9. **Lee et al. (2012)** — the flagged missing key is: *Education and Urban Society*, Vol. 44/1, pp. 97-118.
10. **Two unresolved discrepancies** requiring a source re-read before print: the Austrian "42" (Vorarlberg vs nationwide compulsory schools) and the NRW count (our 1,304 past pension age vs 1,228 aged 66+ in the retrieved Schulportal rendering).
