# 4. Conclusions and recommendations

Most OECD countries face serious teacher shortages, and a growing number of governments have turned to the profession's former members as part of the response. The record those efforts have produced is administrative rather than experimental, and it is uneven across systems, but it is no longer thin. Read against its own denominators, it supports a small set of conclusions with direct consequences for how re-entry policy is designed and accounted for.

## 4.1 What the compiled record supports

Former teachers form three populations whose differences govern which instruments can reach them. Teachers who move to other jobs, teachers who withdraw from the labour market and teachers who retire leave for different motives and face different obstacles to coming back, so each population answers to a different family of instruments (Section 2). Systems in which retirements dominate departures are addressed most directly by the pension and employment rules of family A, while systems dominated by working-age resignations, whose leavers cite workload and hours ahead of pay, are addressed by the part-time and defined-role designs of family E more plausibly than by money attached to a return the motives evidence has already priced (Table 1). The evidence therefore suggests that education systems could benefit from decomposing their own departures into the three populations before an instrument is chosen, since a measure that succeeds with one population says little about the others.

Across the five families, the largest documented return flows follow changes to the statutory terms of re-employment. Among the many campaigns implemented to re-attract teachers, both those providing incentives and those easing certification, there is suggestive evidence that they weigh less than changes to the legal rules under which a returner is employed and paid, which appear more successful in bringing former teachers back (Section 3). The two documented episodes in which returns exceeded a fraction of one per cent of the relevant workforce, the North Carolina window and Japan's standing re-employment of retirees, each involved an institution changing rather than a message being sent (Jarrold-Grapes and Mahler, 2024; Sakuma and Shimazaki, 2024), while marketing-led and subsidy-led schemes converted registrations into placements at rates their own sponsors described as disappointing (Buchanan et al., 2018; Ministerie van OCW, 2019a). This reading rests on uncontrolled episodes counted against different outcome definitions (Table 4), so it orients policy without measuring it.

The rules that appear to weigh most are also the rules a government can describe most precisely. The price of a return, the sum of salary-step placement, pension-salary concurrency, waiting periods and licensing costs (Section 3), is written in statute and scheme regulation, and the settings it comprises were assembled entirely from public sources (Table 3); any system can compile and publish what its own rules charge a returner without new expenditure. Most of those rules, however, sit with pensions and finance authorities rather than with education ministries. In France the waiting period belongs to the general pension framework (Assemblée nationale, 2020), and in Ontario the re-employment ceiling moves only by agreement between the pension plan's co-sponsors (Ontario Ministry of Education, 2022). Education authorities seeking to lower the price of a return should therefore expect to negotiate it with the bodies that set it, and to treat re-entry as a matter of cross-portfolio co-ordination as much as of education policy.

What returners are offered matters as much as what they are charged. The motives evidence indicates that workload, working hours and work-life balance drive the resignations of working-age teachers more than pay does, and the best-documented returner segment sought part-time work that schools rarely advertise (Section 2; Worth and Sharp, 2018). Offers of partial intensity, reduced-hours contracts in Austria, day-capped supply work in Ontario, pro-rated returner contracts in Portugal, adjunct posts in Japan, match that evidence in a way that a full-time return to the post the leaver gave up does not. Within the defined-role family, the only purchases whose learning value the record demonstrates are specified roles with evaluation attached. The tutoring role that was scripted, dosed and trialled at scale reproduced its measured effect (Gersten et al., 2015), while the mentoring role that was left to the veteran's discretion was tested once and returned an indeterminate answer (What Works Clearinghouse, 2019). On the current evidence, what a system can defensibly buy from its former teachers is a specified role at a specified dose, with the evaluation written into the instrument.

## 4.2 What education systems could do

Little of the counting that would most improve this record requires new data systems. Education systems could benefit from compiling and publishing a netted count of their potential-returner population, discounting lapsed certificates, residence outside the jurisdiction and subject mismatch in the way the Michigan analysis did (Lindsay, Gnedko-Berry and Wan, 2021), since gross register counts overstate the stock that could plausibly be recruited. The records needed are already held in teacher registers and pension files, and the gap such a count would close is documented, with many education systems lacking accurate data on the number and characteristics of potential returners (OECD, 2024). A published netted figure would discipline both the ambitions announced for this population and the budgets attached to them.

Governments should also audit the price of a return that their own rules set, and publish the result. The four regulatory dials (Table 3) give such an audit its structure, and the exercise separates charges that serve a stated purpose from settings that persist on inertia; a waiting period designed to prevent abusive re-hiring may be judged worth its cost, while a crediting rule that returns an experienced teacher at the starting salary is at least a setting its owner can be asked to defend (BMBWF, 2023). Where the audit identifies settings that no authority defends, those settings are the natural first candidates for change, and because most of them belong to pension and finance portfolios, the audit itself supplies the agenda for the co-ordination described above.

On the offer side, the evidence points towards specificity. Schools could advertise the part-time and job-share posts that the documented returner segments seek, and public authorities could tie returner support to identified vacancies instead of open registration, the correction England made after its pilot converted wide interest into few posts (NCTL, 2017; Buchanan et al., 2018). Licensing waivers appear most credible when keyed to a job offer as a standing rule, treating the offer as the market test of whether a return is real (Teaching Council of Aotearoa New Zealand, 2026). Where the purpose is instructional capacity, systems should specify the role and the dose and write evaluation into the instrument, since the one role designed that way is the one whose value was measured (Section 3).

Reporting should extend past the moment of placement. The one programme whose returners were followed in linked workforce records found them more likely than the average entrant to have left again after the first year back (Buchanan et al., 2018), and the destination studies show how quickly leavers settle into work outside the classroom (Brummet et al., 2024; Cuervo and Vera-Toscano, 2025). Placement counts therefore overstate what a programme durably adds, and sponsors should report destinations and one- and three-year survival against a stated denominator, so that a return that lasts a term is distinguishable from one that lasts a career stage.

The record also documents conditions under which spending less is the better course. The Netherlands lowered its own subsidy ceilings because returners were not claiming the money (Ministerie van OCW, 2019a; Tweede Kamer, 2019), Dutch regional partnerships reported more returner applications than schools could absorb (De Jong et al., 2022), and England recorded its largest returner flows in years with no returner-specific scheme in operation (UK Parliament, 2026). An instrument whose budget goes unclaimed, or whose binding constraint is school absorption capacity, gains little from renewal. Policymakers must therefore be prepared to conclude that the appropriate expenditure on a given margin is zero, and to let an instrument lapse when its own take-up record points that way.

Whether any of this is learned depends on who is obliged to count. Across the documented systems, a published take-up record exists where the duty to count sits outside the sponsoring authority, and is generally absent where it sits with the sponsor or with nobody (Table 5). Of particular interest is the case of Georgia (United States), where the statute wrote the audit and the expiry date into the same law, so that the renewal decision was taken with the take-up evidence on the table (Georgia Department of Audits and Accounts, 2025; Teachers Retirement System of Georgia, 2026); the audit existed because the law required it, and the sunset gave it a decision to inform. It is crucial that new re-entry instruments carry the same pairing, a counting duty lodged outside the sponsoring authority and a sunset matched to the decision the count is meant to inform, since the alternative documented in the same table is reporting that stops after the launch announcement (NSW Government, 2024; NSW Department of Education, 2025).

## 4.3 What remains unknown

The research agenda that follows from this record is narrow and mostly inexpensive. No quasi-experimental study anywhere prices re-entry incentives, so the causal literature on teacher pay concerns retention only and cannot be carried over to people who have already left (Fleck et al., 2025). The decomposition of departures into the three exit populations likewise lacks a cross-country source; comparable data now separate resignations from retirements, without distinguishing job switchers from labour-market exits, so the composition question can be answered only nationally (OECD, 2025a). Take-up of licensing waivers remains unpublished in every system that grants them, although every waiver and every restored certificate passes through a regulator's records. The two cheapest items on this agenda, the netted count of the potential-returner population and the audit of the price of a return, rest on data systems that already exist, which suggests that the binding constraint on learning in this field is the assignment of duties rather than the cost of data.

Encouraging the re-entry of former teachers is consistent with a view of teaching as a profession that people may leave and rejoin over a working life, and not one they exit permanently on first departure (OECD, 2024). The compiled record does not show that any instrument resolves a shortage, and the best-documented flows remain small against the stocks they draw on. What the record does indicate is that former teachers respond to the terms on which a return is priced and defined more than to the invitations extended to them. Systems that publish what their own rules charge a returner, and that count what follows under a duty lodged outside the sponsoring authority, are likely to learn more from the next decade of re-entry policy than the record of the last one has preserved.
