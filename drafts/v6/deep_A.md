# DEEP RESEARCH — FAMILY A (pension and employment rules that set the price of a return)

Built 19 August 2026 by web research on top of `drafts/v6/fichas_ABC.md` (Family A) and `research/cases_us_legislation.md`, `research/cases_other_legislation.md`, `research/cases_retiree_return.md`, `research/fix_austria_portugal.md`, `research/fix_netherlands_more_cases.md`.

Conventions. **VERIFIED** = the statement was read in the cited source, opened on the access date shown. **NOT FOUND** = searched and not located; it is not a claim that the thing does not exist. **CORRECTION** = a primary source contradicts or materially qualifies something in the existing fichas. Sources marked **NEW** are absent from `drafts/references.md` (checked 19 August 2026).

Cross-cutting finding worth carrying into the paper's argument: the single most consequential parameter in this family is not the earnings cap but the **break in service**, because that is what pension actuaries price. Four independent public actuaries — North Carolina (Gallagher and Aon, 2025), New Mexico (LFC/ERB, 2022 and 2025), Virginia (VRS, 2026) and Louisiana (Legislative Auditor, 2024 and 2026) — all model the same behavioural channel: shortening the wait induces earlier retirement, which lengthens the payout period and shortens the funding period. Only Virginia has published a full quantification. The evidence base of this family is therefore asymmetric: **rich on cost, thin on effect**.

### United States (North Carolina) — Return-to-work window 1999–2009 (S.L. 1998-212 et seq.); high-need programme 2019–2021 (S.L. 2019-110); revival bill HB 106 (2025)

- **CONTEXT.** VERIFIED. TSERS allows an unreduced benefit at any age with 30 years' service, 60/25 or 65/5, which concentrates retirements sharply at 30 years (Jarrold-Grapes and Mahler, 2024, pp. 5, 12). The 1999 window was legislated to pre-empt the baby-boom retirement wave. System scale (12/31/2023 valuation, from the 2025 actuarial note): **299,037 active members**, 252,036 retired members, 230,130 inactive, 11,336 new retirees in 2024, accrued liability USD 99.8bn, funded 89%, employer contribution 17.14% of pay (North Carolina General Assembly Fiscal Research Division, 2025, pp. 2-3). NEW. Vacancy context used by the authors: ~1,700 NC teaching vacancies in 2021-22 (Nguyen et al., cited in Jarrold-Grapes and Mahler, 2024, p. 5).
- **MECHANISM.** VERIFIED. Baseline (before 1999 and after 2009): a retiree returning **full-time** loses pension and retiree health coverage; part-time return keeps them only under an earnings cap of **half the previous full-time salary**. The window suspended that constraint: full salary **plus** full annuity, up to ~50% more income than a serving full-time teacher. Year 1 (1999) restricted returns to low-performing schools, matched certification, and interim/substitute roles only; those restrictions were lifted in June 2000. Repeatedly extended (S.L. 1998-212, 1998-217, 2000-67, 2001-424, 2002-126, 2004-124, 2005-144, 2005-276, 2005-345, 2007-145, 2007-326); expired autumn 2009 (Jarrold-Grapes and Mahler, 2024, pp. 5-6 and n. 3).
  - **Successor instrument not in our fichas.** VERIFIED, NEW. **S.L. 2019-110 (SB 399)** created a separate, tightly targeted "high-need retired teacher" route: retired on or before 1 February 2019 with an unreduced benefit and no work for a TSERS employer in the following six months; re-employment only at a **Title I school or a school graded D or F**; pay fixed at **step 1** of the state schedule (**step 6** for STEM and special education), no state supplement or bonus, local supplement allowed; one-year contracts; earnings excluded from the post-retirement earnings limit; **no employee or employer TSERS contributions and no service credit**; employer reporting within 90 days with a 10% penalty (min. USD 25) for failure; the State Treasurer must seek an **IRS private letter ruling**, and the provisions are "repealed 30 days from receipt" if the IRS finds plan qualification jeopardised. "This act becomes effective July 1, 2019, and expires June 30, 2021."
  - **Current baseline (2026).** VERIFIED, NEW. Six-month break before any work for a TSERS employer; thereafter earnings in a non-membership position are capped at the greater of 50% of gross pre-retirement compensation or **USD 43,300 (2026; USD 42,160 in 2025)**, indexed annually to CPI; exceeding it suspends the benefit from the first of the following month to 31 December. Substitute teaching counts as work (My NC Retirement, n.d.).
- **TAKE-UP.** VERIFIED (1999–2009 window, independent evaluators): ~**3,500 teachers, 2.1%** of a sample of ~169,000 full-time teachers in 2,900 schools; **1,500+ schools (52%)** hired at least one; the RTW share of the full-time workforce peaks just under **2% in 2008**; the flow measure — RTW returners as a share of the prior year's retirement-eligible teachers — rises from zero in 1999 to **just over 35% in 2009** and **falls to zero in 2010** when the window lapses (fewer than a quarter of RTW teachers were still working full-time in 2010). Composition: −9.6 pp White, **+8.7 pp Black**, +1.0 pp other race; +16.5 years of experience; −7.9 pp with no advanced degree but +9.3 pp from "less competitive" colleges; +4.6 pp math, +2.3 pp communication, −5.2 pp "no discipline" classes; −4.8 pp elementary, +3.7 pp high school, +3.8 pp rural, −6.3 pp city; schools with **+3.71 pp** economically disadvantaged and **+4.86 pp** Black students; 29.6% of returners in the top quartile of disadvantage vs 20.7% in the bottom, 30.4% vs 17.8% for share Black; **54% returned to their pre-retirement school** (Jarrold-Grapes and Mahler, 2024, Tables 1-2, Figures 1-2). Denominator note: coefficients come from 807,156–927,456 teacher-year observations.
  - **NOT FOUND**: any published participation count for the 2019–2021 high-need programme, despite the statute's employer-reporting duty. Searched NCGA, Department of State Treasurer and press, 19 August 2026.
- **EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION.**
  - VERIFIED. *Peer-reviewed*: Jarrold-Grapes and Mahler (2024), *Education Policy Analysis Archives* 32(4). **Method: descriptive, not causal** — OLS of each teacher/school characteristic on an RTW indicator with year fixed effects, on NCERDC administrative data 1999–2009; RTW status identified from payroll **budget codes**. The authors state plainly that they "cannot say whether this policy incentivized more retirees to return" (no pre-policy identification of retirees), have **no vacancy or hiring data**, and that "the structure of the policy does not provide a natural experiment" (pp. 6, 12). Robustness: quartiles fixed at 1999 levels; and 5-6% of teachers in every quartile were retirement-eligible, which rules out compositional supply as the driver of the high-need skew.
  - VERIFIED. *Cost claim*: the annuity is paid whether or not the retiree works, so the marginal cost of an RTW hire versus a novice is the salary difference minus the novice's health benefits — "costlier than hiring just the novice teacher", but less than it appears (p. 12).
  - VERIFIED. *Double-dipping*: not formally tested. The authors infer it is unlikely because most RTW teachers had 30-40 years of experience, i.e. they were following the plan's own retirement incentive (pp. 12-13).
  - **VERIFIED, NEW — the counterweight our fichas lack.** The **Legislative Actuarial Note on HB 106 (2025)**, prepared by the Fiscal Research Division with two independent actuaries, prices exactly the risk the paper leaves untested. Gallagher (the systems' actuary) assumed **an additional 2.5%–10% probability of unreduced retirement when first eligible** during the bill's window for teachers in high-need positions, and that **65% of the teacher population** meets the high-need criteria; Aon (the General Assembly's actuary) assumed **+5% to +10%**. Both put the cost at **0.01%–0.02% of pay**, i.e. **USD 4.10m in FY2025-26 rising to USD 4.66m in FY2029-30** (General Fund USD 2.69m–3.06m).
  - NOT FOUND: any evaluation of the 2019–2021 programme; any study of RTW effects on student achievement or vacancies anywhere in NC.
- **AFTERMATH.** VERIFIED. The window lapsed in 2009 and returns went to zero. The 2019 programme sunset on schedule, 30 June 2021. **HB 106 (2025-26), "Revive High-Need Retired Teachers Program"** (Reps. Carver, Scott, Willis) would re-enact S.L. 2019-110 with two changes — a **two-month** rather than six-month gap, and re-employment by any "public school unit" rather than only local boards of education — applying from 2025-26 and expiring 30 June 2029. It passed Pensions and Retirement (1 April 2025) and a second committee (16 April 2025) and was re-referred to Appropriations, where it **has not moved**; no chamber vote recorded (NC General Assembly, 2026). Sponsor framing was modest: Rep. Carver told the committee "I don't consider this bill to be a long-term solution to the problem" (WRAL, 2025). NEW.
- **WHAT THIS CASE CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** That removing the pension penalty, with no bonus attached, can move a measurable share of a state's recently retirement-eligible teachers back into full-time classrooms — and that they land disproportionately in schools with more disadvantaged and more Black students without any targeting requirement — while the state's own actuaries price the same design as inducing 2.5–10 percentage points more early retirement at roughly USD 4m a year.
- **WHAT IT CANNOT.** It cannot support any claim about vacancies filled, student outcomes, displacement of other candidates, or cost-effectiveness; the study is descriptive with no counterfactual, and the "35% of retirement-eligible teachers returned" figure is a ratio of two administrative flows, not a re-entry rate for the retiree stock.

### United States (Georgia) — HB 385 (2022), O.C.G.A. § 47-3-127.1; performance audit 24-20 (April 2025); SB 150 (signed 5 May 2026)

- **CONTEXT.** VERIFIED. ~125,000 teachers statewide against ~6,000 vacant or not-fully-certified positions; statewide retention averaged **86%** between FY2020 and FY2024, with attrition dominated by unspecified resignations (75%), retirement (14%) and system-switching (6%); certification-pathway enrolments rose 41% and completions ~30% between 2016 and 2024 (5,300 completers). Systems report 30-59 days to fill a vacancy, longer in special education, maths, science and world languages (Georgia Department of Audits and Accounts, 2025, pp. 13-14). TRS assets grew from USD 78.8bn (FY2019) to USD 106.2bn (FY2024) (p. 15). Push side: the Professional Association of Georgia Educators campaigned for extension and liberalisation, citing "more than 5,000 certified teaching vacancies" (PAGE, n.d.). NEW.
- **MECHANISM.** VERIFIED. Certified teacher, retired **at least one year**, **≥30 years** creditable TRS service, employed **full-time** in preK-12 in one of the **three highest-need content areas of the employer's RESA**, determined annually by GaDOE from a five-year average of vacancy surveys; **employer pays both employer and employee contributions**, no new creditable service; early retirees excluded. Statutory sunset **30 June 2026**; the statute itself commissioned the audit. Price of a hire, from the audit (pp. 18-19): average salary **USD 78,487** (FY2023-24) plus **USD 20,391** average annual TRS contributions = **USD 98,878** per rehired retiree per year, against an FY2025 novice starting salary of **USD 43,592**; the auditors modelled an alternative eligibility rule (age 60 with 10 years) at USD 90,593, a saving of USD 8,285 per retiree. NEW detail.
- **TAKE-UP.** VERIFIED (auditor, not sponsor): **635** retired teachers worked full-time under HB 385 for at least one school year between FY2023 and FY2025, in **133 of ~240 school systems (55%)**; roughly **350 in any given year**, i.e. **under 1%** of ~125,000 teachers and against ~6,000 vacancies; associated TRS contributions **USD 11.1m**, under 1% of USD 31.5bn total contributions (USD 3.5m in FY2023, USD 7.5m in FY2024, <0.1% of contributions). Sponsor-side figure for comparison: TRS Executive Director Buster Evans told the Senate Retirement Committee that **428** retired educators were then serving under the programme, most in special education, maths and science (PAGE, 2025). NEW.
- **EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION.** VERIFIED. *Performance Audit Report 24-20* (Georgia Department of Audits and Accounts, April 2025), mandated by O.C.G.A. § 47-3-127.1. **Method**: review of law; interviews with GaDOE, the Professional Standards Commission, TRS and systems; analysis of TRS employment verifications and GaDOE CPI personnel data FY2019-FY2025; a comparison of **12 other southern states**; and a **census survey of school systems, 163 of 236 responding (69%)** — the auditors state results "should not be generalized to the entire population". Findings: the effect on both the shortage and on TRS is "minimal"; **73 respondents (45%)** said statutory provisions stopped them hiring a candidate they would otherwise have taken; **45 of the 72 non-users (63%)** could not find candidates both willing to work full-time and meeting the criteria; **103 (63%)** intend to use or continue using it. Exhibit 12 shows Georgia is the only one of nine compared states limiting eligibility by **both subject and geography** or requiring **30 years**, and one of only two requiring a **one-year** break (Arkansas 4 months, Florida and Virginia 6, Kentucky 1, Tennessee 60 days, Louisiana and North Carolina none). The auditors also flag the pension side — "return-to-work laws may incentivize earlier retirement" — and recommend an actuarial study before any loosening, plus repair of GaDOE's high-need survey (low response rate; questions that do not quantify vacancies or budgeted posts). **Independence caveat, worth stating in the paper:** the report notes that "the State Auditor is an ex-officio member of the Board of Trustees of the Teachers Retirement System of Georgia" (p. 29). NEW detail.
- **AFTERMATH.** VERIFIED, and this **resolves the flag in our ficha**. SB 150 (Sen. Billy Hickman) **as introduced in February 2025** would have extended the programme to **2034**, cut service from 30 to **25 years**, cut the wait from 12 months to **60 days**, and opened return in any area except athletics (PAGE, 2025; PAGE, n.d.). The **House committee substitute restored the 30-year requirement** and folded in HB 372 (Rep. Ballard), which moved determination of the three highest-need areas from the RESA to the **individual school system** and extended the sunset to **2030**; the Senate agreed to the House version unanimously (PAGE, 2026). The enacted law is therefore **2030 + 30 years + district determination**, confirmed by TRS Georgia ("expands the return-to-work program to allow an extension to 2030, maintaining the 30-year service requirement and allows the public school system to determine high-need areas") and by the Governor's office ("extending the program to 2030"), signed **5 May 2026**, effective **1 July 2026**; BillTrack50's reading adds a bar on dismissing a rehired retiree solely because designated high-need areas change. **CORRECTION to the fichas' hedge**: the "25 years / 2034" figures circulating in press are not wrong reporting of the Act — they are accurate reporting of the **Senate version**. Say so; do not merely say the claim is contradicted.
- **WHAT THIS CASE CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** That an arms-length audit of a stacked-eligibility rehire law found take-up under 1% of the workforce and judged the effect on both the shortage and the pension fund minimal, with 45% of surveyed employers reporting that the statutory screens blocked a hire they wanted to make — and that the legislature responded by relaxing the *targeting* screen while keeping the *service* screen the audit identified as binding.
- **WHAT IT CANNOT.** It cannot establish what a looser design would have produced (no counterfactual, no actuarial study of the alternative), nor cost-effectiveness: the audit prices inputs (USD 98,878 per retiree-year) but measures no outcome.

### United States (New Mexico) — HB 73 (2022, Ch. 20) and SB 133 (2025, Ch. 144), NMSA 1978 § 22-11-25.1

- **CONTEXT.** VERIFIED. NMERB at 30 June 2024: **62,970 active members**, **55,706 retirees and beneficiaries**, retiree payroll USD 1.34bn, average annual benefit USD 26,171, 220 participating employers, actuarial assets USD 17.2bn (NMERB, 2025b). At the time of HB 73, ERB estimated **7,628 active members (12.9%) were retirement-eligible** (LFC, 2022). Shortage denominator used in 2025: the New Mexico Educator Vacancy Report counted **1,259 vacancies**, and PED noted the USD 25,000 cap is below a full teacher salary of USD 50,000-70,000 (LFC, 2025). NEW.
- **MECHANISM.** VERIFIED. Return to any NMERB employer for up to **36 months** (60 since 2025), consecutive or not, **no salary or hour limits**, pension continues, conditional on: a **90-day layout**; **no pre-arranged return** (IRC bona fide retirement); **NMERB approval before starting**; **non-refundable employee and employer contributions** with **no new service credit**. Parallel tiers: ≤0.25 FTE; earnings below USD 15,000/yr (USD 25,000 since 2025); and the legacy 12-month-layout programme, **closed to new applicants from 1 January 2024**. 2024 rule changes added a **"safe harbor" for qualified retirees who return without prior approval**, allowed one programme switch per fiscal year, obliged every local administrative unit to adopt and publicise an internal RTW policy, and (from 1 July 2025) let NMERB fine employers for wage-report errors (NMERB, 2025b). NEW.
- **TAKE-UP.** VERIFIED, and this **extends our fichas by two years**. NMERB's own participation table (presented to the Investments and Pensions Oversight Committee, 18 July 2025):

  | Programme | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | ≤0.25 FTE | 686 | 671 | 642 |
  | Earnings < USD 15,000 | 390 | 390 | 414 |
  | **36-month RTW (HB 73)** | **154** | **361** | **506** |
  | Legacy 1-year programme | 1,716 | 1,637 | 1,396 |
  | PERA RTW | n/a | 997 | 1,009 |
  | **Total** | **2,792** (ERB only) | **4,056** | **3,967** |

  FY23 column from NMERB (2023); FY24-FY25 from NMERB (2025b). The flagship programme **more than tripled in two years (154 → 506)** while the legacy programme it replaces decayed; the ERB-only total is roughly flat. Against 55,706 retirees and beneficiaries, all NMERB return-to-work routes together engage under 6%. Publisher: the pension administrator, reporting to a legislative oversight committee — neither sponsor nor independent auditor.
- **EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION.** No outcome evaluation exists (NOT FOUND, searched 19 August 2026). But two **Legislative Finance Committee fiscal impact reports** — NEW, and the most useful new material for this case — evaluate the *cost* channel:
  - **HB 73 (2022)**: "HB73 may increase fund liabilities by providing an incentive for early retirement… Shortening the career and lowering the average age of pension recipients lengthens the time a benefit must be paid… The actual impact to the fund is very likely to be negative, but the extent… is unknown because it is not possible to determine how this provision may affect employee decisions to retire." The FIR explicitly contrasts the companion PERA bill (HB 50), which **removed the early-retirement incentive by restricting eligibility to those already retired at 31 December 2021** — a design device worth naming in the paper.
  - **SB 133 (2025)**: quantified. Raising the earnings exception from USD 15,000 to USD 25,000 costs the fund **USD 220,000 a year** in foregone contributions plus **USD 1.1m a year** if retirees migrate from the contributory route to the exception; the 36→60-month extension adds roughly **USD 2m** (scaled from ERB's USD 4m estimate for the rival 84-month bill), giving a recurring revenue loss of **USD 2.22m-3.10m per year from FY26**. ERB "cautioned that this amount should not be considered an upper limit… as any changes to retirement rates from increasing benefits are not known for several years". The report also names the binding constitutional constraint: **Article XX, Section 22 of the New Mexico Constitution** bars the legislature from increasing benefits without adequate funding, and gives ERB sole power to adopt actuarial assumptions.
- **AFTERMATH.** VERIFIED. SB 133 (Stewart/Wirth/Figueroa), as amended by the Senate Education Committee, became **Laws 2025, Chapter 144** (NMERB, 2025b); the competing HB 254 (84 months) did not pass. **CORRECTION to a minor point in our fichas**: the retiree denominator "~48,000" comes from a 2022 press release; NMERB's own 2024 valuation reports **55,706 retirees and beneficiaries** — use the valuation figure and state the definition.
- **WHAT THIS CASE CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** That the most permissive US design on record produced steadily rising but still small take-up — 506 users of the no-caps route in FY25 against ~56,000 retirees and beneficiaries — and that its legislature priced the loosening at roughly USD 2-3m a year of foregone pension funding while declining to close the early-retirement channel it had itself identified.
- **WHAT IT CANNOT.** Nothing about vacancies filled, subjects covered or retention of returners: NMERB counts participants, not posts, and no evaluation exists.
- **SOURCES.**
  - New Mexico Educational Retirement Board (2023), *Discussion on Return to Work*, IPOC, 31 October 2023, https://www.nmlegis.gov/handouts/IPOC%20103123%20Item%204%20E.%20Educational%20Retirement%20Board--Return%20to%20Work.pdf (accessed 4 August 2026, per case file).
  - **NEW** — New Mexico Educational Retirement Board (2025b), *Educational Retirement Board Updates*, handout to the Investments & Pensions Oversight Committee, 18 July 2025, https://www.nmlegis.gov/handouts/IPOC%20071825%20Item%201%20-%20ERB%20-%20Information%20and%20Updates.pdf (accessed 19 August 2026).
  - **NEW** — New Mexico Legislative Finance Committee (2022), *Fiscal Impact Report: HB 73/aHEC, Educational Retirees Returning to Work*, 1 February 2022, https://www.nmlegis.gov/Sessions/22%20Regular/firs/HB0073.PDF (accessed 19 August 2026).

### United States (Michigan) — PA 184 of 2022 (HB 4375) and PA 147 of 2023 (HB 4752), Public School Employees Retirement Act

- **CONTEXT.** VERIFIED. The nine-month sit-out descends from the 2010 anti-double-dipping rules; district leaders pressed for change on staffing grounds, describing it as "one tool… but not a permanent nor complete solution" (Michigan ORS, 2023; MEA, 2023). The **Michigan Education Association supported** HB 4752 while warning that "retirees returning to work is a temporary and limited solution to the educator shortage — continued efforts to recruit and retain educators are still essential, especially improvements in pay and respect for the profession" (MEA, 2023). NEW — this is the clearest union endorsement in the family, and the mirror image of Ontario.
- **MECHANISM.** VERIFIED. **PA 184 (effective 25 July 2022)** replaced the critical-shortage patchwork with two conditions — a **bona fide termination** and **nine consecutive months** retired — after which a retiree may hold any public-school position with **no earnings limit** and no loss of pension or insurance subsidy, whether hired directly, through a third party or as an independent contractor; **no sunset**. **PA 147 (signed 10 October 2023)** cut the wait to **six consecutive months** and permits work during the wait up to **USD 15,100 per calendar year**; exceeding it means temporary forfeiture of pension and insurance subsidy until the school employment ends; the six-month clock restarts in that case; special limits remain for retired superintendents; retirees who on 10 October 2023 combined active employment with third-party contracting may continue both. The FAQ states the provisions "are in effect until Oct. 10, 2028" (Michigan ORS, 2023).
- **TAKE-UP.** **NOT FOUND.** No official count of retirees working under PA 184 or PA 147 could be located on ORS/PSRU pages, in MPSERS reporting, or in press, searched 19 August 2026. This is a genuine and citable absence: Michigan simplified the rule and did not build a reporting series around it — the exact opposite of Georgia and New Mexico.
- **EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION.** None located. House and Senate Fiscal Agency analyses of HB 4752 and HB 4375 exist (2023-HLA-4752; 2023-SFA-4752-L; 2021-SFA-4375-N) but **could not be retrieved** on 19 August 2026 — legislature.mi.gov returned HTTP 503 to the fetch tool and a TLS chain error to curl. Flag as *not retrieved*, not as *non-existent*.
- **AFTERMATH.** VERIFIED. PA 184's framework is permanent; only PA 147's loosening carries the **10 October 2028** expiry, after which the nine-month regime returns unless renewed. No 2025-26 bill extending or repealing it was located (NOT FOUND, 19 August 2026).
- **WHAT THIS CASE CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** That a state can convert a discretionary, list-based critical-shortage regime into two bright-line conditions (bona fide termination plus a fixed wait) with union support, and can time-limit the loosening to the shortage episode by statute.
- **WHAT IT CANNOT.** Anything empirical. There is no participation series, no fiscal note we could read, and no evaluation; the case is a design exhibit only. The "most permissive Midwest state" claim remains unsourced and should stay out.
- **SOURCES.**
  - Michigan Office of Retirement Services (2022), "PA 184 of 2022 – Change to working after retirement rules", https://www.michigan.gov/psru/administration-and-compliance/archive-legislative-updates/pa-184-of-2022---change-to-working-after-retirement-rules.
  - Michigan Office of Retirement Services (2023), "Public Act 147 of 2023 working after retirement FAQ", https://www.michigan.gov/orsschools/pa-147-of-2023-faqs (accessed 19 August 2026).
  - **NEW** — Michigan Education Association (2023), "Changes to post-retirement return to work rules signed into law", https://mea.org/changes-to-post-retirement-return-to-work-rules-signed-into-law/ (accessed 19 August 2026). ### United States (Virginia) — Va. Code § 51.1-155(B)(3) critical-shortage rehire (2001), Chapters 690/707/708 of 2023, Executive Directive Three (2022), HB 1306 (2026, failed)

- **CONTEXT.** VERIFIED. The critical-shortage teacher programme dates from **2001**, when the 12-month break in service was set "as the result of considerable analysis by VRS, the Joint Legislative Audit and Review Commission (JLARC), and VRS and JLARC actuaries" (VRS, 2026, p. 9) — a much stronger provenance than our fichas record. Teacher plan scale at 30 June 2025: **160,949 active members**, of whom **8,738 (5%)** are eligible for an unreduced benefit (3,926 of them already past age 65/SSNRA) and **28,421 (17.7%)** for a reduced benefit — **37,159 (23.1%) eligible to retire**; service retirements ran 4,355-5,037 a year over FY2021-FY2025 (VRS, 2026, pp. 7, 13).
- **MECHANISM.** VERIFIED. A VRS retiree may draw salary and pension in a **critical shortage position** — teacher, principal/assistant principal, specialised student support (social workers, psychologists, nurses, behaviour analysts and other licensed health/behavioural roles), school bus driver, school security officer — identified through annual VDOE surveys, provided there is a Virginia licence, **no pre-arranged return**, and a **six-month break in service** (12 months before Chapters 690, 707 and 708 of 2023); employer VRS contributions are due; ERIP and disability retirees are excluded; sunset **1 July 2028**. Adjacent routes: part-time up to 80% of full-time after a one-calendar-month break; an interim position of up to six months after a one-month break with VRS approval (VRS, 2026, p. 3). Two non-pension constraints our fichas do not record, both material: (i) **VRS policy does not count the summer break** toward the break in service for teachers, on IRS bona-fide-severance grounds; (ii) VRS understands that **all positions in educational institutions require a 26-week separation** to avoid an Affordable Care Act penalty (VRS, 2026, pp. 13-14). NEW.
- **TAKE-UP.** **VERIFIED — and this is a direct CORRECTION to our fichas, which say "None published".** VRS's mandated report RD577 publishes annual counts of retirees filling critical shortage teacher positions, with denominators:

Parallel series: critical-shortage **bus drivers** 20 (2020-21) → 27 (2022-23) → 49 (to 28 Sept 2023) against 2,761 needed and 596 vacancies; **school security officers** 10 → 26 → 36, with a single division accounting for 16 (VRS, 2023, Tables 10-12). VRS's 2026 summary of this experience: "when the break in service was reduced from twelve months to six months, the number of retirees returning to work in eligible full-time positions generally doubled" (VRS, 2026, p. 4). Part-time returns are **not** measured at all — part-timers are not reported to VRS — so a 2022 employer survey was used instead: of 726 employers surveyed, 206 replied, **126 (61%)** had hired at least one retiree part-time in five years, mostly 1-5 at a time; the commonest part-time roles were administrative (69 employers), **substitute teaching (35)**, hazardous duty (24) and bus driving (21) (VRS, 2026, pp. 10-12). NEW.
- **EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION.** VERIFIED, NEW, and the strongest actuarial document in the family.
  - **RD577 (1 November 2023)**, mandated by Chapters 690/707/708, concluded that letting retirees work part-time *during* the break "does not meet the IRS and related requirements for a complete break in service", warned against further changes before impact data accumulate, and imported the **Idaho** evidence: PERSI's 2018 actuarial study found teachers returning to work in critical shortage positions were increasing liabilities for that group by about **4.3%**, at roughly **USD 28,192 of additional liability per returning teacher** (against a USD 25,000 average starting salary), and PERSI told a 2023 NASRA conference that in the four years after Idaho shortened its break in service **teachers were retiring nearly two years earlier** than before.
  - **VRS Fiscal Impact Statement on HB 1306 (29 January 2026)** prices the same mechanism for Virginia. Method: because "credible data on employee behavior related to these RTW programs" are lacking, VRS assumed that with a one-month break **all members reaching unreduced eligibility retire at the earliest allowed date**, excluding those eligible for reduced benefits (so the estimate is a stated lower bound). Result: Teacher plan unfunded liability rises from **USD 11.18bn to USD 13.91bn, +USD 2.73bn**; employer contribution rate from **12.20% to 14.63% of payroll (+2.43 pp)**, about **USD 290.8m a year** (USD 116.3m General Fund, USD 174.5m local); if all 8,738 unreduced-eligible teachers retired at once that would be an **85% increase** on normal annual retirements, a cash-flow risk for a fund already paying out over USD 7bn a year. VRS adds the qualitative point that matters most for the paper: such a change "likely will not net additional active teachers but instead will reclassify a portion of the current active population", and, because the teacher plan is pooled, divisions that use it heavily **shift costs onto divisions that do not**. It also notes that employer contributions on rehired retirees mitigate but do not neutralise the effect, and that subsidised **active-employee health insurance** is an independent driver of earlier retirement.
- **AFTERMATH.** VERIFIED. **HB 1306 (Del. Cornett, 2026)**, which would have cut the teacher break in service from six months to one, was **stricken from the docket by House Appropriations on 13 February 2026 by 22-0** after the VRS statement. The six-month rule and the 1 July 2028 sunset stand. Executive Directive Three (1 September 2022) remains what our fichas say it is — administrative signalling over a 2001 statute.
- **WHAT THIS CASE CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** That Virginia is the one jurisdiction where the same instrument has been measured on both sides — an eightfold within-year rise in critical-shortage teacher rehires after the break in service was halved, against an official actuarial estimate that halving it again would add USD 2.7bn to unfunded liabilities and mostly reclassify serving teachers rather than add new ones — and that the estimate was decisive: the bill died 22-0.
- **WHAT IT CANNOT.** The counts are of *positions filled by retirees*, not of vacancies closed or of pupils taught; the doubling is a before/after administrative comparison with no control group; and the USD 2.7bn figure rests on a deliberately extreme behavioural assumption that VRS itself labels unmodelled.

### United States (Louisiana) — La. R.S. 11:710 / 11:710.1; Act 394 of 2024 (HB 967, Riser); Act 344 of 2026 (SB 14, Price)

- **CONTEXT.** VERIFIED for the legislative track; the "~7,000 educators left in 2021-22" context figure remains press-sourced (KLFY, 2024) and is still **[KEY MISSING]** from references.md. Louisiana's distinguishing feature is that it uses a **market test** rather than a subject list, and that both liberalisations were scored by the Legislative Auditor's actuary before passage.
- **MECHANISM.** VERIFIED, with two parameters our fichas do not record.
  - The **critical-shortage certification** requires the employer to advertise and to receive **fewer than three certified applicants** — and the enacted text of Act 394 adds an explicit anti-displacement clause: "If a certified applicant who is not a retiree applies for an advertised position, such person shall be hired before any certified retiree is employed, unless fewer than three applicants have applied for the position, each of whom is certified in the critical shortage area being filled" (Act 394 of 2024, amending R.S. 11:710.1(G)(2)(a)). NEW — this is the only explicit new-entrant priority rule found in the whole family and is the exact mechanism Ontario reached for in 2012 by cutting the day cap instead.
  - **Act 344 of 2026** (effective 1 July 2026), per the Legislative Auditor's actuarial note on the enrolled bill: benefits are suspended for any retiree returning within 12 months; retirees **aged 65+**, retirees **certified in any area and reemployed in a critical shortage position**, and most contract/corporate-contract hires may return without suspension after that 12 months; all other retirees may earn up to **50% of final average compensation**, with a **dollar-for-dollar** reduction above it; **disability retirees are carved out** to their own regime; any directly employed full-time RTW retiree may instead **suspend the benefit and accrue a supplemental benefit**; **both employee and employer contributions are required for all RTW retirees**, with employee contributions **refunded** at the end of re-employment where no supplemental benefit accrues. The pre-2010 grandfathered group is reorganised into a separate section and retained.
- **TAKE-UP.** **NOT FOUND.** No TRSL or state count of retirees working under R.S. 11:710/710.1 was located (searched TRSL publications, LLA reports and press, 19 August 2026). The only mandated look-back is actuarial and distant.
- **EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION.** VERIFIED, NEW. Two actuarial notes by the Louisiana Legislative Auditor's Director of Actuarial Services (Kenneth Herbold):
  - **HB 967 (enrolled, 21 May 2024)**, rated *EN INCREASE APV*: "When retirees are permitted to return-to-work without a suspension of retiree benefit payments, participants are incentivized to retire earlier than they otherwise might… While it cannot be determined with any certainty the number of TRSL-covered positions that currently are eligible… this change is likely to have a significant impact on retirement behavior for anyone teaching in such a position, resulting in more active members retiring years earlier than they might have." The five-year fiscal table records only "Increase" — **the cost is asserted, not quantified**, in explicit contrast to Virginia.
  - **SB 14 (enrolled, 19 May 2026)**, also *EN INCREASE APV*: no immediate change in the present value of benefits, but "over the long-term we can expect to see retirement patterns shift earlier than currently assumed", putting upward pressure on employer contributions.
  - Both notes operate under the Louisiana Constitution's requirement that unfunded liabilities created by a benefit improvement be amortised over **no more than ten years**.
  - **Caveat on a wording conflict.** The HB 967 note describes the new route as available "without a waiting period or a suspension"; the enacted text conditions the critical-shortage subsection on "Except as provided in Subsection C" (the 12-month suspension rule), and TRSL's member guidance states a 12-month wait applies. Read the note's phrase as describing the removal of the *permanent* suspension, not of the 12-month wait; the 2026 note is unambiguous that the 12-month rule stands.
- **AFTERMATH.** VERIFIED. Act 344 orders **TRSL and the Legislative Auditor to report any identifiable actuarial impact after TRSL's experience study ending in FY2032**, at least 120 days before the 2034 Regular Session — i.e. the review is tied to an experience study, not to a calendar date, which is a better-designed audit loop than it looks. A companion 2026 act (HB 42, Bacala) creates a **phased retirement programme for public postsecondary institutions**, implementable no earlier than 1 July 2027 (TRSL, 2026). NEW — a Family A instrument on the exit rather than the re-entry margin.
- **WHAT THIS CASE CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** That a state can substitute a market test (fewer than three certified applicants) plus an explicit new-entrant priority rule for a centrally determined shortage list, and can attach a delayed, experience-study-triggered actuarial review instead of a sunset — while its actuary records the early-retirement cost qualitatively because the exposed population cannot be identified.
- **WHAT IT CANNOT.** No take-up, no effect, no cost figure. This remains a design case.

### Canada (Ontario) — OTPP re-employment day cap, Schedule 1 to the Teachers' Pension Act, 1990; temporary 95-day agreements 2020-2023; refusal of March 2024

- **CONTEXT.** VERIFIED and unchanged: the cap is a standing plan rule, not a programme, and it can only be moved by agreement of the two **co-sponsors** — the Ontario government and the **Ontario Teachers' Federation**. The Ontario College of Teachers is the certification regulator and has no role. Denominator for the pandemic episode: **142,000 retired members**, average age 72, of whom only about **60** had expressed interest by January 2022 (Global News, 2022).
- **MECHANISM.** VERIFIED. 50 days per school year for a participating employer, directly or indirectly, without effect on the pension; a retiree may finish the calendar month in which the limit is passed but receives no pension for subsequent months worked, resuming in the first clear month or on 1 September, whichever is earlier; tracking is the member's own responsibility. History of the dial: 20 days pre-1990 → **95 days for the first three post-retirement years, 20 thereafter** from 1990 (severe shortage) → **flat 50 days from September 2012**, announced May 2010 after the Globe and Mail investigation → temporarily **95 days** for OCT-certified retirees in publicly funded schools for 2020-21, 2021-22 and 2022-23, expiring **30 June 2023**, with monthly board reporting to the Ministry, shared with the OTF, as an OTF condition (streamlined for 2022-23) → back to **50 days** since 1 July 2023. OTPP confirms "no change to the 50-day re-employment limit" as of its January 2026 page (OTPP, 2026).
- **TAKE-UP.** Unchanged and still weak. The 2010 cost/crowding-out numbers stand: **CAD 108.3m** spent on retired teachers by the ten largest boards in 2008-09; **CAD 432.69** per day for retirees against **CAD 227.36** for new teachers; ~12,774 newly certified teachers in 2008 chasing ~6,500 openings; 2,861 supply assignments to retirees versus 1,638 to new graduates at one board (Hammer and Alphonso, 2010). **NOT FOUND**: any published aggregate from the monthly board reports required in 2021-22 and 2022-23 — the reporting existed, but the data have not been published (searched Ministry EFIS memoranda, OTF, OTPP and Ontario open data, 19 August 2026). This is itself a finding: Ontario collected the take-up data as a union condition and never released it.
- **EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION.** No evaluation exists. The only quantitative basis for either direction of movement is journalism (2010) plus the OTF's interest count (2022). The 2012 reduction is, as our fichas say, the only documented rollback of a re-entry incentive for equity reasons; the supporting context — Ontario's post-2005 teacher surplus, with early-career unemployment and underemployment peaking in 2013 — is documented in the College of Teachers' *Transition to Teaching* series (Ontario College of Teachers, 2016). NEW.
- **AFTERMATH.** VERIFIED. In **March 2024** the OTF declined the Ministry's request for a fourth consecutive extension for 2023-24. Attribution, which our fichas should keep straight: the Globe and Mail quotes **OTF President Yves Durocher** — "The pension plan is designed to provide an income to retired teachers; it is not a tool for addressing labour market challenges" — while CBC quotes **secretary-treasurer Ian Pettigrew**: "Deflecting responsibility onto retired teachers is neither a sufficient nor a sustainable option to address staffing challenges." Both are March 2024 and both are usable; the four major teachers' unions backed the refusal, calling retiree hiring a "Band-Aid". The OTF's own notice (18 March 2024) confirms the refusal but its detail sits in a PDF that could not be parsed on 19 August 2026.
- **WHAT THIS CASE CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** That where a pension rule is jointly sponsored, the re-entry price is a bargained variable rather than a policy lever: Ontario's cap has moved up in shortages and down for new-graduate equity, and in 2024 organised labour simply refused to move it again — with the monitoring data it had extracted as the price of earlier movements never published.
- **WHAT IT CANNOT.** It cannot say how many retirees the 95-day episodes actually mobilised, nor whether the 2012 reduction improved new-graduate hiring; both are unmeasured.

### France — Cumul emploi-retraite as it applies to retired State teachers (CPCMR arts L.84–L.86-1; Loi 2023-270 art. 26; Décret 2023-753)

- **CONTEXT.** VERIFIED, NEW, and much sharper than our fichas. The Senate Finance Committee's information report on teacher replacement (Olivier Paccaud, rapporteur spécial, 12 June 2025) documents the demand side: **unreplaced absences rose 49% in primary and 93.2% in secondary between 2018 and 2024**; the replacement pool is **10.9% of primary staff (17% of them contractuels)** and only **2.5% of secondary staff (46% contractuels)**. The report explicitly endorses the retiree route: the proposition de loi by Senator Else Joseph creating a **"réserve opérationnelle d'enseignants à la retraite"** for replacement duty "constitue une initiative intéressante, qui pourrait permettre de répondre à certaines problématiques de remplacement".
- **MECHANISM.** VERIFIED, and this contains a **CORRECTION**. Our fichas give the legal basis as Code de la sécurité sociale arts L.161-22 / L.161-22-1. For a retired **State civil servant** — which a career public-school teacher is — the governing regime is the **Code des pensions civiles et militaires de retraite, arts L.84 to L.86-1**, with only the first paragraph of CSS L.161-22 applying; the Service des Retraites de l'État states this explicitly. Parameters in force at 1 January 2026:
  - **Capped cumul (cumul plafonné)**: a retiree who does not meet the full-cumul conditions may earn, per calendar year, up to **EUR 8,198.04 plus one third of the gross pension** before any deduction; above that ceiling **only the excess is deducted** from the pension, and if the excess exceeds the pension, payment is suspended entirely. The ceiling applies to gross income of any description (salary, vacations, indemnities, premiums, fees), excluding the indemnité de résidence and family supplements.
  - **Full cumul (unlimited)**: from the exemption age (62 to 64 depending on birth year) with the full-rate quarters (166 to 172) **and all French and foreign pensions liquidated; or from the décote-cancellation age (65 to 67) with all pensions liquidated**.
  - **New pension rights**: since 1 September 2023 (for service from 1 January 2023) a returning retiree accrues new rights **only if** the full-cumul conditions are met **and** a **six-month carence** is respected where the return is to the **last employer** — which is what returning to the Éducation nationale means.
  - The SRE also stresses a point the paper should quote: this legislation "n'a d'effet que sur le paiement de la pension. Elle ne régit ni les conditions de recrutement, ni de rémunération des pensionnés" — the pension rules set the price, not the job.
  - **Announced reform**: from **1 January 2027** the cumul regime for new civil-service pensioners abandons the full-rate criterion in favour of an age-plus-liquidation test; for those who liquidated before the relevant age, activity income is deducted **from the first euro**. NEW — this is almost certainly the "2026 harmonising legislation" the Government invoked in its answer to QE 11063.
- **TAKE-UP.** **NOT FOUND**, as before: no official aggregate of retired teachers re-employed by rectorats under CER. Neither the Senate report nor the ministerial answers give one. The absence is now better evidenced: a Senate finance-committee inquiry into replacement capacity, with full access to ministry data, published no such figure.
- **EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION.** None on returns. The Senate report (2025) is an evaluation of the **replacement system**, not of retiree re-entry; its ten recommendations concern redeployment of posts freed by falling pupil numbers, individualised replacement targets for recteurs and DASEN, pooling between schools, departmental centralisation of primary replacement brigades, and one hour a month of short-term replacement duty inside secondary teachers' service. Retirees appear once, as an endorsement of a pending bill.
- **AFTERMATH.** VERIFIED. **Proposition de loi n° 633 (2023-2024)**, tabled in the Senate on **28 May 2024** by Else Joseph and colleagues, would insert a single article **L. 911-6-2** into the Code de l'éducation creating a reserve whose members — "notamment" retired teachers — would sign engagement contracts on terms set by the minister, and could be called on by heads of schools for ordinary teaching duties. It was referred to the Committee on Culture and Education; **no examination is recorded** as of 19 August 2026. Combined with the unanswered Bellamy question (QE 7305, lapsed June 2024) and the Government's defence of the contributory logic against Odoul (QE 11063, answered 12 May 2026), the pattern is consistent: repeated parliamentary initiative, no legislative movement.
- **WHAT THIS CASE CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** That in a single-employer system the general anti-abuse architecture of cumul emploi-retraite — a ceiling of EUR 8,198 plus a third of the pension, and a six-month carence attached to the last employer — operates as an unlegislated re-entry tax on teachers, and that four years of parliamentary attempts to carve out a teaching exception have produced an endorsement in a Senate report and a bill that has never been debated.
- **WHAT IT CANNOT.** Nothing quantitative: there is no measure of how many retired teachers return, how the ceiling binds in practice, or what the proposed reserve would cost or yield.

### Austria — Reactivation of retired teachers; prior-service exclusion (§ 26 VBG / BDG); ASVG earnings rules; Teilpension from 1 January 2026

- **CONTEXT.** VERIFIED, with an important **measurement tension** to state rather than resolve. Our fichas use OECD EAG 2025: **4,778 unfilled positions, 4.6%**, equal to 107% of the prior year's teaching graduates. The Court of Audit reports that the ministry's **first-ever** national shortage survey, at the start of 2023/24, found **267 open teaching posts** nationwide — "gering" relative to the workforce — precisely because the real shortage was absorbed by other means: overtime (**Mehrdienstleistungen +20%** over the audited period), out-of-field teaching in Mittelschulen (**+7% in Upper Austria, +9% in Tyrol**), and **Sonderverträge up +46% nationally** for staff without completed teacher training (Rechnungshof, 2025, TZ 2.1, pp. 23-24). The two numbers measure different things; the paper should present the pair as a caution about vacancy denominators, not correct one with the other. The RH's central charge: forecasts since **2009** predicted the shortage from 2018/19, no systematic measures followed, and the 2013 initial-training reform opened a one-year gap in graduations.
- **MECHANISM.** VERIFIED verbatim from the ministry's answer to a parliamentary question (BMBWF, 2023, reply to Question 19): for **beamtete** (established civil-servant) teachers the statement that returners are placed on the lowest salary step "ist diese Aussage korrekt" — the law excludes from prior-service crediting, across the whole federal service and the Landeslehrpersonen, any period that already generates a Ruhegenuss, because the legislator "will vermeiden, dass sich Zeiten sowohl für den Ruhegenuss als auch für die Entlohnung in einem neuen Dienstverhältnis auswirken". Result: "diese Personen beziehen ihre Pension und für die Unterrichtstätigkeit das Lehrpersonen-Einstiegsgehalt." For former **Vertragsbedienstete**, federal or Land teaching service **is** counted in setting the Entlohnungsstufe, so pre-retirement pay is broadly restored — "auf etwaige Ruhensbestimmungen im Zusammenhang mit der Alterspension wird jedoch hingewiesen."
  - **That last clause is a second price our fichas do not record.** VERIFIED, NEW. Under ASVG rules, a person drawing an **early** old-age pension (Korridorpension, Langzeitversichertenpension, Schwerarbeitspension) **loses it** for any month in which earnings exceed the **Geringfügigkeitsgrenze — EUR 551.10 a month in 2026** (frozen at the 2025 level), subject since 2024 to a tolerance of 40% of the monthly threshold (EUR 220.44) over the calendar year; from the **Regelpensionsalter** there is **no earnings limit at all**. A returning ex-contract teacher below the standard pension age therefore faces a near-total pension forfeiture, while one past it faces none.
  - **New standing instrument.** VERIFIED, NEW. From **1 January 2026** Austria offers a **Teilpension**: those eligible for an old-age or early old-age pension may reduce working time by 25%, 50% or 75% and draw the corresponding share of the pension account, the remainder continuing to accrue; a written agreement with the employer and an application to the pension insurance are required. From 2027 the employee's 10.25% pension contribution falls away for earnings after the Regelpensionsalter. These are the general labour-market instruments a teacher re-entry policy would now sit on top of.
- **TAKE-UP.** VERIFIED, unchanged and audited: **1,062** retired or retired-status teachers resumed teaching nationwide across **2018/19–2023/24** (Upper Austria 227, Tyrol 49), concentrated in **2021/22 and 2022/23**, after which "dieser Trend setzte sich nicht fort"; the RH acknowledges the contribution but stresses the measure's temporary character (Rechnungshof, 2025, pp. 23-25). Denominator ~123,000 teachers. The March-2023 snapshot (Vorarlberg 42, Upper Austria 36, Tyrol 10; 42 at compulsory schools plus ~30 at federal schools) remains citable with its differing definitions (APA/ORF, 2023). Note that Upper Austria's own recruitment plan lists "Wiedereinstieg von Pensionistinnen und Pensionisten" as one item under "direkte Personalmaßnahmen", alongside restricting sabbaticals, part-time and unpaid leave — i.e. retiree recall was one of several levers pulled on the same margin (Rechnungshof, 2025, p. 24 n. 4). NEW detail.
- **EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION.** VERIFIED. Two arms: the Court of Audit report *Lehrpersonaleinsatz* (Reihe Bund 2025/18, published 23 May 2025), covering the ministry and the Länder of Upper Austria and Tyrol for 2018/19–2023/24; and the qualitative work of Resch (2025a, 2025b) — nine episodic interviews with retired Austrian teachers (six returners, three non-returners) within a 14-interview study including experts, finding return decisions to be biographically rooted rather than pay-driven. As previously flagged: do **not** attribute the crediting-rule barrier to Resch; that comes from BMBWF (2023).
- **AFTERMATH.** VERIFIED. Salzburg flagged the crediting rule as needing federal legislative change; the ministry's 2023 answer describes the exclusion as deliberate policy, and **no amendment removing it was located** (NOT FOUND, 19 August 2026). The RH report was debated in the Nationalrat's Rechnungshofausschuss on 9 September 2025 (parliamentary record PK0760), which is the accountability step the paper's "audit loop" argument needs. Not opened in detail — flag as available.
- **WHAT THIS CASE CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** That Austria priced returning ex-civil-servant teachers at the beginner salary by deliberate anti-double-counting design, and — on audited national data — obtained 1,062 returners over six years concentrated in the two crisis years, with the trend then reversing, while the underlying shortage was mostly absorbed by overtime, out-of-field teaching and unqualified special contracts rather than by returners.
- **WHAT IT CANNOT.** It cannot attribute the modest take-up to the pay rule: there is no design variation, no comparison group, and the qualitative study finds pay is not the dominant motive.

### England — Teachers' Pension Scheme abatement (final-salary benefits only); the abatement that was *not* suspended in 2020-22

- **CONTEXT.** VERIFIED, NEW denominators. England measures the thing most systems cannot: the **return flow**. The DfE School Workforce Census records **17,274 teachers returning to state-funded schools in 2024/25**, up from 16,985 in 2023/24 and the **highest since 2014/15**, against a workforce of **468,300 FTE / 513,400 headcount**, **41,200 FTE leavers (2023-24)** and **2,200 vacancies (5 per 1,000 teachers, down from 2,800 the year before)**. So returners are of the same order of magnitude as leavers, and roughly eight times the vacancy count — the reserve is already flowing, without any pension incentive attached.
- **MECHANISM.** VERIFIED, unchanged. Only **final-salary Age or Premature** benefits are abated: in any tax year the retiree may earn up to their index-linked **"salary of reference" minus the annual pension** before the pension is suspended for the remainder of the scheme year. **Career-average benefits — all service since 2015 — are not abated** (except ill-health), and actuarially reduced early-retirement benefits are never abated. Compliance runs through employers' Monthly Data Collection or a **Certificate of Re-employment**, with overpayments recoverable (Teachers' Pensions, n.d.).
- **TAKE-UP.** No count of pensioners returning under abatement rules is published (NOT FOUND). The national returner series above is the nearest available and is **not** a measure of this rule: it counts all returners to service, most of whom are not pensioners.
- **EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION.** No evaluation. But there is one **decision** worth more than an evaluation, and it is NEW to our material. Asked in January 2022 whether TPS abatement would be suspended for retired teachers answering the call to cover COVID absence — as **section 45 of the Coronavirus Act 2020** had done for the NHS scheme — the Government declined. The answer to **PQ 104312** (asked by Alec Shelbrooke, 14 January 2022; answered 24 January 2022 by Robin Walker, Minister of State for School Standards) reasons that returning retirees "will not be subject to potential abatement of their teachers' pension because it is expected that they will be employed by supply teacher agencies, which tend not to be participating Teacher Pensions' Scheme (TPS) employers", that short and part-time appointments would in any case produce earnings below the threshold, and that the teaching situation differed in scope and duration from the NHS case. **The barrier was left in place because the labour market routes around it** — through agencies that are outside the scheme, and therefore outside pension accrual. That is the single most quotable sentence in the England case, and it reframes the "dissolving barrier" story: the barrier dissolves partly by scheme succession and partly by casualisation.
- **AFTERMATH.** The rule stands; the abated population shrinks mechanically as final-salary entitlements age out. No amendment or consultation on TPS abatement was located for 2023-2026 (NOT FOUND, 19 August 2026).
- **WHAT THIS CASE CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** That England's pension barrier to re-entry is both narrowing by scheme succession and, in the one episode where suspending it was on the table, judged unnecessary because returning retirees would be engaged through agencies outside the scheme — with a national returner flow of about 17,000 a year that owes nothing to the pension rule.
- **WHAT IT CANNOT.** It cannot show that abatement deters anyone, nor that its narrowing has produced any returns: no behavioural evidence exists in either direction.
- **SOURCES.** Existing: Teachers' Pensions (n.d.); Department for Education (2025a), *School Workforce in England: Reporting Year 2024*.
