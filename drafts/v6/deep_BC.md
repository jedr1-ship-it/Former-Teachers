### New Zealand — certification-barrier package (Education and Training Act 2020, Sch. 3; TER exemption; Budget 2025 fee funding), standing as at 7 August 2026

- **CONTEXT.** Not a programme but a stack of three separate instruments operated by two bodies: the Ministry of Education (Te Tāhuhu o te Mātauranga) for money, the Teaching Council of Aotearoa New Zealand for registration. Our team label "Returning to Teaching pathway" remains a label, not an official name (confirmed again this session — no such programme name appears on either body's site). The system context has changed materially since the fichas were written: on 23 June 2026 Parliament gave a third reading to the **Education and Training (System Reform) Amendment Bill**, transferring responsibility for setting teaching-workforce standards and criteria (including the Code of Conduct and Initial Teacher Education programmes) from the Teaching Council to the **Secretary for Education**, with effect from **6 July 2026** subject to Royal assent (Ministry of Education, 2026b — VERIFIED, page dated 24 June 2026). The Council continues to exist; it loses standard-setting authority. Any statement in the paper about "the Teaching Council decides X" must therefore be dated.
- **MECHANISM.** Three layers, all confirmed live on the Ministry's relief-teaching page as updated **7 August 2026** (Ministry of Education, 2026a — VERIFIED, quotations below are verbatim from that page):
  1. *Statutory on-ramp without a certificate*: "If a registered teacher has no practising certificate, they can relief teach for up to 20 half days per calendar year." Beyond that, "The Teaching Council can issue an Extension to Teach beyond 20 half days when their practising certificate has expired and there is an offer of employment." Statutory basis Education and Training Act 2020, Sch. 3 (Council registration policy cites cl. 10(9)).
  2. *Refresh exemption keyed to a job offer*: for a returner renewing with a relief-teaching offer, "it does not matter if they have not taught in five years or more and will not need to complete a Teacher Education Refresh (TER) programme"; instead "The Teaching Council may ask that a Kia Maia | Future-Ready Teaching Plan is completed alongside the hiring principal or tumuaki." The Council's own TER page confirms that a **job offer** is one element the exemption panel weighs (Teaching Council, 2026b — PARTIAL; the TER page returned HTTP 503 on direct fetch, content from the Council's indexed text). No expiry date is now shown for the exemption — consistent with the fichas' "mainstreamed, not sunset" reading.
  3. *Zero certification cost*: "The government will pay the fees and levies for issuing or renewing teaching practising certificates until 30 June 2028."
- **NEW — the money behind layer 3, and its true scope.** The fee waiver is not a returner measure. It is **Budget 2025**: announced 28 April 2025 by Education Minister Erica Stanford, **NZD 53.3 million over three financial years**, covering fees and levy for practising certificates issued or expiring (and renewed) between **1 July 2025 and 30 June 2028**, worth **up to NZD 550 per teacher**, benefiting **around 115,000** school and early-learning teachers, and also covering Limited Authority to Teach application fees (New Zealand Government, 2025b; Ministry of Education, 2025e — PARTIAL: Beehive release and Ministry fact-sheet content obtained via search index; the underlying Ministry fact-sheet PDF URL is recorded below). This is a **universal** cost removal that happens to fall on returners, not a targeted returner subsidy.
- **TAKE-UP.** NOT FOUND, again and specifically. Searched the Teaching Council's publications/annual-report series for counts of Extensions to Teach granted, TER completions and TER exemptions granted: no such series located. No participant counts on any Ministry page. Denominator for the fee measure is the whole workforce (≈115,000), so it cannot be read as returner take-up.
- **EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION.** None. No evaluation, audit or study of any of the three layers was located.
- **AFTERMATH.** Layers 1-3 all live as at 7 August 2026. Layer 2 has hardened into standing policy (no expiry shown). Layer 3 has a hard sunset of 30 June 2028. The Open Polytechnic TER programme remains closed to new learners. The regulator itself is being restructured from 6 July 2026 (above).
- **WHAT IT CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** That New Zealand is the corpus's cleanest example of *standing* de-frictioning keyed to the market test — the refresh requirement is waived precisely when an employer has already offered work — combined with a low-commitment 20-half-day on-ramp and, since July 2025, zero certificate cost. That the design is now mainstreamed rather than sunset, which is the opposite of the annual-waiver anti-pattern (cf. Ireland).
- **WHAT IT CANNOT SUPPORT.** Any effectiveness or take-up claim whatsoever. It cannot support calling the fee waiver a returner incentive: it is a NZD 53.3m universal measure for ~115,000 teachers. It cannot support present-tense claims about Teaching Council authority without dating them to before 6 July 2026.

### United States (Virginia) — Executive Directive Number Three, signed 1 September 2022

- **CONTEXT.** Signed by Governor Glenn Youngkin at Colonial Forge High School, Stafford County, on 1 September 2022, alongside the launch of the "Bridging the Gap Initiative"; the directive issues **nine actions**, of which the licensing action is the one that mattered for re-entry, since the pension side rested on pre-existing statute (Commonwealth of Virginia, 2022; SwailLandis, 2023, p. 2 — VERIFIED via the VDOE-distributed brief). The stated trigger in the directive itself is learning loss: "The widening achievement gaps in the latest results from our Standards of Learning state assessments demand action" (quoted in SwailLandis, 2023, p. 2).
- **MECHANISM.** The operative licensing sentence — the one to quote — directs the Superintendent of Public Instruction to **"use all discretion within law to issue teaching and renewal licenses, including to teachers licensed in another state and retired teachers whose licenses may have lapsed"** (Commonwealth of Virginia, 2022; PARTIAL — the directive PDF is no longer served from governor.virginia.gov (404 on the URL cited by SwailLandis, 2023; the Youngkin-era newsroom is gone) and web.archive.org is unreachable from this environment. The wording is corroborated identically across the VDOE-distributed brief, the Governor's September 2022 release as indexed, and contemporaneous Virginia Mercury/Dogwood coverage). The nine actions also include altering retirement-system treatment so retired teachers can choose to return; discretionary recruitment/retention grants; teacher apprenticeship programmes; childcare provision and subsidies for school staff; and improving the annual division survey to target "critical, unfilled teaching positions" (SwailLandis, 2023, p. 2, quoting Commonwealth of Virginia, 2022, p. 2 — VERIFIED).
- **TAKE-UP.** **NOT FOUND for the target group.** No count exists anywhere of licences expedited to *retired teachers whose licences had lapsed*. What Virginia does publish is a different number for a different population, and the paper must not merge them (see AFTERMATH).
- **EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION.** None. No evaluation of ED-3 exists. The only official self-assessment is the VDOE news release below, which is a package claim, not an evaluation.
- **AFTERMATH — NEW, and it changes the story.** The directive's licensing thrust was carried into statute *for out-of-state teachers, not for lapsed-licence returners*: **House Bill 632 (2024)** "established universal licensure by reciprocity for teachers holding valid out-of-state licenses with full credentials and at least three years of teaching experience in non-virtual classroom settings" (Virginia Department of Education, 2025, p. 7 — VERIFIED from the statutory report PDF, 1 November 2025). VDOE subsequently reported **4,535 universal teacher licences granted to date**, alongside a fall in the statewide teaching-vacancy rate from **3.9% (2023/24) to 2.5% (2025/26)** — a 35.9% decrease, the third consecutive annual decline — with 18 divisions and all Governor's Schools reporting zero vacancies, state support for teacher salaries up 19.3% over four years, and 84.3% of teachers responding favourably on intention to keep teaching in Virginia (up 6.3 points on 2022) (Virginia Department of Education, 2026 — PARTIAL: doe.virginia.gov blocks automated retrieval (403); figures taken from the indexed text of the release; **publication date not established**). Note also that Virginia's own 2025 report to the General Assembly credits ED-3 only in general terms: it "bolsters and strengthens the Department's ability to remove obstacles that prevented qualified individuals from filling teacher vacancies in critical shortage areas" (Virginia Department of Education, 2025, cover letter, 1 November 2025 — VERIFIED).
- **WHAT IT CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** That an executive directive is a fast, legislature-free lever on the licensing margin, and that its verbatim instruction — use all discretion within law to issue renewal licences to retired teachers whose licences may have lapsed — is a clean statement of the lapsed-licence problem as a *discretionary administrative* obstacle rather than a legal one. That the durable statutory follow-through (HB 632, 2024) went to out-of-state reciprocity, i.e. the easier margin, with 4,535 licences granted.
- **WHAT IT CANNOT SUPPORT.** Attributing Virginia's vacancy decline (3.9%→2.5%) to ED-3, or to the licensing action within it: the release bundles salaries, reciprocity, apprenticeships and pipeline work. It cannot support presenting 4,535 as returners — those are out-of-state, currently-licensed, experienced teachers, the opposite of the lapsed-licence group. And no number exists for the group the directive actually named.

### United States (Louisiana) — Act 344 of 2026 (formerly SB 14, Sen. Price), effective 1 July 2026

- **CONTEXT.** Act 344 replaced Louisiana's stratified return-to-work rules with "a single RTW law for all TRSL retirees regardless of retirement date". The 2026 Regular Session ran 9 March – 1 June 2026; eight bills and one resolution affecting TRSL passed (Teachers' Retirement System of Louisiana, 2026 — VERIFIED in full, session news dated 2 June 2026).
- **MECHANISM — the Family B element, verbatim.** Act 344 "**Revises Critical Shortage:** Expands eligible critical shortage areas/positions to include adult education, orientation and mobility specialists, and educators of the visually impaired; **specifies that retirees hired under critical shortage only have to be certified teachers (instead of certified in subject area of critical shortage)**; and streamlines employer advertising and certification requirements for declaring a critical shortage" (Teachers' Retirement System of Louisiana, 2026 — VERIFIED).
- **NEW — the Family A elements in the same Act, which the fichas under-report.** (i) *Age 65 provision*: "Enables retirees who are at least age 65 to RTW without an impact to their benefit" — a new, categorical, age-based exemption. (ii) *Earnings limit*: raised from 25% to **50% of final average compensation** for all retirees in any position. (iii) *Grandfathered group retained*: pre-1 July 2010 retirees return with no benefit impact. (iv) *12-month waiting period retained*. (v) *Contract/corporate-contract scope narrowed* to enumerated K-12 functions (instructional programmes, pupil support, instructional staff services, school administration, food services) and, in postsecondary, professor/adjunct/instructor/researcher roles. (vi) **A statutory audit loop**: Act 344 "requires TRSL and the Louisiana Legislative Auditor to determine any identifiable actuarial impact of the new RTW law and provide a report to the chairs of the House and Senate retirement committees prior to the 2034 Regular Legislative Session" (all VERIFIED, same source).
- **TAKE-UP.** None — the Act took effect on 1 July 2026, seven weeks before this research date. NOT FOUND, and structurally cannot exist yet.
- **EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION.** None yet, by design: the first evidence instrument is the TRSL/Legislative Auditor actuarial report due before the 2034 session.
- **AFTERMATH.** In force since 1 July 2026. Reported in the state press as letting "some retired Louisiana teachers return to classrooms while keeping their benefits" (KSLA, 2026 — PARTIAL, headline and framing only).
- **WHAT IT CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** That certification *matching* — not certification itself — is an adjustable screen, and that dropping the subject-match requirement is a distinct, cheap licensing lever separable from relicensing. That legislatures bundle licensing relief inside pension bills, so a paper looking only at licensure statutes will miss it. And — a stronger point than the ficha makes — that Louisiana is the corpus's second case (with Georgia) of a legislature writing its own **audit trigger** into a return-to-work liberalisation, here with an eight-year fuse.
- **WHAT IT CANNOT SUPPORT.** Any outcome claim. Nor the implication that the certification change is the Act's main content: it is one of six revisions, and the earnings-limit doubling and the age-65 exemption are the larger levers.

### Ireland — Career Break Scheme, Chapter 7 of Circular 0054/2019; annual suspension of substitute restrictions (TC/IN 0005/2025, 26 June 2025)

- **CONTEXT — NEW, and it corrects our framing.** The Department's own framing of the 2025/26 renewal is *not* crisis. In the announcement, the Department reports that "the number of unfilled teaching posts continues to be low": as of **November 2024, 2.1% of the 74,611 allocated posts were unfilled**; **79,390 teachers** were employed (43,163 primary including special schools, 36,227 post-primary); **3,700 newly qualified teachers** registered in 2024; teaching held ~6% of first-choice CAO applications consistently 2017-2024 (Department of Education and Youth, 2025d — VERIFIED, press release 27 June 2025, last updated 1 July 2025). The Minister's own words position the measures as stop-gaps: "While these are not long-term solutions, they will continue to support schools to access qualified teaching for the students in their schools."
- **MECHANISM.** Two measures were extended together for 2025/26 (VERIFIED, same release): (i) **the relaxation of the limits on the maximum substitute cover a teacher may provide while on career break or job-sharing**, at both primary and post-primary — "Teachers who are job sharing will continue to be able to work as a substitute teacher in any school, as long as they are off duty. Additionally, teachers who are on a career break can continue to work as a substitute teacher"; and (ii) the **Teaching Hours Extension Scheme**, under which post-primary teachers on full 22-hour contracts may provide up to **35 additional substitution hours per designated term**. The underlying restriction suspended is Chapter 7, Paragraph 8.1 of Circular 0054/2019; the operative instrument for 2025/26 is Information Note **TC/IN 0005/2025** (26 June 2025), which replaced TC/IN 0009/2024.
- **TAKE-UP.** NOT FOUND, again. No count of career-break teachers substituting, and the Department's *Teacher Statistics* career-break series remained unretrievable this session. Denominator context is now available (74,611 allocated posts; 79,390 teachers employed), which is more than the fichas had.
- **EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION.** None. No evaluation of the suspension exists; the 27 June 2025 release makes no effectiveness claim for it.
- **AFTERMATH.** The suspension has now run at least 2021/22 → 2025/26 on annual renewal. **A 2026/27 renewal could NOT be confirmed** as at 19 August 2026: no gov.ie information note or press release for 2026/27 was located, and no INTO notice (into.ie blocks automated retrieval, HTTP 403). Treat 2026/27 status as **NOT FOUND**, not as lapsed. Separately, a national **workforce plan for the education sector** and an **education convention** were in preparation as of June 2025 — the place where a standing rule could replace the annual note.
- **WHAT IT CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** The Lesson-8 anti-pattern in its purest form: a measure renewed annually for five consecutive years, each time as a temporary suspension, which returners and schools cannot plan around — and which the Minister herself describes as "not long-term solutions". Also that Ireland activates *temporary leavers* (people on career break, people job-sharing) rather than leavers, the cheapest reserve to reach.
- **WHAT IT CANNOT SUPPORT.** Any take-up or effectiveness claim. It should not be presented as re-entry legislation for former or retired teachers. And — flagged below — it should not be motivated by an acute-shortage story the Department itself does not tell.

VERIFIED. **NEW.**
  - Department of Education (2019), *Circular 0054/2019*; Department of Education and Youth (2025), *Information Note TC/IN 0005/2025* (both already in references.md).
- **CONTRADICTIONS WITH EXISTING FICHAS.** The ficha motivates the Irish case with "1,847 vacant posts reported July 2025 (press snippet)". The Department's own published figure for the nearest period is **2.1% of 74,611 allocated posts unfilled as of November 2024** (≈1,570), presented explicitly as "relatively low". **Correction: either drop the 1,847 press figure or present it beside the Department's own measure and denominator (Department of Education and Youth, 2025d).** Using an unsourced press number to assert an Irish crisis, when the responsible ministry publishes a low-vacancy framing in the same document that extends the measure, is the kind of mismatch a reviewer will find.

### United States (Michigan) — Lindsay, Gnedko-Berry and Wan (2021), REL 2021-076: licence survival and what returners actually want

- **CONTEXT.** REL Midwest descriptive study for the Michigan Department of Education, published **April 2021** as REL 2021-076. Two data layers: (a) Michigan Department of Education certification records linked to employment records; (b) a survey of certified non-teaching teachers administered 2019/20 (VERIFIED from the full report PDF).
- **MECHANISM (what it measures).** Sample: **141,810** teachers initially certified 1961-2018 whose certificate had not been revoked, aged 18-60 plus over-60s with recent certification activity. Split: **80,558** teaching in 2017/18; **18,367** not teaching in 2017/18 but had taught at some point since 2013/14; **42,885** had not taught since 2013/14 — i.e. **61,252** not teaching, of whom 70% had not taught recently. Survey: **17,551 responses, a 30% response rate**, explicitly non-representative; 7,709 excluded on screening; analytic sample **9,842** non-teaching (9,089 previously taught, 753 never taught).
- **KEY FINDING FOR FAMILY B (verbatim table, VERIFIED).** Certificate status by group — **valid 94.3% / expired 5.7%** for those teaching in 2017/18; **valid 55.8% / expired 44.2%** for those who had taught since 2013/14 but not in 2017/18; **valid 51.5% / expired 48.5%** for those who had not taught since 2013/14 (Table 1, p. 6). Roughly half the visible reserve would have to re-certify before returning.
- **NEW FINDING THAT CUTS AGAINST OUR OWN FRAMING.** The report's regression of incentives on respondent characteristics (Table 3, p. 11) finds that, after adjustment, respondents **who had previously taught** were significantly more likely to select **financial** incentives — "Ability to maintain teacher retirement benefits\*\*\*", "An increase in salary\*\*\*", "An improvement in benefits\*\*", "Better support from school leaders\*\*" — whereas respondents who had **never taught** were the ones significantly more likely to select **certification and job-availability** incentives: "Availability of full-time teaching positions\*\*\*", "A less costly way to renew or earn certification\*\*\*", "An easier way to renew or earn certification\*\*\*". In the report's own words: "respondents who had never taught were more likely than those who had previously taught to select incentives related to certification and job availability, including a less costly or easier way to renew certification (or earn certification if their certificate had expired) and the availability of full-time teaching positions" (p. 11).
- **NEW — the report's own recruitment denominator.** REL recommends targeting "about **50,000** nonteaching certified teachers", excluding the ~22% who left classroom teaching to become administrators or instructional leaders and are unlikely returners (p. 15). Also: 16% of the 61,252 may live 20+ miles outside Michigan; 93% were younger than 60; 76% women; 57% elementary-certified; 11% from a racial/ethnic minority group.
- **TAKE-UP / AFTERMATH.** Not applicable — a study, not a programme. No follow-up wave located. The companion REL Midwest study *Supports Associated with Teacher Retention in Michigan* (REL 2021-108, September 2021) is a different, retention-side product and must not be conflated with it.
- **WHAT IT CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** That licence lapse is a leading, measurable marker of reserve decay: 44-49% expired among the non-teaching against 5.7% among the teaching, on administrative records with N=141,810 — the empirical warrant for Family B existing at all. That the honest recruitable denominator is ~50,000, not 61,252, by the authors' own recommendation.
- **WHAT IT CANNOT SUPPORT — sharpened.** It cannot support the claim that removing relicensing friction is what would bring *former teachers* back. On this evidence the certification-cost/ease incentives are what the **never-served** want; actual former teachers rank pension retention and salary first. The defensible formulation is: **licence lapse is a barrier that must be cleared, but clearing it is not by itself an incentive for people who have taught.** Also: motivations are non-representative (30% response rate, stated in the report), and the 61,252 is not available supply (expired certificates, ~16% out-of-state, subject mismatch, 22% in leadership roles).
- **SOURCES.** Lindsay, J., N. Gnedko-Berry and C. Wan (2021), *Michigan Teachers Who Are Not Teaching: Who Are They, and What Would Motivate Them to Teach?* (REL 2021-076), REL Midwest / IES, https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED611790.pdf (accessed 19 August 2026), pp. 5-6, 11, 15. VERIFIED. (already in references.md)
- **CONTRADICTIONS WITH EXISTING FICHAS.** (i) The ficha's Family B header sentence — "the family's core evidence is that licence lapse is a leading marker of reserve decay... and that friction removal works best as a standing rule" — overreaches on the second clause: Michigan supplies no evidence about what friction removal achieves. (ii) The ficha's survey rendering ("33% put higher salary in their top-three reasons... an increase in salary was the most frequently selected incentive") is right but incomplete: add the previously-taught vs never-taught split, which is the finding with policy teeth. (iii) The precise group sizes (80,558 / 18,367 / 42,885) and the authors' ~50,000 target were not in the ficha; add them.

### Netherlands — *Regeling tegemoetkoming herintreders primair onderwijs* (Stcrt. 2017, 62002), Nov 2017 – Oct 2020; and the RAL → RAP → *onderwijsregio's* succession

- **CONTEXT.** Unchanged and correct as in the fichas: EUR 2,500 one-off to the **school board** (not the teacher) per returner appointed for ≥6 months who had not taught for ≥12 consecutive months; announced in the 26 June 2017 Kamerbrief as a measure to activate a silent reserve then estimated at **at least 31,000** in primary education alone; original ceiling EUR 1.25 million, "room for 500"; first come, first served; administered by DUS-I.
- **TAKE-UP — re-searched, still short of a final answer.** Just over **100** returners subsidised in 2018; **nearly 200 cumulative** by autumn 2019; the 2019 amendment (Stcrt. 2019, 28510) *lowered* annual ceilings to EUR 300,000 (2019) and EUR 250,000 (2020) — at most **120 and 100 grants** — with the toelichting stating the ceiling was set lower "omdat de subsidie toen niet was uitgeput" (because the budget had not been exhausted). **Final 2019 and 2020 grant counts remain NOT FOUND** after a second round of searching (DUS-I, OCW annual reports/*OCW in cijfers*, rijksbegroting, Kamerstukken). The honest formulation stands: *ceilings of 120 and 100; final uptake never published.* One caution: a secondary aggregator renders the regulation as having "expired on 1 January 2020"; the authoritative status line at wetten.overheid.nl (BWBR0040130) gives applications closing 31 October 2020 and repeal on **1 January 2022**. Use the latter.
- **EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION.** No dedicated evaluation of the regeling — confirmed again. For the successor infrastructure, the RAP evaluation (De Jong et al., 2022) remains the only substantive evidence: process-focused, self-reported, no causal estimate; side-entry goals mostly met, **tailored support to herintreders among the least-realised activities** (p. 60), and several regions reporting more side-entrant and returner applications than they had places (p. 37).
- **NEW — what happened after RAP, and the disappearance of returners from the evidence base.** RAP was folded into the national *onderwijsregio's* structure. Berenschot is running a **two-year, four-report evaluation for OCW (2025-26)**. The **first interim report (19 June 2025)** lists herintreders only as one of several recruitment target groups — "Specifieke wervingsstrategieën gericht op verschillende doelgroepen, waaronder leerlingen uit het voortgezet onderwijs, studenten van het middelbaar beroepsonderwijs, zij-instromers en herintreders" (p. 24) — with no goals, counts or results attached (Berenschot, 2025a — VERIFIED, PDF text-mined). The **second interim report (3 December 2025)** describes a now nationally complete network of **50 onderwijsregio's** and reports "veelbelovende ontwikkelingen" alongside governance complexity, but **contains no mention of herintreders or the stille reserve at all** (Berenschot, 2025b — VERIFIED, full text searched; zero hits for "herintre" and "stille reserve"). The trajectory is therefore: dedicated instrument (2017-20, under-subscribed, unevaluated) → named fundable goal inside a regional scheme (RAP, least-realised activity) → not separately reported at all (onderwijsregio's, 2025).
- **WHAT IT CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** The emblematic mismatch: the country with the best-measured reserve attached a marginal, board-paid, under-subscribed EUR 2,500 instrument to it — and the regulator itself wrote the under-subscription into law by cutting the ceilings. And a **new, sharper** point: as returner activation was mainstreamed into ever-larger regional structures it became progressively less visible, until by the second evaluation of the successor structure it is not reported on at all. Mainstreaming an activation goal can be a way of losing it.
- **WHAT IT CANNOT SUPPORT.** "Subsidies don't work" (tiny, paid to boards, never evaluated); any causal effect on shortages (no evaluation attempts one); and any claim about final uptake in 2019-20.

### Portugal — Decreto-Lei n.º 51/2024, de 28 de agosto: Article 5 (returners), Article 6 (stay-on supplement), Article 7 (higher-education staff)

- **CONTEXT.** Part of "+Aulas +Sucesso" (announced June 2024; minister Fernando Alexandre); exceptional and temporary staffing measures for public pre-school, basic and secondary education. **Primary text re-verified in full this session** from the DGAE PDF of *Diário da República*, 1.ª série, n.º 166, 28-08-2024.
- **MECHANISM — verbatim from the decree (VERIFIED).**
  - **Art. 5(1)-(2):** fixed-term public-employment contracts with retired teachers to meet temporary needs in a **grupo de recrutamento deficitário** or **escola carenciada**, requiring ministerial authorisation grounded in "interesse público excecional"; **not applicable to teachers retired more than five years**.
  - **Art. 5(3):** the contract requires "o exercício efetivo de funções letivas" — actual classroom teaching.
  - **Art. 5(4)-(5):** returners "mantêm a respetiva pensão de aposentação ou de velhice, acrescida de uma compensação adicional correspondente ao índice remuneratório do **1.º escalão** da escala indiciária... em função do número de horas letivas atribuídas", with a full timetable defined as **20 weekly teaching hours** (pre-school and 1st cycle) or **14 hours** (other levels, including special education).
  - **Art. 5(6)-(11):** proposal by the school director, instructed with CGA/Social Security information; authorisation delegable to the Director-General of DGAE; wholly electronic procedure; a **bolsa** of retired teachers ranked by recruitment group according to professional graduation held at retirement; and an **annual contingente** fixed by joint despacho of the ministers for finance, public administration and education.
  - **Art. 6:** a **monthly acréscimo remuneratório of EUR 750.00** for teachers who meet the legal conditions for retirement and remain in effective teaching duties; on application to the school director; cumulative conditions of having a teaching component in their recruitment group and actually teaching; payable from the month after reaching pensionable age; and conditional on teaching **until the end of the school year**.
  - **Art. 7 (NEW to our record):** fixed-term contracts with **higher-education teachers and doctoral researchers** with scientific training matching the recruitment groups, paid at the 1st, 2nd or 3rd salary step by service length. This is the source of the "227" in the 2026 balance sheet and must not be folded into the returner count.
- **TAKE-UP — 2024/25 (unchanged) and NEW for 2025/26.** 2024/25: government target 200; 79-80 retirees applied; **63 accepted**; 55 validated and placed in the January-2025 wave; average age ≈68. **2025/26 — new official balance sheet, announced by minister Fernando Alexandre on 1 July 2026:** the +Aulas +Sucesso measures attracted **6,543 teachers** into public schools, against roughly 4,000 retirements, comprising **5,535 new teachers**, **1,008 teachers who returned after being absent for at least one year**, and **227 higher-education teachers/doctoral researchers**; **2,232 teachers postponed retirement** under the Art. 6 supplement; the extraordinary relocation support reached 7,122 teachers; and recovery of frozen service time had benefited 89,922 teachers, lifting the share of teachers at salary step 7 or above from 32% to 63% (June 2026) (ECO, 2026 — VERIFIED; Observador, 2026a — PARTIAL, paywalled at HTTP 402, figures corroborated by ECO and by search-index text).
- **NEW — the retirement counter-flow and the cost of Article 6.** In 2025/26 **3,295** teachers and pre-school educators retired (September 2025: 384; roughly 400/month October-December 2025; June 2026: 112 — the lowest month), with **557** more projected for September 2026. More than 2,000 teachers drew the EUR 750 supplement at a cost of **about EUR 10 million** for the school year; the January-2026 count was **1,514**; the 2024/25 comparison is ~1,500. Figures cover Ministry of Education teachers only — excluding higher education, private institutions and the autonomous regions (Executive Digest, 2026, reporting government data cited by *Público* — PARTIAL).
- **EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION.** Still none: funnel counts and ministerial balance sheets, no counterfactual, no independent evaluation. The 6,543 is a gross inflow figure presented by the minister who designed the measures.
- **AFTERMATH.** On 30 July 2026 the Council of Ministers narrowed the Art. 6 supplement for 2026/27 to shortage areas only — 8 QZP frameworks covering 235 school units in Greater Lisbon, the Setúbal Peninsula and the Algarve (down from 10 QZP / 258 units) — the minister conceding the blanket version "in many cases was not justified". The Art. 5 retiree route continued into 2025/26 and 2026/27; **its own annual contingente and its 2025/26 placement count were NOT FOUND** — DGAE publishes the procedure, not the outcome.
- **WHAT IT CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** The corpus's cleanest revealed-preference asymmetry, now with a second year: **63 retirees returned under Art. 5 in 2024/25 against 2,232 who postponed retirement under Art. 6 in 2025/26**, under one law, at broadly comparable money — preventing an exit outperforms reversing one by roughly 35:1. That the government then corrected a blanket instrument toward targeting after observing where it was "not justified". And, with care, that Portugal now publishes an official returner count of a *different* kind: 1,008 teachers who had been out for at least a year came back in 2025/26.
- **WHAT IT CANNOT SUPPORT.** "€750 failed to attract returners" — returners were never offered the €750. Presenting **1,008 as retiree returners under Art. 5**: the published definition is teachers "absent for at least one year", a much broader group than retirees within five years of retiring, and the 63/55 Art. 5 figures are the only retiree-route numbers we have. Presenting 6,543 as net gain (retirements were ~4,000, and the 6,543 is a ministerial claim). Any evaluation-grade verdict.

### China — Silver Age Lecturing Plan (教师〔2018〕7号) and National Silver Age Teachers Action Plan (教师〔2023〕6号), with annual implementation notices to 2025 — **and the research literature**

- **CONTEXT.** Two-layer instrument. (a) *银龄讲学计划*, MOE + MOF, July 2018, target 10,000 recruits 2018-2020 for rural compulsory-education schools in deeply impoverished areas. (b) *国家银龄教师行动计划*, **教师〔2023〕6号, dated 14 July 2023**, issued by ten bodies (MOE, MOST, MIIT, Civil Affairs, MOF, MOHRSS, National Health Commission, National Healthcare Security Administration, Chinese Academy of Sciences, China Association for Science and Technology), setting a corps of **about 120,000 (12万人左右) within about three years** across five strands — higher education, vocational education, basic education, lifelong education, private education. Both texts re-verified in full this session from gov.cn and moe.gov.cn.
- **MECHANISM — verified from the 2025 implementation notice (教师厅函〔2025〕16号, signed 16 September 2025, published 30 September 2025), the most detailed primary text in the file.**
  - **Recruitment target 2025: 7,000** lecturing teachers at compulsory-education level, with provinces and the XPCC encouraged to run their own additional schemes and to extend coverage to ordinary senior secondary schools at their discretion.
  - **Eligibility:** retired principals, teaching-research staff (教研员), special-grade and backbone teachers; **generally aged 65 or under**; in principle holding an **intermediate or higher professional title, mainly senior**.
  - **A design rule the fichas do not carry, and should:** "原单位返聘退休教师工作不列入银龄讲学计划" — **re-employment of a retiree by their own former employer does not count under the plan.** The same exclusion appears in the 2023 Action Plan. The instrument is deliberately a *redeployment* scheme, not a rehiring scheme; it buys geographic movement, not return per se.
  - **Money:** personnel relationship and existing retirement entitlements unchanged; **working funds (工作经费) paid monthly**, used for the teacher's work subsidy, travel expenses **and accident insurance premiums** — i.e. the 20,000 yuan is not net pay. For compulsory education the standard is **20,000 yuan per person per year**, shared central/local: **western provinces fully central; central provinces 1:1 central-provincial; eastern provinces provincially funded**; central settlement on actuals; provinces may raise the standard at their own expense.
  - **Non-cash guarantees:** receiving counties must provide 周转宿舍 (turnover accommodation) with basic living facilities; medical costs handled under the teacher's own medical arrangements; priority in honours and awards for strong performers.
  - **Service and control:** service normally **not less than one school year**, agreements **signed annually**, a **health check covering the previous six months required before signing**, tracked assessment by the receiving county, and termination for non-performance or ill health. Roles: classroom teaching, lesson observation and appraisal, open and seminar lessons, thematic lectures, mentoring young teachers; recruited principals may serve as **vice-principals**.
  - **Targeting:** county as the basic unit — formerly poor counties (contiguous destitute areas, national and provincial poverty-work key counties, deeply impoverished counties), underdeveloped ethnic-minority counties, old revolutionary base counties, border counties and XPCC regiment farms, tilted toward national rural-revitalisation key counties and "三区三州".
  - **Reporting and enforcement:** all reporting through the National Teacher Management Information System; provinces must "提高招募完成率" (raise the recruitment completion rate) and those with poor implementation, "特别是完成率不高" (especially low completion rates), face investigation, mandated rectification and public criticism.
- **TAKE-UP.** Annual targets now form a series: **2023: 6,000; 2024: 7,000 (教师厅函〔2024〕14号); 2025: 7,000.** Cumulative to mid-2023 the two predecessor schemes had recruited **over 20,000** retired primary/secondary teachers and nearly 1,000 retired professors. **No published figure for actual recruitment against any annual target was found**, in Chinese or English — and the notices' own enforcement language about low completion rates implies that shortfalls occur and are tracked internally. No official tally of progress toward 120,000 as of August 2026. **One province-level datapoint located in the literature:** Jiangxi recruited **378** lecturing teachers in 2018-2019 against a plan of 200 — 241 of them for primary posts, 120 Chinese-language and 112 mathematics teachers (Jiang, 2023).
- **EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION — this is the section the lead author asked for. Four research items, none of them an impact evaluation.**
  1. **Cheng, H. (2025) — the peer-reviewed case study.** "External input and internal generation: the Chinese experience of how urban retired teachers maximize support for impoverished rural schools", *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications*, Vol. 12, article 968, published **1 July 2025**, DOI 10.1057/s41599-025-05421-7, open access (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0). **Method:** qualitative single-site case study; unstructured in-depth interviews with **10 participants** — 1 principal, 1 vice-principal, 4 young/middle-aged local teachers and **4 urban retired teachers** (a deputy principal, a music teacher, a maths teacher and a Chinese teacher) — at *Guang Ming* Primary School, Linxia Prefecture, **Gansu**, a national deep-poverty area; two fieldwork visits (**6 March 2024** and **12 April 2025**); the principal and one retired teacher interviewed three times, others twice; 40-60 minutes each; audio-recorded and transcribed; thematic coding combining deductive and inductive approaches with inter-rater agreement **Kappa = 0.92**. It studies the **2018** instrument ("Program of Support to Impoverished Rural Schools by Retired Teachers with Professionalism, Experience and Wisdom"). **Findings:** two complementary practice models — *external input*, where retirees teach, rebuild classroom practice and take part in school governance (local curriculum design; five-dimensional moral/intellectual/physical/aesthetic/labour education; dialogic teaching; home-school cooperation; a shift from centralised to collaborative decision-making); and *endogenous development*, where retirees build local capacity through collective lesson planning, lesson observation and monthly professional training covering policy interpretation, case sharing and theory, with young teachers reporting improved competitive-teaching performance and classroom management. **Author-stated limitations:** the framework is culturally specific and may not transfer; Chinese best practice is hard to generalise across social structures; **only successful practices were examined and failed interventions were not scrutinised**; and students' perceptions were not assessed. Compensation and take-up are not addressed.
  2. **Zhang, M., T. Fang and Q. Jiang (2025) — the comparative policy article** (this is almost certainly the "Zhang, Fang and Jiang (2025)" flagged as KEY MISSING in the fichas; identity now confirmed). "Addressing teacher shortages: Policy and practical experiences from China", *Education and Lifelong Development Research*, Vol. 2/2, pp. 92-100, published 25 June 2025, DOI 10.46690/elder.2025.02.05; all three authors at the College of Teacher Education, South China Normal University, Guangzhou. **Method:** qualitative design; systematic collection and coding of **official policy documents issued 2000-2025**. **Findings:** shortages are especially acute in rural regions; China's strategy runs in three phases — pre-service preparation (including free normal-student programmes), initial placement of graduates in underserved regions, and **redistribution of experienced or retired teachers through rotation mechanisms**. It is a document-analysis paper: it locates the Silver Age plans inside a supply architecture, it does not measure their effects.
  3. **Zhang, M., Q. Jiang and J. Jiang (2025) — the implementation-constraints study.** "'银龄讲学'政策执行的制约因素与路径选择" ["Constraints on the implementation of the 'Silver Age Lecturing' policy and pathways for improvement"], *Journal of Hangzhou Normal University (Humanities and Social Sciences)*, 2025, No. 1, pp. 85-93, DOI 10.19925/j.cnki.issn.1674-2338.2025.01.009. **Method:** policy-implementation analysis using **Smith's policy implementation process model**. **Findings — four constraints:** (i) ambiguity in the policy text itself; (ii) insufficient standardisation/regulation of implementing agencies; (iii) multiple challenges facing the target group (the retired teachers); (iv) limitations of the external environment. Proposed remedies: more precise policy specification, stronger administrative execution capacity, better guarantees and deeper participation for the target group, and a better external environment and publicity. **This is the closest thing to an implementation evaluation that exists**, and it is a framework-based analysis, not a measurement study.
  4. **Jiang, F. (2023) — the policy-formation study.** "'银龄教师行动计划'：人口老龄化背景下教师供给的替代性路径" ["The Silver-Age Teacher Action Plan: an alternative pathway for teacher supply in the context of population ageing"], *Journal of Teacher Education* (教师教育学报), Vol. 10/6, pp. 123-130, DOI 10.13718/j.cnki.jsjy.2023.06.014. **Method:** Kingdon multiple-streams analysis of the plan's formation (political stream: population ageing; policy stream: accumulated NGO practice such as the Youcheng Foundation's "Evergreen Volunteer Teaching" from 2010; problem stream: rural teacher shortage). Carries the **Jiangxi 378-vs-200** over-recruitment datapoint and NGO baseline figures (2010-2011 Youcheng: 578 volunteers, 47,156 service hours, 297 schools, 3,185 teachers directly and 52,346 students indirectly reached). Notes implementation gaps: inconsistent regional application, local fiscal constraints, and friction between retirement entitlements and the requirements of new posts.
  5. **Adjacent, for the higher-education strand only:** Yang, Q. (2026), "Silver-haired teachers going to the west: Retired female university teachers' spatial praxis and self-reconstruction in Western China", *Journal of Women & Aging*, advance online 12 July 2026, DOI 10.1080/08952841.2026.2701478 — ethnography, **six months' fieldwork at one western university plus in-depth interviews with seven female participants**, framed as active ageing and spatial praxis. Relevant to the *高校银龄教师支援西部计划* (universities strand), **not** to the rural compulsory-education strand; do not cite it for rural schooling.
- **Independent critique on the record.** Xiong Bingqi (21st Century Education Research Institute) called the 2025 round a "win-win" but warned against treating silver-age teachers as a **main force rather than a supplement**, and against schools using such hiring to cut education costs as pupil numbers decline, arguing instead for smaller classes (CCTV, 2025 — VERIFIED, 11 October 2025). This is a named, on-record version of the caution our fichas currently source only to R.I.S.E. Magazine.
- **AFTERMATH.** Continuing and institutionalised: annual implementation notices in 2023, 2024 and 2025; the 2025 notice requires provinces to file, by 30 November 2025, a work summary, the 2025/26 recruited-teacher roster **and the 2026/27 demand table**, with the following year's plan set from those returns — i.e. the scheme now runs on a rolling demand-driven cycle rather than a fixed three-year target. The ~2026 horizon for the 120,000 has arrived with no published tally.
- **WHAT IT CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** The targeting principle: money attached to service exactly where need and friction are highest, with the deadweight case explicitly closed by rule — **rehiring by one's own former employer is excluded**, so the state pays only for movement it would not otherwise get. That the design is unusually complete for a re-entry instrument: annual contract, health check, county-level tracked assessment, termination clause, accommodation, insurance, an information system, and enforcement against provinces with low completion rates. That there is now a small peer-reviewed literature — one qualitative case study of how retirees actually work in a receiving rural school (Cheng, 2025), one implementation-constraints analysis (Zhang, Jiang and Jiang, 2025), one policy-formation analysis (Jiang, 2023) and one document-based supply-policy overview (Zhang, Fang and Jiang, 2025).
- **WHAT IT CANNOT SUPPORT.** Any effectiveness claim: there is no impact evaluation, no outcome measurement, and no published actual-recruitment figure against any annual target. Cheng (2025) is N=10 at one school, examines only successful practice by the author's own admission, and does not assess students. The 120,000 must never be described as re-employment of classroom retirees: it spans five strands including volunteers, part-timers and online service. The 20,000 yuan must not be described as salary: it is 工作经费 covering work subsidy, travel and accident insurance, on top of an unchanged pension.

### Germany (Länder, esp. Hesse) — retiree outreach with earnings-cap relief and supplements

- **CONTEXT.** No register-based reserve statistics; the debate defaults to retirees. The anchor survey remains Deutsches Schulportal's exclusive canvass of all 16 Kultusministerien (2 August 2023): **13 of 16 Länder** actively approach pensioned teachers (exceptions Brandenburg, Rhineland-Palatinate, Saarland); Hesse wrote to **~7,000** retired teachers in 2022/23; reactivated counts Baden-Württemberg 531 (2022/23), Bavaria ~690 (Oct 2022), Berlin ~400, Hamburg 285, Schleswig-Holstein 390 (2022/23), Thuringia ~220 (Jan 2023); **>5,000 retirees working nationwide, under 1% of the ~800,000 workforce**.
- **MECHANISM — NEW detail on the instruments.** The earlier Schulportal piece on the same topic sets out the instrument menu by Land (Deutsches Schulportal, 2023b — VERIFIED, updated 28 July 2023): Bremen, Hesse and North Rhine-Westphalia wrote to "hundreds" of pensioners; **Hamburg abolished the mandatory retirement age of 65 years 7 months, allowing two further years**; **Berlin proposed a 20% salary supplement for teachers continuing three years past 65**; **North Rhine-Westphalia suspended the earnings limit for retired civil servants to end-2019**, so pension income was no longer offset against teaching earnings; Baden-Württemberg said limits "could be suspended under certain conditions"; Bavaria imposed a temporary freeze on early retirements (2018). Note the range: the fichas' "~10% salary supplements" is the modal figure from the 2023 survey, but Berlin's proposal was 20% — do not present ~10% as universal.
- **NEW — a further, federal-level lever.** A temporary special rule raised the **Hinzuverdienstgrenze** for pensioned civil servants to **1.5 times last active pay**, available to those who left on reaching the statutory age limit or who deferred retirement; introduced in connection with the pandemic and Ukrainian-refugee reception, and running **to the end of 2025** at federal level, while some Länder made it permanent — Bavaria set the 1.5x limit from **1 January 2024** in its own law (PARTIAL — the rule is described consistently across German civil-service advisory sources and the Bavarian Beamtenbund's summary of the *Modernisierungsgesetze*; **the precise federal legal citation and any post-2025 extension were NOT FOUND** and must be verified before the paper asserts the end-date).
- **TAKE-UP — NEW, and better than the figure the fichas warn against.** For Hesse specifically, official answers by Kultusminister Armin Schwarz (CDU) to two *Kleine Anfragen* from the Greens show that the number of teachers **aged 66 and over** in Hessian **primary** schools rose from **130 (2018) to 282 (as at 1 October 2024)**, with "significant increases" also at integrated comprehensive, special-education and grammar schools (figures not given); average retirement age 63-64; career-changer posts in primary schools rose from 40 (2021) to 100 (2024) and in Haupt-/Realschulen from 40 to 60; the Hessian teaching workforce is ~67,000, with teaching posts up 18% over ten years against a 5% rise in pupils (news4teachers, 2025 — VERIFIED, 7 April 2025). This is a **parliamentary-answer-based, dated, single-Land time series** and is a better citation than the circulating "Hesse won back ~200 retirees" press figure, which should stay out.
- **EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION.** None anywhere. No Land publishes an evaluation, and the national picture exists only because one journalistic outlet asked all 16 ministries once, in 2023. Definitions of "reactivated" vary by Land and are not harmonised.
- **AFTERMATH.** Hesse's *Handlungskonzept* (March 2024, §1.10, p. 8) keeps recruitment of **recently retired** teachers among its short-term measures to "unlock existing resources", with "rund 550 Stellen" filled by the short-term package **as a whole** — retirees plus service extensions plus part-time top-ups, never retirees alone. The nationwide picture has not been re-surveyed since 2023.
- **WHAT IT CAN SUPPORT SAYING.** Near-universal outreach with small yields (matching Austria and Portugal): 13 of 16 systems writing to retirees, >5,000 working, under 1% of the workforce. That the binding instrument in a civil-service pension system is the **earnings cap**, not a bonus: the recurring lever is suspending or raising the Hinzuverdienstgrenze, with supplements (~10%, Berlin's proposed 20%) as a second-order top-up where the cap still bites. That the one Land with a published time series shows the retiree share creeping up (Hesse primary, 66+: 130 → 282 over six years).
- **WHAT IT CANNOT SUPPORT.** Cross-Land comparison of instrument effectiveness (survey-collected, unharmonised definitions, one-off). Attributing Hesse's ~550 to retirees. Asserting the 1.5x earnings-limit rule's exact federal legal basis or its status after 2025 without the further verification flagged above.
