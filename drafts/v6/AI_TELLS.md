Research complete. Below is the documented proscription list, compiled from Wikipedia's editor-maintained "Signs of AI writing" catalog, the tropes.fyi directory, Momentic's "34 types of AI slop", Hacker News threads on LLM writing tropes, and press analyses of the Washington Post's 328,744-message ChatGPT corpus study.

**Why "The answer divides cleanly." was flagged as 100% AI.** That one sentence hits four separately documented tells at once: (1) it is a *meta-sentence announcing structure* — it tells the reader an organized answer is coming instead of just giving it (sources file this under "meta-commentary" / "collaborative communication"); (2) it is a *dramatic one-line aphorism* — the staccato fragment-for-gravitas cadence sources call "manufactured emphasis"; (3) "divides cleanly" promises an *over-tidy dichotomy*, the suspiciously neat binary that RLHF-trained models default to; and (4) it *anthropomorphises an abstraction* — an "answer" performing an action. Human academic prose would simply write "There are two cases: ..." or state the first case directly.

## Proscription list (ordered by how strongly sources flag each)

| # | Pattern | Why it reads as AI | Example | Academic fix |
|---|---------|--------------------|---------|--------------|
| 1 | **Negative parallelism ("not X, but Y" / "It's not X — it's Y")** | Called "the single most commonly identified AI writing tell"; WaPo corpus found "Not just X, but Y" a signature cliché; fakes depth via surprise reframe, often negating a claim nobody made | "It's not a representation of self, but a mechanism for its constant reinvention." | State the positive claim directly; only contrast when a real rival view exists, and cite it |
| 2 | **"Isn't just X — it's Y" (escalation variant)** | The template "[Topic] isn't just [description]; it's [metaphor]" is the most-mocked ChatGPT-ism on Reddit/Substack | "This isn't just a policy shift — it's a paradigm change." | Make the stronger claim once, with evidence, without the ladder |
| 3 | **Em-dash overuse** | The most infamous tell; not the mark itself but density — "humans use 2–3, AI uses 20+", several per paragraph as dramatic pauses | "The problem — and this is key — is systemic — not local." | Restrict em dashes to true parentheticals; prefer commas, colons, subordinate clauses |
| 4 | **AI vocabulary: delve, crucial, pivotal, tapestry, landscape, realm, intricate, underscore, showcase, foster, meticulous, testament** | Corpus studies show "delve" usage in papers spiked post-ChatGPT; Wikipedia keeps era-stamped lists of these words | "This paper delves into the intricate landscape of fiscal policy." | Use the plain term: examines, important, area; delete ornamental nouns entirely |
| 5 | **Rule of three / tricolon abuse** | LLMs reflexively emit triads ("adjective, adjective, adjective") for rhythm regardless of whether three items exist | "better targeting, stronger creative, and consistent follow-up" | List exactly as many items as the analysis supports — two, four, or one |
| 6 | **Dramatic short sentences / staccato clusters** | AI clusters short sentences whenever it wants tension; "feels like a cadence rather than a choice" | "Traffic dropped 40% overnight. Rankings disappeared. The client called." | Fold fragments into full sentences; let evidence, not rhythm, carry emphasis |
| 7 | **One-line aphoristic paragraphs / punch-lines** | Standalone fragment-paragraphs for manufactured profundity ("X. That's it. That's the point.") are a hallmark of unedited LLM output | "The answer divides cleanly." / "That's the point." | No single-sentence paragraphs in formal register; merge into the argument |
| 8 | **Meta-sentences announcing structure** | "Meta-commentary": announcing what you're about to say instead of saying it; Wikipedia files it under "collaborative communication" | "The answer is twofold." / "The picture is clear." | Replace with content: "There are two reasons: first, ..." or delete and proceed |
| 9 | **Rhetorical question as transition ("The X? A Y.")** | Self-posed questions nobody asked, answered immediately, to fake momentum | "The result? Devastating." / "The worst part?" | Convert to declarative: "The result was a 40% decline (Table 2)." |
| 10 | **False-suspense pivots ("Here's the kicker / But here's where it gets interesting")** | Manufactures drama before unremarkable information; flagged across tropes.fyi, Momentic, Substack lists | "But here's the kicker: costs rose anyway." | Plain adversative: "However, costs rose." |
| 11 | **Hedging boilerplate ("It's important to note", "It's worth noting")** | Filler transition signaling nothing; among the most-cited ChatGPT phrases; includes "notably", "interestingly" | "It is important to note that results may vary." | If it matters, the sentence shows why; delete the frame |
| 12 | **Signposted conclusions ("In conclusion", "To sum up")** | Five-paragraph-essay scaffolding; "competent writing doesn't signal this" | "In conclusion, the evidence suggests..." | End with the strongest implication or limitation, unannounced |
| 13 | **Fractal summaries / self-referential narration** | Tell-tell-tell at every level: "this section explores... as we've seen... this paper has shown" | "In this section, we will explore three factors." | One roadmap sentence per paper maximum; sections start with substance |
| 14 | **Uniform sentence rhythm** | WaPo/linguists: sentences of near-identical length and complexity produce a "metronomic" feel; low burstiness is what detectors measure | Every sentence 18–24 words, subject-verb-object | Vary deliberately: one long analytic sentence, then a short one; irregular syntax is a documented sign of human writing |
| 15 | **Uniform paragraph shape** | Identical 3–4-sentence visual blocks, every paragraph the same length | Ten paragraphs, each exactly four sentences | Let paragraph length follow argument size, including occasional long ones |
| 16 | **Over-tidy dichotomies** | LLMs resolve everything into clean binaries/balanced antitheses ("optimists say X; skeptics say Y") that flatten messy reality | "Views divide into two camps." | Report the actual distribution of positions, including overlaps and outliers |
| 17 | **"Not X. Not Y. Just Z." countdown** | Dramatic serial negation before the reveal | "Not a bug. Not a feature. A choice." | State Z with its justification |
| 18 | **"The truth is simple" / privileged-insight reveals** | Asserts instead of proves; claims access to "the real story" | "The reality is simpler and less flattering." | Prove it: evidence, then conclusion |
| 19 | **Transition-word chains (Furthermore / Moreover / Additionally)** | Formal connectors stacked at paragraph heads; "Additionally" is on Wikipedia's AI-vocabulary list | "Moreover... Furthermore... Additionally..." | Connect via logical content ("This mechanism also explains...") or juxtaposition |
| 20 | **Copula avoidance ("serves as", "stands as", "represents", "boasts")** | Wikipedia: systematic dodging of plain "is/has" with pompous or marketing verbs | "The dataset serves as a foundation for..." | Write "is", "has", "provides" |
| 21 | **Significance inflation ("testament", "pivotal moment", "underscores the importance")** | Inflates mundane facts into world-historical ones; core of Wikipedia's "undue emphasis" category | "...marking a pivotal moment in the evolution of the field" | State the fact; let the reader judge significance, or quantify it |
| 22 | **Trailing "-ing" pseudo-analysis** | Participial phrases tacked on to simulate insight without source support | "...highlighting the enduring legacy of the reform, underscoring its role..." | Cut, or make the causal claim explicit and cite it |
| 23 | **Anthropomorphised abstractions** | Abstract nouns performing human actions; related to "quiet intelligence" magic-adverb slop | "The data tells a compelling story." / "The answer divides cleanly." | Human or measurable subjects: "We find...", "The estimates show..." |
| 24 | **Vague authority attribution** | "Experts argue", "industry reports suggest" with no citation; presents 1–2 sources as consensus; sometimes fabricated | "Observers have cited concerns about..." | Name and cite the specific source or delete the claim |
| 25 | **Collaborative/pedagogical voice ("Let's unpack", "Let's dive in")** | Chat-register hand-holding leaking into prose | "Let's break this down step by step." | Impersonal academic register: "We first decompose..." |
| 26 | **Patronising analogies ("Think of it as...")** | Unrequested metaphors, often less clear than the original concept | "Think of the tax code as a highway system." | Use analogies only when they carry analytic weight; define terms instead |
| 27 | **False exclusivity ("what nobody talks about")** | Engagement-bait scarcity claims about well-known points | "Here's what most people miss." | Position the point in the actual literature ("less attention has been paid to X (refs)") |
| 28 | **Grandiose stakes inflation** | Every finding "fundamentally reshapes" or "defines the next era" | "This will fundamentally reshape how we think about welfare." | Scope claims to what the evidence supports |
| 29 | **"Despite its challenges..." redemption formula** | Rigid challenge-acknowledgment-optimism arc ending every section; Wikipedia's "outline-like conclusions" | "Despite these challenges, the sector continues to thrive." | Let negative findings stand; discuss limitations without forced uplift |
| 30 | **Anaphora abuse** | Same sentence-opener repeated in quick succession for oratorical effect | "They assume users... They assume developers... They assume ecosystems..." | One rhetorical repetition per paper at most; vary openers |
| 31 | **Listicle in a trench coat ("The first... The second... The third...")** | Enumeration disguised as prose; also "Phase 1 / Step 2" section stamps | "The first factor is... The second factor is..." | Either a genuine numbered list or genuinely connected prose |
| 32 | **False ranges ("from X to Y")** | "From... to..." spans where no real spectrum exists | "from innovation to implementation to transformation" | Name the actual items or the actual dimension of variation |
| 33 | **Elegant variation / synonym cycling** | Repetition-penalty artifact: unnatural synonym rotation for one concept ("constraints/confines/limitations") | Same variable called "constraint", then "confine", then "restriction" | Academic convention: one term per concept, used consistently |
| 34 | **Formatting overkill (bold-first bullets, headed lists, title-case headings)** | Every bullet opening with a bolded label is a telltale ChatGPT export | "**Efficiency:** the reform improves..." | Continuous prose; bullets only for genuinely parallel enumerable items |
| 35 | **Compliment sandwich / relentless positivity** | Uniform enthusiasm and softened criticism; RLHF-trained agreeableness | "X is a valuable contribution; however..." (before every critique) | State disagreements directly with reasons |
| 36 | **Hedge-adverb inflation ("arguably", "quietly", "deeply", "fundamentally", "truly")** | Magic adverbs conveying unearned weight or defensive qualification, removable without loss | "This arguably represents a deeply significant shift." | Delete the adverb; quantify uncertainty properly (confidence intervals, explicit caveats) |

**Cross-cutting note for the paper:** individual items are weak signals (humans have used em dashes and triads for centuries — several sources caution against "AI-detection paranoia"); what sources agree marks text as AI is *density and co-occurrence* — several tells per paragraph, at uniform rhythm, with no citations anchoring the flourishes. "The answer divides cleanly." is a four-tell sentence, which is why it pattern-matched instantly.
