## FAMILY D — ACTIVE RECRUITMENT AND ACCOMPANIMENT

The state locates its own former teachers — through departmental staffing records, pension files or a marketing funnel — contacts them directly, and accompanies the return: a named adviser, re-licensure bundled into one contact, refresher training, placement support. This is the only family that has ever published a full contacts-to-placements funnel, and its consistent verdict is that interest is cheap and conversion is the constraint: registrations run to the thousands, placements to the dozens or low hundreds, and no scheme in the family has measured additionality. It operationalises a recommendation the OECD made in 2005 — "maintaining contact with former teachers to keep them informed of educational developments and job opportunities, and targeted retraining schemes" (OECD, 2005, pp. 88-89).

### England — Return to Teaching pilot (2015-16), Returners Engagement Programme, and the Return to Teaching Adviser service

**CONTEXT.** England holds the OECD's largest measured out-of-service pool (260,000 qualified teachers of working age out of service plus 106,000 never in service, 2018; Worth, 2023, citing DfE) and a routine annual return flow of roughly 17,000 teachers, about a third of all new entrants (Department for Education, 2023). Active recruitment has a long lineage: the Returning to Teach Programme of the early 2000s already ran a help line, a termly magazine and ~100 refresher courses a year with bursaries (OECD, 2005, Box 3.5, p. 70).

**WHAT IT DOES.** The 2015-16 pilot, run by the NCTL through grant-funded partnerships of School Direct schools, offered non-financial conversion support to returners to EBacc subjects in state secondaries — subject-knowledge refreshers, classroom experience, mentoring — behind a national marketing campaign; no payments to returners (Buchanan et al., 2018, pp. 16-17). Its successor, the Returners Engagement Programme (from 2017, maths/physics/MFL only), tied funded places to actual vacancies, correcting the pilot's timing-and-jobs mismatch (NCTL, 2017). The standing Return to Teaching Adviser (RTTA) service gives free one-to-one advice from a named adviser — applications, CV, signposting to refreshers and vacancies — restricted to maths/physics/MFL until autumn 2022, then all secondary subjects, and by 2023 primary and secondary including teachers overseas (UK Parliament, 2022; Department for Education, 2023; Department for Education, 2025a).

**PUBLISHED SCALE.** Pilot: 5,729 registrations of interest → 354 returners supported (Jan-Sep 2016; denominator: registrants) → 27 in a permanent EBacc post in a state-funded secondary school by November 2016 (outcome defined by the 2016 School Workforce Census; 8% of supported), 62 in any state-funded teaching post; grant payments GBP 586,900; GBP 17,800 per supported returner employed and GBP 37,400 per *additional* returner assuming 50% deadweight (Buchanan et al., 2018, pp. 56-58). RTTA: cost GBP 361,344.69 (2019/20) and GBP 325,562.73 (2020/21); recorded returns while subject-restricted ≈128 over two years (maths 25+24, physics 8+15, MFL 29+27; outcome: returns recorded by the DfE, undercounting unreported and other-subject returns), implying ≈GBP 5,400 per recorded returner, additionality unknown (UK Parliament, 2022; Walker, 2022). No independent evaluation of the RTTA has been published.

**LEARNING.** The pilot is one of very few re-entry programmes anywhere with a full independent evaluation, and it was negative: "unlikely to represent good value for money", at a cost per additional returner comparable to training a new teacher, with returners retaining *worse* than new entrants (32% of the 2011 returner cohort out of service four years later, plus 2% retired, vs 29% of the 2011 NQT cohort; returner odds of leaving after the first year back 60% above average) (Buchanan et al., 2018, pp. 56-58, 91, 57). What it cannot support: any claim that adviser-based support is cost-effective (the ≈GBP 5,400 figure has no additionality control), or that demand is the constraint — registrations ran at nearly double target while placements collapsed at conversion.

**SOURCES.** Buchanan et al. (2018); NCTL (2017); UK Parliament (2022); Walker (2022); Worth and Sharp (2018); Department for Education (2023); Department for Education (2025a); UK Parliament (2026); Worth (2023); OECD (2005). All keys in references.md.

**SINGULARITY.** The only scheme in the corpus with a full independent evaluation including a cost-per-additional-returner estimate — and the estimate killed the design.

### Austria — Bildungsdirektion outreach to retirees (Vienna, Styria, Salzburg, Vorarlberg)

**CONTEXT.** Austria entered the baby-boom retirement wave with the OECD's second-highest vacancy rate (4,778 unfilled posts, 4.6% of positions; unfilled positions equal to 107% of the prior year's teaching graduates; OECD, 2025a, p. 491) and no register of qualified people outside schools, so the visible pool is retirees. There is no national programme and no legal instrument; nine regional directorates act ad hoc under the federal "Klasse Job" umbrella (APA/ORF, 2023; Rechnungshof, 2025).

**WHAT IT DOES.** Vienna wrote to 300 already-retired teachers in December 2022 and instructed principals to speak with staff nearing retirement; Styria encouraged principals to ask retirement-age teachers about continuing, including at reduced hours; Salzburg contacted all retirees up to age 67, offering transport job tickets and higher premiums; Vorarlberg created a standing service point ("Arbeitsplatz Schule") that sends re-entry information out with the retirement paperwork (APA/ORF, 2023). The price the returner faces is set elsewhere: formerly civil-servant teachers are excluded by law from prior-service crediting for pension-generating periods, so they return on full pension plus the teacher *starting* salary, while former contract teachers have service credited and broadly recover pre-retirement pay (BMBWF, 2023, answer to Question 19, p. 18 — supersedes all "beginner scale for everyone" renderings).

**PUBLISHED SCALE.** Audited: 1,062 retired teachers resumed teaching nationwide across the six school years 2018/19-2023/24 (denominator: a teaching workforce of roughly 123,000; outcome: re-employment in school service recorded by the Court of Audit), concentrated in 2021/22 and 2022/23 and falling thereafter; Upper Austria 227, Tyrol 49 (Rechnungshof, 2025, pp. 24-25). March 2023 snapshot, different definitions: 42 at compulsory schools nationwide plus ~30 at federal schools in 2022/23 (APA/ORF, 2023). No outreach-specific conversion figures (letters → returns) exist.

**LEARNING.** Near-universal outreach coexists with modest, declining, audited take-up, which is consistent with the qualitative finding that post-retirement return is a biographical decision — individual, family, school and system factors, with continuing contact with the former school the key facilitator — rather than a marketing problem (Resch, 2025a, abstract-verified; Resch, 2025b: six returning and three non-returning retirees interviewed). It cannot support attribution of the 1,062 to the letters (the series starts four years before the outreach), nor the claim that pay drove the shortfall — the crediting rule is documented, its behavioural effect is not.

**SOURCES.** APA/ORF (2023); Rechnungshof (2025); BMBWF (2023); Resch (2025a); Resch (2025b); OECD (2025a). Huber, Pruitt and Helm (2024) (school-leader shortage survey, hedged replacement for Resch's ">550 school leaders" sentence) — [KEY MISSING from references.md].

**SINGULARITY.** The only audited national take-up figure for retiree re-entry in Europe — produced by a court of audit, not the sponsor.

### Germany (Hesse) — letters to retirees, inside a 13-of-16-Länder pattern

**CONTEXT.** Germany has no register of qualified teachers outside schools; what is countable is 457,000 retired or pensioned teachers (2021) against a workforce of roughly 800,000, and a KMK-estimated shortage of ~25,000 by 2025 (Deutsches Schulportal, 2023). Thirteen of sixteen Länder actively recruit pensioned teachers (exceptions: Brandenburg, Rhineland-Palatinate, Saarland).

**WHAT IT DOES.** Hesse wrote to around 7,000 retired teachers in school year 2022/23; its ministry's action plan lists hiring pensioners, service extension and part-time top-ups as short-term measures aimed at "recently retired teachers", packaged with lifted earnings limits, ~10% supplements where pension caps bind, flexible hours and no age caps — the typical instrument set across Länder (Deutsches Schulportal, 2023; Hessisches Ministerium für Kultus, Bildung und Chancen, 2024, section 1.10, p. 8).

**PUBLISHED SCALE.** Nationwide: just over 5,000 pensioners teaching, under 1% of the ~800,000 workforce (outcome: retirees in service, Länder self-reports compiled by Schulportal); Land counts include Baden-Württemberg 531 (2022/23), Bavaria ~690 (Oct 2022), Berlin ~400, Hamburg 285, Schleswig-Holstein 390, Thuringia ~220, NRW 1,304 past pension age (2022) (Deutsches Schulportal, 2023). Hesse attributes ~550 filled posts to the *combined* short-term package — retirees plus service extensions plus part-time top-ups, never retirees alone (Hessisches Ministerium für Kultus, Bildung und Chancen, 2024, p. 8). No letters-to-returns conversion rate is published anywhere.

**LEARNING.** The German record supports the generalisation that mass personalised outreach to retirees yields sub-1%-of-workforce activation even when most of the federation attempts it; it cannot support any cost-effectiveness claim (no costs published), any Hesse-specific yield for the 7,000 letters, and the circulating "Hesse won back ~200 retirees" figure must not be used without its press source.

**SOURCES.** Deutsches Schulportal (2023); Hessisches Ministerium für Kultus, Bildung und Chancen (2024). Both keys in references.md.

**SINGULARITY.** The largest documented single mailing in the family — ~7,000 letters — with no published count of what came back.

### Australia (NSW) — Teachers Re-Engage

**CONTEXT.** Launched November 2023 by the NSW Department of Education after public-school vacancies had peaked above 3,000 under the previous government (roughly 1,800-2,250 at launch), alongside the October 2023 pay deal and workload measures (NSW Government, 2024; NSW Department of Education, 2024).

**WHAT IT DOES.** Direct outreach from the Department's own staffing records to teachers who had resigned in the previous five years, with re-employment friction packaged into one contact: NESA accreditation reactivation, approval to teach and the Working with Children Check; placement into casual and temporary work, with permanent posts and small-group tutoring roles also open to the cohort; no programme-specific bonus (NSW Government, 2024).

**PUBLISHED SCALE.** The only complete published funnel in the corpus: >1,500 former teachers contacted (since Nov 2023) → >260 expressed interest → 145 placed in casual/temporary roles collectively covering >600 schools, ~60% returning from retirement (denominator: contacted leavers; outcome: placement in casual/temporary work, not permanent posts) (NSW Government, 2024; NSW Department of Education, 2024, p. 14: "around 150 former teachers... across 600 public schools"). No cost, no additionality, no evaluation; the programme is absent from the 2024-25 Annual Report and post-May-2024 figures do not exist (NSW Department of Education, 2025; 2026).

**LEARNING.** The funnel suggests, without proving, that employer-records outreach bundled with one-stop re-licensure converts interest at far lower cost than England's marketing-led pilot — a ~10% contact-to-placement rate against the pilot's 0.5% registration-to-permanent-post rate, though the outcomes differ in kind (casual days versus permanent posts). It cannot support attribution of NSW's vacancy decline (a whole-package effect), nor durability claims: reporting stopped after one media cycle.

**SOURCES.** NSW Government (2024); NSW Department of Education (2024); NSW Department of Education (2025); NSW Department of Education (2026). EducationHQ (2024) corroborates the funnel — [KEY MISSING from references.md].

**SINGULARITY.** The only scheme anywhere publishing the full contacts→interest→placements funnel with a retiree share — which then vanished from official reporting within a year.

### Netherlands — herintreders subsidy and the RAP regional infrastructure

**CONTEXT.** The country with the best-measured reserve (62,000 hidden reserve, Somers et al., 2024b; DUO, 2026) also ran the family's only demand-side accompaniment subsidy: the toelichting to the 2017 scheme cited a silent reserve of at least 31,000 qualified persons in primary education and invoked the 1998-2002 "Terug voor de klas" project as evidence that structured support aids reintegration (Ministerie van OCW, 2017).

**WHAT IT DOES.** The *Regeling tegemoetkoming herintreders primair onderwijs* (Nov 2017-Oct 2020) paid school boards — not teachers — a one-off EUR 2,500 per returner appointed for at least six months after at least twelve months out, to fund the board's induction, guidance and support of the returner (Ministerie van OCW, 2017, Arts. 1, 3, 4, 9). From 2019-2023 the regional RAP scheme required each funded partnership to run an information desk for prospective (re-)entrants and listed "activating the silent reserve including tailored guidance of herintreders" among fundable goals (Ministerie van OCW as consolidated; De Jong et al., 2022).

**PUBLISHED SCALE.** Budget for 500 returners (EUR 1.25 million ceiling); just over 100 returners subsidised in 2018 and nearly 200 cumulatively by late 2019 (denominator: the 500-place budget; outcome: subsidised board appointments of ≥6 months) (Ministerie van OCW, 2019b; Tweede Kamer, 2019). Ceilings were then *lowered* — to at most 120 grants in 2019 and 100 in 2020 — explicitly because the budget "was not exhausted"; final uptake unpublished; the regulation lapsed 1 January 2022 without evaluation (Ministerie van OCW, 2019a). The RAP evaluation found tailored support to returners among the least-realised activities, and several regions reported more side-entrant and returner applications than schools could place (De Jong et al., 2022, pp. 37, 60).

**LEARNING.** The Dutch pairing supports the claim that even a well-measured reserve plus a funded accompaniment instrument under-subscribes when the instrument is marginal — and, more pointedly, that school absorption capacity rather than applicant interest can be the binding constraint (the applications-exceed-places finding). It cannot support a causal effect on shortages (the evaluation is process-based and self-reported) and the absorption finding rests on a single self-reported row covering side-entrants and returners jointly.

**SOURCES.** Ministerie van OCW (2017); Ministerie van OCW (2019a); Ministerie van OCW (2019b); Tweede Kamer (2019); De Jong et al. (2022); DUO (2026); Somers et al. (2024b). All keys in references.md.

**SINGULARITY.** The only scheme whose sponsor cut its own budget ceilings because returners were not claiming the money — under-subscription written into the Staatscourant.

## FAMILY E — RETURN AS A DEFINED ROLE

Instead of re-selling the classroom post the leaver already rejected, the system defines a different job — tutor at a protocol and dose, mentor of novices, standing reservist, post-retirement re-employee — and recruits former teachers into it. This is the only family with randomised evidence, and the evidence tracks role specification exactly (P4 as amended): where the role was specified and dosed it was measured and worked; where it was left to the veteran's discretion it was measured once, small, and rated indeterminate. The asymmetry is one of measurement effort, not a verdict on craft.

### United States — Number Rockets: retired and substitute teachers as structured maths tutors

**CONTEXT.** Tutoring is among the best-evidenced interventions in education (pooled ES 0.288 SD across preK-12 RCTs, largest for teacher and paraprofessional tutors, in early grades, at ≥3 sessions/week during school; Nickow, Oreopoulos and Quan, 2024), but the efficacy trials used graduate students as tutors (Fuchs et al., 2005) — leaving open whether a realistic workforce could deliver the effect.

**WHAT IT DOES.** A scripted Tier-2 small-group (2-3 pupils) maths protocol for at-risk grade 1 students: ~40-minute sessions, ~3 times a week for 17 weeks, delivered by 86 tutors recruited and trained by the study team from local pools of retired and substitute teachers (Gersten et al., 2015; Rolfhus et al., 2012).

**PUBLISHED SCALE.** RCT: 994 at-risk grade 1 students in 76 schools in four urban districts, each in a different state of the REL Southwest region (states unnamed; formula per ch5_synthesis D6); effect size +0.34 on the TEMA-3 general maths test (denominator: randomised at-risk students; outcome: standardised maths achievement); WWC "meets standards with reservations", Evidence for ESSA "Strong" (Gersten et al., 2015; Rolfhus et al., 2012).

**LEARNING.** The trial supports the hedged claim that a tutor workforce drawn from retired and substitute teachers can reproduce, at scale, effects first obtained with graduate students — the strongest evidence anywhere for former-teacher deployment in a defined role at a specified dose. What it cannot support: attribution to *retirees* specifically (tutors were retired AND substitute teachers with no published split, and substitutes are working casual teachers, not former ones); nor the claim that hiring through tutoring organisations avoids pension complications (not in the paper; state-dependent).

**SOURCES.** Gersten et al. (2015); Rolfhus et al. (2012); Fuchs et al. (2005); Nickow, Oreopoulos and Quan (2024). All in references.md. Kraft and Falken (2021) (tutor-supply scaling logic) — [KEY MISSING from references.md; used only in lit files].

**SINGULARITY.** The only large-scale RCT in which the tutor workforce itself — recruited from retiree and substitute pools — was the replication question, and it passed.

### United States (Colorado) — Retired Mentors for New Teachers, Aurora Public Schools

**CONTEXT.** Mentoring capacity is scarce across the OECD (22% of novice teachers have an assigned mentor; 38% have had induction; OECD, 2019), and the correlational literature on induction is positive while the largest US RCT found no retention effect (Glazerman et al., 2010) — the frame in which the one direct causal test of retired mentors must be read.

**WHAT IT DOES.** Recently retired master educators from the district (averaging 22 years in its high-need schools), screened for excellence, paid USD 41/hour, mentored probationary teachers in Title I elementary schools for two years — weekly one-on-ones plus monthly cohort meetings, caseloads 3-7, no role in evaluation, layered on top of the district's business-as-usual buddy mentor (DeCesare, McClelland and Randel, 2017).

**PUBLISHED SCALE.** RCT: 77 probationary teachers in 11 schools, randomised within school (38/39); year-1 maths +1.4 MAP points (ES 0.064, p = .045) — not surviving the report's own multiple-comparison adjustment; reading n.s. both years; teacher evaluation ratings no effect; retention no significant effect (11 programme vs 5 control leavers — point estimate the wrong way); cost ≈USD 171 per student per year local (denominators: teachers randomised and their ~1,100-1,200 students; outcomes: NWEA MAP achievement, district retention). The WWC (August 2019, Standards 4.0) rated it "meets standards without reservations" and characterised all three reviewed domains — reading, maths, retention — as indeterminate, with no statistically significant positive findings; evaluation ratings was not a WWC-reviewed domain (DeCesare, McClelland and Randel, 2017; What Works Clearinghouse, 2019 — WWC date per ch5_synthesis D7, never "2017").

**LEARNING.** The single RCT of retirees as new-teacher mentors is a qualified null under WWC standards: mentoring claims must stay modest and be justified on instructional support and retiree engagement, never on mentee retention. What it cannot support in the other direction: a verdict against the craft rationale — one underpowered two-year trial in one district that set no dose ("indeterminate" is a statement about evidential power, not a negative result); its exploratory, non-causal dose-response (odds of staying doubling per additional 10 mentoring hours) is the design hint for the next trial.

**SOURCES.** DeCesare, McClelland and Randel (2017); What Works Clearinghouse (2019); OECD (2019); Glazerman et al. (2010). All in references.md.

**SINGULARITY.** The most rigorous causal test anywhere of retirees mentoring novices — high internal validity wrapped around an indeterminate answer, with cost data.

### United States — AARP Foundation Experience Corps

**CONTEXT.** A national nonprofit running since the mid-1990s, placing trained volunteers aged 50+ in high-poverty elementary schools as reading tutors; 2023/24 scale: 1,284 volunteers tutoring 3,659 students in 18 US communities (AARP, 2025). Being a former teacher is *not* an eligibility requirement; retired educators are an undocumented subset.

**WHAT IT DOES.** Structured reading tutoring for K-3 students reading 1-1.5 years behind: typically two 30-minute sessions a week, one-on-one or groups ≤3, after ≥20 hours of initial training; some slots historically stipended (up to ~USD 278/month via AmeriCorps streams) (What Works Clearinghouse, 2010; Morrow-Howell et al., 2009).

**PUBLISHED SCALE.** Student RCT: 883 grade 1-3 students randomised in 23 schools in Boston, New York City and Port Arthur, TX (analytic sample 881: 430 treatment / 451 control; posttest 822 — figures per ch5_synthesis D7, which purges the "434/454" split); 174 volunteers; significant gains in passage comprehension (ES 0.13) and teacher-rated grade-specific reading skills (ES 0.16), no effects on vocabulary or phonemic awareness, gains larger at ≥35 sessions (denominator: randomised struggling readers; outcome: standardised and teacher-rated reading) (Morrow-Howell et al., 2009; What Works Clearinghouse, 2010 — a Quick Review of February 2010, "consistent with WWC evidence standards"). Volunteer-side RCTs: increased physical, cognitive and social activity (Fried et al., 2004, n=128); causal increases in physical activity (Varma et al., 2016); 0.7-1.6% two-year *gains* in cortical and hippocampal volumes in men, against age-typical decline (Carlson et al., 2015, n=111).

**LEARNING.** Experience Corps supports a rare dual-benefit claim — modest, dosage-dependent reading gains for students and RCT-documented health benefits for the older volunteers themselves — which lets re-entry policy borrow a healthy-ageing rationale and a second ministry's budget. What it does NOT show: anything specific about former teachers (volunteers are older adults, share of retired educators unpublished), and the health findings are small-sample and subgroup-driven (brain-volume effects significant in men only).

**SOURCES.** Morrow-Howell et al. (2009); What Works Clearinghouse (2010); Fried et al. (2004); Carlson et al. (2015); Varma et al. (2016); AARP (2025). All in references.md. Lee et al. (2012) (journal version of the student RCT) — [KEY MISSING from references.md].

**SINGULARITY.** The only deployment of older adults in schools with randomised evidence on both sides of the exchange — students and volunteers.

### Japan — post-retirement re-employment as the default staffing route

**CONTEXT.** MEXT's first national shortage survey found 2,558 unfilled teaching positions (0.31% of the 836,079 required) at 1,897 schools (5.8%) at the start of AY2021, rising to 3,827 unfilled positions in the 2025 survey (Nippon.com, 2022; Nippon.com, 2026 — do not mix the 2,558 and 2,065 figures, different reference dates).

**WHAT IT DOES.** No branded programme: boards of education conventionally re-employ teachers after mandatory retirement until pension age, and retirees dominate the substitute/adjunct pool. Re-employment is routine staffing, an institution rather than a campaign (Sakuma and Shimazaki, 2024).

**PUBLISHED SCALE.** In the one prefecture studied in depth: 52.7% of part-time adjunct hires in their sixties and a further 7.1% in their seventies — retirees roughly 60% of all adjuncts (denominator: the prefecture's adjunct hires; outcome: composition of the substitute pool, not returns to establishment posts) (Sakuma and Shimazaki, 2024, p. 203). Fragility: only about 35% of age-based retirees in elementary schools (55% in junior high) now seek full-time re-employment, and ageing adjuncts increasingly decline the most demanding replacement work (pp. 201, 203). No national count of re-employed retirees is in the base.

**LEARNING.** Japan supports the claim that making re-employment the default route absorbs retirees at a scale no campaign approaches — one of the two cases that break the fraction-of-one-per-cent band (with North Carolina), by changing an institution rather than sending a message. It cannot support sustainability: a reserve composed mainly of retirees depletes on its own schedule, so part of what reads as policy failure is demography (tension F in the spine).

**SOURCES.** Sakuma and Shimazaki (2024); Nippon.com (2022); Nippon.com (2026). All in references.md. [No MEXT circular instructing boards to recruit retirees is verified — do not cite one.]

**SINGULARITY.** The one system where retiree return is not a policy at all but the labour market's standing answer — and where the answer is now running out of retirees willing to take it.

### Bahamas — Teacher Reservist Program

**CONTEXT.** A national shortfall of ~300 teachers across 162 public schools on 24 islands (~40,000 students), driven by retirements, expiring contracts and new specialised subjects; announced by the education minister in July 2026 (Cooper, 2026; ZNS Bahamas, 2026).

**WHAT IT DOES.** Creates a standing, registered reserve of qualified Bahamians on the platform of the pre-existing Supply Teacher Program: retired educators, trained teachers who left for other professions, and recent graduates enlist once and are called on by schools; a multi-agency task force fast-tracks vetting, credential assessment and processing; no reservist-specific financial incentive published (ZNS Bahamas, 2026; Cooper, 2026).

**PUBLISHED SCALE.** None published for the reservist pool itself. For the broader recruitment drive as of mid-July 2026: target of up to 100 Bahamians before September 2026, "between 60 and 100" being enlisted, 42 applications submitted to the Public Service Commission (denominator: the ~300-teacher gap; outcome: applications to the whole drive, not reservist enrolments). Announcement-stage; no results, evaluation or cost data.

**LEARNING.** The design contrast is the value: institutionalising a standing reserve rather than running a one-off rescue campaign — and it formalises an existing practice, since the union president noted retired educators were already working full-time in supply roles. It cannot support any effectiveness claim; it is weeks old at the time the base was compiled and must be dated "as of mid-2026".

**SOURCES.** Cooper (2026); ZNS Bahamas (2026). Both in references.md. Press corroboration (The Gallery, Tribune, Nassau Guardian pieces) — [KEYS MISSING from references.md; cited only in the case file].

**SINGULARITY.** The only scheme that names the reserve as a standing institution — a register former teachers join once, rather than a campaign that finds them each time.

### China — National Silver Age Teachers Action Plan (also documented in the base)

**CONTEXT.** Successor to the 2018 Silver Age Lecturing Plan (MOE and MOF; target 10,000 recruits 2018-2020 for rural compulsory education); expanded July 2023 by the MOE and nine other bodies into the world's largest retiree-redeployment scheme (MOE et al., 2023).

**WHAT IT DOES.** Recruits retired principals, teaching-research staff, special-grade and senior teachers (generally ≤65 in basic education; ≤70 other strands) into defined roles in poverty-lifted, ethnic-minority and border counties: teaching, demonstration lessons, serving as vice-principals, and explicitly mentoring young local teachers. Full pension kept plus working funds of ¥20,000 per person per year for the rural strand (cost split varies by region), plus housing, travel and insurance (MOE and MOF, 2018; MOE et al., 2023; State Council, 2023).

**PUBLISHED SCALE.** Target: a silver-age corps of ~120,000 within about three years of 2023 — across five strands including higher, vocational, lifelong and private education, full-time, part-time and volunteer, not only rural re-employment (denominator: the national retiree pool, unstated; outcome: corps membership). Cumulative: >20,000 retired school teachers recruited by the predecessor schemes by mid-2023; 2025 round targets 7,000 (State Council, 2023; MOE, 2025). No independent impact evaluation exists; no official tally of progress toward 120,000 as of August 2026.

**LEARNING.** China supports the design lesson that targeting the allowance where friction and need are highest minimises deadweight; it cannot support any effectiveness claim (government-reported scale, no counterfactual), and secondary Chinese coverage warns that some localities use silver-age recruits to delay hiring new full-time teachers (R.I.S.E. Magazine, 2025 — secondary, treat as such).

**SOURCES.** MOE and MOF (2018); MOE et al. (2023); State Council (2023); MOE (2025); R.I.S.E. Magazine (2025). All in references.md.

**SINGULARITY.** The inverse of the family's US pole: maximal scale, explicit mentoring mandate, zero counterfactual evidence.

### Spain (Zaragoza) — "10 mirrors" (brief mention only)

**CONTEXT.** An internal University of Zaragoza guidance project (presented at CIOU 2018, published 2019), inside the Faculty of Education's orientation plan — a defined mentoring role for retirees aimed at *pre-service* teachers.

**WHAT IT DOES.** Paired 10 retired teachers with recognised track records with 57 education-degree students (36 primary, 21 early childhood) for three in-person sessions on vocation, professional development and the teaching career — not continuous mentoring (Lizalde Gil and Cortés Pascual, 2019).

**PUBLISHED SCALE.** 10 mentors, 57 students, one run; evaluation by satisfaction questionnaires only (37.50% valued training improvements; 56.25% valued the guidance). No control group, no learning or labour-market outcomes; no evidence of continuation.

**LEARNING.** Usable only as an illustration that the defined-role logic extends to initial teacher education; it supports no effectiveness claim of any kind.

**SOURCES.** Lizalde Gil and Cortés Pascual (2019). In references.md.

**SINGULARITY.** The corpus's only documented Spanish case — a one-off micro-pilot of retirees mentoring future teachers.
