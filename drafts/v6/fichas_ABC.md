## FAMILY A — Pension and employment rules that set the price of a return

Governments in this family do not spend new money: they move the statutory rules — earnings caps, waiting periods, service thresholds, suspension and abatement provisions — that determine what a retiree or leaver forfeits by teaching again. The route enabled is re-employment of pension-drawing retirees (occasionally all leavers) at a price the pension system will tolerate. The family's evidence is the strongest in the corpus because rule changes generate administrative take-up data and, in three cases (North Carolina, Georgia, Austria), arms-length evaluation or audit; the recurring finding is that stacked eligibility screens strangle take-up while clean removal of the pension penalty produces the only large-scale revealed-preference success on record.

### United States (North Carolina) — Return-to-work window, 1999–2009
- CONTEXT: A state whose Teachers' and State Employees' Retirement System (TSERS) allowed retirement at any age with 30 years' service; the General Assembly opened a return-to-work window in 1999 (initially restricted to low-performing schools, lifted June 2000) and let it expire in autumn 2009. Evaluated on administrative data covering ~169,000 teachers in 2,900 schools (Jarrold-Grapes and Mahler, 2024).
- WHAT IT DOES: Suspended the baseline rule under which a retiree returning full-time lost pension and retiree health benefits (part-time return allowed only under an earnings cap of half the previous full-time salary). Under the window, retirees could return full-time on full salary while drawing their full pension — up to ~50% more income than a regular full-time teacher. Originally set to expire 2003; repeatedly extended; expired fall 2009, reverting to the half-salary cap (Jarrold-Grapes and Mahler, 2024).
- PUBLISHED SCALE: ~3,500 teachers (2.1% of the ~169,000-teacher NCERDC sample, 1999–2009) returned under the policy; over 1,500 schools (52%) hired at least one; RTW retirees peaked at up to 2% of the full-time workforce; by 2009 just over 35% of teachers who had been retirement-eligible in the previous year returned to full-time work. Returners were disproportionately Black (+8.7 pp), taught math (+4.6 pp), and worked in schools with more economically disadvantaged (+3.71 pp) and Black (+4.86 pp) students; 54% returned to their pre-retirement school. Returns collapsed when the window closed (lit_reserve Claim 6: dropped to zero after expiry in 2010).
- LEARNING: Supports saying that removing the pension penalty, without any bonus, can re-activate retirees at scale and that they flow disproportionately to high-need schools without a mandate; the authors argue marginal cost is the salary difference versus a novice (the annuity is paid regardless). It cannot support claims about effects on vacancies or student achievement (no vacancy data, no counterfactual), nor rule out that some hires displaced other candidates.
- SOURCES: Jarrold-Grapes and Mahler (2024).
- SINGULARITY: The only large-scale, peer-reviewed evaluation of a pension-rule re-entry window anywhere — the family's benchmark case.

### United States (Georgia) — HB 385 (2022), SB 150 (2026) and the statutory audit loop
- CONTEXT: A ~125,000-teacher state workforce with ~6,000 vacant or not-fully-certified positions; HB 385 (O.C.G.A. § 47-3-127.1), in effect from July 2022 with an automatic sunset of 30 June 2026, let selected TRS retirees teach full-time on full pension — and wrote a performance audit into the statute itself (Georgia Department of Audits and Accounts, 2025).
- WHAT IT DOES: Full-time rehire on full pension for certified teachers retired at least one year with at least 30 years of creditable TRS service, teaching primarily in one of the three highest-need content areas of their RESA region (determined annually by GaDOE from five-year vacancy surveys); employers pay both employer and employee TRS contributions. SB 150 (signed 5 May 2026) extends the program to 2030, keeps the 30-year requirement and lets school systems determine high-need areas (Georgia Department of Audits and Accounts, 2025; Teachers Retirement System of Georgia, 2026). Note: an early press snippet claiming SB 150 cut the requirement to 25 years is contradicted by the TRS Georgia page — do not use.
- PUBLISHED SCALE: 635 retired teachers worked full-time under HB 385 for at least one school year between FY2023 and FY2025, in 133 (55%) of eligible school systems; roughly 350 employed in any given year — under 1% of the ~125,000 workforce against ~6,000 vacancies. Associated TRS contributions USD 11.1 million (<1% of total); average salary USD 78,487. 45% of surveyed systems said statutory provisions blocked a hire they would otherwise have made; the audit judged the effect on both the shortage and TRS "minimal" (Georgia Department of Audits and Accounts, 2025).
- LEARNING: Supports the claim that stacking eligibility screens (30 years + one-year break + subject-and-region caps — the most restrictive combination among the eight southern states with similar laws) depresses take-up, on the authority of an official audit rather than advocacy. It cannot support cost-effectiveness comparisons (no counterfactual) or the claim that a looser design would have filled the ~6,000 vacancies.
- SOURCES: Georgia Department of Audits and Accounts (2025); Teachers Retirement System of Georgia (2026).
- SINGULARITY: The only re-entry law in the corpus with a statutorily mandated arms-length audit — the model "audit loop" for Lesson 12 (announce less, account more).

### United States (New Mexico) — HB 73 (2022) / SB 133 (2025), the no-caps regime
- CONTEXT: NMERB system with ~48,000 retirees; HB 73 passed near-unanimously (62-1 / 41-0) and was signed 1 March 2022, amid post-COVID shortages; it sits at the permissive end of the US design spectrum (New Mexico Legislature, 2022).
- WHAT IT DOES: NMERB retirees may return to any local administrative unit for up to 36 months (extended to 60 months by SB 133 of 2025) with no limits on salary or hours and no pension suspension, subject to: a 90-day layout after retirement, no pre-arranged return (IRC "bona fide retirement"), NMERB approval before starting, and non-refundable employee and employer contributions (no new service credit). Parallel tiers: work at ≤0.25 FTE; earnings below USD 15,000/year (raised to USD 25,000 by SB 133); the older 12-month-layout program (sunset 1 January 2024) (New Mexico Legislature, 2022; New Mexico Educational Retirement Board, 2025).
- PUBLISHED SCALE: FY23 (first year): 154 retirees used the new 36-month program; 390 worked under the sub-USD 15,000 option, 686 at ≤0.25 FTE, 1,716 under the old program — 2,792 working ERB retirees in total, with 56 violations (<2%), against ~48,000 potentially eligible retirees (New Mexico Educational Retirement Board, 2023). No outcome evaluation exists.
- LEARNING: Supports describing the "no-caps" end of the design spectrum and the value of tiered annual participation reporting; the 154-in-year-one figure also cautions that even a maximally permissive rule converts only a small fraction of the retiree stock. It cannot support any claim about effects on shortages.
- SOURCES: New Mexico Legislature (2022); New Mexico Educational Retirement Board (2023); New Mexico Educational Retirement Board (2025).
- SINGULARITY: The clean permissive counterpoint to Georgia, with official tiered take-up data but no evaluation.

### United States (Michigan) — PA 184 of 2022 and PA 147 of 2023
- CONTEXT: Michigan's nine-month sit-out dated from 2010 anti-"double-dipping" rules and was described as uniquely strict; two amendments to the Public School Employees Retirement Act simplified the regime after COVID-era shortages (Michigan Office of Retirement Services, 2022; Michigan Office of Retirement Services, 2023).
- WHAT IT DOES: PA 184 (effective 25 July 2022) replaced the critical-shortage patchwork with two criteria — a bona fide termination plus nine consecutive months retired — after which a retiree may work in any public-school position with no earnings limit and no loss of pension or insurance subsidy; no sunset. PA 147 (signed 10 October 2023) cut the wait to six months and allows work during the waiting period up to USD 15,100/calendar year; it sunsets 10 October 2028, when rules revert to PA 184's nine-month regime unless renewed (Michigan Office of Retirement Services, 2022; Michigan Office of Retirement Services, 2023).
- PUBLISHED SCALE: None published — no official counts of retirees returning under PA 184/147 and no evaluation. The claim that Michigan is now "the most permissive Midwest state" rests on a personal communication and is excluded from the paper.
- LEARNING: Supports a design point — simplification of rules plus an explicit sunset tying the loosening to the shortage episode — and nothing more. Cannot support any take-up or effectiveness claim.
- SOURCES: Michigan Office of Retirement Services (2022); Michigan Office of Retirement Services (2023). (Dellinger (2023), Chalkbeat, exists in the case file but is **[KEY MISSING]** from references.md — only needed if the "only state with a nine-month sit-out" quote is used.)
- SINGULARITY: **THIN** (no uptake data). Notable solely for the sunset-by-design and for pairing with Lindsay et al.'s Michigan reserve estimates (Family B).

### United States (Virginia) — Executive Directive Three (2022) on a 2001 statutory base
- CONTEXT: Vacancies reported rising from 1,063 (2019) to 2,563 (2021); Gov. Youngkin's Executive Directive Number Three (1 September 2022 — a directive, not an executive order) rode on a statutory mechanism dating from 2001 under Va. Code § 51.1-155 et seq. (Will, 2022).
- WHAT IT DOES: The statute lets VRS-retired teachers in designated critical shortage positions (teachers, principals, specialized student support, bus drivers, identified via annual VDOE surveys) draw full salary plus pension, conditional on a Virginia licence, a six-month break in service with no work for any VRS employer, and no pre-arranged return; closed to early-retirement-incentive and disability retirees. The six-month break (cut from 12), the specialized-support positions, employer VRS contributions and a sunset of 1 July 2028 come from the 2023 Acts of Assembly (Chapters 690, 707, 708). The directive itself added licensure fast-tracking and required all vacant K-12 posts to be classified so retirees can fill them (Commonwealth of Virginia, 2023; Virginia Retirement System, n.d.; Will, 2022).
- PUBLISHED SCALE: None published — no Virginia-specific uptake counts in official sources (Will (2022) gives counts only for other jurisdictions, e.g. ~36 rehired retirees in New Jersey).
- LEARNING: Supports distinguishing administrative signalling (the directive) from the statutory price-setting machinery beneath it (2001 statute, 2023 amendments); cannot support any effectiveness claim. The licensure element is treated in Family B.
- SOURCES: Will (2022); Commonwealth of Virginia (2023); Virginia Retirement System (n.d.).
- SINGULARITY: **THIN** (no uptake data). Illustrates how an executive announcement can repackage a two-decade-old pension rule as shortage policy.

### United States (Louisiana) — Critical-shortage re-employment: R.S. 11:710, Act 394 (2024), Act 344 (2026)
- CONTEXT: Not one law but an evolving TRSL framework; context of nearly 7,000 educators leaving Louisiana classrooms in 2021-22 (+14% on the prior year, per press coverage of Act 394). Progressive liberalisation culminated in a single unified law effective 1 July 2026 (Teachers' Retirement System of Louisiana, 2024; Teachers' Retirement System of Louisiana, 2026).
- WHAT IT DOES: Long-standing rule: retirees returning within 12 months face benefit suspension; earnings outside exceptions were capped at 25% of the annual benefit; a "critical shortage" hire requires the employer to advertise and receive fewer than three certified applicants. Act 394 of 2024 extended the no-suspension critical-shortage route to all TRSL retirees regardless of retirement date (12-month wait; contributions but no new service credit). Act 344 of 2026 consolidates the rules: keeps the 12-month rule and the pre-2010 grandfathered group; adds a no-impact route for retirees aged 65+; expands shortage areas; requires only general teacher certification for shortage hires (see Family B); raises the earnings limit for other retirees to 50% of final average compensation; and mandates a TRSL/Legislative Auditor actuarial report before the 2034 session (Teachers' Retirement System of Louisiana, 2024; Teachers' Retirement System of Louisiana, 2026).
- PUBLISHED SCALE: None published — no state-level uptake counts or shortage-effect evaluation; the only mandated review (actuarial) is due before 2034. Caution: the press claim that returning retirees are paid a first-year teacher salary conflicts with R.S. 11:710 (pay follows the salary schedule crediting prior experience) — treat as unverified.
- LEARNING: Supports the pattern of progressive liberalisation of an enabling pension framework and the "fewer-than-three-certified-applicants" test as a market-test alternative to subject lists; cannot support any take-up or effectiveness claim.
- SOURCES: Teachers' Retirement System of Louisiana (2024); Teachers' Retirement System of Louisiana (2026). (KLFY (2024), source of the ~7,000-leavers context, is **[KEY MISSING]** from references.md — flag or drop the context figure.)
- SINGULARITY: **THIN** (no uptake data). The age-65+ no-impact route and the built-in 2034 actuarial review are the design details worth keeping.

### Canada (Ontario) — OTPP re-employment day cap and co-sponsor governance
- CONTEXT: A standing pension rule (Schedule 1 to the Teachers' Pension Act, 1990) administered by the Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan, whose ceiling has been moved with labour-market conditions since 1990. Changes require agreement of the plan's co-sponsors: the Ontario government and the Ontario Teachers' Federation — not the Ontario College of Teachers, which is only the certification regulator (Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan, 2026; fix_austria_portugal D8 supersedes earlier renderings).
- WHAT IT DOES: A retired member may work 50 days per school year for a participating employer without affecting the pension; exceeding the limit suspends the pension for subsequent re-employed months. Cap history (two-way dial): pre-1990, 20 days; 1990, raised to 95 days for the first three post-retirement years (20 thereafter) amid a severe shortage; September 2012, cut to a flat 50 days after a Globe and Mail investigation; 2020/21–2022/23, temporarily 95 days by government–OTF agreement (expired 30 June 2023, with monthly board reporting on retiree hiring as an OTF condition); since July 2023, back at 50 — the OTF refused a further 95-day extension in March 2024 (Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan, 2026; OSSTF, n.d.; Hammer and Alphonso, 2010; Ontario Ministry of Education, 2022; OTF, 2023; OCEOTA, 2024; Alphonso, 2024).
- PUBLISHED SCALE: The 2010 investigation: Ontario's 10 largest boards spent CAD 108.3 million on retired teachers in 2008-09; retirees on supply earned nearly double new teachers (CAD 432.69/day vs CAD 227.36/day); ~12,774 newly certified teachers in 2008 chased ~6,500 openings (Hammer and Alphonso, 2010). Pandemic uptake signal: only about 60 of 142,000 retired members had expressed interest by January 2022; average retiree age 72 (Global News, 2022).
- LEARNING: Supports two claims found nowhere else: (i) a re-employment cap can be moved in both directions — including *down*, for documented equity reasons (crowding-out of new graduates); (ii) union co-sponsorship gives organised labour a veto, with the OTF's on-record position that "the Pension Plan is... not a tool for addressing labour market challenges". Cannot support claims about how many retirees the 95-day episodes actually mobilised (no systematic counts beyond the interest figure).
- SOURCES: Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan (2026); OSSTF (n.d.); Hammer and Alphonso (2010); Ontario Ministry of Education (2022); OTF (2023); OCEOTA (2024); Alphonso (2024); Global News (2022).
- SINGULARITY: The best-documented case of a pension cap used as a two-way policy dial, with hard numbers on cost and crowding-out — and the only documented rollback for equity reasons.

### France — Cumul emploi-retraite and the six-month carence
- CONTEXT: An enabling statutory framework, not a program: retired public-school teachers are re-hired by rectorats as contractuels or vacataires, mostly for replacements. Policy salience: ~330,000 teacher retirements expected by 2030 (press estimate, seen in snippet only). Legal basis: Code de la sécurité sociale Arts. L. 161-22 / L. 161-22-1; capped cumul and the six-month rule from the 2003 pension reform; liberalised by Art. 26 of Loi 2023-270 (Assemblée nationale, 2023).
- WHAT IT DOES: The six-month waiting period runs from the pension's effective date and applies to resuming work with one's *last employer* — which is what returning to the Éducation nationale entails; its stated purpose is to stop employers re-hiring their own just-retired staff at lower pay (Assemblée nationale, 2020; Assemblée nationale, 2023). Retirees meeting full-cumul conditions face a more liberal regime, and since the 2023 reform accrue new pension rights from resumed work. Live barrier debate: CER conditions (all pensions liquidated, 172 quarters, age 63.5) cap combined income for teachers who left before 2015 with early pension rights (~EUR 1,500 vs ~EUR 2,600 plus full salary for complete careers); the Government defended the contributory logic and pointed to 2026 harmonising legislation (Assemblée nationale, 2026). The flagship parliamentary plea to lift CER obstacles for substitute work (Bellamy, QE 7305) went unanswered and lapsed in June 2024 (Assemblée nationale, 2024).
- PUBLISHED SCALE: None published — no official aggregate figures on retired teachers returning under CER; anecdotally, the rectorat de Toulouse wrote to recently retired teachers in December 2021 and conceded the initiative had "not had the desired impact" (press, snippet only).
- LEARNING: Supports a barrier-case reading: a carence designed for anti-abuse reasons functions as a de facto re-entry tax on teachers because the state is a single employer, and income caps penalise pre-2015 leavers. Cannot support any uptake or effectiveness claim; also note France has no register-based estimate of its qualified-but-inactive pool (lit_reserve Claim 8).
- SOURCES: Assemblée nationale (2020); Assemblée nationale (2023); Assemblée nationale (2024); Assemblée nationale (2026). (Légifrance (2023a/b), France Bleu Occitanie (2022) and VousNousIls (2025) are **[KEY MISSING]** from references.md — cite the Assemblée nationale answers instead.)
- SINGULARITY: **THIN** on outcomes. The clearest documented case of an unanswered parliamentary plea — regulatory inertia as a finding in itself.

### Austria — Retiree reactivation and the prior-service crediting rule
- CONTEXT: A ~123,000-teacher workforce hit by the baby-boomer retirement wave (EAG 2025: 4,778 unfilled positions, 4.6% — vacancies equal to 107% of the prior year's teacher-training graduates; OECD, 2025a). No single program: ad hoc measures by the nine Bildungsdirektionen from late 2022 — Vienna wrote to 300 retired teachers; Salzburg contacted all retirees up to age 67 with job tickets and premiums; Styria pushed principal outreach; Vorarlberg created the "Arbeitsplatz Schule" service point (APA/ORF, 2023).
- WHAT IT DOES: Returning retirees are hired on new contracts alongside their full pension. The price rule (superseding earlier renderings, per fix_austria_portugal A4.1): formerly civil-servant (beamtete) teachers are excluded by law from prior-service crediting for any period already generating a pension, so they are re-employed at the teacher *starting salary*; former contract-scheme teachers (Vertragsbedienstete) have their service credited and broadly recover pre-retirement pay (BMBWF, 2023, answer to Question 19, p. 18). Salzburg flagged the crediting rule as requiring federal legislative change (APA/ORF, 2023).
- PUBLISHED SCALE: Audited: 1,062 retired teachers in total resumed teaching across Austria between school years 2018/19 and 2023/24 (Upper Austria 227, Tyrol 49), peaking in 2021/22–2022/23 and declining thereafter — the Court of Audit stresses the measure's temporary character (Rechnungshof, 2025, pp. 24-25). The March-2023 snapshot (Vorarlberg 42, Upper Austria 36, Tyrol 10; definitions differ) remains citable as a same-year snapshot (APA/ORF, 2023). Qualitative mechanism: nine episodic interviews with retired Austrian teachers (six returners, three non-returners, within a 14-interview study) find return decisions are biographically rooted — individual, family, school and system factors — with continuing professional contact with the former school aiding return (Resch, 2025a; Resch, 2025b; abstract-verified only; do not attribute the crediting-rule barrier to Resch).
- LEARNING: Supports saying that near-universal outreach yields modest, declining returns when the pay rule prices ex-civil-servants at beginner salary, and that Austria offers Europe's only audited national take-up figure. Cannot support "dozens nationwide" (superseded) or any claim that the crediting rule causally explains low take-up (no design variation).
- SOURCES: APA/ORF (2023); Rechnungshof (2025); BMBWF (2023); Resch (2025a); Resch (2025b); OECD (2025a).
- SINGULARITY: The only European case with both an audited national take-up count and a peer-reviewed qualitative study of the return decision.

### England — Teachers' Pension Scheme abatement, dissolving by scheme succession
- CONTEXT: England's regulatory posture toward returning pensioners is milder than the US states and softening by design: abatement is confined to legacy final-salary benefits as career-average membership (all service since 2015) matures (Teachers' Pensions, n.d.; fix_netherlands_more_cases §4).
- WHAT IT DOES: Only final-salary Age or Premature retirement benefits are abated: a retiree may earn, in any tax year, up to their index-linked "salary of reference" minus their annual pension before the pension is suspended for the remainder of the scheme year. Career-average benefits are not abated at all (except ill-health); actuarially reduced early-retirement benefits are never abated. Compliance runs through employers' monthly data returns or a Certificate of Re-employment, with overpayments recoverable (Teachers' Pensions, n.d.).
- PUBLISHED SCALE: None published (rule, not program).
- LEARNING: Supports the observation that a pension barrier can disappear passively through scheme succession rather than active reform — the constraint attaches to a shrinking cohort of entitlements. Cannot support any behavioural claim about returns induced.
- SOURCES: Teachers' Pensions (n.d.).
- SINGULARITY: The "dissolving barrier" case — useful as the zero-reform endpoint of the Family A spectrum.

### Japan — Routine re-employment of retirees as standing staffing practice
- CONTEXT: MEXT's first national shortage survey found 2,558 unfilled positions (0.31% of 836,079 required) at 5.8% of public schools at the start of AY2021, rising to 3,827 unfilled positions in the 2025 survey (Nippon.com, 2022; Nippon.com, 2026).
- WHAT IT DOES: Boards of education conventionally re-employ teachers after mandatory retirement until pension age — a routine rule-based practice rather than a branded scheme; retirees also dominate the part-time adjunct/substitute pool. No specific MEXT circular instructing boards to recruit retirees is verified — do not cite one (fix_netherlands_more_cases §2).
- PUBLISHED SCALE: In one prefecture studied in depth: 52.7% of part-time adjunct hires were in their sixties and 7.1% in their seventies (~60% of adjuncts are retirees); but only about 35% of age-based retirees in elementary schools (55% in junior high) now seek full-time re-employment, and ageing adjuncts increasingly decline demanding replacement work (Sakuma and Shimazaki, 2024, pp. 201, 203).
- LEARNING: Supports the caution that a reserve composed mainly of retirees depletes on its own schedule — heavy routine dependence is fragile as aspiration rates fall. Cannot support national generalisation (single-prefecture case study) or any claim about a deliberate policy design.
- SOURCES: Nippon.com (2022); Nippon.com (2026); Sakuma and Shimazaki (2024).
- SINGULARITY: The system where retiree re-employment is the default, not the intervention — and is visibly running out.

## FAMILY B — Certification and licensing routes back

This family removes the regulatory tollgates between a qualified former teacher and a classroom: lapsed practising certificates, refresher requirements, re-accreditation paperwork, vetting. Governments seek conversion of an already-qualified stock at low fiscal cost; the routes enabled run from statutory relief-teaching allowances and refresher waivers to one-stop administrative outreach. The family's core evidence is that licence lapse is a leading marker of reserve decay (Michigan: 44–49% of the non-teaching hold expired certificates) and that friction removal works best as a standing rule tied to a job offer, not an annually renewed crisis waiver.

### New Zealand — Certification-barrier removal package (20-half-days rule, TER waiver, fees paid)
- CONTEXT: The Ministry of Education and the Teaching Council operate a bundle of instruments (there is no official "Returning to Teaching pathway" — a team label) that together constitute the OECD's clearest example of regulatory barrier removal for re-entry (cases_england_nz §3).
- WHAT IT DOES: (a) Standing statutory rule: a registered teacher whose practising certificate has expired may relief teach up to 20 half-days per calendar year without a certificate (Education and Training Act 2020, Sch. 3, cl. 10(9)), with Council extensions where there is an employment offer (New Zealand Legislation, 2020; New Zealand Ministry of Education, 2026). (b) TER waiver: a returner with a relief-teaching job offer does not need the normally required Teacher Education Refresh even after 5+ years out — instead a light-touch "Kia Maia | Future-Ready Teaching Plan" agreed with the principal; repeatedly extended, the waiver has effectively been mainstreamed — the Council's 2026 registration policy makes TER contingent mainly on *not* having a job offer, and the Open Polytechnic TER programme is closed to new learners (Teaching Council of Aotearoa New Zealand, 2026). (c) Government pays practising-certificate fees and levies until 30 June 2028 (New Zealand Ministry of Education, 2026).
- PUBLISHED SCALE: None published — no uptake figures or evaluation for the TER waiver / relief-teaching route; the Ministry's pages carry no participant counts. This is the case's key evidence gap.
- LEARNING: Supports the design principle of making re-licensure friction removal a standing rule keyed to a job offer (the market test) rather than an annual waiver, combined with a low-commitment relief-teaching on-ramp and zero certification fees. Cannot support any effectiveness claim — well documented as policy design, undocumented as outcomes.
- SOURCES: New Zealand Legislation (2020); New Zealand Ministry of Education (2026); Teaching Council of Aotearoa New Zealand (2026). (Ministry of Education (2025a-d) relief-teaching/returning pages are **[KEY MISSING]** from references.md if pinpoints are needed.)
- SINGULARITY: The most complete de-frictioning of re-certification in the corpus — mainstreamed, not sunset — with zero published outcomes.

### United States (Virginia) — Fast-track relicensing by executive directive
- CONTEXT: Companion to the Family A Virginia ficha: Executive Directive Three (1 September 2022) was, on the licensing margin, the directive's real innovation, since the pension mechanism predated it by two decades (Will, 2022).
- WHAT IT DOES: Directs the Superintendent of Public Instruction to expedite/issue renewal licences to retired (and out-of-state) teachers whose licences lapsed, and to coordinate with VRS so all vacant K-12 teaching positions are classified to allow retired teachers to fill them (Will, 2022; Commonwealth of Virginia, 2023).
- PUBLISHED SCALE: None published — no counts of licences expedited or hires enabled.
- LEARNING: Supports describing executive action as a fast, legislature-free lever on the licensing margin; cannot support any claim that it moved returns.
- SOURCES: Will (2022); Commonwealth of Virginia (2023).
- SINGULARITY: **THIN.** The corpus's example of relicensing by executive stroke — pure administrative de-frictioning.

### United States (Louisiana) — Relicensing fold-in under Act 344 (2026)
- CONTEXT: Element of the unified return-to-work law (Family A ficha): before Act 344, critical-shortage hires had to match specific certification categories (Teachers' Retirement System of Louisiana, 2026).
- WHAT IT DOES: From 1 July 2026, a critical-shortage hire requires only general teacher certification (not subject-specific certification), alongside streamlined advertising of the vacancy; the shortage test remains fewer than three certified applicants (Teachers' Retirement System of Louisiana, 2026).
- PUBLISHED SCALE: None published.
- LEARNING: Supports noting that certification-matching requirements are themselves an adjustable screen, and that legislatures bundle licensing relief inside pension bills; nothing more.
- SOURCES: Teachers' Retirement System of Louisiana (2026).
- SINGULARITY: **THIN.** A one-line but concrete example of licensing relief folded into pension legislation.

### Ireland — Career Break Scheme substitute rules (annual suspension of restrictions)
- CONTEXT: A leave-retention scheme, not re-entry legislation: registered teachers may take unpaid career breaks of at least one school year, up to 5 years at a time and 10 over a career, with a right of return (Chapter 7, Circular 0054/2019). Substitute shortages (1,847 vacant posts reported July 2025, press snippet) turned the career-break population into a substitute pool (Department of Education, 2019).
- WHAT IT DOES: The scheme proper bars career-break teachers from publicly funded teaching posts and caps substitute work (90 substitute days/year primary, 300 hours post-primary under earlier suspensions). Information Note TC/IN 0005/2025 suspends those restrictions for 2025/26 — career-break teachers may substitute without restriction, including part-time, paid as regular substitutes — an annual, temporary suspension renewed since at least 2021/22 (Department of Education and Youth, 2025). Substitute days count toward incremental salary progression and superannuation, and can help build Supplementary Redeployment Panel eligibility — but via separate incremental-credit and panel rules, not the information note itself (do not attribute causally to the 2025/26 change).
- PUBLISHED SCALE: None published for the substitution flexibility — the Department publishes career-break counts in its Teacher Statistics series, but figures were not retrievable; no evaluation exists. Union context: ASTI attacked curtailment proposals as "cosmetic" measures that could push career-break teachers to resign outright.
- LEARNING: Supports the idea of activating *temporary* leavers as a substitute pool while preserving increment and panel incentives, and illustrates the anti-pattern of annually renewed waivers that returners cannot plan around (ch5_synthesis Lesson 8). Cannot support uptake or effectiveness claims, and should not be presented as re-entry legislation for former or retired teachers.
- SOURCES: Department of Education (2019); Department of Education and Youth (2025). (St. Senan's Education Office (n.d.), TUI (2024), RTÉ News (2022) and Circular 0074/2025 are **[KEY MISSING]** from references.md — needed only for the day-cap and panel-threshold detail.)
- SINGULARITY: **THIN** on outcomes. The corpus's only case targeting people on leave rather than leavers — the cheapest reserve to activate because it never fully left.

### United States (Michigan) — Licence-survival evidence (Lindsay et al.)
- CONTEXT: Not a policy but the evidence base a licensing policy needs: REL Midwest's analysis of Michigan certification and employment records plus a Michigan Department of Education survey (administered December 2019 – January 2020; report April 2021). Note: no "2020 Preliminary Findings" document exists — citing one would fabricate a reference (ch5_synthesis B1).
- WHAT IT DOES (finds): Of 141,810 certified teachers, 61,252 (43%) were not teaching in a Michigan public school in 2017/18; 70% of the non-teaching had not taught at any point in the previous five years; 93% were younger than 60. Critically for Family B: 44–49% of the non-teaching held expired certificates, versus 6% of active teachers. Survey layer (30% response rate, explicitly non-representative): 33% put higher salary in their top-three reasons for not teaching; an increase in salary was the most frequently selected incentive to return, with retirees keeping retirement benefits, better benefits and loan forgiveness also ranking highly (Lindsay, Gnedko-Berry and Wan, 2021).
- PUBLISHED SCALE: As above (61,252 stock; 44–49% expired certificates among them; 17,551/59,433 survey responses, analytic sample 9,842).
- LEARNING: Supports saying that licence lapse is a leading, measurable marker of reserve decay — roughly half the visible reserve would face re-certification before any return — which makes relicensing friction a first-order policy margin. Cannot support representative claims about motivations (response-rate caveat is stated in the report itself) or treat the 61,000 as an available supply (expired certificates, out-of-state residence ~16%, subject mismatch).
- SOURCES: Lindsay, Gnedko-Berry and Wan (2021).
- SINGULARITY: The only case in the corpus that quantifies how fast certification decays once teachers leave — the empirical warrant for the whole family.

### Australia (NSW) — Teachers Re-Engage: record-based outreach with one-stop re-accreditation
- CONTEXT: Launched November 2023 by the NSW Department of Education after vacancies had peaked above 3,000 under the previous government (roughly 1,800–2,250 at launch); an administrative outreach initiative needing no legislation (NSW Government, 2024; NSW Department of Education, 2024).
- WHAT IT DOES: Direct contact of teachers who had resigned from the NSW public system in the previous five years, using the Department's own staffing records, plus a simplified re-employment process — support for regaining NESA accreditation, approval to teach and the Working with Children Check bundled into one contact; placement into casual/temporary work with permanent and tutoring roles also available. No program-specific bonus; the pull factors cited are the October 2023 pay deal and workload measures (NSW Government, 2024).
- PUBLISHED SCALE: Full funnel as of 8 May 2024: >1,500 contacted since November 2023 → >260 expressed interest → 145 placed in casual/temporary roles, collectively covering >600 schools; ~60% returning from retirement; the Annual Report 2023-24 confirms "around 150 former teachers return... across 600 public schools" (NSW Government, 2024; NSW Department of Education, 2024). No formal evaluation or cost analysis; the program is absent from the 2024-25 Annual Report and no figures have been published since May 2024 (NSW Department of Education, 2025), though the returner webpage remains live (NSW Department of Education, 2026).
- LEARNING: Supports the design principle of using the employer's own records for personalised outreach with a one-stop re-licensure route at near-zero marginal cost — the only published contacts→interest→placements funnel anywhere, with a retiree share. Cannot support attribution of NSW's vacancy decline to the program (government credits the whole package), any cost-effectiveness claim, or durability (reporting stopped; lapsed into business-as-usual).
- SOURCES: NSW Government (2024); NSW Department of Education (2024); NSW Department of Education (2025); NSW Department of Education (2026).
- SINGULARITY: The only complete published re-entry funnel in the corpus — and a textbook Lesson-12 case of reporting that stops after the launch year.

### England — Return to Teaching pilot (2015-16) and the RTTA adviser service
- CONTEXT: One narrative arc, not two schemes: the NCTL's 2015-16 pilot (grant-funded school partnerships supporting returners to EBacc secondary subjects), its successor Returners Engagement Programme (from 2017, places tied to actual vacancies), and the standing DfE Return to Teaching Adviser service (costed from 2019/20; expanded to all secondary subjects autumn 2022, then primary and secondary by 2023). Context: ~243,900 qualified teachers under 60 out of service in 2016; c. 17,000 return to state schools each year, about one-third of all new entrants (Buchanan et al., 2018; Department for Education, 2023).
- WHAT IT DOES: Pilot — non-financial support only (subject-knowledge and refresher training, classroom experience, mentoring) via 60 school partnerships; no payments to returners; grants to schools totalled £586,900. Broad eligibility: QTS holders without a current post, including never-served and overseas-trained. RTTA — free one-to-one adviser support (application/interview preparation, signposting to courses and vacancies); England has no return-specific bursary (Buchanan et al., 2018; Department for Education, 2023; NCTL, 2017).
- PUBLISHED SCALE: Pilot: 5,729 registrations (target ~3,000) → 426 recruited, 354 supported → 27 (8% of supported) in permanent EBacc posts in state secondaries by November 2016, 62 (18%) in any state post; £17,800 per supported returner employed; assuming 50% deadweight, £37,400 per additional returner — "unlikely to represent good value for money"; returner retention is worse than new teachers' (odds of leaving after the first year back 60% higher than average) (Buchanan et al., 2018). Typology: career breakers (11% of participants) returned at double the rate of other groups and want part-time work schools rarely advertise (Worth and Sharp, 2018). RTTA: £361,345 (2019/20) and £325,563 (2020/21); ~128 recorded returns in the three restricted subjects over the two years (physics: 23 in two years, per Walker, 2022) — implied ~£5,400 per recorded returner, additionality unknown (UK Parliament, 2022). No independent evaluation of RTTA exists. By July 2026 the Government reported more returners than at any point in ten years, without a returner-specific scheme (UK Parliament, 2026).
- LEARNING: Supports the family's sharpest conversion lessons: interest is cheap and conversion is the constraint; support must be synchronised with the hiring cycle and tied to vacancies; cheap advisory support converts little but costs little; additionality must be measured or the cost-per-returner number is meaningless. Cannot support "advice works" (no evaluation) or "pilots fail" in general (the failure mechanisms — timing, jobs mismatch — were identified and corrected in the successor design).
- SOURCES: Buchanan et al. (2018); Worth and Sharp (2018); NCTL (2017); UK Parliament (2022); Walker (2022); Department for Education (2023); UK Parliament (2026).
- SINGULARITY: The only re-entry programme anywhere with a full independent evaluation including cost-per-additional-returner — and the negative verdict is itself the policy lesson.

### The Bahamas — Teacher Reservist Program (standing reserve with fast-track vetting)
- CONTEXT: A national shortfall of ~300 teachers across 162 public schools on 24 islands (~40,000 students); announced 16 July 2026 by DPM/Education Minister Chester Cooper, on the platform of the pre-existing Supply Teacher Program — formalising an already-heavy de facto reliance on retirees in supply roles (Cooper, 2026; ZNS Bahamas, 2026).
- WHAT IT DOES: A standing, registered reserve of qualified Bahamians — recent graduates, retired educators, and trained teachers who left for other professions (especially maths, science, technology, trades, arts) — that schools can call on; a multi-agency Teacher Recruitment Task Force (foreign affairs, public service, labour, police, NAECOB) fast-tracks vetting and credential assessment; physical application unit plus online portal. No financial incentives published; international recruitment (e.g. Ghana) only where local recruitment is exhausted (Cooper, 2026; ZNS Bahamas, 2026).
- PUBLISHED SCALE: Announcement-stage: target of up to 100 Bahamians before September 2026 within the ~300 gap; 42 applications submitted (27 received) and 60–100 "in the process of enlisting" by 17 July 2026 — figures for the whole drive, not the reservist pool, and reported in press whose keys are **[KEY MISSING]** from references.md (The Tribune/Nassau Guardian items). No results, evaluation or cost data.
- LEARNING: Supports a design contrast only — institutionalising a standing reserve rather than running a one-off rescue campaign — and must be dated "as of mid-2026". Cannot support any outcome claim.
- SOURCES: Cooper (2026); ZNS Bahamas (2026).
- SINGULARITY: **THIN** (weeks old at research date). The only "standing reserve register" design in the corpus.

## FAMILY C — Money attached to the return

Here governments attach cash to the act of returning: per-head subsidies to employers, monthly supplements, annual allowances, relocation grants, place-based bonding payments. The aim is to buy conversions the rules alone do not produce; the routes enabled range from classroom re-entry contracts to rural postings and paid tutoring roles. The family's evidence is sobering: modest money is under-subscribed where conditions caused the exit (Netherlands, Portugal), works best where it compensates a specific, targeted friction (China's rural allowance), and everywhere shows that paying people to *stay* outperforms paying them to *come back* at equal money (Portugal's 63-vs-~2,200 asymmetry).

### Netherlands — Herintreders subsidy (2017-20) inside the RAL/RAP regional infrastructure
- CONTEXT: The paper's benchmark reserve-measurement country (hidden reserve ~62,000, ~4.5 times the 13,600 FTE shortage at end-2023; Somers, Groot and van Merode, 2024b; DUO's stricter 2026 series: 34,000 primary + 64,000 secondary/vocational, shrinking 17-18% over 2015-24; DUO, 2026) also ran a dedicated activation instrument (Ministerie van OCW, 2017).
- WHAT IT DOES: The Regeling tegemoetkoming herintreders primair onderwijs (Staatscourant 2017, 62002; November 2017 – October 2020 applications; lapsed 1 January 2022) paid school boards — not teachers — a one-off EUR 2,500 per returner appointed for at least six months who had not taught for at least twelve consecutive months; it financed induction and guidance costs, drawing on lessons from the 1998-2002 "Terug voor de klas" project; first come, first served; original ceiling EUR 1.25 million ("room for 500 herintreders") against an estimated primary-only silent reserve of at least 31,000 (Ministerie van OCW, 2017; Ministerie van OCW, 2019a). Returner activation was then mainstreamed into regional schemes: RAL (2019/20, EUR 9 million) and RAP (2020-23, c. EUR 30 million for the first two school years across 71 partnerships of boards plus teacher-training institutions), which required a regional information desk for prospective (re-)entrants and listed "activating the silent reserve" among fundable goals (De Jong et al., 2022).
- PUBLISHED SCALE: Just over 100 returners subsidised in 2018 (Ministerie van OCW, 2019b); nearly 200 cumulative by autumn 2019 (Tweede Kamer, 2019); the 2019 extension *lowered* annual ceilings to at most 120 (2019) and 100 (2020) grants explicitly because the budget "was not exhausted" — final 2019/2020 counts unpublished; the regulation lapsed without a published evaluation (Ministerie van OCW, 2019a). RAP evaluation (process-focused, self-reported, no causal estimate): side-entry goals mostly met, but tailored support to herintreders was among the least-realised activities, and several regions reported more side-entrant and returner applications than schools could place (De Jong et al., 2022, pp. 37, 60).
- LEARNING: Supports the emblematic mismatch — the country with the best-measured reserve attached a marginal, under-subscribed EUR 2,500 employer subsidy to it — and the finding that absorption capacity in schools, not applicant interest, emerged as a binding constraint. Cannot support "subsidies don't work" (the instrument was tiny, paid to boards, and never evaluated) nor any causal effect on shortages.
- SOURCES: Ministerie van OCW (2017); Ministerie van OCW (2019a); Ministerie van OCW (2019b); Tweede Kamer (2019); De Jong et al. (2022); Somers, Groot and van Merode (2024b); DUO (2026).
- SINGULARITY: The under-subscription is documented in the regulator's own words (ceilings cut "because the budget was not exhausted") — a rare official admission of weak demand for return money.

### Portugal — Decreto-Lei 51/2024: the returner route (Art. 5) versus the stay-on supplement (Art. 6)
- CONTEXT: Part of the "+Aulas +Sucesso" plan (announced June 2024, minister Fernando Alexandre) against shortage recruitment groups and understaffed schools; the decree created two instruments that press coverage — and earlier drafts — conflated (Decreto-Lei n.º 51/2024; fix_austria_portugal D1 supersedes all earlier renderings).
- WHAT IT DOES: Article 5 — teachers retired five years or less may return on fixed-term contracts in shortage subjects or understaffed schools, keeping their full pension plus an additional compensation indexed to the first step of the teaching career salary scale, pro-rated by teaching hours; annual quota by government despacho; DGAE electronic ranking. Article 6 — a monthly EUR 750 supplement for teachers who meet retirement conditions but postpone retirement and stay in the classroom until the end of the school year. The EUR 750 belongs to Article 6, not to returners. In July 2026 the government narrowed the supplement for 2026/27 to shortage areas only (eight pedagogical zones, 235 school units in Greater Lisbon, Setúbal Peninsula, Algarve), the minister conceding the blanket version "in many cases was not justified" (RTP, 2026).
- PUBLISHED SCALE: Returner route, 2024/25: government target 200; 79-80 retirees applied; 63 accepted (Diário de Notícias, 2024); 55 validated and placed in the first January-2025 wave, average age ~68 (Renascença, 2024). Stay-on supplement: ~2,200 teachers extended their careers with the EUR 750 in 2025/26 (RTP, 2026). Union counter-narrative: retirement itself as revealed preference ("the idea that the retired would return is an illusion" — Fenprof; "accumulated fatigue and the school we live in is not inviting" — FNE) (SÁBADO, 2024). EPO 2024 cites the Portuguese measure as salary increases for retirement-eligible teachers — consistent with the Art. 6 reading (OECD, 2024, p. 51).
- LEARNING: Supports the corpus's cleanest revealed-preference asymmetry: at comparable money, preventing an exit (~2,200) vastly outperforms reversing one (63) — and supports targeting corrections after blanket roll-outs. Cannot support "€750 failed to attract returners" (returners were never offered the €750) nor an evaluation-grade verdict (funnel counts only, no counterfactual).
- SOURCES: Decreto-Lei n.º 51/2024; Diário de Notícias (2024); Renascença (2024); SÁBADO (2024); RTP (2026); OECD (2024). (CNN Portugal (2026) and DGAE (2025) are **[KEY MISSING]** from references.md — RTP (2026) covers the 2,200 and the narrowing.)
- SINGULARITY: A natural experiment running the retention and re-entry margins simultaneously under one law — the paper's headline Family C result.

### China — National Silver Age Teachers Action Plan (rural retiree teaching allowance)
- CONTEXT: Successor to the 2018 Silver Age Lecturing Plan (MOE + MOF; target 10,000 recruits 2018-20 for rural compulsory-education schools in deeply impoverished areas); expanded 14 July 2023 into a ten-agency Action Plan (MOE and MOF, 2018; MOE et al., 2023).
- WHAT IT DOES: Builds a silver-age teacher corps of roughly 120,000 within about three years of 2023 — across five strands (higher, vocational, basic, lifelong, private education), including part-time and volunteer service, not only rural re-employment. Basic-education strand: retired principals, teaching-research staff, special-grade and senior teachers, generally aged 65 or under (70 for other strands), keep their full pension plus working funds of 20,000 yuan per person per year for the rural lecturing strand — fully centrally funded in western provinces, 50:50 in central, provincially funded in eastern — plus housing, once-per-semester travel and insurance from hosts; targeted at poverty-lifted, ethnic-minority, old-revolutionary-base and border counties; roles include teaching, demonstration lessons, vice-principalships and mentoring young local teachers (MOE et al., 2023; State Council, 2023; MOE and MOF, 2018).
- PUBLISHED SCALE: By mid-2023 the predecessor schemes had cumulatively recruited over 20,000 retired primary/secondary teachers and nearly 1,000 retired professors (State Council, 2023); the October-2025 round targets 7,000 retired teachers for county schools (MOE, 2025). No official tally of progress toward 120,000 as of August 2026; no independent impact evaluation. Secondary-press caveat: some localities reportedly use silver-age recruits to cut formal posts or delay hiring new teachers (R.I.S.E. Magazine, 2025 — secondary, use cautiously).
- LEARNING: Supports the targeting principle — money attached to service exactly where friction and need are highest (remote rural counties), minimising deadweight — and the observation that all figures are government-reported targets/recruitment counts, not audited outcomes. Cannot support effectiveness claims, and the 120,000 must not be described as re-employment of classroom retirees only (it spans five strands including volunteers).
- SOURCES: MOE and MOF (2018); MOE et al. (2023); State Council (2023); MOE (2025); R.I.S.E. Magazine (2025). (People's Daily Online (2025) and Zhang, Fang and Jiang (2025) are **[KEY MISSING]** from references.md if used.)
- SINGULARITY: The world's largest retiree re-deployment scheme in education, fully documented in primary government instruments — and entirely unevaluated.

### Germany (Länder, esp. Hesse) — Retiree outreach letters with earnings-cap lifts and supplements
- CONTEXT: No register-based reserve statistics exist for Germany; with 457,000 retired/pensioned teachers (2021) and a KMK-estimated shortage of ~25,000 by 2025, the policy debate defaults to the only visible pool — retirees (Deutsches Schulportal, 2023; lit_reserve Claim 7).
- WHAT IT DOES: 13 of 16 Länder actively recruit pensioned teachers (exceptions: Brandenburg, Rhineland-Palatinate, Saarland); typical instruments are lifting/raising earnings limits (Hinzuverdienstgrenzen), ~10% salary supplements where pension caps bind, flexible hours and no age caps. Hesse wrote to ~7,000 retired teachers in 2022/23; its ministry's Handlungskonzept (March 2024, §1.10) targets recently retired teachers among short-term measures to "unlock existing resources" (Deutsches Schulportal, 2023; Hessisches Ministerium für Kultus, Bildung und Chancen, 2024).
- PUBLISHED SCALE: Reactivated counts per Land (2022/23-era): Baden-Württemberg 531, Bavaria ~690, Berlin ~400, Hamburg 285, Schleswig-Holstein 390, Thuringia ~220; >5,000 retirees working nationwide — under 1% of the ~800,000-strong workforce (Deutsches Schulportal, 2023). Hesse's "~550 posts filled" figure covers the combined short-term package (retirees + service extensions + part-time top-ups), NOT retirees alone (Hessisches Ministerium für Kultus, Bildung und Chancen, 2024, p. 8). Do not use the circulating "Hesse won back ~200 retirees" figure without its press source.
- LEARNING: Supports the pattern of near-universal outreach with small yields (matching Austria and Portugal), and the measurement point that without a register the reserve debate collapses onto retirees. Cannot support cross-Land comparison of instrument effectiveness (counts are survey-collected, definitions vary) or attribute Hesse's ~550 to retirees.
- SOURCES: Deutsches Schulportal (2023); Hessisches Ministerium für Kultus, Bildung und Chancen (2024).
- SINGULARITY: A 16-system natural comparison of outreach-plus-supplement designs — visible in one journalistic survey, but with no harmonised outcome data.

### New Zealand — Teacher Bonding Scheme and Overseas Relocation Grant (money open to returners)
- CONTEXT: The money layer paired with New Zealand's certification de-frictioning (Family B): from October 2025 the Teacher Bonding Scheme replaced the Voluntary Bonding Scheme (which was open to new teachers only, capped at NZD 17,500 over five years) and the BeTTER Jobs Programme (New Zealand Government, 2025).
- WHAT IT DOES: TBS: any teacher — beginning, experienced or overseas-trained — appointed to a designated vacancy at a Priority Staffing School receives rising after-year payments of NZD 5,000 / 6,500 / 8,000 / 9,500 / 11,000, up to NZD 40,000 before tax over five years; 185 places per year, first-come-first-served, one place per school per year (New Zealand Government, 2025). Overseas Relocation Grant: up to NZD 10,000 toward relocation for NZ-trained teachers returning from overseas (and overseas-trained teachers), conditional on registration/certification, a 12+-month employment agreement, application within 90 days of starting, and no NZ teaching role in the previous 12 months; annual funding capped.
- PUBLISHED SCALE: None published for returners — TBS's first cohort was placed from late 2025 (applications closed 26 November 2025), too new for results; the "1,360 relocation grants per year" figure comes from a recruitment agency, not official sources — treat as indicative only.
- LEARNING: Supports describing place-based, back-loaded bonding money newly opened to experienced/returning teachers (only from October 2025 — earlier drafts claiming experienced-teacher eligibility before then are wrong); cannot support any take-up or effectiveness claim.
- SOURCES: New Zealand Government (2025). (Ministry of Education (2025b/2025c) TBS and relocation pages, RNZ (2025) and NZ Herald (2025) are **[KEY MISSING]** from references.md.)
- SINGULARITY: **THIN** (too new). The only scheme in the corpus that prices *location* rather than return per se.

### United States — Paid tutoring and mentoring roles for retirees (money for partial return)
- CONTEXT: The best-evidenced US deployments of retirees attach modest money to redesigned part-time roles rather than full classroom return — the corpus's role-redesign endpoint of Family C (ch5_synthesis Lesson 9).
- WHAT IT DOES: (a) Number Rockets: 86 tutors recruited from local pools of retired and substitute teachers, hired and trained by the study team, delivered scripted Tier-2 grade-1 math tutoring. (b) Aurora (CO) Retired Mentors for New Teachers: recently retired master educators paid USD 41/hour, caseloads of 3-7 probationary teachers in Title I elementary schools, two years, weekly one-on-ones. (c) AARP Foundation Experience Corps: volunteers aged 50+ (retired educators are a subset, share undocumented — not a former-teacher programme per se) tutor K-3 readers; historically stipended slots up to USD 278/month via AmeriCorps streams.
- PUBLISHED SCALE: Number Rockets: 994 at-risk first graders, 76 schools, four urban districts each in a different REL Southwest state; ES +0.34 on TEMA-3 general math proficiency; WWC "meets standards with reservations" (Gersten et al., 2015; Rolfhus et al., 2012). Aurora: RCT of 77 probationary teachers in 11 schools; the REL report's year-1 math effect (+0.064 SD, p=.045) does not survive its own multiple-comparison adjustment; WWC (Study #88756, reviewed August 2019, standards met *without* reservations) characterises all reviewed domains — reading, math, teacher retention — as indeterminate; cost ~USD 171 per student per year (DeCesare, McClelland and Randel, 2017; What Works Clearinghouse, 2019). Experience Corps: RCT of 883 randomised grade 1-3 students (analytic sample 881: 430 T/451 C) — reading gains ES 0.13 (passage comprehension) and 0.16 (teacher-rated skills) (Morrow-Howell et al., 2009; What Works Clearinghouse, 2010 — Quick Review, February 2010); volunteer-side RCTs show health benefits including physical activity and cortical/hippocampal volume gains (Fried et al., 2004; Varma et al., 2016; Carlson et al., 2015); scale 2023/24: 1,284 volunteers, 3,659 students, 18 communities (AARP, 2025).
- LEARNING: Supports saying that a tutor workforce of retired and substitute teachers can reproduce, at scale, effects first obtained with graduate students, and that tutoring by older adults is the rare dual-benefit intervention (students and volunteers); mentoring claims must stay modest — the one rigorous RCT of retirees as new-teacher mentors is a qualified null under WWC standards, so mentoring should be justified on instructional support and retiree wellbeing, not mentee retention. Cannot support the "pension savings fund mentor stipends" mechanism (rests on a single personal communication, dropped per team decision) or Experience Corps as a former-teacher programme.
- SOURCES: Gersten et al. (2015); Rolfhus et al. (2012); DeCesare, McClelland and Randel (2017); What Works Clearinghouse (2019); Morrow-Howell et al. (2009); What Works Clearinghouse (2010); Fried et al. (2004); Varma et al. (2016); Carlson et al. (2015); AARP (2025).
- SINGULARITY: The only corner of the whole corpus with RCT-grade evidence — obtained precisely where retirees are *not* returned to full classrooms.
